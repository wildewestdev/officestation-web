How liquidation works for a Surprise office

The process is built around one document: a written net offer. You know what the furniture is worth, what it costs to remove, and what the difference is, before anyone lifts a desk.

  1. Tell us what you have. Send a rough count of workstations, chairs, desks, conference tables, files and storage. Photos help. If you want a ballpark first, try the liquidation estimator.
  2. Walkthrough. In Greater Phoenix, including Surprise, walkthroughs are typically scheduled within 48 hours. We note brands, condition, quantities and access details like stairs, parking and loading.
  3. Written net offer. Within 5 business days you receive an offer equal to the resale value of the furniture minus the removal cost. A positive number means we pay you.
  4. Removal. Crews work days, nights or weekends. We provide certificates of insurance for your landlord and an itemized inventory of what leaves.
  5. Handback. The space is returned broom clean with photo documentation, plus certificates of data destruction for e-waste and a diversion summary for your records.
Why the offer can be negative. Resale value depends on brand, age, condition and demand. A suite full of name-brand panel systems and task chairs often nets positive. A room of worn laminate desks may not cover the labor to remove it. The net offer shows you which situation you are in so you can plan around a real number instead of a guess.

What affects your offer in Surprise

Several factors move the number up or down. Knowing them helps you decide whether to liquidate everything, keep some pieces, or time the removal differently.

  • Brand and model. Pre-owned furniture from manufacturers like Herman Miller, Steelcase, Knoll, Haworth and Humanscale generally resells better than unbranded pieces.
  • Condition. Stains, broken mechanisms and missing parts reduce resale value. Complete sets of matching workstations are easier to resell than mixed lots.
  • Access. A ground-floor suite with parking close to the door is faster to clear than a second-floor space with a long carry. Faster removal means lower removal cost.
  • Schedule. A flexible timeline gives the crew room to plan. A lease-end deadline is fine, but tell us about it at the walkthrough.
  • What else is in the room. Electronics, filing contents and personal items need to be sorted before removal. E-waste is handled with certificates of data destruction.
