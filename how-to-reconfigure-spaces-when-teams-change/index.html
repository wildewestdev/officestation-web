Guide: Office Reconfiguration

How to Reconfigure Your Office When Teams Change

Teams grow, shrink, merge and go hybrid faster than floor plans do. This guide walks you through an office reconfiguration that frees up surplus furniture for cash and fills gaps with pre-owned stations in days, not months.

6 minute read

An office reconfiguration rarely starts with a design brief. It starts with a reorg email, a new hire plan, or a department that moved to three days a week. Suddenly one side of the floor is empty and another team is sharing desks. The goal is to rebalance the space quickly without buying furniture you will not need next year.

Here is a practical sequence that works for most offices, whether you are adjusting 10 stations or 200.

Step 1: Start with headcount, not furniture

Before you move a single panel, build a simple table of each team, its current headcount, its expected headcount in 12 months, and how often people are actually in the office. Your badge or booking data is better than anyone's guess.

Then decide on a seating model for each group:

  • Assigned stations for people who are in four or five days a week.
  • Shared or bookable stations for hybrid staff, usually at a ratio that matches real attendance on your busiest day.
  • Collaboration space for teams that come in mainly to work together.

This step keeps you from recreating last year's floor plan with new names on it.

Step 2: Audit what you already own

Walk the floor and count. Note workstation sizes, panel heights, storage pieces, chairs and any damaged components. Photos of each area help later when you compare options or request an offer on surplus.

Step 3: Decide what to keep, sell and add

Compare your future seating plan against the inventory. Most reconfigurations land in one of three situations.

You have too much furniture

Shrinking or hybrid teams leave whole neighborhoods unused. Storing surplus stations costs you square footage, and furniture loses resale value as it ages. Selling it while it is still in demand is usually the better move.

Office Station gives you a written net offer: the resale value of the furniture minus the cost to remove it. A positive number means you are paid. You also get an itemized inventory, so you can see exactly what is leaving. Crews can work nights or weekends so the teams still using the floor are not disrupted.

You have the right amount in the wrong places

Sometimes the count is fine and the arrangement is not. Two teams that need to sit together are on opposite ends, or a row of 8'x8' stations would work better as 6'x6' to add seats. In this case you may sell a portion of the larger stations and add smaller pre-owned ones, which often costs less than you would expect once the offer on the surplus is factored in.

Do the sell and the add in one plan. If you remove surplus on one weekend and install new stations the next, you pay for two disruptions. Coordinating removal and installation around the same layout lets you shift the floor once and keeps people working in the meantime.

Step 4: Plan the layout before you order

Space planning is free with Office Station. Share your floor plan or a measured sketch, your team headcounts and any fixed items such as columns, electrical feeds and private offices. A plan shows how many stations fit at each size, where aisles fall, and which existing pieces can stay put.

Things to check while reviewing a plan:

  • Aisles are wide enough for accessibility and fire code in your building.
  • New stations can reach existing power and data, or you have budgeted for an electrician.
  • Teams that collaborate daily are grouped together, with quieter roles away from high-traffic paths.
  • You have left some flexibility, such as a few extra shared stations, for the next change.

Step 5: Schedule the physical work

Reconfiguration is a construction project in miniature. A little coordination avoids most problems:

  1. Confirm building rules for after-hours work, certificates of insurance, freight elevator reservations and dock time.
  2. Have teams clear personal items and files from stations that are moving or leaving.
  3. Book your electrician for the same window as installation if any feeds are changing.
  4. Send any retired computers and electronics through e-waste handling that provides certificates of data destruction.
  5. Walk the finished floor with the crew before signing off, and note any missing parts.

Plan for the next change

Teams will shift again. A few habits make the next reconfiguration easier: standardize on one or two workstation sizes, keep a written inventory up to date, and store a small number of spare components rather than whole stations. When your needs change again, you will know what you have and what it is worth.

Frequently asked questions

How long does an office reconfiguration take?

It depends mostly on furniture lead time. Liquidation-grade workstations can be available in as little as 72 hours when in stock, remanufactured stations usually take 7-14 days, and new furniture typically runs 4-6 weeks. Planning, the walkthrough and scheduling the crew add a few days on top of that.

Can I sell the workstations we no longer need?

Often, yes. Office Station provides a written net offer that equals the resale value of your furniture minus the cost to remove it. If the number is positive, you get paid; if it is negative, you know the removal cost up front before you commit.

Should I buy new or pre-owned stations for a new team?

If your timeline is short or the team size is uncertain, pre-owned usually makes more sense. A 6'x6' workstation runs $615 liquidation-grade or $1,245 remanufactured versus $1,920 new, furniture only. New is a better fit when you need a specific finish or configuration that is not available pre-owned.

Do you help plan the new layout?

Yes. Space planning is free. Send your floor plan or measurements and your headcount by team, and we can lay out workstation counts and sizes before you order anything.
