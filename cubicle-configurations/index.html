Panel height

How high should the walls be?

Height is the single biggest lever on how a floor feels. It changes privacy, daylight, supervision and price, and you can mix heights deliberately across one floor.

Collaborative

Low 47″

Sightlines stay open when you are seated. Best for teams that talk constantly and for keeping daylight in the middle of the floor.

Powered or non-powered

Panels can carry an electrical raceway in the base with receptacles at the work surface, or stay unpowered and take power from the wall. Decide before you buy: it is not a later add-on. Electrical is licensed work, quoted separately.

Finishes you actually choose

Worksurface laminate, panel fabric and trim are all specified on a remanufactured order. Current tones like white, light gray, espresso and walnut cost the same as dated ones.

What to send us

Getting a layout back in a day

Space planning is free and it is the fastest way to turn a square-footage number into a seat count you can budget against. To draw your floor we need three things:

  1. Square footage or a floor plan. A PDF from the landlord or broker is ideal. A rough sketch with dimensions works.
  2. Headcount and roles. How many people, and which of them are heads-down, phone-heavy or supervising.
  3. Your date. Lease commencement, move-in or the day the space has to be usable.

We come back with a layout, a seat count and a written number. If you want to sanity-check the budget first, the instant quote builder gets you an estimate in about a minute.

Questions

Configuration FAQ

What is the difference between an inline and a cluster cubicle layout?

Inline stations sit side by side in a straight run, all facing the same direction along one shared spine. Cluster stations are grouped around a shared cross of panels, usually back to back in a 2-pack or pinwheeled in a 4-pack or 6-pack. Clusters share more panels per person, so cost per seat is lower, while inline runs fit narrow rooms and extend one station at a time.

What is a bullpen cubicle configuration?

A bullpen puts two or more workstations face to face across a single low screen instead of full-height panels. Everyone can see and talk to each other, which suits sales floors, support teams and project pods. It uses the least material of any configuration, so it is also the least expensive way to seat a talkative team.

How many cubicles fit in my office?

As a planning range, allow 110 to 175 usable square feet per person for a cubicle floor once aisles, conference rooms and break areas are counted, or 80 to 110 for open benching. A 6-pack cluster occupies 216 square feet of floor before circulation. Office Station includes space planning free, so send us your square footage and we will lay it out.

Does buying pre-owned limit which configurations I can have?

No. Configuration is a function of how the parts are assembled, not how old they are. Every layout on this page can be built from liquidation-grade or remanufactured inventory. The real constraint is matching parts, which is why we quote matched runs from single installations rather than mixing generations.

What does a cubicle configuration cost?

Furniture only, a 6′ × 6′ station is $615 liquidation grade, $1,245 remanufactured and $1,920 new, so a 4-pack cluster starts at $2,460 in liquidation grade. Prices exclude electrical, delivery and professional installation, which are quoted separately. Space planning is free.

Can I mix panel heights on the same floor?

Yes, and it usually looks better than one height everywhere. A common approach is tall panels around heads-down and confidential roles, mid panels through the general floor, and low panels or screens on collaborative pods. Mixing deliberately reads as design; mixing by accident reads as leftovers.
