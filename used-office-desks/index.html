Used Office Desks, Ready When You Are

When a new hire starts Monday or a lease clock is ticking, a used desk that is in stock can beat a new one by weeks. Here is how to pick the right type, the right grade and the right timeline.

6 minute read

Desks rarely wear out the way people expect. The top may pick up scuffs, but the frame, pedestals and hardware on a commercial desk are built to handle years of daily use. That is why used office desks are one of the easiest ways to furnish a space quickly without paying for new.

The bigger advantage is often time. A pre-owned desk that is already in inventory can be on your floor in days, while a new order is still waiting on production.

Types of used office desks

Used desk inventory comes from office closings and relocations, so you will see a wide range of styles. Each suits a different kind of work.

  • Freestanding rectangular desks. Simple, flexible and easy to rearrange. Good for small offices and touchdown spaces.
  • L-shaped desks. A desk with a return adds surface area for dual monitors or paperwork without taking up a full private office.
  • U-shaped and executive suites. Desk, return, bridge and credenza combinations for private offices and managers.
  • Height-adjustable tables. Electric or crank bases that let people sit or stand through the day.
  • Benching. Shared-spine rows of desks for open plans and teams that value collaboration over privacy.
  • Workstation worksurfaces. Desks built into panel systems. If you need privacy and storage in one package, look at used cubicles instead.

Condition grades for desks

Desk wear shows up in predictable places: front edges, corners, the top surface where keyboards and elbows rest, and drawer fronts.

GradeLabelTypical desk condition
ALike newClean top, sharp edges, drawers glide smoothly, no visible chips
BVery goodLight surface scuffs or edge wear, all hardware working
CGoodVisible scratches or small edge dings, sturdy frame and working drawers

Grade C desks make a lot of sense where a monitor, desk pad and keyboard cover most of the top anyway. Save Grade A for spaces clients see.

Laminate, veneer or steel

The top material affects how a used desk has aged and how it will keep aging.

  • Laminate. The most common commercial surface. Resists stains and scratches well. Check edge banding, since peeling edges are the usual failure point.
  • Wood veneer. Warmer look, common in executive casegoods. Look for lifting veneer, water rings and deep scratches that cannot be touched up easily.
  • Steel. Older steel desks are nearly indestructible. Check for dents, rust and drawers that bind.

Used desk inspection checklist

  1. Level and wobble. Push on a corner. The desk should not rack side to side. Check that leveling glides are present.
  2. Edges. Run a hand along the front edge for peeling banding, chips or lifting veneer.
  3. Drawers. Open every drawer fully. Slides should glide and stop cleanly. Confirm file drawers have hanging rails.
  4. Locks and keys. Ask whether keys are included, or plan to have locks rekeyed.
  5. Cable management. Look for grommets, wire troughs and modesty panels that are intact.
  6. Height-adjustable bases. Run the base through its full range, listen for grinding and confirm the controller and power supply come with it.
  7. Dimensions. Measure doorways, elevators and your planned footprint before you buy.
Measure the path, not just the room. Large executive desks and returns do not always break down small. Check hallway turns, door widths and elevator depth so delivery day does not stall at the front door.

Frequently asked questions

How fast can I get a used office desk?

Liquidation-grade desks can be available in as little as 72 hours when they are in stock. Remanufactured furniture usually takes 7 to 14 days, and new furniture typically takes 4 to 6 weeks.

What does condition grade mean for a desk?

Grade A is like new with little or no visible wear. Grade B is very good, with light scuffs or edge wear. Grade C is good, with visible cosmetic wear but a sound structure and working hardware.

Are used height-adjustable desks a good idea?

They can be, as long as you test the full range of travel, listen to the motor and check that the controller and power supply are included. Manufacturer warranties generally do not transfer to pre-owned furniture, so a hands-on check matters.

Do you deliver and install used desks?
