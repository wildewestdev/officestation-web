How to Hide Office Clutter With Smarter Storage

Clutter is almost always a storage problem wearing a behavior costume. Match the right cabinet to what is piling up, and the surfaces clear themselves.

6 minute read

Every office has the same sequence. A few papers stay on the desk because there is nowhere convenient to put them. A bag ends up on the floor. A box of supplies lands in a corner. Within a month the space looks disorganized, and asking people to be tidier does not change much.

The fix is usually physical. When closed storage is close to where things land, clutter disappears. This guide shows you how to diagnose what is piling up and which storage pieces actually solve it.

Step 1: Figure out what the clutter actually is

Walk the office at the end of a normal day and sort what you see into categories. Most clutter falls into a handful of types:

  • Active paper. Files people are working on this week.
  • Archive paper. Records people rarely touch but are required or afraid to throw out.
  • Personal items. Bags, jackets, lunch, shoes.
  • Shared supplies. Paper, toner, packaging, samples.
  • Dead electronics. Old monitors, laptops, cables and phones nobody knows what to do with.
  • Display items. Awards, binders and décor that crowd work surfaces.

Each category needs a different answer. A lateral file will not help with bags, and a coat locker will not help with paper.

Pedestals: the personal clutter fix

A pedestal is the small drawer cabinet under or beside a desk. It is the single most effective piece for keeping one person's surface clear.

Mobile vs. fixed

Mobile pedestals roll, so they work well with shared desks and height adjustable tables, where a fixed cabinet could block travel. Fixed pedestals attach under a worksurface and help support it in some workstation systems.

Drawer layout

A box drawer over a file drawer suits most people. Two file drawers suit paper-heavy roles. Some pedestals have a cushion top and double as a guest seat in tight stations.

Lateral files: the team clutter fix

Lateral files are wide cabinets with side-to-side drawers. They hold much more than a pedestal and can do double duty:

  • Room divider. A row of two-drawer laterals separates departments without closing off light.
  • Counter surface. The top of a low lateral becomes a spot for a shared printer or mail sorting.
  • Row end cap. A lateral at the end of a cubicle run gives the team shared storage within a few steps.

Plan for weight. Full lateral drawers are heavy, and many models require anti-tip hardware and a rule that only one drawer opens at a time.

Storage towers: the vertical fix

Storage towers and wardrobe towers use height instead of floor area. They are ideal for jackets, bags and personal items that otherwise end up draped over panels or on the floor. In open plans, towers placed at the end of a desk run also add a little visual privacy.

Closed beats open. Open shelving and cubbies look tidy on day one, then display everything placed on them. If the goal is hiding clutter, choose drawers and doors.

Buying pre-owned storage: what to check

Steel storage is built to last, which makes it one of the best categories to buy used. Office Station grades pieces A like new, B very good and C good. Before you buy, check:

  1. Drawer travel. Every drawer should open fully and close smoothly, even when loaded.
  2. Slides and stops. Look for bent slides and missing drawer stops.
  3. Locks and keys. Confirm the lock works and ask whether keys are included.
  4. File hardware. Check for hanging file rails or bars if you need them.
  5. Anti-tip parts. Make sure lateral files have their interlocks or counterweights where required.
  6. Finish. Scratches on a Grade C cabinet may not matter in a records room but will show in a reception area.
  7. Fit. Measure the space under desks, including clearance for keyboard trays and chair arms.

Liquidation-grade pieces can be available in as little as 72 hours when in stock. Remanufactured furniture usually takes 7 to 14 days, and new runs 4 to 6 weeks. In Greater Phoenix, delivery is $195 central or $295 metro. Elsewhere nationwide, freight is quoted per project.

Clear the backlog before you add storage

New cabinets fill up with old junk if you skip this step. Before storage arrives:

  • Shred confidential records that are past their retention period.
  • Move archive paper to a dedicated records area or off-site storage.
  • Remove broken chairs, extra desks and old cabinets that take up floor space.
  • Send old computers and drives through e-waste handling that provides certificates of data destruction.

Frequently asked questions

What is the fastest way to hide office clutter?

Put closed storage within arm's reach of where the clutter lands. A mobile pedestal under each desk handles personal items and active files, and a shared lateral file handles team paper. Open shelving tends to display clutter rather than hide it.

Should I choose a pedestal or a lateral file?

A pedestal belongs to one person and fits under or beside a desk. A lateral file is wider, holds more, and works best as shared storage at the end of a row or along a wall. Many offices use both.

Is used office storage a good buy?

Steel storage is durable, so pre-owned pedestals, laterals and towers are often a strong value. Check drawers, slides, locks and keys before you buy. Office Station grades pre-owned furniture A like new, B very good and C good.

Do used filing cabinets come with keys?

It varies by piece, so always ask. Missing keys are one of the most common issues with pre-owned storage. If you store anything confidential, confirm working locks and keys before delivery.

What should I do with old files and electronics when clearing clutter?

Shred confidential paper through a secure shredding process. For old computers and drives, use an e-waste process that provides certificates of data destruction. Office Station includes e-waste handling with data destruction certificates on its decommissioning projects.
