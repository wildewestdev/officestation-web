Office Space Planning Services With a Real Budget Attached

A floor plan is only useful if you can afford to build it. Our space planning is free, and every layout is priced against actual workstation tiers so you see the cost before you commit.

6 minute read

Most office layouts go wrong in one of two ways. Either the plan looks great and the quote that follows is double what anyone expected, or the budget gets set first and the layout gets squeezed until the space does not work. Good office space planning services solve both problems at once by designing and pricing together.

That is how Office Station approaches it. Space planning is free, and every layout is priced against the same workstation tiers you can actually buy. You see the plan and the number side by side.

What a space plan includes

  • A furniture layout showing workstation positions, aisles, private offices, conference areas and storage.
  • A station count by type and size, so you know exactly how many 6'x6' or other configurations the plan calls for.
  • A priced budget using liquidation-grade, remanufactured or new pricing, or a mix.
  • Lead time by tier, so the schedule and the budget line up with your move date.
  • Line items for installation and delivery, with notes on what is not included, such as electrical.

How the planning process works

  1. Share the space. Send a floor plan, a CAD file or a hand-measured sketch. Mark columns, windows, doors and any existing floor or wall power.
  2. Share the people. Tell us current headcount, expected growth, and which teams need to sit together. Note roles that need more privacy or more surface area.
  3. Set priorities. Is the goal maximum seats, a quick opening date, a client-facing look, or the lowest total cost? The answer shapes which tier fits each area.
  4. Review the layout and budget. You get a plan and pricing together. Adjust station sizes, swap tiers, or move rooms, and the budget updates with it.
  5. Order and schedule. When the plan is final, it becomes your order, delivery schedule and installation plan.

Mixing tiers is a planning decision, not a compromise. The front row of a sales floor and a records room do not need the same furniture. Deciding tier by area during planning usually saves more than negotiating a discount on a single-tier order later.

What to check in any space plan

Whether the plan comes from us or anyone else, run it through this list before you approve it.

  • Aisle widths and egress. Confirm the layout works with your building's code requirements and fire exits. Your landlord or architect can confirm specifics.
  • Power and data. Check where floor boxes, wall outlets and power poles land relative to stations. Moving a station three feet is cheap; moving a floor box is not.
  • Panel heights. Taller panels add privacy and reduce light. Lower panels keep a space open and help sight lines.
  • Growth room. Plan where the next five or ten stations go before you need them.
  • Storage. Count file drawers and pedestals per person. Storage is easy to forget and hard to add later.
  • Chairs. A plan with stations and no seating is not a full budget. See pre-owned office chairs.
  • Schedule fit. Match each tier's lead time to your move date, especially if new furniture is part of the plan.

Brands, condition and warranties

Pre-owned and remanufactured options in a plan can include furniture from Herman Miller, Steelcase, Knoll, Haworth, Humanscale, Teknion, Allsteel, Kimball, AIS and Friant, depending on availability. Office Station sells these as an independent reseller, not an authorized dealer, and manufacturer warranties generally do not transfer to pre-owned furniture. Pre-owned pieces are graded A (like new), B (very good) or C (good), and the plan notes the grade so expectations are clear.

Frequently asked questions

Is space planning really free?

Yes. Space planning is free with Office Station. You get a layout and a budget tied to real workstation pricing, and you are under no obligation to buy.

What do I need to send to get a layout started?

A floor plan or measured sketch, your headcount now and in the next year or two, and a list of rooms you need such as conference, private offices or storage. Photos of existing power and data locations help a lot.

Can one layout mix furniture tiers?

Yes, and it is often the smartest budget move. You might put new or remanufactured stations where clients see them and liquidation-grade stations in back-office areas. The plan shows the cost of each area separately.

Does the budget include electrical and delivery?

Workstation prices exclude electrical, delivery and professional installation. The plan lists installation at $95 per workstation with a $495 minimum, and delivery at $195 or $295 in Greater Phoenix, with freight quoted per project elsewhere. Electrical work should be priced by a licensed electrician.

Can you plan around furniture I already own?

Yes. If you are keeping some pieces, the layout can work them in. If you are replacing furniture, we can also give you a written net offer on what you are leaving behind through our liquidation service.
