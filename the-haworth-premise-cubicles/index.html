Used Haworth Premise Cubicles

Haworth Premise is a panel-based workstation system that shows up often in corporate offices. Here is how to buy it pre-owned with confidence, from condition grades to what to check before you order.

Why buyers look for pre-owned Premise

Premise is a panel system, which means walls, work surfaces, storage and power attach to a structural panel run. That style of system suits rows and pods of workstations in corporate offices, call centers and operations floors. Because so many offices installed panel systems like this, pre-owned inventory tends to come up when companies downsize, consolidate or relocate.

  • Consistency with an existing floor. Adding pre-owned stations to a Haworth floor is often cheaper than replacing everything with a different system.
  • Budget. A pre-owned 6x6 workstation can cost a fraction of new.
  • Speed. In-stock liquidation-grade stations can move far faster than a new order.
  • Reuse. Keeping good panels in service keeps them out of the landfill.

How condition grades work

Every pre-owned workstation is sold with a condition grade so you know what you are paying for. The grade reflects the cosmetic state of the station. Structural parts that are broken or missing should be replaced, not graded.

GradeDescriptionTypical wear you may seeGood fit for
ALike newLittle or no visible wear on fabric, laminate or paintClient-facing areas, executive rows
BVery goodLight scuffs, minor edge wear, faint fabric marksGeneral staff areas
CGoodNoticeable cosmetic wear, fully functionalBack office, warehouse offices, training rooms

A practical approach is to mix grades by zone. Put Grade A where visitors walk and Grade C where nobody but the team will see the panels.

What to check before you buy used Premise stations

Panel systems reward a little homework. Use this checklist before you place an order, especially if you are matching stations already on your floor.

  • Panel heights. Measure your current panels. Mixing heights in one run looks uneven and can complicate connections.
  • Connectors and hardware. Confirm that the connectors, brackets and end-of-run finishes match your configuration. Missing hardware is a common source of install delays.
  • Electrical components. Powered panels need compatible power infeeds and receptacles. Electrical work is priced separately and should be handled by a qualified electrician.
  • Fabric and finish. Ask for the fabric and paint color, or request photos in daylight. Fabric can shift in tone across batches.
  • Work surface depth and shape. Check whether you need straight, L-shaped or corner surfaces for your monitor setup.
  • Storage. Decide on overheads, pedestals or lateral files per station.
  • Count spares. A few extra panels and surfaces can save a second order when a part is damaged in a move.

Adding to an existing Haworth floor

The most common Premise request is expansion: a team grows and needs six or twelve more stations that look like the rest of the floor. Start with photos of your current stations, the labels on the underside of work surfaces or panel edges if you can find them, and panel height measurements. That information makes it much easier to confirm a match and flag anything that will not connect cleanly.

If an exact finish match is not available, a separate pod in a complementary finish often looks more intentional than a near-miss inside the same run.

What do the condition grades mean?

Grade A is like new, Grade B is very good and Grade C is good. Grades describe cosmetic condition such as fabric, laminate edges and paint. Many buyers use Grade A or B for client-facing areas and Grade C for back-office rows.

Can I add Premise stations to the ones I already have?

Often, but do not assume. Panel heights, connectors, finishes and electrical components need to match your existing stations. Send photos and panel labels from your current setup so the match can be confirmed before you order.
