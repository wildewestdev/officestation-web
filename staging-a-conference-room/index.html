Conference Room Setup: How to Size and Stage the Room

A conference room works when the table, chairs and walkways fit the room and the way you meet. Here is how to size the setup, pick a layout, and furnish it with pre-owned pieces.

6 minute read

Most conference rooms fail for the same few reasons. The table is too big for the room, the chairs cannot slide out, the screen is at the wrong end, or the power is nowhere near where people sit. None of those problems are about style. They are about measurement. This guide walks you through a conference room setup from the tape measure out, so the furniture you buy, new or pre-owned, actually fits.

Start with the room, not the table

Before you shop, gather four numbers and a few facts about the space.

  • Wall-to-wall length and width. Measure at the narrowest point, including any column, credenza or radiator.
  • Door swing and location. A door that swings into the room eats clearance at that corner.
  • Screen or whiteboard wall. Decide which wall is the focal point before placing the table.
  • Floor and wall power. Note outlet and floor box locations. A table that does not line up with a floor box means cords across the carpet.

Then subtract clearance. Plan roughly 3 to 4 feet on every side where people will sit, and more on any side that doubles as a path to another door. What remains is your maximum table footprint.

Sizing the table to headcount

A practical rule is about 30 inches of table edge per person, or about 36 inches if you want generous elbow room or use wide chairs with arms. The table below gives typical starting points. Always check against your actual room after clearance.

Size down one step if in doubt. A table that is slightly small still works. A table that is slightly large makes every meeting uncomfortable, and it is the harder mistake to undo.

Conference room layouts compared

The shape of the setup should follow how the room is used. A board-style table is not the best answer for every meeting.

Boardroom

One long rectangular or boat-shaped table with chairs on all sides. Best for discussion among equals and decision meetings. Boat shapes, wider in the middle, help people at the ends see each other.

U-shape

Tables arranged in a U with the open end facing a screen or presenter. Good for training and presentations where everyone needs a clear view. It uses more floor area per seat than a boardroom layout.

Modular or flip-top tables

Several smaller tables that push together or fold away. Ideal for a multipurpose room that hosts meetings one day and a training class or all-hands the next. Check that the connecting hardware is present on pre-owned sets.

Huddle room

A small round or square table for 2 to 4 people, often with a wall-mounted screen. These rooms get booked constantly, so durable, simple furniture pays off.

Video-first room

A table with one end left open toward the camera, sometimes a trapezoid or V-shaped top so every face is visible. Keep chairs off the camera end and avoid seating anyone with their back to the screen.

Choosing conference chairs

Chair choice changes how many people fit and how long meetings stay comfortable.

  • Arms or armless. Arms add comfort but need more table edge. Confirm arm height clears the table apron so chairs tuck in.
  • Adjustability. Height and tilt adjustment matter for any meeting longer than 30 minutes.
  • Casters. Match casters to the floor. Hard casters on carpet, soft casters on hard floors.
  • Consistency. A matched set looks deliberate. When buying pre-owned, ask whether the full count is available in the same model and finish.

Checklist for pre-owned tables and chairs

Conference furniture is a strong candidate for pre-owned because tables have few mechanisms. Office Station sells pre-owned designer furniture as an independent reseller, not an authorized dealer, and manufacturer warranties generally do not transfer. Each piece carries a condition grade: A like new, B very good, C good. Before you commit, check:

  1. The top surface under good light for chips, scratches, water marks and edge banding that is lifting.
  2. Stability. Push on a corner and watch for rocking or loose base connections.
  3. Power and data modules in the top. Test every outlet and confirm grommet covers are included.
  4. For multi-piece tables, all connector plates, bolts and leg sets.
  5. Chair mechanisms: height lift, tilt lock and any seat depth adjustment.
  6. Upholstery for wear at the front seat edge and armrests.
  7. The grade you want. A-grade suits a client-facing boardroom. B or C grade can be a smart value for a training or project room.

Finishing the room

A credenza along a side wall adds storage for supplies and hides AV equipment. Keep at least 3 feet between it and the backs of the nearest chairs. Plan cable paths from the table power module to the floor box or wall, and route display cables before the table goes in, not after.

Frequently asked questions

How much space do I need around a conference table?

Plan for about 3 to 4 feet between the table edge and the wall so people can pull out a chair and walk behind someone who is seated. If the room is a main walkway to another space, lean toward the larger number. Tight clearance is the most common reason a room feels cramped.

How many people fit at a conference table?

A common rule of thumb is about 30 inches of table edge per seated person, and closer to 36 inches for extra elbow room or wide chairs. Divide the usable perimeter by that figure, and leave the end nearest the screen open if you use video.

Are pre-owned conference tables a good idea?

They often are, because tables have few moving parts and quality laminate or veneer tops hold up well. Check the top for chips, scratches and edge damage, confirm the base is stable, and ask about the condition grade. Office Station sells pre-owned furniture as an independent reseller, so manufacturer warranties generally do not transfer.

What chairs work best in a conference room?

For rooms used for meetings of an hour or more, a mid-back task or conference chair with a tilt and height adjustment is more comfortable than a fixed guest chair. Chairs with arms need more table edge per person, so check that the arms clear the table apron.
