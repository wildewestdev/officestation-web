The Benefits of Used Conference Tables

A conference table is built to last decades, which makes it one of the best pieces of office furniture to buy pre-owned. Here is how to size one, read condition grades and inspect it before it arrives.

6 minute read

Your conference room is where clients form impressions and where decisions get made, so it is tempting to buy the table new. But used conference tables are one of the smartest buys in office furniture. They take very little daily abuse compared to task seating, they are built from heavy, durable materials, and a good one can look sharp for many more years. The key is knowing how to size the table and read its condition.

Why used conference tables make financial sense

Low wear by design

A desk gets used eight hours a day by one person. A conference table gets used in meetings, often a few hours a day, with people resting notebooks and laptops rather than grinding a chair base into it. The structural parts, like bases, frames and connectors, rarely wear out.

More table for the budget

The savings from buying pre-owned can move you up a size or a tier. Instead of a basic laminate table, you may be able to fit a larger table from a brand like Herman Miller, Steelcase, Knoll, Haworth, Teknion, Allsteel, Kimball, AIS or Friant, depending on what is in inventory.

Faster timelines

New conference furniture often has to be manufactured to order. In-stock pre-owned furniture can move much faster. As a reference point from our workstation pricing, liquidation-grade product can be ready in as little as 72 hours, remanufactured usually takes 7 to 14 days and new typically runs 4 to 6 weeks.

Less waste

Reusing a table keeps a large, heavy piece of furniture in service instead of in a landfill. For companies tracking sustainability goals, that is a straightforward win.

Condition grades explained

Every pre-owned piece we sell carries a condition grade. Here is what each one means for a conference table specifically:

GradeDefinitionWhat it usually looks like on a tableBest use

BVery goodLight surface marks or minor edge wear visible up closeMain conference rooms, executive huddle rooms
CGoodVisible wear from prior use, structurally sound and levelInternal meeting rooms, break rooms, training rooms

Grade C tables are often the best value for internal rooms. Nobody grading your quarterly planning session cares about a scuff on the edge.

How to size a conference table

Table size drives almost every other decision, so start here.

  1. Count real attendance. Plan for your typical meeting size, then decide whether extra people can sit along the wall.
  2. Allow about 30 inches of edge per person. This is a common planning guideline that gives each person room for a laptop and elbows.
  3. Leave clearance for chairs. Plan roughly 3 to 4 feet between the table edge and walls or credenzas so people can pull chairs out and walk behind seated colleagues.
  4. Consider the shape. Rectangular tables fit most rooms. Boat-shaped tables improve sightlines to a screen. Round tables suit small, discussion-style rooms.
  5. Check the base. Pedestal and panel bases can interfere with legroom at certain seats. Picture where each chair lands.
  6. Plan the path in. Large tops can be heavy and long. Confirm door widths, corridor turns and elevator dimensions.

Inspection checklist before you buy

  • Surface finish: laminate, veneer or solid surface, and the grade assigned to it
  • Edge condition, especially corners that take bumps during moves
  • Level and stable once assembled, with no wobble at the joints
  • All connecting hardware present for multi-piece tops
  • Power and data grommets or modules, and whether they are functional
  • Dimensions confirmed against your room plan, not just the listing
  • Finish compatibility with any existing credenzas or chairs

Budgeting beyond the table

Frequently asked questions

Are used conference tables worth it?

Usually, yes. Conference tables see far less daily wear than task chairs or desks, and quality tables are built with durable surfaces and heavy bases. Buying pre-owned lets you afford a larger or better-built table than the same budget would cover new.

What do condition grades A, B and C mean?

Grade A means like new, with minimal to no visible wear. Grade B means very good, with light signs of use and full function. Grade C means good, with visible wear consistent with prior office use but still structurally sound and functional.

How big a conference table do I need?

A common planning rule is to allow roughly 30 inches of table edge per seated person, plus about 3 to 4 feet of clearance between the table edge and walls so chairs can move. Send us your room dimensions and target seat count and we will check the fit during free space planning.

Do used conference tables come with power and data?

Some do, with built-in grommets or power modules, and some do not. If you need outlets at the table, say so up front and confirm the power module works with your connection needs before purchase. Any building electrical work is separate from furniture pricing.

Does the manufacturer warranty transfer?

Generally no. Manufacturer warranties usually do not transfer to pre-owned furniture. Office Station is an independent reseller, not an authorized dealer, so we grade condition openly before you buy.
