Types of Office Desks: Which Style Fits Your Office

Desk style shapes how your team works, how much space you use and how quickly you can furnish. This guide compares the main types of office desks and shows which are easiest to find pre-owned.

6 minute read

Why desk style matters more than it looks

A desk is not just a surface. Its shape decides how many people fit on a floor, how cables get managed, where storage goes and how much privacy each person gets. Pick the wrong style and you end up with wasted corners, cramped monitors or a layout that is hard to change when the team grows.

Style also affects how fast and how affordably you can furnish. Some desk types are everywhere on the pre-owned market because companies bought them by the hundred. Others are rare enough that matching a full set can take weeks. Knowing the difference up front saves both time and money.

The main types of office desks

Straight rectangular desk

The simplest option: a single flat surface on legs or a panel base. It works for hot desks, training rooms and small offices, and it lines up easily into rows. The trade-off is limited surface area. If someone needs two monitors, documents and a laptop, a straight desk can feel tight.

L-shaped desk

An L-shaped desk adds a return at one end, giving you a primary work zone and a secondary zone for a second screen, paperwork or a phone. It tucks neatly into corners and is the backbone of most cubicle workstations. It uses more floor space than a straight desk, so plan your aisles carefully.

U-shaped desk

A U-shaped layout wraps the user on three sides with a desk, bridge and credenza or return. It suits managers, analysts with heavy paper workflows and anyone who meets one-on-one at their desk. It needs a larger footprint and is usually found in private offices rather than open floors.

Executive desk

A large freestanding desk, often paired with a credenza and hutch. Executive desks are about presence as much as function. On the pre-owned market, they tend to come as individual pieces, so a full matched suite may take more searching.

Benching

Benching places several work surfaces along a shared beam, often with people facing each other across a low screen. It is the most space-efficient way to seat a team and makes power distribution simple. Privacy is limited, so it works best for collaborative groups. Learn more on our desking systems and modular casegoods page.

Height-adjustable (sit-stand) desk

An electric or manual base lets the user change height through the day. Sit-stand desks come in straight, L-shaped and benching versions. When buying pre-owned, the frame usually outlasts the electronics, so testing the motor and controller is essential.

Cubicle work surface

In a panel workstation, the desk is a surface hung from panels or supported by brackets. It is the most private style for open floors and handles phone-heavy work well. Cubicles are sold as full workstations, which is covered on our used cubicles page.

Collaborative and training tables

Flip-top, nesting and fixed tables used for training rooms, project spaces and touchdown areas. They are flexible and easy to store, but not ideal for all-day individual work.

Which styles work best in each tier

Liquidation-grade is strongest for high-volume styles: straight desks, L-shaped workstations, cubicle surfaces and benching. When a company decommissions a floor, these come out in matching quantities, which is exactly what you need to furnish a team.

Remanufactured makes sense when you want a consistent finish across many seats, or when you are furnishing a client-facing area and want Grade A appearance with a shorter wait than new.

New is the fallback for unusual sizes, specific finishes or matched executive suites that simply are not available secondhand in the quantity you need.

Grades tell you what to expect. Pre-owned pieces are graded A (like new), B (very good) or C (good). Office Station is an independent reseller, not an authorized dealer, and manufacturer warranties generally do not transfer to pre-owned furniture.

How to choose in five steps

  1. List roles, not people. Group your team by how they work: focus, phone, collaborative, leadership.
  2. Match a style to each group. Benching for collaborative teams, cubicles for phone work, L-shaped or U-shaped for heavy individual workloads.
  3. Measure the floor. Confirm aisle widths, column locations and where power and data come in. Our space planning is free.
  4. Decide on sit-stand. Choose whether all, some or none of the desks need height adjustment.
  5. Pick a tier per zone. Many offices use remanufactured or Grade A pieces in visible areas and liquidation-grade across the main floor.

Do not forget seating. The right desk style still needs a chair that adjusts to it, so browse our pre-owned office chairs as you plan.

Frequently asked questions

What is the most common type of office desk?

For open offices, the most common styles are straight rectangular desks, L-shaped workstations and benching runs. Private offices usually use an executive desk or a desk with a return. The right choice depends on how much privacy and surface area each role needs.

Which desk styles are easiest to buy used?

Styles that companies buy in large quantities show up most often on the secondary market. That usually means benching, straight desks, L-shaped workstations and cubicle work surfaces. One-off executive suites and custom reception desks are harder to match in sets.

Are used sit-stand desks a good idea?

They can be, as long as you test the motor, controller and full height range before buying. Ask about the condition grade and confirm the desk is stable at its highest setting. Manufacturer warranties generally do not transfer to pre-owned furniture.

How long does it take to get desks from Office Station?

Liquidation-grade pieces can be ready in as little as 72 hours when in stock. Remanufactured furniture usually takes 7 to 14 days, and new furniture runs 4 to 6 weeks. Your quote confirms timing.
