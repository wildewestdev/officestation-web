Can I Get a Little Privacy, Please? Comparing Cubicle Privacy Options

Privacy at work comes in degrees, from a taller panel to a nearly enclosed station. Here is how each option compares on what it blocks, what it costs and how fast you can get it.

6 minute read

Nobody wants to take a benefits call or review a salary spreadsheet while a coworker reads over their shoulder. Privacy is one of the most common workplace complaints, and it is also one of the easiest to address with the right furniture. The question is how much privacy each role actually needs, and which option delivers it at a price that makes sense.

Three kinds of privacy to solve for

Privacy is not one thing. Before comparing products, decide which of these matters most for each team:

  • Visual privacy. Keeping screens, documents and faces out of view.
  • Acoustic privacy. Keeping conversations from carrying to neighbors.
  • Territorial privacy. A defined, personal space with storage that feels like your own.

HR, finance, legal and management usually need all three. A customer support team may need acoustic privacy most. Rotating hybrid seats may need very little beyond a clean, defined surface.

The privacy options, from least to most enclosed

Option 1: Low panels with desktop screens

Low panels define each station while keeping the room open. They offer little visual or acoustic privacy once someone is standing nearby. Best for collaborative teams and short-stay seating where openness is the point.

Option 2: Mid-height panels

Panels around seated eye level block screen-to-screen views and the constant motion of neighbors. They are the workhorse choice for general office roles and a big step up from benching without making the floor feel closed.

Option 3: Mid-height panels with glass toppers

Glass toppers stack on top of a panel to add height without blocking light. They keep sightlines across the room for a sense of openness while stopping the over-the-wall glance and some direct sound. Clear glass preserves daylight, while frosted or textured glass adds more visual privacy. Keep in mind that glass reflects sound instead of absorbing it, so toppers alone are not a fix for loud phone areas. Topper availability depends on the system and inventory, so they are quoted per project.

Option 4: Tall panels

Panels that rise above a standing person's head give the most separation a cubicle system offers. Fabric-covered acoustic panels absorb some conversation and fully block sightlines. They suit confidential and phone-heavy roles, though they reduce daylight and can feel closed in if used across an entire floor.

Option 5: Private stations

A private station combines tall panels on three sides with a narrow or offset opening, generous work surface and storage. It is the closest a cubicle system gets to a private office without construction. Pair it with a door-side orientation away from main aisles to maximize the effect.

What to check before buying pre-owned privacy panels

  1. Compatibility. Panels generally need to match the manufacturer and product line of your existing system to connect.
  2. Condition grade. A is like new, B is very good and C is good. Tall panels are seen up close, so fabric condition matters more.
  3. Finish matching. Confirm fabric and trim colors across a run so the upgrade does not look patched together.
  4. Power and data. Taller panels may carry or block cabling. Electrical is not included, so plan it with your building.
  5. Warranty expectations. Office Station is an independent reseller, not an authorized dealer, and manufacturer warranties generally do not transfer to pre-owned furniture.
  6. What happens to the old panels. Surplus low panels may have resale value. A written liquidation net offer shows the resale value minus removal cost.

Frequently asked questions

What is the most affordable way to add privacy to cubicles?

Adding or swapping to taller panels on an existing system is usually the lowest-cost change, provided the components are compatible. If you are replacing stations, a liquidation-grade 6'x6' workstation is $615, furniture only. Prices exclude electrical, delivery and professional installation.

Do glass toppers provide acoustic privacy?

Glass toppers add height and block some direct sound between neighbors, but glass reflects sound rather than absorbing it. Their main benefit is visual separation while keeping daylight moving across the floor. For phone-heavy roles, taller fabric panels usually do more for acoustic privacy.

Can I mix pre-owned panels with the system I already have?

Sometimes. Panels generally need to be the same manufacturer and compatible product line to connect correctly, and finishes should be matched. Share your existing system details during space planning so compatibility can be checked before you buy.

Does pre-owned furniture include a manufacturer warranty?

Generally no. Office Station is an independent reseller, not an authorized dealer, and manufacturer warranties generally do not transfer to pre-owned furniture. Use the A, B and C condition grades to understand exactly what you are getting.

How quickly can privacy upgrades be installed?

Liquidation-grade stations can be ready in as little as 72 hours when in stock, remanufactured usually in 7 to 14 days, and new in 4 to 6 weeks. Installation is $95 per workstation with a $495 minimum, and crews can schedule around your workday.
