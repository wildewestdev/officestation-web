How to Plan an Office Furniture Reconfiguration

A reconfiguration lets you reshape your office around how people work now, using furniture you already own. Here is how to plan one so you reuse the most, buy the least and get paid for what is left over.

7 minute read

Headcount changes, hybrid schedules and new teams all put pressure on a floor plan designed years ago. Before you replace everything, consider an office furniture reconfiguration. You keep the stations that still work, reshape them into a better layout, buy only what is missing and turn the leftovers into cash or credit.

The plan below works whether you are adjusting one department or an entire floor.

Step 1: Define what the new layout must do

Start with the work, not the furniture. Write down the answers to a few questions before anyone measures a panel.

  • How many people need an assigned desk, and how many share?
  • Which teams need focus space, and which need collaboration space?
  • How many meeting rooms, call spots and storage areas do you need?
  • What is changing in the next year or two, such as growth or a lease decision?

These answers tell you whether you are shrinking stations, adding them, lowering panels or converting rows into shared space.

Step 2: Inventory what you already own

A good inventory is the foundation of a reconfiguration. Without it, you will order parts that do not fit or miss furniture you could have reused. Record each group of furniture in a simple spreadsheet.

Take photos of labels under worksurfaces and on panel frames. They make matching parts much easier later.

Step 3: Draw the new layout

With goals and inventory in hand, lay out the floor. Work within the constraints of your building: columns, windows, exits, and where power and data come into the floor. Try to reuse existing power points where you can, since electrical changes add cost and time.

Office Station offers free space planning when you buy furniture. A plan shows exactly how your existing components fit the new layout and produces a clear list of what is missing.

Compare three buckets, not one. Every item in your plan should land in one of three columns: reuse as is, fill with pre-owned or new, or sell as surplus. If an item is not in a column, the plan is not finished.

Step 4: Fill the gaps with pre-owned stock

Most reconfigurations need some additional pieces: extra worksurfaces, taller or shorter panels, pedestals or a handful of complete stations. Pre-owned furniture keeps those purchases affordable and often ships faster than new.

Step 5: Sell the surplus through a net offer

Almost every reconfiguration leaves furniture you no longer need. Storing it costs space, and throwing it away costs money. Selling it can offset your project.

Office Station makes a written net offer: the resale value of your surplus minus the cost of removing it. If the number is positive, you are paid. Walkthroughs typically happen within 48 hours in Greater Phoenix and within 2 to 3 business days in other service markets, and offers arrive within 5 business days. Removal comes with an itemized inventory, and crews can work nights and weekends so your office keeps running.

Step 6: Sequence the work

The order of operations determines how much disruption your team feels.

  1. Confirm lead times for any gap parts so furniture arrives before crews start.
  2. Clear surplus first to open floor space for rebuilding.
  3. Coordinate the building: certificates of insurance, freight elevator and dock time.
  4. Schedule electrical with a licensed electrician for any moved power.
  5. Rebuild in phases by department, ideally after hours or on weekends.
  6. Walk the finished floor and log any missing or damaged parts.

Professional installation from Office Station is $95 per workstation with a $495 minimum. Greater Phoenix delivery is $195 for central zones and $295 metro, and freight to other locations nationwide is quoted per project.

Reconfiguration planning checklist

  • Goals written down by team
  • Inventory complete with brand, line, sizes and condition
  • Layout drawn around columns, exits and existing power
  • Every item assigned to reuse, fill or sell
  • Gap parts matched to your product line and lead times confirmed
  • Net offer requested for surplus
  • Building paperwork and elevator time booked
  • Phased schedule shared with staff

Frequently asked questions

What is an office furniture reconfiguration?

It is rearranging, resizing or repurposing the furniture you already own to fit a new layout, headcount or way of working. It can range from moving a few rows of cubicles to converting tall panel stations into lower benching. The goal is to reuse as much as possible instead of buying everything new.

Is reconfiguring cheaper than buying new furniture?

Usually, because you are paying mainly for labor and gap parts rather than complete stations. For comparison, a new 6'x6' workstation from Office Station runs about $1,920 furniture only, while remanufactured is $1,245 and liquidation-grade is $615. Reuse costs depend on how much disassembly and part matching your layout needs.

Can I mix pre-owned parts with my existing system?

Often, if the parts come from the same manufacturer and product line. Different lines, even from one brand, may not connect. Record the brand, product line and panel sizes of what you own before you shop.

What happens to furniture I no longer need?

You can sell it through a written net offer. Office Station calculates resale value minus removal cost, and when the result is positive, you get paid. Offers typically arrive within 5 business days of the walkthrough.

How long does a reconfiguration take to plan?

Allow time for the inventory, a layout plan, sourcing gap parts and scheduling installation. Liquidation-grade stock can ship in as little as 72 hours when in stock, remanufactured usually takes 7 to 14 days and new takes 4 to 6 weeks, so the parts you need often set the timeline.
