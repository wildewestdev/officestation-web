Making Hybrid Work: How to Plan a Hybrid Office Layout That Fits Your Team

Most hybrid offices still carry the floor plan of a five-day week. Here is how to measure what you actually use, sell what you do not, and rebuild the layout at a known cost per workstation.

6 minute read

A hybrid office layout is a floor plan built around how many people are actually in the building, not how many people are on payroll. If your team comes in two or three days a week, a traditional one-desk-per-person plan leaves rows of empty stations, and those stations still take up square footage you pay for every month.

This guide walks through a practical process: measure real use, decide which seats stay assigned, turn surplus furniture into cash or at least a zero-cost removal, and reconfigure with pre-owned furniture priced per workstation.

Step 1: Measure how the space is really used

Before you move a single panel, get a clear picture of attendance. Badge data, room booking logs and a simple two-week walk count all work. What you want are three numbers:

  • Peak day attendance. The busiest regular day, usually midweek. This sets your ceiling.
  • Average attendance. Useful for sizing shared stations and meeting rooms.
  • Core in-office roles. People who need a fixed station most days, such as reception, finance teams handling documents, or anyone with specialized equipment.

A common mistake is designing to average attendance. Your layout has to handle the Tuesday when everyone comes in for the all-hands, so plan to peak plus a modest buffer.

Step 2: Choose a seating model

Most hybrid floor plans mix a few seating types. The right blend depends on your peak numbers and the kind of work people do on site.

Seating typeBest forPlanning note
Assigned workstationsPeople in the office most daysKeep these in quieter zones with storage
Bookable shared stationsTwo or three day a week staffStandardize monitors and chairs so every seat works
Benching or open tablesShort visits and team daysPlan power access and personal lockers nearby
Collaboration areasMeetings and project workOften the reason people come in, so size them generously

A team of 120 with a peak of 75 might keep 40 assigned stations, add 35 shared stations, and convert the rest of the footprint into meeting space or give it back at lease renewal.

Once you know how many stations you need, the rest is surplus. Storing it costs money and throwing it away costs more. The better path is usually a liquidation.

Hybrid office layout checklist

  1. Collect two to four weeks of attendance data and find your peak day.
  2. List roles that truly need assigned seats.
  3. Decide the ratio of assigned, shared and collaborative space.
  4. Tag furniture to keep, then request a liquidation walkthrough for the rest.
  5. Choose a furniture tier for new stations based on budget and move date.
  6. Confirm power locations with your electrician, since electrical is not included.
  7. Book removal and installation on one schedule so the floor is never half-empty and half-built for long.
  8. Review attendance again after 90 days and adjust.

Details that make shared seating work

Shared stations fail when every seat is a little different. Standardize the basics: the same chair model, the same monitor arm and a consistent desk height range. Pre-owned office chairs are an easy way to match seating across a floor without buying new. Add lockers or mobile pedestals so people are not carrying everything in each day.

Finally, leave room to change. Hybrid attendance keeps shifting. A layout built from modular pre-owned components is easier to expand or shrink again than a one-time custom build.

Frequently asked questions

How many workstations does a hybrid office need?

Start with peak attendance, not headcount. Count how many people are in the office on your busiest regular day, add a buffer for visitors and growth, and plan assigned seats only for people who come in most days. Everyone else can share bookable stations.

What can I do with the workstations I no longer need?

Office Station can walk the space and give you a written net offer, which is the resale value of the furniture minus the cost of removal. If the number is positive, you get paid. Walkthroughs are typically within 48 hours in Greater Phoenix and 2 to 3 business days in our other markets.

Can I sell surplus furniture and buy reconfigured stations at the same time?

Yes. Many teams pair a partial liquidation with a smaller order of pre-owned stations in a new configuration. Coordinating both sides together keeps removal and installation on one schedule and helps you avoid paying for space you are about to give back.
