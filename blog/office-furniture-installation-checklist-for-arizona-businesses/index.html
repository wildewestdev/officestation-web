Office Furniture Installation Checklist for Arizona Businesses

A smooth install day is decided weeks before the truck arrives. Use this checklist to prepare your space, your building and your budget so workstations go up once and stay up.

6 minute read

Most installation problems are not assembly problems. They are scheduling, access and utility problems that show up on the morning of the install: the freight elevator is booked, the electrician is a day behind, or nobody knows where the 40 panels are supposed to go. This checklist walks you through each phase so your crew spends its time building workstations instead of waiting.

Step 2: Lock the layout before you order

Changing a layout after panels arrive costs you time and sometimes parts. Take advantage of free space planning and settle these points first:

  • Get a current floor plan with column locations, doors, windows and existing power poles or floor boxes marked.
  • Confirm aisle widths and egress paths with your property manager or architect so the plan meets fire and accessibility requirements.
  • Decide where power enters each workstation run: from a wall, a floor monument or a ceiling pole.
  • Count seats by team and label each workstation on the plan with a number that will match the install tags.
  • Note any stations that need a different height, extra storage or a monitor arm.

Step 3: Coordinate the building

In multi-tenant buildings across Phoenix, Scottsdale and Tempe, access is often the tightest constraint. Office Station crews coordinate freight elevators and docks and can work nights and weekends, but the building has to say yes first.

  • Certificate of insurance. Ask your property manager for the exact COI wording, including additional insured names, and send it with your quote request.
  • Freight elevator. Reserve it for the full install window, and confirm the cab dimensions and weight limit.
  • Loading dock. Book a dock time and ask about truck length limits and any overnight parking rules.
  • Building hours. Find out whether moves must happen after hours and whether a building engineer or security officer must be present.
  • Floor and wall protection. Ask whether the building requires protection on corridors, elevator cabs and door frames.
Plan around Arizona summers. A truck sitting at a dock in the afternoon heat is hard on crews and on some materials. If your building allows it, early morning or evening delivery windows tend to keep the day moving and keep your lobby cooler for tenants.

Step 4: Schedule electrical and data

Because workstation pricing does not include electrical, your electrician is a separate trade on the calendar. Most workstation systems need panels set before the electrician can connect base feeds, and data cabling usually follows.

  1. Share the final plan and the workstation electrical specs with your electrician.
  2. Confirm whether permits or landlord approvals are required for new circuits.
  3. Schedule the electrician for the day after panels are set, or overlap if the crews agree.
  4. Book your IT or cabling vendor to run data after power is connected.
  5. Plan a final test of every outlet and data port before employees move in.

Step 5: Clear the space

If old furniture is still on the floor, install day stalls. You can handle the outgoing side at the same time: Office Station provides office furniture liquidation in Phoenix with a written net offer, meaning the resale value of your existing furniture minus the cost to remove it. When that number is positive, you get paid.

  • Remove or liquidate old workstations, chairs and files before the new delivery.
  • Arrange e-waste pickup for old computers and phones, with certificates of data destruction.
  • Patch, paint and clean carpet while the floor is empty.
  • Mark the floor with tape where workstation runs start.

Step 6: Install day checklist

  • Designate one on-site contact with authority to make layout decisions.
  • Walk the crew lead through the plan and station numbering.
  • Confirm the elevator, dock and building access are live at the scheduled time.
  • Check that the delivery count matches the order before assembly starts.
  • Keep a staging area near the elevator for components and packaging.

Step 7: Walk the punch list

Before you sign off, walk every station with the plan in hand.

  • Panels are plumb, connected and stable with no gaps.
  • Work surfaces are level and brackets are fully seated.
  • Drawers, locks and overhead doors open and close smoothly, and keys are collected and labeled.
  • Power and data work at every station.
  • Chairs are adjusted and placed, and aisles are clear.
  • Packaging and debris are removed from the floor.

Build your timeline backward

Start from your move-in date and count back by lead time. Liquidation-grade workstations can be ready in as little as 72 hours when in stock, remanufactured usually takes 7 to 14 days and new furniture typically takes 4 to 6 weeks. Add time for building approvals and your electrician, and you have a realistic order date.

Frequently asked questions

How much does office furniture installation cost?

Office Station installs workstations at $95 per workstation with a $495 project minimum. That rate covers assembling and setting the furniture. Electrical work, data cabling and building fees are separate, so ask your electrician and property manager for those numbers early.

What does delivery cost in Greater Phoenix?

Delivery is $195 in the central zone and $295 across the wider Phoenix metro. Outside Greater Phoenix, freight is quoted per project based on the size of the order and the destination.

Do workstation prices include electrical and installation?

No. Workstation prices are furniture only. Electrical connections, delivery and professional installation are priced separately, which lets you see exactly what each part of the project costs.

Can you install after hours?

Many office buildings restrict freight elevators and loading docks to certain hours, and some require moves at night or on weekends. Tell us your building rules when you request a quote so the schedule and crew plan match them.

How soon can furniture be installed?

It depends on the grade you choose. Liquidation-grade stock can be ready in as little as 72 hours, remanufactured workstations usually take 7 to 14 days, and new furniture typically runs 4 to 6 weeks.
