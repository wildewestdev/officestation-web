Buying Used Office Furniture: A Plain Guide

Used office furniture can cut your bill by more than half, but only if you understand what you are buying. This guide explains condition grades, the three price tiers, lead times and what happens to warranties.

7 minute read

Buying used office furniture is not a gamble when you know the vocabulary. Most of the confusion comes from three things: what a condition grade actually promises, how liquidation-grade differs from remanufactured, and what happens to the warranty. Get those straight and you can make a clear decision about where to save and where to spend.

Why used office furniture makes sense

Commercial office furniture is built to last far longer than the average office lease. When a company downsizes, relocates or closes a floor, workstations, chairs and storage that still have years of use left come out of the building. That supply is where used furniture comes from.

The practical benefits are lower cost, shorter lead times and furniture that is already proven in a real office. The trade-offs are limited choice at any given moment and the loss of manufacturer warranty coverage. Both trade-offs are manageable if you plan for them.

Condition grades A, B and C

A condition grade tells you how a piece looks and has been used, so you can match it to where it will go.

  • Grade A, like new. Minimal to no visible wear. Suits executive offices, reception areas and anywhere clients will see it.
  • Grade B, very good. Light signs of use you notice up close, fully functional. Suits most open-plan work areas.
  • Grade C, good. Visible cosmetic wear such as scuffs or faded finishes, still sound and functional. Suits back offices, call centers, warehouses and training rooms.

A useful rule: spend on grade where people see the furniture, and save where they do not. A Grade C lateral file in a storage room works exactly as well as a Grade A one.

How to choose a tier

  1. Deadline under two weeks? Liquidation-grade is usually the only tier that fits, and remanufactured may work if you have about two weeks.
  2. Need a uniform look across a large floor? Remanufactured or new gives you more consistency than mixing liquidation lots.
  3. Tight budget and a back-office team? Liquidation-grade delivers the most workstations per dollar.
  4. Specific finish, fabric or configuration required? New gives you full control, at the longest lead time.

Many offices mix tiers: new or remanufactured in client-facing areas, liquidation-grade everywhere else.

Warranty realities

This is the part many sellers gloss over, so here it is directly. Manufacturer warranties generally do not transfer to pre-owned furniture. That is true whether the piece carries a Herman Miller, Steelcase, Knoll, Haworth, Humanscale, Teknion, Allsteel, Kimball, AIS or Friant label.

Office Station sells these brands as an independent reseller. We are not an authorized dealer for any manufacturer, and we do not represent that a manufacturer will honor its warranty on a used piece.

How to protect yourself without a warranty. Buy on condition grade, ask for specifics about any piece that matters to you, inspect on delivery, and put your money into the right grade for each area instead of paying extra for coverage you will not get.

What to check when furniture arrives

  • Chairs. Test height adjustment, tilt lock, armrests and casters. Sit for a minute and feel for sagging seats or mesh.
  • Workstations. Confirm panel connectors, worksurface brackets and any power components you expect are present.
  • Storage. Open every drawer, test interlocks and check for keys.
  • Counts and finishes. Match the delivery against your quote, including colors and sizes.
  • Grade consistency. Pieces should reflect the grade you purchased.

Budgeting the full cost

Furniture price is only one line. Build your budget with every item included.

  • Furniture. Use the tier prices above, then apply volume pricing: 5% at 20 or more workstations, 8% at 50 or more, 12% at 100 or more.

  • Electrical. Not included. Coordinate with your electrician or building if workstations need powered panels.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between liquidation-grade and remanufactured furniture?

Liquidation-grade furniture is sold in the condition it came out of a corporate office, cleaned and graded. Remanufactured furniture has been reworked, which typically means refreshed components or surfaces, so it costs more and takes longer. For a 6x6 workstation, furniture only, liquidation-grade runs $615 and remanufactured runs $1,245.

Does used office furniture come with a warranty?

Manufacturer warranties generally do not transfer to pre-owned furniture. Office Station is an independent reseller, not an authorized dealer for any brand. Condition grading and inspection before you buy are how you manage that risk.

How fast can I get used office furniture?

Liquidation-grade workstations in stock can be available in as little as 72 hours. Remanufactured usually takes 7 to 14 days. New furniture typically takes 4 to 6 weeks.

Are there volume discounts on used workstations?

Yes. Volume pricing is 5% off at 20 or more workstations, 8% at 50 or more, and 12% at 100 or more.

What do workstation prices not include?

Workstation prices are furniture only. They exclude electrical, delivery and professional installation. Installation is $95 per workstation with a $495 minimum, and Greater Phoenix delivery is $195 central or $295 metro.
