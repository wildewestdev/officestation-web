Used Herman Miller Aeron Chairs

A used Aeron can be one of the best values in office seating, if you get the right size and know what to inspect. This guide covers fit, condition grades and the wear points that matter.

6 minute read

The Herman Miller Aeron is built around a mesh seat and back, a sturdy frame and parts that can be serviced. That design is why so many Aerons from corporate offices are still fully usable years later. It is also why the used Aeron market rewards buyers who look closely.

Two sets of letters matter when you shop: the chair's size (A, B or C) and its condition grade (A, B or C). They mean completely different things, and mixing them up is an easy way to end up with the wrong chairs.

Size is not condition. An Aeron "size B" describes how big the chair is. A "Grade B" describes how worn it is. When you ask for a quote, list both, for example "10 chairs, size B, Grade A or B."

Aeron sizing: A, B and C

Unlike most task chairs, the Aeron is made in three frame sizes. The mesh seat does not compress like foam, so a chair that is too small or too large stays that way no matter how you adjust it.

BMediumThe widest range of body typesVery tall or broad users may feel the frame edges
CLargestTaller and larger usersSmaller users' feet may not rest flat; seat edge can press behind knees

A quick fit test: sit all the way back, feet flat. You should have a few fingers of space between the front of the seat and the back of your knees. Your hips should not touch the side frame. The backrest should support you up to roughly your shoulder blades.

If you cannot sit in one before buying, a size B is the safest default for an unknown user. For a team, collect heights and preferences first. Buying a few A and C chairs alongside a majority of B chairs usually beats forcing everyone into one size.

Generations and options

Aerons on the used market come from different product generations and were ordered with different options. Herman Miller refreshed the design in the mid-2010s, and the updated version uses a different mesh and adjustment controls from the original. Neither is automatically better for your purposes, but you should know which one you are getting, especially when matching a set.

Options vary chair to chair. Ask about each of these:

  • Arms: fixed, height-adjustable, or fully adjustable with pivot and depth.
  • Back support: an adjustable lumbar pad, a two-pad posture support, or no lumbar option at all.
  • Tilt: tilt limiter settings and whether forward tilt is included.
  • Base and casters: standard or polished base, and caster type for carpet or hard floors.

Condition grades on pre-owned Aerons

Every chair we sell carries a condition grade:

  • Grade A, like new: mesh taut and clean, frame with little or no visible wear, all adjustments smooth.
  • Grade B, very good: light scuffs on the frame or arms, mesh intact, everything works.
  • Grade C, good: visible cosmetic wear such as arm pad marks or frame scuffs, fully functional.

For executive offices or client-facing rooms, Grade A is worth the difference. For a busy open-plan floor, Grade B usually looks the same to everyone except the person sitting in it.

What to inspect on a used Aeron

Whether you are buying from us or anywhere else, run through this checklist. It takes about five minutes per chair.

  1. Seat mesh: press firmly in the center. It should spring back without a permanent dip. Look along the frame edge for fraying or pulled strands.
  2. Back mesh: lean back hard. Listen for creaks and check that the mesh stays attached around the perimeter.
  3. Height cylinder: raise the seat, sit for a minute and confirm it does not sink.
  4. Tilt: work the tilt tension and tilt limiter through every setting. Stuck knobs or a tilt that will not lock are red flags.
  5. Arms: raise and lower them, and test pivot and depth if equipped. Check arm pads for cracks and peeling.
  6. Lumbar: confirm the lumbar pad or posture support is present and slides or adjusts.
  7. Base and casters: look for cracks at the base arms and make sure every caster rolls freely.
  8. Labels: check the label under the seat for model and size information where it is still legible.

The warranty question

New Aerons carry a manufacturer warranty for the original purchaser. Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between Aeron size A, B and C?

Aeron size letters describe the chair's frame and seat size. A is the smallest, B is the medium size that fits a wide range of people, and C is the largest. Size is separate from condition grade, so a chair can be size B and condition Grade A at the same time.

How do I know which Aeron size I need?

Sit in one if you can. Your feet should rest flat on the floor, your thighs should be supported without the seat edge pressing behind your knees, and the backrest should reach your shoulder blades. If you are buying for a team, a mix weighted toward size B is a common starting point, with some A and C chairs for smaller and larger users.

Does a used Aeron come with the Herman Miller warranty?

Generally, no. Office Station is an independent reseller, not an authorized dealer, and manufacturer warranties usually do not transfer to pre-owned buyers. Rely on the condition grade and a careful inspection.

What should I inspect on a used Aeron?

Check the seat and back mesh for tears, sagging or loose edges, test every adjustment including height, tilt and arms, and make sure the cylinder holds height without sinking. Look at the casters and base for cracks, and confirm whether the lumbar support is included.

Can I order used Aerons for a whole office?

You can ask for quantity. Availability depends on what comes out of corporate liquidations, so matching size, generation and options across a large order can take planning. Email hello@officestation.com with your count and size mix.
