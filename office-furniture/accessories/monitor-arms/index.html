Monitor Arms for Office Workstations

A monitor arm is one of the cheapest ergonomic upgrades you can add to a workstation order. Here is how to pick arms that fit your screens, your desks and the way your team works.

6 minute read

Most offices buy monitor arms after the fact, when someone complains about neck strain or a laptop stand topples. It is easier and cheaper to plan them into the workstation order. The desks, the screens and the arms all have to agree on dimensions, and that is much simpler to sort out on paper than on install day.

Why monitor arms belong in the workstation order

A fixed monitor stand puts the screen at one height and one distance for everyone. People are not one height. An arm lets each person set the top of the screen near eye level and push it back to a comfortable viewing distance, then move it out of the way when they need worksurface.

That last part matters on compact layouts. On a 6'x6' workstation, lifting two screens off the desk frees real space for paperwork, a second laptop or a document holder. It also makes hot-desking more workable, because each new user can readjust in seconds.

  • Posture: screens set at eye height instead of propped on books or boxes.
  • Space: the footprint of two stands returned to usable worksurface.
  • Flexibility: quick adjustment for shared or rotating seats.
  • Cable control: most arms route cords along the arm and off the desk.

Fit guide: what to measure before you choose

Monitor arm returns almost always come down to one of five mismatches. Check each one against your actual desks and screens.

Monitor weightScreen weight without its stand falls inside the arm's rated rangeLight screens on a heavy-duty spring float upward; heavy screens sag
VESA pattern75 x 75 mm or 100 x 100 mm holes on the monitor backSlim or curved displays with no VESA mount need an adapter

Panel workstations need a different plan. On cubicles where the worksurface meets a panel, a standard edge clamp often has nowhere to go. Look at grommet mounts through the worksurface, or ask whether a panel-compatible mounting option makes sense for your system before you order a full floor of arms.

Single, dual or multi-screen arms

Single arms

The right fit for laptop-and-one-monitor setups, reception desks and most private offices. Easy to install and easy to reposition.

Dual arms

The most common choice for task seating in operations, finance and support teams. A shared post with two independent arms keeps both screens aligned. Check that the combined screen width fits the worksurface without overhanging into a neighbor's station.

Three or more screens

Trading desks, dispatch and monitoring roles. These put real load on the desk, so a grommet mount or a heavier base is usually the safer choice. Confirm the worksurface and its support brackets can handle the leverage.

Clamp vs grommet vs freestanding

  • Clamp: no drilling, quick to move. Needs an exposed desk edge and a clamp opening that matches your worksurface thickness.
  • Grommet: bolts through an existing or drilled hole. Stronger on thin tops and better for heavy or multi-screen loads.
  • Freestanding base: no hardware into the desk at all. Takes up some worksurface and is less stable for heavy screens, but useful when you cannot modify the furniture.

New or pre-owned monitor arms

Good monitor arms last a long time, and brands like Humanscale make arms that often come back into circulation when offices close or consolidate. A pre-owned arm can be a sensible buy if it is complete.

Before accepting a used arm, check that:

  • The tension adjustment still holds the screen where you leave it.
  • The mounting hardware (clamp or grommet base, bolts, VESA screws) is included.
  • Joints move smoothly with no grinding or loose play.
  • Cable management clips are present.

Office Station sells pre-owned furniture as an independent reseller, not an authorized dealer. Manufacturer warranties generally do not transfer to pre-owned products, so judge a used arm on its condition and completeness, not on a warranty you may not receive.

Quick ordering checklist

  1. Count screens per seat and note each monitor's size and weight.
  2. Confirm VESA holes on every monitor model.
  3. Measure worksurface thickness and depth.
  4. Decide clamp, grommet or freestanding for each desk type.
  5. Note whether arms should be mounted during installation.

Frequently asked questions

Can I add monitor arms to a used cubicle or workstation order?

Yes. The simplest way is to list your screen count and sizes when you request a workstation quote. Planning arms alongside the desks lets us check worksurface depth, edge thickness and panel clearance before anything ships.

Do monitor arms fit every monitor?

Most business monitors have a VESA pattern on the back, usually 75 x 75 mm or 100 x 100 mm. Some slim or curved screens lack VESA holes and need an adapter. Always check the monitor weight against the arm's rated capacity, since an underloaded or overloaded gas spring will drift.

Clamp or grommet mount, which is better?

A clamp is faster and needs no drilling, but it needs an open desk edge and enough clearance behind the worksurface. A grommet mount goes through a hole in the desk and is more secure on thin or shared worksurfaces. On panel-based workstations, a clamp is often blocked by the panel, so check before ordering.

Are pre-owned monitor arms covered by a manufacturer warranty?

Generally no. Office Station is an independent reseller, not an authorized dealer, and manufacturer warranties generally do not transfer to pre-owned products. Ask about condition and included hardware on any pre-owned arm before you buy.

Do monitor arms change installation pricing?

Installation is quoted at $95 per workstation with a $495 minimum. If you want arms mounted during install, tell us up front so the scope is written into your quote rather than added on site.
