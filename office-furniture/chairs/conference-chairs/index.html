Pre-Owned Conference Room Chairs

Conference room chairs are seen by every visitor and sat in for hours at a time. Pre-owned designer chairs, graded honestly, let you furnish a room properly without paying new prices.

6 minute read

The conference room is where clients form an impression and where your team sits through long planning sessions. Mismatched or worn-out chairs undermine both. Pre-owned designer seating is one of the most practical ways to fix that, provided you know what grade you are buying and size the room correctly.

Why pre-owned designer chairs make sense here

Conference chairs get far less daily use than task chairs. A chair from a meeting room that was booked a few hours a day often shows less wear than a desk chair of the same age. That makes conference seating a strong category for pre-owned buying.

A, B and C grades for conference chairs

GradeWhat it meansWhere it fits best
A: Like newLittle or no visible wear on upholstery, arms or baseBoardrooms, client-facing rooms, executive suites
B: Very goodLight cosmetic wear, not noticeable at normal distanceStandard meeting rooms and team huddle rooms
C: GoodFully functional, with visible wear such as scuffed bases or faded fabricTraining rooms, internal project rooms, overflow seating
Mix grades by room, not within a room. A room of grade B chairs looks consistent. A room with four grade A chairs and four grade C chairs looks mismatched even when every chair works. Put your best grade in the rooms visitors see and use grade C where only your team meets.

Sizing chairs to the table

Most conference rooms are either overcrowded or under-seated because chair count was guessed. Use table edge length as the guide.

  • Per seat: allow about 28 to 30 inches of table edge for a typical conference chair with arms.
  • Behind the chair: leave at least 36 inches from the table edge to the wall so people can pull out and walk behind seated colleagues. More is better in rooms people move around in.
  • Arm height: check that the chair arms slide under the table apron. Chairs that bump the table get left pushed out, which clutters the room.

Typical seat counts

  • 6-foot table: 4 to 6 chairs
  • 8-foot table: 6 to 8 chairs
  • 10-foot table: 8 to 10 chairs
  • 12-foot table: 10 to 12 chairs

Choosing a conference chair style

Mid-back swivel with casters

The default for most meeting rooms. Low enough to keep sightlines clear across the table and to a screen, adjustable enough for hour-long sessions.

High-back conference chairs

A more formal look for boardrooms. They block sightlines in smaller rooms, so reserve them for larger tables and generous clearance.

Sled-base and four-leg chairs

Good for huddle rooms, rooms with glass walls where a tidy look matters, and rooms without the clearance for rolling chairs. Less comfortable for long meetings.

Mesh vs upholstered

Mesh backs stay cooler in long sessions and show wear less. Upholstered or leather-look chairs feel more formal but show stains and fading, which is where the A, B and C grade matters most.

What to check on pre-owned conference chairs

  1. Tilt and tension: the chair should recline smoothly and return upright.
  2. Height adjustment: the cylinder should hold its position without sinking.
  3. Casters: all five roll freely and suit your floor type.
  4. Arms: pads intact, no cracks at the mounting points.
  5. Upholstery: check seat fronts and arm pads, where wear shows first.
  6. Set consistency: same model, color and base finish across the room.

How to request conference chairs

The fastest way to get an accurate answer is to describe each room rather than send a single total. For every room, include:

  • Table size and shape: length, width and whether it is rectangular, boat-shaped or round.
  • Chair count: the number you want at the table plus any wall seating.
  • Grade: A, B or C, based on who uses the room.
  • Style preference: mesh or upholstered, mid-back or high-back, casters or sled base.
  • Color matching: whether chairs must match exactly or just coordinate.
  • Location and timing: your address and move-in date, so delivery can be planned.

Frequently asked questions

How many chairs fit around a conference table?

Allow roughly 28 to 30 inches of table edge per chair for task-style conference chairs with arms. An 8-foot table typically seats 6 to 8, a 10-foot table 8 to 10 and a 12-foot table 10 to 12, depending on chair width and whether you seat people at the ends.

What do the A, B and C grades mean?

Grade A is like new, with little or no visible wear. Grade B is very good, with light cosmetic wear that most people will not notice from a normal distance. Grade C is good, fully usable with more visible wear such as scuffed bases or faded upholstery.

Do pre-owned designer conference chairs come with the manufacturer warranty?

Generally no. Office Station is an independent reseller of brands such as Herman Miller, Steelcase, Knoll and Haworth, not an authorized dealer, and manufacturer warranties generally do not transfer to pre-owned furniture. Inspect or ask about condition before you buy.

Can I get a matching set of conference chairs?

Matching sets are common when chairs come out of a single office, but availability varies. Tell us the room count, chair count and whether exact color matching matters, and we will confirm what can be matched before you commit.

Should conference chairs swivel and roll?

For most meeting rooms, yes. Casters and swivel make it easy to turn toward a screen or a speaker. Boardrooms with tight clearance or formal rooms sometimes use sled-base or four-leg chairs instead to keep the room tidy.
