Used Task Chairs

Graded pre-owned task chairs from major commercial brands. We are an independent reseller, so you get honest condition grades and straight answers about warranties before you buy.

7 minute read

A task chair is the piece of furniture people touch for eight hours a day, and it is also where commercial brands put most of their engineering. That combination makes used task chairs one of the best-value purchases in pre-owned furniture. A well-built commercial chair is designed to outlast many years of office use, so a second owner often gets most of its working life at a fraction of the new price.

Office Station resells pre-owned task chairs from brands including Herman Miller, Steelcase, Knoll, Haworth, Humanscale, Teknion, Allsteel, Kimball, AIS and Friant. Inventory changes with each project we take in, so tell us what you need and we will tell you what is realistic.

What buying from an independent reseller means

We want to be direct about this because it shapes what you should expect.

  • We are not an authorized dealer for any manufacturer. Brand names identify the chairs we resell.
  • Manufacturer warranties generally do not transfer to pre-owned chairs.
  • Condition grades do the work a warranty would. Every chair is graded A, B or C so you know what you are buying.
Ask before you order. If a specific feature matters to you, such as adjustable arms, seat depth or lumbar support, confirm it on the exact chair being quoted. Commercial task chairs are sold in many configurations, and two chairs with the same model name can have very different controls.

The adjustments that matter

A task chair only helps if it fits the person in it. When you compare pre-owned chairs, look past the logo to the controls.

Seat height

The cylinder range should let feet rest flat with thighs roughly level. Very short or very tall users may need a different cylinder.

Seat depth

A sliding seat pan lets users leave a few fingers of space behind the knee. It is one of the most useful and most often missing adjustments.

Arms

Height-adjustable arms are the minimum for keyboard work. Width and pivot adjustments help people with narrower or broader frames.

Lumbar support

Some chairs have adjustable lumbar pads, some have fixed shaping, and some rely on the back design. Test it rather than assuming.

Tilt and tension

Look for tilt tension that can be tuned to body weight and a tilt lock that holds an upright position for focused work.

Used task chair inspection checklist

Use this list when you review a chair in person or evaluate photos and notes.

  1. Cylinder: holds height under weight, no slow sinking.
  2. Tilt mechanism: no grinding, clunking or loose play when reclining.
  3. Controls: every lever and knob engages and releases.
  4. Mesh: no sagging, fraying at the frame edge or snagged strands.
  5. Upholstery and foam: no tears, stains or collapsed front edge.
  6. Arm pads: cracked or peeling pads are common and worth noting.
  7. Base and casters: no cracks in base arms, casters roll freely and suit your floor.
  8. Back attachment: no flex or looseness where the back meets the seat.

Buying task chairs for a whole team

Ordering one chair is about fit. Ordering fifty is about consistency and sensible trade-offs. A few habits make team purchases go smoothly.

  • Standardize on one or two models. Matching chairs look intentional and make it easy to swap parts or seats between desks later.
  • Prioritize adjustability over prestige. A mid-range chair with seat depth and adjustable arms often serves a varied team better than a famous model missing those controls.
  • Plan for outliers. Very tall, petite or larger users may need a different cylinder, seat size or weight rating. Identify them before you order.
  • Order a few spares. New hires and the occasional broken caster are easier to handle with two or three extra chairs in storage.
  • Match the grade to the room. Mixing grades across different areas is fine; mixing grades inside one visible row usually is not.

Frequently asked questions

Is Office Station an authorized dealer for these brands?

No. Office Station is an independent reseller of pre-owned furniture and is not an authorized dealer for Herman Miller, Steelcase, Knoll, Haworth or any other manufacturer. Brand names describe the products we resell, not a dealer relationship.

Do manufacturer warranties transfer on used task chairs?

Generally, no. Manufacturer warranties are typically tied to the original purchaser and an authorized sale, so they usually do not follow a chair to a second owner. That is why we grade each chair and why you should review the grade and any noted wear before ordering.

What do the A, B and C grades mean?

Grade A is like new, with minimal visible wear. Grade B is very good, with light cosmetic wear from normal office use. Grade C is good, with visible wear but full function. All three grades are working chairs; the grade reflects appearance.

How do I choose between a mesh and an upholstered task chair?

Mesh backs breathe well and are popular in warm offices and for people who sit long hours. Upholstered backs feel softer, are quieter visually and are often easier to match to finishes. Mesh should be checked for sagging or fraying, and upholstery for stains and foam breakdown.

Can I order task chairs with workstations?

Yes. Task chairs are commonly added to used cubicle and benching orders. Note that the 6'x6' workstation prices on our cubicle pages are furniture-only and do not include seating, so add chairs to your quote builder request.
