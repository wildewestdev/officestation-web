Stools are the seat people forget until the lab bench, reception counter or high-top collaboration table is already in place. Then someone discovers the task chairs sit eight inches too low. Buying used office stools is a practical way to fix that without paying new-furniture pricing for seating that often gets lighter daily use than a task chair.

Types of stools and where they work

Not every tall seat does the same job. Picking the right style matters more than the brand name on the base.

Drafting stools

Drafting stools are task chairs on a taller cylinder, usually with a foot ring and often with a backrest and arms. They are the right choice for people who sit at a counter or standing desk for hours: lab technicians, designers at tall tables, and reception staff behind a transaction counter.

Counter-height and bar-height stools

These are simpler seats built for short stays. You see them in break rooms, cafe counters and open collaboration zones where people perch for a quick meeting. Many sit on glides rather than casters so they stay put.

Perch and sit-stand stools

Perch stools let a person lean rather than fully sit. They work well next to height-adjustable desks and in huddle areas where you want people to stay loose and mobile.

The rule of thumb is roughly 10 to 12 inches of clearance between the top of the seat and the underside of the work surface. Measure your surfaces before you shop and you avoid the most common stool mistake.

How we grade pre-owned stools

Every stool is assigned one of three grades so you can decide where to spend and where to save.

  • Grade A, like new. Minimal to no visible wear. A good fit for client-facing reception areas.
  • Grade B, very good. Light cosmetic wear from normal office use. The usual choice for staff areas and collaboration zones.
  • Grade C, good. Visible wear but fully functional. Sensible for labs, shops and back-of-house spaces where appearance is secondary.

We carry pre-owned seating from brands such as Herman Miller, Steelcase, Knoll, Haworth, Humanscale, Teknion, Allsteel, Kimball, AIS and Friant, depending on what is available at the time.

Warranty reality check. Office Station is an independent reseller, not an authorized dealer. Manufacturer warranties generally do not transfer to pre-owned furniture, so read the condition grade closely and ask about any specific concern before you buy.

What to check on a used stool

Stools see more cylinder travel than most chairs because people raise and drop them constantly. Whether you are inspecting in person or reviewing photos, look at these points:

  1. Cylinder hold. The seat should stay at height under full weight without sinking.
  2. Foot ring. Confirm it tightens firmly and does not slip down the column.
  3. Base and casters. Check for cracked base arms and casters or glides suited to your floor.
  4. Seat surface. Look for tears, seam splits or foam breakdown, especially on the front edge.
  5. Backrest and controls. Test tilt and height levers if the stool has them.
  6. Height range. Confirm the minimum and maximum seat height fit your surface.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between a drafting stool and a counter stool?

A drafting stool has a pneumatic cylinder that raises the seat to counter or standing-desk height, usually with a foot ring and often with a backrest and casters. A counter stool is typically a fixed-height seat, sometimes on glides instead of wheels, built for cafe counters and bar-height tables. Drafting stools suit people who work at the surface all day, while counter stools suit short visits.

How do I know what height stool I need?

Measure from the floor to the top of the work surface. Standard counters run about 36 inches and bar-height tables about 42 inches, and you generally want roughly 10 to 12 inches between the seat and the underside of the surface. Share the surface height when you ask for a quote and we will match stools with the right cylinder range.

Do used stools come with a manufacturer warranty?

Office Station is an independent reseller, not an authorized dealer for any brand, and manufacturer warranties generally do not transfer to pre-owned furniture. That is why every piece is condition graded A, B or C. Ask about the grade and any specific wear before you order.

Can you supply stools for a lab or cleanroom-adjacent space?

We can source pre-owned stools with wipeable vinyl or polyurethane seats and glides or casters to suit the floor. We do not certify stools for cleanroom, ESD or clinical compliance, so if your facility has a written standard, confirm the specification with your safety or facilities team before ordering.

Can I buy stools along with desks and workstations?

Yes. Many projects pair counter-height stools with benching, reception desks or standing-height tables. Combining pieces on one order also simplifies delivery, which is $195 central or $295 metro in Greater Phoenix and quoted per project elsewhere.
