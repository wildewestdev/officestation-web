Office Side Chairs

Side chairs are the seats your visitors remember. Plan them alongside your workstations and reception area so the finishes match and everything arrives on one quote.

5 minute read

Office side chairs do quiet but important work. They seat clients in a private office, give a coworker a place to sit at a workstation and fill the reception area where visitors form their first impression. Because they are often an afterthought, they end up mismatched, undercounted or ordered late. Planning them with the rest of your furniture fixes all three.

Where side chairs belong in an office

Before choosing a style, map out every place a guest or colleague might sit for a few minutes. Most offices have more of these spots than they expect.

  • Private offices. One or two chairs across the desk for meetings and client conversations.
  • Manager workstations. A single guest chair tucked beside the work surface for quick check-ins.
  • Reception and lobby. A group of chairs, often with a table, sized for your busiest visitor period.
  • Small huddle spaces. Two to four chairs around a round table for short meetings.
  • Hallway waiting spots. Outside HR, interview rooms or a medical or legal consultation room.
  • Break rooms. Durable side chairs that handle spills and constant use.

Matching side chairs to your workstation and reception order

The easiest way to get a finished look is to choose side chairs at the same time as your workstations and reception pieces. A few decisions keep everything consistent:

  1. Pick one frame finish. Match the metal or wood on side chairs to workstation legs, table bases or reception furniture.
  2. Choose a fabric family. Use one fabric color for reception and a complementary neutral in offices, or run one fabric throughout.
  3. Standardize on one or two models. Two models, an armed chair for offices and reception plus an armless chair for workstations, cover most offices.
  4. Count by room, then add a buffer. Add a few extra chairs for new hires and meetings that run larger than planned.
  5. Put everything on one quote. One order makes delivery and installation simpler to coordinate.
Count before you quote. Walk your floor plan room by room and write down the number of side chairs each space needs. A precise count is the difference between a reception area that is ready on move-in day and one waiting on a second delivery.

Buying pre-owned side chairs

Office Station sells pre-owned furniture from lines including Herman Miller, Steelcase, Knoll, Haworth, Humanscale, Teknion, Allsteel, Kimball, AIS and Friant. We are an independent reseller, not an authorized dealer, and manufacturer warranties generally do not transfer to pre-owned pieces.

Every item carries a condition grade: A is like new, B is very good and C is good. For side chairs, it often makes sense to buy A grade for reception and client offices, where appearance matters most, and B or C grade for break rooms and internal huddle spaces. When you inspect a side chair, check that:

  • The frame does not rock on a flat floor and joints are tight.
  • Upholstery has no tears, pilling or stains that will not clean.
  • Arm caps and glides are intact, since missing glides can scratch floors.
  • Chairs in a set share the same fabric and frame color.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between a side chair and a task chair?

A task chair is built for hours of work with height, tilt and arm adjustments. A side chair is a simpler, usually non-swiveling seat for guests, meetings and short visits. Side chairs are lighter, take less floor space and cost less to buy and move.

How many side chairs does a typical office need?

A common starting point is one or two guest chairs for each private office, a pair at manager workstations that host frequent one-on-ones, and reception seating sized to your busiest visitor hour. Walk through how visitors actually move through your space and adjust from there.

Can side chairs be added to a workstation order?

Yes. You can request side chairs on the same quote as workstations and reception furniture, and free space planning is included. Volume pricing applies to workstations at 5% for 20 or more stations, 8% at 50 or more and 12% at 100 or more, so ask how chairs on the same order will be priced.

Do pre-owned side chairs match new furniture?

They can, but finishes vary by manufacturer and year. Ask for exact frame and fabric colors, and consider buying a full set of one model so reception and offices look consistent.
