Used Stacking Chairs

Used stacking chairs let a training room become an event space by the afternoon. Here is how to choose, grade and count them, plus exactly how delivery works.

5 minute read

Training rooms and event spaces change shape constantly. A morning onboarding session needs rows facing a screen, a lunch meeting needs tables, and an all-hands needs every seat you own. Used stacking chairs make those changes fast and keep the room clear when it is not in use. Buying them pre-owned lets you seat a large room for less, as long as you know how to judge condition and plan for storage and delivery.

Why stacking chairs work for multi-use rooms

  • Fast resets. One or two people can clear or set a room without a facilities crew.
  • Compact storage. Chairs stack in a closet or corner instead of filling the floor.
  • Durability. Simple frames with few moving parts hold up to repeated handling.
  • Easy cleaning. Poly shells wipe down quickly between sessions.

The trade-off is comfort for long sessions. Stacking chairs work well for sessions of an hour or two. For all-day training, consider upholstered seat pads or a stacking model with a padded seat and back.

What condition grades mean for stacking chairs

Every piece Office Station sells is graded A like new, B very good or C good. On stacking chairs, the grade mostly reflects cosmetic wear, since a chair that is structurally unsound should not be sold as seating at any grade.

  • Grade A: Clean shells or upholstery with minimal marks. A good fit for client-facing event rooms.
  • Grade B: Light scuffs or minor frame wear. A strong value for internal training rooms.
  • Grade C: Visible cosmetic wear but fully usable. Practical for warehouses, break rooms and overflow storage.

When you buy a large set, ask whether the chairs are one model and one color. A room full of chairs that nearly match looks less finished than a room full of B grade chairs that match exactly.

Inspection checklist for used stacking chairs

  1. Shell. Flex the seat and back gently. Look for cracks or stress whitening, especially where legs attach.
  2. Legs and frame. Set the chair on a flat floor. It should not rock. Sight down each leg for bends.
  3. Fasteners. Check that screws and rivets are tight and none are missing.
  4. Glides. Every foot should have a glide. Missing glides scratch floors and cause rocking.
  5. Stack test. Stack five or more together. They should nest straight, sit level and separate without sticking.
  6. Upholstery. On padded chairs, check for tears, stains and foam that no longer rebounds.
  7. Accessories. Confirm tablet arms, link clips and any chair dollies work.
Plan the stack, not just the chair. Stack height limits come from the manufacturer and vary by model, and arms or tablet arms reduce how many fit together. Measure your storage closet and doorway and ask whether dollies are available for the model before you commit to a quantity.

Counting chairs for a training or event room

Start from the layouts you actually use, then order for the largest one. A few practical steps:

  1. Measure the room and note doors, columns and the presenter area.
  2. Decide which layouts you need: theater, classroom with tables, U-shape or banquet rounds.
  3. Count seats for the largest layout, then add a small buffer for guests.
  4. Confirm storage space for the full quantity when stacked.

Frequently asked questions

What should I check on a used stacking chair?

Look for cracks in the shell, especially near the leg mounts, bent legs, missing floor glides and loose fasteners. Stack several chairs together to be sure they nest cleanly and sit level. A chair that rocks or stacks crooked will be a problem in daily use.

How many stacking chairs fit in a training room?

It depends on the layout. Classroom rows with tables need far more floor space per person than theater-style rows of chairs alone. Measure the room, pick a layout and use free space planning to confirm a count before ordering.

How much does delivery cost?

In Greater Phoenix, delivery is $195 in the central zone and $295 across the metro. Outside Greater Phoenix, nationwide freight is quoted per project. Ask for the delivery figure with your quote so you know your full cost up front.

Are used stacking chairs covered by a manufacturer warranty?

Generally no. Office Station is an independent reseller, not an authorized dealer, and manufacturer warranties usually do not transfer to pre-owned furniture. Condition grades and a careful inspection are how you manage that risk.

How quickly can I get stacking chairs?

It depends on what is in stock. Liquidation-grade furniture that is in stock can be ready in as little as 72 hours. Ask about current inventory and quantities when you request a quote.
