Ergonomic Haworth seating

Used Haworth Zody Chairs

The Haworth Zody is known for its adjustable lumbar support and fine-tuning for long workdays. A pre-owned Zody can deliver that ergonomic range at a lower cost, as long as every adjustment still works.

6 minute read

Why people look for a used Zody

The Haworth Zody earned its following with ergonomics. Its signature feature is lumbar support that, on many configurations, can be adjusted separately on the left and right sides of the back. Most people do not sit perfectly symmetrically, and that fine-tuning is a big part of why Zody owners tend to stay loyal to the chair.

New ergonomic chairs carry a price that makes outfitting a full team difficult. Pre-owned is how many offices put a well-adjusted chair at every desk. The catch is that an ergonomic chair is only as good as its working adjustments, so a used Zody deserves more careful inspection than a simpler chair.

The adjustments to confirm

Zody chairs were sold with different option packages. Use this table to confirm what a specific chair has and how to test it.

AdjustmentWhat it doesHow to test it
Lumbar supportChanges lower-back support, often side by sideAdjust each side through its range and feel for a clear difference in pressure
Seat heightSets thighs level and feet flatRaise fully, sit a minute and check for sinking
Seat depthMoves the seat pan forward or backSlide through the full range and confirm it locks in place
Recline tensionControls how much effort leaning back takesTurn tension from light to firm and lean back at each end
Recline lockHolds the back at a set angleLock upright, lean back hard and make sure it holds
ArmsHeight, width, depth or pivot, depending on arm typeMove through every setting and check pads and mounts for play
A Zody with a dead lumbar is just an expensive task chair. Cosmetic wear is a grading question. A lumbar, seat depth or tension control that does not work is a function question, and that is what should decide whether a chair belongs in your office.

How condition grading works on a Zody

Office Station grades pre-owned chairs A, B or C. Grades describe cosmetic condition on chairs that function. For a Zody, here is where the differences usually show.

  • Grade A, like new. Back material and seat look fresh, arm pads are intact and the base has little or no scuffing. Choose A for executive desks and client-visible areas.
  • Grade B, very good. Light wear from regular use, such as slight arm pad wear or minor base marks. B is the best value for most dedicated-desk rollouts.
  • Grade C, good. More visible wear, possibly faded fabric or worn pads, with the chair still fully usable. C works for budget ergonomic upgrades where comfort matters more than looks.

Setting up a used Zody for each person

A chair with many adjustments helps only if someone actually adjusts it. When your Zody chairs arrive, walk each person through this order. It takes about five minutes.

  1. Seat height first. Feet flat on the floor, thighs roughly level, knees at about a right angle.
  2. Seat depth next. Leave about two to three finger widths between the front edge of the seat and the back of the knees.
  3. Lumbar support. Adjust until the lower back feels supported without being pushed forward. If the chair adjusts each side, tune them separately.
  4. Recline tension. Set it so leaning back feels controlled, not like falling or fighting the chair.
  5. Arms last. Set height so shoulders stay relaxed and elbows rest near desk height, then bring arms in close enough to use.
  6. Check the desk. If the chair is set correctly but the work surface is too high, the fix is the desk or a keyboard tray, not the chair.

Red flags that should rule a Zody out

Some issues are worth walking away from, whatever the grade or the price.

  • A lumbar control that spins without changing support. The adjustment is the reason to buy this chair.
  • A seat that drifts down after you sit for a minute, which points to a failing cylinder.
  • Grinding or popping in the recline that does not go away after a few cycles.
  • Cracks in the back frame or base spokes, which are structural, not cosmetic.
  • Arms that wobble at the mount even when every adjustment is locked.

Zody or a different Haworth chair?

If you are comparing within Haworth, think in terms of seat time. The Zody suits people who sit most of the day and benefit from fine adjustments. The Very is a lighter, cleaner-looking choice that also works well in conference rooms. The Improv is a budget-friendly legacy chair for high-volume and shared seating. Many offices use a mix, putting Zody chairs at dedicated desks and simpler chairs everywhere else.

Frequently asked questions

What makes the Haworth Zody an ergonomic chair?

The Zody is built around adjustability, most notably lumbar support that can be tuned differently on each side of the back on many configurations. Depending on the options, it can also adjust seat depth, arm position and recline tension. That range lets one chair fit a wider variety of body types.

Is a used Zody worth it compared with a basic task chair?

If people sit most of the day, a working Zody is usually worth the step up because of its adjustments. For short-stay seating like training or conference rooms, a simpler chair often makes more sense.

How are pre-owned Zody chairs graded?

Office Station uses three grades. A is like new, B is very good with light cosmetic wear, and C is good, fully usable with more visible wear. On a Zody, every adjustment should function regardless of grade.

Does a used Zody come with a Haworth warranty?

Generally no. Office Station is an independent reseller, not an authorized Haworth dealer, and manufacturer warranties generally do not transfer to pre-owned furniture.

Are all Zody chairs configured the same?

No. The Zody was offered with different options, including arm types, seat depth adjustment and back materials, and there is more than one generation. Ask for the specific adjustments on a lot before you buy so you get the ergonomic features you are paying for.
