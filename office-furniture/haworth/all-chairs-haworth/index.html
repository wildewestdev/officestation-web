Used Haworth Chairs

Haworth task and conference chairs are built for long days and many owners. This guide shows you how grading works, what to test on each chair, and how to buy seating for a whole office.

5 minute read

Seating is the piece of office furniture people notice most. A chair that adjusts properly makes an eight-hour day more comfortable, and one with a sinking cylinder or a stuck tilt lock gets complained about daily. Haworth task chairs are engineered for commercial use, which is why they are worth buying pre-owned, and why checking the mechanics matters just as much as the look.

Office Station sells used Haworth chairs as an independent reseller, not an authorized Haworth dealer. Every chair is graded so you can decide where cosmetic wear is acceptable and where it is not.

Haworth seating by type

Inventory varies, so treat these as the categories you will see rather than a stock list.

Ergonomic task chairs

The everyday desk chair. Haworth task models such as Zody, Fern and Very are common on the secondary market. Look for seat depth adjustment, adjustable lumbar support and arms that move in several directions if your team sits for long stretches.

Conference chairs

Usually simpler mechanisms with a clean look. They need to be consistent in color and height across a room, so buy a matched set when you can.

Side, guest and stacking chairs

Fewer moving parts and lower cost. Good for private office guest seating, training rooms and break areas. Check frame welds and glides.

Stools and lounge seating

Drafting stools suit counter-height and standing workstations. Lounge pieces show wear on upholstery first, so grade matters more here.

Grades describe appearance. Function is a separate question, so run through the checklist below on any chair you are considering.

Checklist: testing a used Haworth chair

This takes about two minutes per chair. On a large order, test a sample from each batch and spot check the rest.

  • Cylinder. Raise the seat to the top, sit down firmly and wait a minute. The seat should not creep down. Then lower it fully and raise it again.
  • Tilt and tension. Recline and return. Adjust tension from light to firm and confirm you feel the difference.
  • Tilt lock. Lock the back upright and in at least one reclined position, if the chair has multiple positions. It should hold under pressure.
  • Seat depth. Slide the seat forward and back. It should lock at each stop.
  • Arms. Test height, width and pivot adjustments. Look for cracked arm caps and loose posts.
  • Lumbar and back. Move the lumbar support through its range. On mesh backs, check for tears, fraying or sagging.
  • Upholstery. Look for stains, tears and seam separation. Sit on the front edge, where foam breaks down first.
  • Base and casters. Roll the chair on a hard floor. Casters should spin freely, and the base should have no cracks near the center hub.
  • Noise. Squeaks and clunks during recline often point to worn mechanism parts.
Why the checklist matters. Manufacturer warranties generally do not transfer to pre-owned chairs, and Office Station is an independent reseller, not an authorized Haworth dealer. Your inspection is the warranty conversation. If something on this list matters to your team, ask about it before you order.

Buying chairs for a whole office

Outfitting 30 or 100 seats is a different exercise from buying one chair. A few tips:

  1. Count by role. Separate task seats, conference seats and guest seats. They rarely need the same model or grade.
  2. Decide where grade matters. Put Grade A where clients sit, and Grade B or C where chairs get used hard and seen less.
  3. Keep colors consistent within a room. Mixed grades are fine. Mixed colors in one conference room usually are not.
  4. Buy a few spares. Extra chairs from the same batch cover new hires and replacements with an exact match.
  5. Coordinate with desks. If you are adding workstations, the same delivery can bring seating. Installation is $95 per workstation with a $495 minimum, Greater Phoenix delivery is $195 central or $295 metro, and freight elsewhere is quoted per project.

Frequently asked questions

Which Haworth chair models are available used?

Availability changes as inventory comes in from office removals, so the models on hand vary. Haworth task chairs such as Zody, Fern and Very are well known on the pre-owned market, along with conference and side seating. Contact Office Station with the model, quantity and grade you want and ask what is currently available.

What do the condition grades mean for a chair?

Grade A is like new, with minimal to no visible wear. Grade B is very good, with light wear such as minor scuffs on the base or arms. Grade C is good, with noticeable cosmetic wear such as fabric pilling or armpad wear, while remaining usable.

Do used Haworth chairs have a warranty?

Office Station sells pre-owned Haworth as an independent reseller, not an authorized dealer. Manufacturer warranties generally do not transfer to pre-owned chairs. That is why checking the mechanism, cylinder and adjustments matters before you buy.

Can I get a matched set of chairs for a large office?

Matching a large set is easiest when a single office's seating is removed together, because the model, color and options tend to be consistent. If a matched set is not available in one grade, a mix of grades in the same model and color is a practical alternative. Ask early if you need a specific count.

Are Haworth chairs a good fit for standing desks?

A chair with a wide seat height range and good arm adjustment pairs well with a height-adjustable desk. Check the lowest and highest seat positions against your desk heights. See the used standing desks page for what to inspect on the desk side.
