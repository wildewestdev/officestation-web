Used Knoll Generation Chairs

A used Knoll Generation chair gives your team a flexible-back task chair at a fraction of new cost. Here is how grading, sourcing and large-order pricing work at Office Station.

6 minute read

The Knoll Generation chair earned its following with a back that flexes with you instead of forcing you into one posture. Lean sideways, turn to a second monitor, sit back on a call, and the chair moves with the body. That flexibility is exactly why a used Knoll Generation chair is one of the smartest seating buys for a growing office: the design ages well, the frame is durable, and pre-owned pricing lets you outfit a whole team for what a handful of new chairs would cost.

Why teams choose a pre-owned Generation chair

Generation is a task chair built for people who move while they work. A few traits make it a strong candidate for buying used:

  • Flexible back design. The back is built to give in more than one direction, so it supports shifting, twisting and reclining without constant adjustment.
  • Durable construction. The polymer back and sturdy base hold up well in commercial use, which means a well-kept chair still has plenty of life after its first office.
  • Clean, neutral look. It fits open plans, private offices and training rooms without looking dated.
  • Common configurations. Many units come with adjustable arms, seat depth adjustment and tension control, which helps one chair model suit a range of body types.

Because Generation chairs were widely specified in corporate offices, they turn up regularly in decommissioning projects. That steady supply is what makes matched sets possible for larger orders.

Condition grades explained

Every pre-owned chair we sell carries a condition grade. Grades describe appearance and wear. A chair should be fully functional in any grade we list.

GradeWhat it meansBest fit
A, like newMinimal to no visible wear. Upholstery, arm pads and base look close to new.Client-facing offices, executive areas, anyone who wants the new look
B, very goodLight, normal signs of use such as minor base scuffs or slight arm pad wear.Most open-plan teams; the best value for everyday work
C, goodMore visible cosmetic wear. Mechanisms work, but the chair shows its history.Training rooms, call centers, back-of-house and budget-first rollouts

If you are buying a large set, you can mix grades on purpose. Grade A for spaces visitors see, Grade B or C for heads-down areas, and you lower the total without anyone sitting in a chair that does not work.

What to check on a used Generation chair

Whether you buy from us or anywhere else, these are the points that separate a great used chair from a tired one:

  1. Back flex and return. Push the back in a few directions. It should flex smoothly and return to shape without creaks or stiffness.
  2. Height adjustment. The seat should rise and lower on command and hold its height under your weight. A slowly sinking seat points to a worn gas cylinder.
  3. Recline tension. Turn the tension control through its range and confirm you can feel the difference.
  4. Arms. If the chair has adjustable arms, test each direction. Look at arm pads for cracks or peeling.
  5. Seat cushion and fabric. Check for flattening, stains and seam wear.
  6. Base and casters. Look for cracked base legs and casters that stick or wobble. Match the caster type to your floor.
An honest note on warranty. Manufacturer warranties generally do not transfer to pre-owned furniture. Plan as if your used Generation chair has no Knoll warranty, and rely on the condition grade, a quick inspection and clear communication with us before purchase.

Generation compared with other pre-owned chairs

Generation is not the only strong choice. If you want a more traditional mesh or upholstered task chair, browse the full range of pre-owned office chairs, including Herman Miller, Steelcase, Haworth and Humanscale models. Knoll also made the Life chair, a different design with its own feel, covered on our used Knoll Life chair page.

A simple rule of thumb: choose Generation when your people move a lot during the day and want a chair that follows them. Choose a more structured chair when users prefer firm, locked-in support.
