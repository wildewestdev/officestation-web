Used Steelcase Gesture Chair Buying Guide

The Gesture is one of the most adjustable task chairs Steelcase makes, which means more parts to check on a pre-owned unit. This guide walks you through grades and a hands-on inspection.

6 minute read

The Steelcase Gesture was designed around how people actually use devices at a desk: leaning in to type, reclining with a tablet, turning sideways to a second screen. Its arms, seat and back adjust to follow those postures. That flexibility is what makes the Gesture a favorite, and it is also why buying a used one takes a little more care than buying a simpler chair. More adjustments mean more mechanisms to test.

This guide covers what the condition grades tell you, what to inspect in person or in photos, and which configuration details to confirm so you get the chair you expect.

Know the chair before you shop

A few features define the Gesture and are worth understanding up front:

  • Multi-directional arms. The arms move up and down, pivot, and slide in and out, so they can support your forearms whether you are typing or holding a phone.
  • Flexible back. The back is built to move with your spine as you shift, rather than acting as a rigid panel.
  • Adjustable seat depth. The seat slides forward and back to fit different leg lengths.
  • Recline control. You can set how much the chair reclines and how firmly it resists.

Gesture chairs have also been offered in different back styles and with options such as adjustable lumbar support and a headrest. On a pre-owned unit, confirm which options are actually on the chair rather than assuming.

Inspection checklist for a pre-owned Gesture

Whether you are sitting in the chair or reviewing photos and video, work through these in order.

Arms

  1. Raise and lower each arm through its full range. It should lock at each height and not drop when you lean on it.
  2. Pivot each arm pad and slide it in and out. Movement should feel controlled, not loose or wobbly.
  3. Look at the arm pads for cracks, tears or separation from the base of the pad.
  4. Compare left and right. Both arms should behave the same way.

Seat and back

  1. Slide the seat depth forward and back. It should move smoothly and stay put.
  2. Sit and lean back into the chair. The back should flex and return without creaks, clicks or grinding.
  3. Check the upholstery at the front edge of the seat, which takes the most wear.
  4. If the chair has adjustable lumbar, move it through its range and confirm it holds.

Recline, height and base

  1. Set the recline limit at a few positions and confirm each one engages.
  2. Adjust the recline tension from light to firm. You should feel a clear difference.
  3. Sit fully and raise and lower the seat. The cylinder should hold height with no slow sinking.
  4. Roll the chair on a hard floor. Casters should turn freely and none should be missing or cracked.
  5. Look at the base for cracks, especially where the legs meet the center.

Configuration details to confirm

Two Gesture chairs can look alike in a thumbnail and still differ in ways that matter. Before you order, confirm:

  • Back style and whether lumbar support is included.
  • Headrest, if you want one. It is easy to assume and hard to add later.
  • Upholstery color and material, especially if you are matching existing chairs.
  • Frame and base finish, which affects how a room of chairs reads together.
  • Casters suited to your floor. Hard floors and carpet behave differently.

If you are buying several chairs, ask for the count available in a single matching configuration and grade. Mixed batches work fine for a back office, but a conference room or leadership row usually looks better matched.

Is the Gesture right for your team?

The Gesture shines for people who spend long days at a desk and move between keyboard, phone and screen. If your team mostly sits in short bursts, a simpler chair may give you better value per seat. Frequently asked questions

Is a used Steelcase Gesture chair worth it?

For many buyers, yes. The Gesture is built for heavy daily use, and a well-kept pre-owned unit can cost far less than new. The key is confirming that the arms, recline and height adjustment all work smoothly before you buy.

What do the condition grades mean?

Grade A is like new, with little or no visible wear. Grade B is very good, with light cosmetic wear. Grade C is good, with more noticeable cosmetic wear but sound function. Ask what specific marks a unit has if you are choosing between grades.

Does a used Gesture come with the Steelcase warranty?

Generally no. Office Station is an independent reseller, not an authorized Steelcase dealer, and manufacturer warranties generally do not transfer to pre-owned furniture. That is why a careful inspection matters.

What is the most common issue on a used Gesture?

The arms deserve the closest look because they move in many directions and get handled constantly. Check that each arm holds its position and that the arm pads are not cracked or loose. Also test the recline and the height cylinder.

Can I buy Gesture chairs in quantity for an office?

Yes. Ask for a count by grade and configuration so your chairs match. For larger rooms, pair them with workstations and we can coordinate delivery and installation together.
