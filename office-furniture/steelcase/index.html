Used Steelcase Office Furniture

Steelcase furniture is built for long commercial service, which makes it a smart pre-owned buy. Here is what to expect on condition, pricing and warranty when you buy it from an independent reseller.

6 minute read

Steelcase is one of the names you see most often in corporate offices, and that is exactly why it shows up so often on the pre-owned market. When a company closes a floor, consolidates or refreshes, its chairs, panel systems and desks do not wear out on the same schedule as its lease. That furniture can go on to serve another office for years.

Buying used Steelcase office furniture is a good way to get commercial-grade construction at a lower price. It also comes with trade-offs you should understand up front, mainly around condition, matching and warranty. This page covers all three.

Buying from an independent reseller

Office Station sells pre-owned Steelcase as an independent reseller. We are not an authorized Steelcase dealer and are not affiliated with Steelcase. That distinction affects two things you care about.

  • Warranty. Manufacturer warranties generally do not transfer to pre-owned furniture. Plan your purchase assuming no manufacturer coverage.
  • Configuration. A dealer can order any current option. A reseller works with what came out of real offices, so exact finishes and quantities depend on inventory.

What you get in return is lower cost, much faster availability and furniture that is kept out of a landfill.

Be skeptical of warranty promises. If any pre-owned seller tells you a manufacturer warranty comes along with used furniture, ask for that in writing and verify it with the manufacturer. The honest default is that it does not. Condition grades and clear photos are the tools that actually protect you.

How condition grades work

Every pre-owned piece is graded so you can compare options without guessing.

  • Grade A, like new. Minimal to no visible wear. Choose it for executive areas and client-facing spaces.
  • Grade B, very good. Light signs of use. The practical middle for most general office seating and workstations.
  • Grade C, good. Visible wear that does not affect function. Ideal for training rooms, call centers, warehouses and budget-sensitive build-outs.

A grade describes appearance and function at the time of sale. It does not replace your own questions. If a specific detail matters to you, such as a fabric color, a stain on one arm or whether a desk base runs quietly, ask about that exact unit and request a photo.

Mixing grades is often the smartest move. You can put Grade A chairs in the conference room and Grade C in a back-office training space, and nobody will notice the difference where it does not matter.

Before you buy used Steelcase

  1. Decide what matters most: exact match, speed or cost. You can usually optimize two.
  2. If you are adding to existing Steelcase furniture, send photos and measurements of what you already have.
  3. Pick a grade per area rather than one grade for the whole office.
  4. Confirm what is included, such as keys, power components, arms or casters.
  5. Assume no manufacturer warranty and ask your condition questions now.
  6. Take advantage of free space planning before you lock in a count.

Frequently asked questions

Is Office Station an authorized Steelcase dealer?

No. Office Station is an independent reseller of pre-owned furniture and is not an authorized Steelcase dealer or affiliated with Steelcase. The Steelcase name is used only to describe the furniture being sold.

Does the Steelcase warranty transfer to used furniture?

Generally, no. Manufacturer warranties generally do not transfer to pre-owned furniture. Rely on the condition grade, detailed photos and your own questions about wear and function before you buy.

What do Grade A, B and C mean?

Grade A is like new, Grade B is very good with light signs of use, and Grade C is good with visible wear that does not affect function. Grades let you spend more where appearance matters and save where it does not.

How fast can I get used Steelcase workstations?

Liquidation-grade pieces in stock can be ready in as little as 72 hours. Remanufactured furniture usually takes 7 to 14 days. New furniture typically takes 4 to 6 weeks.

Can I sell my old Steelcase furniture?

Often, yes. Office Station gives a written net offer equal to the resale value of the furniture minus removal cost. If that number is positive, you are paid for the furniture.
