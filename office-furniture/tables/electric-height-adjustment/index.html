Used Electric Height Adjustable Desks

Pre-owned sit-stand desks from corporate liquidations, with a practical guide to testing the motors, columns and bases that decide how long they last.

6 minute read

Electric sit-stand desks changed what offices expect from a work surface. They also changed what you need to check when buying used. A traditional desk is a top and a base. An electric height adjustable desk adds motors, a control box, a handset, wiring and telescoping columns, and each one can wear differently.

The good news is that commercial-grade bases are built for years of daily cycles, and many come out of corporate offices with plenty of life left. This page explains how these desks work, how to test used electric height adjustable desks before you buy, and how Office Station grades and delivers them.

How an electric sit-stand desk works

Understanding the parts makes testing much easier:

  • Columns. Two or three nested steel tubes per leg that telescope up and down. Three-stage columns offer a wider height range.
  • Motors. Either one motor with a drive shaft running to both legs, or one motor in each leg.
  • Control box. The electronics that power the motors, keep the legs in sync and handle safety cutoffs.
  • Handset. Simple up and down paddles, or a keypad with memory presets.
  • Frame and feet. The crossbar and feet that keep the desk stable at full height.

What to test on motors, columns and bases

Do not judge a used sit-stand desk by its top. Put weight on it and run it through its full range. Here is the checklist we recommend:

  1. Full travel under load. Place a realistic load on the top, such as monitors or an equivalent weight, and run from lowest to highest and back. The desk should not stall, slow sharply or stop short.
  2. Leg synchronization. Watch both legs. The top should stay level the entire way. A tilt that grows as it rises points to a motor or control box issue.
  3. Motor sound. A steady hum is normal. Grinding, clicking, or a pitch that changes between legs is a warning sign.
  4. Handset and presets. Every button should respond on the first press. Save a memory preset and confirm the desk returns to it.
  5. Error codes and resets. If the display shows a code, try the manufacturer's reset procedure. A desk that throws codes repeatedly after a reset needs a closer look.
  6. Anti-collision stop. If equipped, lower the desk gently onto a soft object. It should stop and reverse.
  7. Wobble at full height. Push side to side and front to back at standing height. Some movement is normal, but excessive wobble often means loose frame bolts or worn column glides.
  8. Column condition. Look for scratches along the inner stages, which can signal binding, and check that the columns are straight.
  9. Cables and power supply. Confirm all cables are present, undamaged and properly clipped, and that the power cord is the right one for the control box.
  10. Top mounting. Make sure the top is fully secured to the frame with no stripped screw holes.
Match parts, not just brands. Handsets, control boxes and motors are often specific to a particular base model. When you buy a set of used sit-stand desks, keep each desk's components together so replacements and troubleshooting stay simple.

Planning a room of sit-stand desks

Height adjustable desks need a bit more planning than fixed desks. Leave space for the top to clear windowsills, shelving and panels at full height. Route cables with enough slack for the full range of travel so nothing unplugs or snags. And plan outlet locations, since our prices exclude electrical work.

Frequently asked questions

Are used electric standing desks reliable?

A quality commercial sit-stand base can stay in service for years, but the electrical parts are where problems show up. A used desk that raises and lowers smoothly under load, holds a level top and responds to the controller every time is a good candidate. Test before you commit, and choose the grade that fits your budget.

Should I buy a single-motor or dual-motor base?

Dual-motor bases generally lift more weight, run more evenly and handle wider tops. Single-motor bases use a drive shaft to move both legs and work well for lighter setups. If you use multiple monitors or a heavy top, lean toward dual motor.

Do used height adjustable desks come with a warranty?

Manufacturer warranties generally do not transfer to pre-owned furniture. Office Station is an independent reseller, not an authorized dealer. That is why we grade condition as A, B or C and why a function test matters.

Do height adjustable desks need special electrical work?

Each base plugs into a standard outlet, but a room full of desks can call for more outlets or better cable routing. Our prices exclude electrical work. Plan outlet locations during free space planning so cords reach the full range of travel.

How fast can I get used sit-stand desks?

Liquidation-grade desks in stock can be ready in as little as 72 hours. Remanufactured items usually take 7 to 14 days, and new typically takes 4 to 6 weeks.
