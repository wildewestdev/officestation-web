Used Training Tables

Flip-top and nesting training tables let one room work as a classroom on Monday and an open floor on Tuesday. Here is how to buy them pre-owned, what to inspect, and how to plan the room before you order.

6 minute read

Why training rooms run on flip-top and nesting tables

A training room rarely stays in one configuration. You might run a 24-seat classroom session in the morning, break into small groups after lunch, and clear the floor for an all-hands meeting on Friday. Fixed conference tables cannot keep up with that. Training tables can, because they roll, fold and store.

Buying them used makes sense for most companies. Training tables are simple furniture: a laminate top, a steel base, a tilt mechanism and casters. When those parts are sound, a pre-owned table performs the same as a new one at a lower cost, and it is often available far sooner.

Types of used training tables

Flip-top tables

The top pivots to vertical so tables can be pushed against a wall. They are the most common style in corporate training rooms. Look for a release lever or paddle you can reach from standing height without bending under the table.

Nesting tables

A nesting table flips its top and is designed so the bases interlock. Ten nesting tables can often stand in the footprint of three or four. If the room doubles as event space or you have limited storage, nesting is worth prioritizing.

Fixed-leg training tables

These are narrow rectangular tables without a flip mechanism. They cost less and have fewer parts to wear out, but they are harder to clear. They suit rooms that stay in a classroom layout most of the time.

Modesty panels and wire management

A modesty panel hides legs and bags from the presenter's view. A wire trough or grommet keeps laptop chargers off the floor. Both are practical in a training room, and both are easy to confirm on a used table before you buy.

What to check on a pre-owned training table

Every table Office Station sells carries a condition grade: A is like new, B is very good with light signs of use, and C is good with visible wear but full function. Grades tell you about appearance. The checklist below covers function.

  • Flip mechanism. Tilt the top up and down several times. It should lock firmly flat with no wobble and release without forcing.
  • Casters. Roll the table across the floor. Wheels should turn freely, and locking casters should actually hold.
  • Edge banding. Run a hand along all four edges. Loose or chipped banding catches sleeves and gets worse over time.
  • Top surface. Light scratches are normal on B and C grades. Deep gouges, swelling near seams or lifted laminate are not.
  • Base alignment. Set the table flat and press on each corner. A bent leg shows up as rocking.
  • Nesting fit. If you are buying nesting tables, nest two together. Mixed series from different production runs do not always nest cleanly.
  • Matching finish. For a room of 20 tables, confirm the laminate color and base finish match across the whole lot.
Buy the room, not the table. A set of training tables from one liquidation usually matches in size, finish and mechanism. Piecing a room together from mixed sources is how you end up with tables that will not nest and tops that sit at slightly different heights.

Room layout help before you order

The fastest way to overbuy or underbuy training tables is to count seats without measuring the room. Space planning is free, and a short layout pass answers the questions that drive the order:

  1. Headcount. The maximum number of attendees you need to seat at once.
  2. Layout style. Classroom rows face the front. A U-shape suits discussion. Pods of two or four tables suit group exercises.
  3. Aisles. Plan at least 36 inches between rows so people can pass behind seated attendees, and check your local code requirements for egress.
  4. Power. Note floor and wall outlet locations so laptop cords do not cross walkways.
  5. Storage. Measure the wall or closet where tables will nest when the room is cleared.

Send those details along with the room dimensions and we will suggest table sizes and a count that fit. You can pair the tables with seating from our pre-owned office chairs selection, where stackable and nesting chairs make the same room easy to clear.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between a flip-top and a nesting training table?

A flip-top table has a top that tilts to vertical. A nesting table does that and is also built so several tables slide together front to back, taking far less floor space in storage. Most nesting tables are flip-top, but not every flip-top table nests.

How many people fit at a standard training table?

A 60 inch table seats two people comfortably and a 72 inch table seats two with more elbow room or three in a tight classroom layout. Plan about 30 inches of table length per person if you want attendees to use laptops without crowding.

Do used training tables come with a manufacturer warranty?

Office Station is an independent reseller, not an authorized dealer, so manufacturer warranties generally do not transfer to pre-owned furniture. That is why each table is condition-graded and why you should check the flip mechanism, casters and edges before buying.

Can you help me lay out a training room?

Yes. Space planning is free. Send the room dimensions, door and outlet locations, and your target headcount, and we will suggest a classroom, U-shape or pod layout and the table sizes that fit it.
