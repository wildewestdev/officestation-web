Used Break Room Tables

Durable pre-owned breakroom tables sourced from office liquidations, ready fast when in stock and delivered with pricing you can see up front.

5 minute read

A breakroom is where your team actually spends its downtime, and the tables set the tone. They also take more abuse than almost anything else in the office: hot coffee, wet lunch containers, chairs banging into bases and constant wiping with cleaning spray. That combination makes used break room tables a practical buy. Commercial tables are built to handle this, and there is little reason to pay new prices for a surface that will pick up its first scratch in the first week.

Office Station pulls breakroom tables from corporate liquidations, grades each one, and gets in-stock pieces out the door quickly. Here is how to choose the right tables, what to inspect, and what delivery and installation cost.

Common breakroom table styles

  • Round cafe tables. 30 to 42 inches across, usually on a single disc or X base. They fit tight corners and encourage small-group conversation.
  • Square tables. 30 or 36 inches square. They can be pushed together for larger groups and separated again for daily use.
  • Rectangular tables. 30 by 60 or 30 by 72 inches are typical. Efficient for seating six or more along a wall of windows or in a long room.
  • Standing-height and bar-height tables. 40 to 42 inches tall. Good for quick coffee breaks and for rooms where you want more seats per square foot.
  • Communal tables. Long tables for eight or more that turn a breakroom into an all-hands or lunch-and-learn space.

Sizing your breakroom

Count the number of people who eat at the same time during your busiest window, then plan seating for that number plus a small buffer. The table below gives practical starting points.

Plan the line, not just the tables. The busiest spot in any breakroom is the path from the door to the appliances. Keep tables out of that path and the whole room feels bigger. Our free space planning maps this for you.

What to inspect on used breakroom tables

Breakroom tables fail in predictable ways. Check these before you accept any piece:

  1. Edge banding. Moisture gets under loose edges and swells the core. Run your hand around the full perimeter.
  2. Top-to-base attachment. Rock the top gently. Any wobble at the connection point usually means loose or stripped screws.
  3. Base stability. Single-pedestal tables should not tip when you lean on the edge. Check that all leveling glides are present.
  4. Surface stains and heat marks. Laminate tolerates a lot, but deep burns and rings do not clean out.
  5. Folding or flip mechanisms. If the table nests for storage, confirm the latch locks firmly in the open position.

Frequently asked questions

What size break room table do I need?

Start with how many people eat at the same time, not total headcount. A 36-inch round seats about four, a 42-inch round seats four comfortably, and a 30 by 60 inch rectangle seats six. Leave roughly 36 inches behind each chair so people can get in and out.

How quickly can used break room tables be delivered?

Liquidation-grade tables that are in stock can be ready in as little as 72 hours. Remanufactured pieces usually take 7 to 14 days. Greater Phoenix delivery is $195 central or $295 metro, and freight outside Phoenix is quoted per project.

What condition grade should I choose for a breakroom?

Many offices choose Grade B or Grade C for breakrooms because the tables will see daily wear regardless. Grade C pieces are fully functional and structurally sound with visible cosmetic wear. If the breakroom doubles as a space for client events, Grade A or B is worth considering.
