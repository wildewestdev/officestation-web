Used Teknion Cubicles

Teknion panel and benching systems hold up well on the second market. Here is how used Teknion cubicles are graded, what a 6'x6' station costs at each tier, and what to check before you buy.

6 minute read

Why buyers look for used Teknion cubicles

Teknion builds systems furniture for large corporate floors, and that is exactly why it shows up on the pre-owned market. When a company consolidates or relocates, whole floors of matching panels, worksurfaces and storage come out at once. That gives you a chance to buy a consistent, well-engineered system at a fraction of new cost.

Teknion workstations are generally built around steel frames with replaceable skins, so tired fabric or scuffed tiles can be swapped without scrapping the frame. That makes them strong candidates for remanufacturing, and it means a used station can look far newer than its age.

One important note up front: Office Station is an independent reseller, not an authorized Teknion dealer. Manufacturer warranties generally do not transfer to pre-owned furniture. What you get from us is a clear condition grade, honest pricing and a plan that fits your space.

How we grade pre-owned Teknion pieces

Every piece carries a condition grade so you are never buying blind.

  • Grade A, like new: minimal visible wear, clean fabric, straight tops and working hardware.
  • Grade B, very good: light wear such as minor edge scuffs or small fabric marks that disappear at a normal viewing distance.
  • Grade C, good: fully functional with visible cosmetic wear. A smart choice for back-of-house, training rooms and warehouse offices.

Liquidation-grade stations are sold as found and graded. Remanufactured stations have been cleaned and refreshed so a whole run looks consistent.

What to check before you buy used Teknion cubicles

Systems furniture is only as good as its fit. Walk through this checklist before you commit.

  1. Panel heights. Decide on seated privacy, standing sightlines or a mix. Lower panels open up light; taller panels cut noise and distraction.
  2. Footprint and aisles. A 6'x6' station needs aisle clearance on top of its own footprint. Confirm egress widths with your building before you finalize a count.
  3. Power and data. Note where your floor cores, wall outlets or ceiling feeds sit. Some panels carry base power, and your electrician will need to confirm compatibility with building power.
  4. Matching an existing floor. If you are adding to a Teknion layout already in place, photograph panel labels, connectors and finishes. Mixed product generations do not always connect cleanly.
  5. Worksurfaces. Look for flat tops, intact edge banding and solid brackets. Consider whether some users need height-adjustable surfaces.
  6. Storage. Count pedestals, overheads and lateral files per person. Confirm you are getting keys or can rekey locks.
  7. Fabric and finish. Ask for close-up photos of the actual lot. Remanufactured stations are the easier route to a uniform color.
  8. Delivery access. Confirm dock, freight elevator and after-hours rules at the receiving building.
Match first, count second. The most common problem with used systems furniture is not condition. It is a lot that looks right in photos but uses different heights or connectors than the layout you planned. Lock in the plan, then source the product to match it.
