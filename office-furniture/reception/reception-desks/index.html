Used Reception Desks

Your reception desk is the first thing a visitor sees and one of the hardest pieces to fit after the fact. Here is how to buy a pre-owned one that looks right, fits your lobby and arrives without surprises.

6 minute read

A reception desk is not like a task chair or a filing cabinet. It sits in the most visible spot in your office, it has to work for the person behind it all day, and it has to fit a space that was usually designed around something else. Buying a used reception desk can save a meaningful amount of money, but only if you know what to measure and what to inspect before it shows up on a truck.

This guide walks you through the choices, the condition grades, and the real costs of getting a pre-owned desk into your lobby.

Reception desk styles and where they work

Most reception desks fall into a handful of configurations. Picking the right one first narrows your search and keeps you from falling for a desk that will never fit.

ConfigurationBest forWatch out for
Straight desk with transaction topSmall lobbies, one receptionist, check-in countersLimited worksurface behind the counter
L-shaped with returnA single receptionist who also handles admin workReturn must be on the correct side for your entry path
U-shapedHigh-volume front desks, shared coverageLarge footprint and more components to move
Two-person or extended runMedical, service and multi-tenant lobbiesMatching finishes if components came from different sources
Curved or radius frontStatement lobbies and open entriesHard to reconfigure and hard to add onto later

Handedness matters more than most buyers expect. On an L-shaped desk, the return should sit where it will not block the path from the entrance to the rest of the office. If you find the right desk with the return on the wrong side, ask whether the unit can be reconfigured before you assume it can.

How condition grades apply to reception desks

Every pre-owned piece from Office Station carries a condition grade. On a reception desk, the grade tells you something slightly different than it does on a chair, because a few surfaces carry almost all of the visual weight.

  • Grade A, like new. Minimal to no visible wear. The right pick when the desk is the centerpiece of a client-facing lobby.
  • Grade B, very good. Light signs of use, usually on the working side or on edges that face the receptionist. A strong value for most offices.
  • Grade C, good. Visible wear that does not affect function. Worth considering for back-of-house check-in, internal service counters, or a desk you plan to refinish or add a new top to.

The front face and the transaction top are what visitors see. The worksurface behind the counter is what your receptionist sees. A Grade B desk with a clean front and some wear on the inside worksurface can look better from the lobby than the grade alone suggests, so ask for photos from both sides.

Honest warranty note. Office Station is an independent reseller of pre-owned furniture, not an authorized dealer for any manufacturer. Manufacturer warranties generally do not transfer to pre-owned pieces, so the condition grade and your own inspection of photos are your main protection. Ask questions before you buy, not after delivery.

What to measure before you buy

Most reception desk problems are measurement problems. Run through this checklist before you commit.

  1. The lobby footprint. Measure the full area and mark where visitors enter, where seating goes and where staff pass through to the rest of the office.
  2. Clearance behind the desk. Your receptionist needs room to roll a chair back and stand up. Leave a comfortable walkway behind the worksurface, not just enough space to squeeze by.
  3. The delivery path. Measure the narrowest door, any tight hallway turn and the elevator cab if you are above the ground floor. Compare those to the largest single component, not the assembled desk.
  4. Transaction top height. If visitors sign in, hand over paperwork or use a tablet at the counter, confirm the top height works for standing visitors.
  5. Accessible counter section. Many lobbies need a lower counter section for visitors using a wheelchair. Check your own requirements and whether the desk you are considering includes one.
  6. Power and data locations. Note where floor or wall outlets are. A desk placed away from power means cords crossing the floor or an electrician visit.

Matching the rest of your lobby

A reception desk rarely stands alone. You will likely want a task chair for the receptionist and seating for visitors. If you are furnishing the whole front of house at once, it is easier to coordinate finishes and tones when you choose the pieces together.

For the receptionist, prioritize a chair with real adjustment, since this person sits for long stretches and gets up often. Browse pre-owned office chairs with the same condition grades, or look through the wider pre-owned office furniture selection for storage and lobby pieces.

Frequently asked questions

What do the condition grades on a used reception desk mean?

Office Station uses three grades. Grade A is like new, Grade B is very good with light signs of use, and Grade C is good with visible wear that does not affect function. Ask for photos of the transaction top and the front face, since those are the surfaces visitors actually see.

How much does delivery cost for a used reception desk in Phoenix?

Greater Phoenix delivery is $195 for central zones and $295 for the wider metro. Outside Greater Phoenix, freight is quoted per project based on size, weight and destination.

Will a used reception desk fit through my door?

Many reception desks ship as separate components such as a main unit, a return and a transaction top, which makes most doorways workable. Measure your narrowest door, hallway turn and elevator cab, then compare those numbers against the largest single component, not the assembled desk.

Does a manufacturer warranty come with a pre-owned reception desk?

Generally, no. Office Station is an independent reseller, not an authorized dealer, and manufacturer warranties generally do not transfer to pre-owned furniture. That is why the condition grade and a close look at photos matter before you buy.

Can you help me lay out the lobby?

Yes. Space planning is free. Send a sketch or floor plan with dimensions, door locations and where visitors enter, and the layout can be checked against the desk you are considering.
