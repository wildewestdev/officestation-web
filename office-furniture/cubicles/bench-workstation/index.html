Used Benching Workstations

Benching puts more people in less space with fewer parts. Here is how pre-owned benching compares with panel stations, and what a project actually costs.

7 minute read

Open-plan offices lean on benching for a simple reason: it puts more people in less space with fewer parts. Used benching workstations take that efficiency further by cutting the price per seat. The trade-off is privacy and acoustics, so before you commit to a floor full of shared desks, it is worth comparing benching honestly against traditional panel stations.

Benching vs panel workstations

Best forCollaborative teams, hoteling, hybrid schedulesFocus work, phone-heavy roles, confidential tasks

Many offices use both. Benching goes to teams that collaborate all day and people who are in the office a few days a week. Panel stations go to roles that need quiet or visual privacy.

Plan for the noise before you buy. The most common benching regret is not the desks, it is the sound. Pair a benching floor with enclosed meeting rooms or focus spaces, and consider desk-mounted screens on runs where people take calls.

Choosing a grade for benching

Liquidation-grade is the fastest and least expensive path. It fits startups, project teams and overflow space where speed matters more than a perfect finish. Remanufactured sits in the middle, a good choice when you want a consistent, refreshed look without new-furniture lead times. New makes sense when you need exact finishes or a very specific configuration and can wait 4 to 6 weeks.

For individual components such as chairs, pedestals and screens, pre-owned pieces are condition graded A like new, B very good or C good. Office Station is an independent reseller, not an authorized dealer, and manufacturer warranties generally do not transfer to pre-owned furniture.

Used benching checklist

  1. Measure the run. Confirm total length, desk depth and aisle clearance on your floor plan.
  2. Count seats per run. Double-sided benches seat people facing each other; single-sided runs work along walls.
  3. Check the frame. Look for bent legs, stripped connectors and missing beam hardware.
  4. Confirm power. Know whether the system uses plug-in power or needs hardwiring, and where floor or wall feeds are.
  5. Specify screens. Decide on desk-mounted privacy screens before ordering, not after.
  6. Plan storage. Benching has little built-in storage, so budget pedestals or lockers.
  7. Add seating. Workstation prices are furniture only; order task chairs separately.

Layout tips for a benching floor

A good benching plan looks simple on paper, but a few details decide whether people like working there.

Keep runs a reasonable length

Very long runs turn into corridors. Breaking a floor into shorter runs with cross aisles makes it easier to move around and gives teams a sense of their own neighborhood.

Leave real aisle space

Chairs roll back further than people expect. Allow enough clearance behind each seat that someone can walk past without everyone scooting in, and confirm aisles meet your building's egress requirements.

Put the loud work at the ends

Place collaboration tables, printers and high-top areas at the ends of runs or near meeting rooms, not in the middle of heads-down seating.

Think about power early

Benching usually feeds power down a shared beam, so the location of floor boxes and wall outlets drives where runs can go. Share your electrical plan with us during free space planning, and bring in your electrician before layout is final.

Frequently asked questions

What is a benching workstation?

Benching is a shared-frame desk system where several work surfaces run along a common spine or beam, usually facing each other, with little or no panel between seats. Instead of each person having a walled cubicle, teams sit at a long run of connected desks, often with low screens and shared power running down the middle.

Is benching cheaper than cubicles?

Often, because benching uses fewer components per seat and a smaller footprint. The final cost depends on configuration, screens, power and grade, so we quote benching per project. For a reference point, a 6'x6' panel workstation is $615 liquidation-grade, $1,245 remanufactured or $1,920 new, furniture only.

How much does installation cost?

Professional installation is $95 per workstation with a $495 minimum. Electrical work, including hardwiring power to the system, is not included in furniture or install pricing and should be handled by a licensed electrician.

How quickly can I get used benching?

Liquidation-grade furniture that is in stock can be ready in as little as 72 hours. Remanufactured furniture usually takes 7 to 14 days, and new furniture typically takes 4 to 6 weeks. Benching availability depends on what is in inventory, so confirm counts early.

Are there volume discounts?

Yes. Volume pricing is 5% off at 20 or more stations, 8% at 50 or more, and 12% at 100 or more. Benching projects tend to involve long runs, so they often reach those tiers.
