Used Steelcase Montage for Matching Existing Installs

Most people searching for used Steelcase Montage already have it on their floor. This guide walks you through documenting your install so the pre-owned pieces you add actually connect and match.

6 minute read

When your company already runs on Steelcase Montage, replacing the whole floor to add a handful of people rarely makes sense. Buying used Steelcase Montage lets you grow into the system you have. The challenge is that "Montage" on a listing does not guarantee a piece will fit your stations. Heights, hardware, finishes and electrical components all need to line up, and the only reliable way to get there is to start with a clear record of what you own.

Step 1: Document your existing install

Before you look at inventory, spend 30 minutes gathering the details below. Doing this once saves a lot of back-and-forth and reduces the chance of a delivery that does not fit.

What to recordHow to capture itWhy it matters
Panel heightsTape measure from floor to top of each height you useMismatched heights break sightlines and connections
Panel widthsMeasure each width in a typical runDetermines how new runs meet existing ones
Connector hardwareClose-up photos where panels meet, including cornersHardware is the most common compatibility question
Worksurface depth and shapeMeasure depth and note straight, corner or extended shapesKeeps new stations consistent with the room
Power and data componentsPhotos of the base or wherever power runs, plus the feed pointYour electrician needs to know what connects
Fabric, tile and laminateDaylight photos, plus any finish labels you can findDrives finish matching and remanufacturing decisions
StoragePhotos of pedestals and overheadsMatching storage finishes is easy to overlook
Look under the worksurface. Manufacturer labels on the underside of worksurfaces and inside panel trim often carry part numbers and finish information. A clear photo of a label can answer questions that measurements alone cannot.

Step 2: Decide how exact the match needs to be

Not every expansion needs a perfect match, and being clear about that widens your options.

  • Structural match only. The added stations must connect and stand at the right heights, but the finish can differ. This is the easiest to source and works well for a new team cluster.
  • Close visual match. The stations should read as the same system from across the room. Remanufactured stations are often the practical path here.
  • Exact match within an existing run. The new station sits between existing ones and must be indistinguishable. This is the hardest to source pre-owned, so be prepared for longer searches or for new components.

Condition grades apply to pre-owned pieces: A is like new, B is very good and C is good. When you are matching an older install, a grade B piece sometimes blends better than a grade A piece, because it shows similar light wear to the stations around it.

Step 4: Plan the layout around the seams

Where the new stations meet the old ones is where mismatches show. A few layout moves reduce that risk:

  1. Add stations at the end of a run or as their own pod rather than in the middle.
  2. Use a wall, column or aisle as a natural break between existing and added stations.
  3. Move your best-condition existing stations to high-visibility spots and put added stations where they are seen less.
  4. Keep panel heights consistent within a sightline, even if finishes change.

Step 5: Coordinate electrical early

Workstation pricing does not include electrical. If your added stations need power, involve your building and a licensed electrician before delivery day. Share the photos of your existing power components and feed points so they can confirm what will connect and what additional work is needed.

Frequently asked questions

How do I make sure used Montage will connect to my stations?

Document your existing install first: panel heights and widths, connector hardware, worksurface depths, power components and finishes. Send those measurements and photos with your request so the pieces can be checked against what you have before anything ships.

What if an exact fabric or laminate match is not available?

You have a few options. You can place the added stations as a separate cluster with a deliberate accent finish, choose remanufactured stations, or use the new pieces in less visible areas and move your best-matching existing stations forward.

How quickly can added stations arrive?

Liquidation-grade stations in stock can be ready in as little as 72 hours. Remanufactured stations usually take 7 to 14 days, and new furniture typically takes 4 to 6 weeks.

Can Office Station reconfigure my current stations while adding new ones?

Installation is $95 per workstation with a $495 minimum, and free space planning can help you decide how existing and added stations fit together. Describe any reconfiguration in your request so it can be scoped along with the new stations.

Will the added pieces carry a Steelcase warranty?

Generally, no. Office Station is an independent reseller, not an authorized Steelcase dealer, and manufacturer warranties generally do not transfer to pre-owned furniture.
