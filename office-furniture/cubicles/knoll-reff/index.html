Pre-owned Knoll wood finishes

Used Knoll Reff Workstations

Reff is chosen for its warm, finished look, and that is exactly what you need to inspect closely when buying it pre-owned. This guide covers finishes, grading and cost.

6 minute read

Used Knoll Reff appeals to buyers who want something warmer than a fabric panel system. Reff is associated with wood-look worksurfaces, clean edge details and pieces that suit private offices and executive areas as well as open workstations.

That finished look is what makes Reff attractive, and it is also what makes pre-owned Reff different to evaluate than a steel-and-fabric panel system. This page focuses on those surface considerations.

Veneer versus laminate: know which you are buying

Reff has been specified in both real wood veneer and laminate finishes. On a listing they can look similar. In daily use they behave very differently.

FactorWood veneerLaminate
AppearanceNatural grain variation, richer depthConsistent printed pattern
Scratch resistanceLower; shows scuffs and pen marksHigher for everyday wear
Sun exposureCan darken or lighten over timeGenerally more color stable
Water and heatVulnerable to rings and white marksMore forgiving
RepairabilityLimited touch-up; veneer is thinChips are hard to hide
Best placementPrivate offices, conference, receptionBusy open workstations

A simple tip: look at the edge and underside of a worksurface in photos. Grain that continues naturally around an edge usually points to veneer. Ask directly if you are not sure.

Wood-finish inspection checklist

Use this list when you review photos or see pieces in person:

  • Edges and corners. Chipping is most common where surfaces were bumped during moves.
  • Top surface in raking light. Hold a phone light low across the surface to reveal scratches and dents.
  • Water rings and heat marks. Check near where monitors, plants or coffee usually sit.
  • Fade lines. A lighter or darker rectangle often shows where a monitor or desk pad sat for years.
  • Grommets and cutouts. Make sure cable grommets are present and not cracked.
  • Drawer fronts and pulls. Confirm pulls match across pedestals and drawers run smoothly.
  • Locks and keys. Account for keys or plan for replacement lock cores.
  • Structural parts. Supports, modesty panels and connectors should be complete and stable.
Grade wood differently in your head. On fabric panels a grade C mostly means cosmetic wear you stop noticing. On a veneer desk in a client-facing office, the same level of wear is more visible. For executive or reception spaces, lean toward grade A or B.

Matching wood finishes across a floor

Finish names only get you part of the way. A veneer piece that sat in a sunny corner office will not look the same as one from an interior room, even if both were ordered in the same finish. A few ways to handle that:

  1. Compare photos of your existing furniture and the pre-owned pieces taken in the same type of light.
  2. Keep each private office consistent within itself, even if offices differ from one another.
  3. Use mismatched pieces in separate zones, such as a team area or a training room.
  4. Pair wood-look worksurfaces with neutral panels or storage to reduce the visual contrast.

Free space planning can help you decide where each grade and finish belongs before anything is delivered.

Protecting the surfaces after delivery

Once installed, simple habits keep pre-owned wood finishes looking good longer. Use coasters and desk pads, keep liquids away from edges, avoid harsh cleaners on veneer, and use window treatments where direct sun hits worksurfaces for much of the day.

Where pre-owned Reff makes the most sense

Because wood-look finishes show wear differently, think about placement before you choose a grade:

  • Executive and private offices. Grade A or B veneer gives you the finished look at a fraction of new cost.
  • Reception and client areas. Visible surfaces deserve the best grade you can source.
  • Team workstations. Laminate surfaces in grade B or C hold up well to daily use.
  • Training and project rooms. A good place for mixed finishes or pieces with honest wear.

Mixing grades by room lets you put your budget where visitors and leadership will see it, while keeping costs down in heavy-use areas.

Frequently asked questions

What should I look for in used Knoll Reff?

Focus on the surfaces. Check edges and corners for chips, look for water rings or sun fading on worksurfaces, and confirm whether each piece is wood veneer or laminate. Ask for photos of the actual pieces in natural light.

Can scratched wood veneer be repaired?

Light surface wear can sometimes be improved, but veneer is a thin layer of real wood, so deep gouges or heavy sanding are limited. Laminate resists scratches well but is harder to repair invisibly once chipped. Price and grade should reflect the condition you see.

Will used Reff match my existing Reff furniture?

Wood finishes vary by original finish choice, age and light exposure, so two pieces with the same finish name can look different side by side. Compare photos of both in the same lighting before you buy, and consider placing mismatched pieces in separate rooms or zones.

How much does a used Reff workstation cost?

For a 6'x6' workstation reference, furniture only: liquidation-grade $615, remanufactured $1,245 and new $1,920. Wood-finish pieces and private office components are priced individually based on grade and configuration, and prices exclude electrical, delivery and installation.
