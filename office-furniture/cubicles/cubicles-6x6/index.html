6x6 Cubicles With Published Prices

The 6x6 is the most common workstation footprint in American offices. Here is what one costs from Office Station in each tier, how fast you can get it, and what the price does and does not include.

6 minute read

A 6x6 cubicle gives one person about 36 square feet of working area inside the panels. It is the default size for open-plan offices because it balances privacy, storage and density. If you are pricing 6x6 cubicles, you mainly need to decide which tier fits your budget and your move-in date. The numbers below are published so you can do that math before you ever call anyone.

What each tier actually means

Liquidation-grade

These are pre-owned stations recovered from corporate office liquidations and sold in the condition they came out in, sorted by grade. Office Station uses three condition grades: A is like new, B is very good and C is good. Liquidation-grade is the fastest and least expensive way to furnish a room, and it is often the only option that can meet a deadline measured in days.

Remanufactured

Remanufactured stations start as pre-owned systems and are brought to a more uniform standard before they ship. You pay roughly double the liquidation-grade price and wait one to two weeks. In return you get a more consistent appearance across a full floor.

New

New stations make sense when you need a finish or configuration that is not available pre-owned, or when your timeline already allows four to six weeks. They cost about three times the liquidation-grade price.

Manufacturer warranties generally do not transfer to pre-owned furniture, so factor that into your tier decision.

Will 6x6 cubicles fit your space?

A 6x6 is measured to the panel, not the centerline, so a row of stations takes slightly more floor than the math suggests once you account for panel thickness and end panels. Before you order, check:

  1. Aisle widths. Leave room for primary circulation and for chairs pushed back from the work surface.
  2. Column and window lines. Stations that land on a column lose a run of work surface.
  3. Power and data locations. Floor boxes, poke-throughs and wall feeds determine where powered runs can start.
  4. Panel height. Lower panels feel more open and share light. Taller panels add privacy and acoustic separation.
  5. Doorways and elevators. Confirm the delivery path, especially for upper floors.

If the floor plan says 6x6 does not quite work, an 8x8 cubicle gives managers more room, while a call center layout packs more seats into the same floor.

What to check on a pre-owned 6x6

  • The condition grade quoted (A, B or C) and whether it is consistent across the order.
  • Panel fabric and finish color, so every station in a row matches.
  • Whether pedestals, overhead storage and keyboard trays are included or priced separately.
  • Whether you may need extra panels or connectors to tie into furniture you already own.

What to send for an accurate 6x6 quote

A quote is only as good as the information behind it. To get a firm number quickly, include:

  • The number of stations and the tier or tiers you want priced.
  • Your delivery address, so delivery can be priced by Greater Phoenix zone or quoted as freight.
  • Your target move-in date, which tells you right away whether liquidation-grade, remanufactured or new is realistic.
  • A floor plan or a sketch with dimensions, even if it is rough.
  • Whether you need installation, and any building rules about access hours or elevators.

With those details, you can compare tiers side by side on the same layout instead of guessing.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a 6x6 cubicle cost?

Office Station publishes furniture-only prices for a 6'x6' workstation: $615 liquidation-grade, $1,245 remanufactured and $1,920 new. Those prices exclude electrical, delivery and professional installation. Volume pricing lowers the per-station cost at 20, 50 and 100 or more stations.

How quickly can I get 6x6 cubicles?

Liquidation-grade stations can ship in as little as 72 hours when they are in stock. Remanufactured stations usually take 7 to 14 days, and new stations take 4 to 6 weeks. If your move-in date is fixed, choose the tier that fits the calendar first.

Is a 6x6 cubicle big enough?

For most individual contributors, yes. A 6x6 comfortably holds a corner or L-shaped work surface, a task chair, a pedestal and one or two monitors. Roles that meet visitors at the desk or spread out large documents often do better in a larger footprint such as an 8x8.

Do manufacturer warranties come with pre-owned 6x6 stations?

Generally no. Office Station is an independent reseller, not an authorized dealer, and manufacturer warranties generally do not transfer to pre-owned furniture. Ask about the condition grade of the specific stations you are quoted.

Does the price include installation?

No. Professional installation is $95 per workstation with a $495 minimum. Greater Phoenix delivery is $195 central or $295 metro, and freight elsewhere in the country is quoted per project.
