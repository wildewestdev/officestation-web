Used Office Storage and Filing Cabinets

Filing cabinets and storage pieces are some of the best buys on the pre-owned market. They come out of corporate liquidations built to last, and graded units in stock can ship fast.

6 minute read

Storage is the quiet workhorse of an office. Filing cabinets, pedestals and bookcases rarely get attention until you run out of room, and then you need them right away. The good news is that commercial storage is built from steel and heavy laminate to survive decades of drawers opening and closing, so a pre-owned piece often has most of its working life ahead of it. Buying used lets you put that money toward the things people notice, like chairs and workstations.

How grading works for storage

Every pre-owned piece is graded so you can match condition to the spot it will live in.

  • Grade A, like new. Little or no visible wear. Right for reception, conference rooms and anywhere clients walk by.
  • Grade B, very good. Light cosmetic wear such as small scratches on sides or tops. Right for most open office and private office use.
  • Grade C, good. More noticeable scuffs or dings, fully usable. Right for file rooms, storage closets and warehouse offices.

Storage is where Grade C pays off most. A lateral file in a records room does not need to look perfect, and the savings add up fast when you are buying a long run.

Before you buy: a used filing cabinet checklist

  1. Measure the space and the path. Note width, depth and height, plus door and elevator clearances along the delivery route.
  2. Confirm file size. Check whether drawers are set up for letter, legal or both, and whether hanging file rails are included.
  3. Open every drawer fully. Drawers should glide smoothly and close square, with no grinding or binding.
  4. Test the anti-tip system. On multi-drawer cabinets, only one drawer should open at a time. This matters for safety.
  5. Check locks and keys. Confirm the lock works and whether keys come with the unit. Records that must be secured need a working lock.
  6. Look for rust and dents on drawer fronts, corners and the bottom edge, which takes floor contact.
  7. Match finishes. If you are buying several pieces, ask about color consistency across the run.
  8. Check fit with workstations. Pedestals should clear the underside of your worksurface and not block cable paths.
Plan for weight. A loaded lateral file is heavy, and multi-drawer cabinets can tip if several drawers open at once. Keep the anti-tip interlock working, load bottom drawers first, and ask about anchoring tall pieces in your space.

Where do your used filing cabinets come from?

Office Station's pre-owned storage comes out of corporate office liquidations and decommissioning projects. Pieces are sorted and graded A (like new), B (very good) or C (good) so you know the cosmetic condition before you buy.

Do used filing cabinets come with keys?

Not always, so ask before you order. Confirm whether keys are included for each unit and whether the locks work. If a cabinet needs to secure sensitive records, lock condition should be at the top of your checklist.
