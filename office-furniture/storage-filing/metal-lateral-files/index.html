Storage and Filing

Used Metal Lateral File Cabinets

Steel lateral files sourced from corporate liquidations, with straight answers on sizing, condition and safety. Find the right width and height before you order.

6 minute read

Metal lateral files are the backbone of office storage. They hold client folders, HR records, drawings and supplies, and their flat tops turn into printer stands, credenza surfaces and informal counters. They are also one of the most common pieces to come out of a corporate office when it closes or consolidates, which makes used lateral file cabinets easy to find and a strong value.

The catch is that not every used file is worth buying. Drawer slides wear, interlocks get damaged in moves and keys go missing. This page walks you through sizing, condition and safety so you buy files that will work for years.

Sizing guide: width, height and drawer count

Lateral files are described by width and by how many drawers tall they are. Common widths run from about 30 inches up to 42 inches, and heights from two drawers to five. Confirm exact dimensions on any piece before you order, since they vary by manufacturer and model.

Width matters more than you think

Wider drawers hold more linear feet of files, but they also weigh more when full and need more aisle space to open. Measure the depth of an open drawer, then add room to stand in front of it. A tight corridor that fits a closed cabinet may not fit an open one.

Letter, legal and hanging rails

Most lateral file drawers accept both letter and legal folders using adjustable hanging rails. Check that the rails are present and that they can be set for the direction you file. Missing rails are inexpensive to replace but easy to overlook.

Safety first. A loaded lateral file drawer is heavy. Always confirm the interlock lets only one drawer open at a time, load the bottom drawers first, and anchor tall units. Never open a drawer on a cabinet that is not level.

Inspection checklist for used lateral files

  1. Slides. Open each drawer fully. It should glide smoothly, without grinding, sagging or sticking near the end of travel.
  2. Interlock. Open one drawer and try another. The second should not open.
  3. Lock and key. Test the lock if a key is present. If not, check whether the lock core is removable.
  4. Drawer fronts. Look for dents that prevent a drawer from closing flush.
  5. Case squareness. A cabinet that was dropped can twist, making drawers rub. Look for uneven gaps.
  6. Leveling glides. Confirm they are present and adjustable so you can level the unit on your floor.
  7. Finish. Note scratches or rust spots and decide whether the location can tolerate them.

Condition grades for lateral files

  • Grade A, like new. Clean finish, smooth slides, working interlock. Right for private offices and client-facing areas.
  • Grade B, very good. Light scuffs or minor marks, fully functional. A dependable choice for most office areas.
  • Grade C, good. Visible wear on the finish, still sound and working. Well suited to file rooms, storage and back-of-house use.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between a lateral file and a vertical file?

A lateral file is wide and shallow, with folders that run side to side or front to back inside a wide drawer. A vertical file is narrow and deep, with folders facing the front. Lateral files fit along walls and under windows and double as a work surface, while vertical files take less wall width but need more depth to open.

Why does a lateral file cabinet only let one drawer open at a time?

That is the interlock, an anti-tip feature that keeps more than one loaded drawer from extending at once. If a used cabinet lets two drawers open together, the interlock may be damaged or missing. Do not put a cabinet into service until that is fixed and the unit is anchored or counterweighted as needed.

Do used lateral files come with keys?

Keys are not always available when files leave a corporate space. Many cabinets use a replaceable lock core, so a missing key can often be solved by swapping the core. Ask about key status before you order if the cabinet will hold sensitive records.

How quickly can I get used lateral files?

In-stock liquidation-grade pieces can be ready in as little as 72 hours. Remanufactured options usually take 7 to 14 days, and new furniture typically takes 4 to 6 weeks.

What happens to the files and records when an office is liquidated?

Cabinets should always be emptied by the owner before removal. During a decommissioning, e-waste is handled with certificates of data destruction, and the space is handed back broom-clean with photo documentation. Paper records are your responsibility to remove or have shredded before the crew arrives.
