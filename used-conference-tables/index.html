Used Conference Tables

Pre-owned conference tables, sized to your room and graded for condition. Use the chart below to match seats to square footage, then check the logistics that decide whether a big table actually gets into your space.

6 minute read

A conference table is a long-term piece of furniture. The top rarely wears out, the base rarely fails, and the design of a good table stays appropriate for years. That makes used conference tables one of the easiest places to put a meeting room together for a fraction of what new costs.

The hard part is not finding a table. It is finding the right size, confirming its condition and getting it into the room. This page walks through all three.

Conference table sizing chart

Two rules of thumb do most of the work. First, allow about 30 inches of table edge per seated person. Second, add roughly 8 feet to both the table's length and width to find a comfortable room size. That leaves about 4 feet on every side so people can pull chairs out and walk behind seated colleagues.

If your room is tight, you can shave clearance on a wall that nobody sits against, such as one with a credenza or display. Do not shave it on a side with chairs. A room where people have to squeeze past each other feels smaller than it is.

Shape changes the math

  • Rectangular. The most seats for the footprint and the easiest to find pre-owned.
  • Boat-shaped. Wider in the middle, so everyone can see each other and a screen at the end. Slightly fewer seats than a rectangle of the same length.
  • Racetrack. Rounded ends soften the look and are gentler on hips in narrow rooms.
  • Round or square. Best for 4 to 6 people in small huddle rooms, where nobody sits at the head.

Condition grades for conference tables

Pre-owned tables are graded A like new, B very good or C good. On a conference table, the top gets the closest look, since that is what everyone sees during a meeting.

  • Grade A. Clean top with no noticeable scratches or edge wear. The right choice for boardrooms and client-facing rooms.
  • Grade B. Light surface marks visible up close or under direct light. A strong value for internal team rooms.
  • Grade C. Visible wear such as scratches, edge scuffs or small blemishes, but structurally sound. Good for training rooms, project rooms and break areas.

Surface material affects how wear shows. Laminate hides light use well. Wood veneer looks rich but shows scratches and water rings more readily. Stone and solid surface tops are durable but very heavy, which matters for delivery.

What to check before you buy

  1. Flatness. Seams between multi-piece tops should sit flush, with no ridge you can feel.
  2. Edges. Look for chips, peeling edge banding or dents, especially at corners.
  3. Base and connectors. Confirm all bases, brackets and connector plates are included. Missing hardware on a sectional table is hard to source.
  4. Power and data. Ask whether the power module, grommets and cable management are included, and test that outlets work.
  5. Height. Standard conference height is around 29 to 30 inches. Confirm it works with the chairs you plan to use.
  6. Weight. Heavy tops may need extra crew or special handling. Ask before scheduling.
Measure the path, not just the room. Before you commit to a large table, measure every door opening, hallway turn and elevator cab between the loading area and the conference room. Long tops that cannot tilt or break down into sections can get stuck in a corridor. Share those measurements when you ask about a table.

Getting the table into your building

Moving a conference table is less like moving a desk and more like moving a slab. A few details make the difference between a smooth delivery and a table leaning in a hallway.

  • Sectional tables. Many large tables are built in two or three pieces for this reason. They are easier to move and easier to reconfigure later.
  • Building rules. Office buildings often require a certificate of insurance and a reserved freight elevator or dock time. Find out your building's requirements early.
  • Timing. If the room is in use, plan delivery around meetings so the old table can come out and the new one go in without a gap.

Frequently asked questions

How much does delivery cost for a used conference table?

In Greater Phoenix, delivery is $195 for central zones and $295 for the wider metro. Outside Greater Phoenix, freight is quoted per project. Adding the table to a larger order of chairs or workstations lets everything arrive on one delivery.

Will a long conference table fit in my elevator?

Not always, which is why many large tables are built in sections. Measure your elevator cab diagonally, the door opening and any tight corners on the route, and share those numbers when you ask about a table. A one-piece top that cannot make the turn is not a bargain.

How many people fit around a conference table?

Plan roughly 30 inches of table edge per seated person for comfort, and a little less for short meetings. A 10 foot by 4 foot table typically seats 8 to 10 people. Wider tables also leave more room for laptops and papers.

Are used conference tables covered by a warranty?

Generally no. Office Station is an independent reseller, not an authorized dealer, and manufacturer warranties generally do not transfer to pre-owned furniture. Review the condition grade and ask for photos of the top, edges and base before you buy.

Can you help me pick the right size?

Yes. Space planning is free. Send your room dimensions, door and window locations, and the number of people you want to seat, and we will suggest a size and layout with proper clearance.
