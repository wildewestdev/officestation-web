Office Cubicle Upgrades: Refresh What You Have or Replace It

Some cubicle upgrades cost a few hundred dollars a desk and fix the real complaint. Others quietly approach the price of a better station. Here is how to tell which one you are looking at.

6 minute read

When employees complain about their cubicles, the complaint is usually specific. The desk is too cramped, the panels feel like a cave, there is nowhere to put a bag, or the chair and screen height give them a sore neck by 3 p.m. The right upgrade depends on which of those problems you are actually solving, and on whether the stations underneath are worth keeping.

This guide walks through both paths: targeted upgrades to existing cubicles, and replacement at three price tiers. It uses real per-station prices so you can compare the two honestly.

Start with the complaint, not the catalog

Before you price anything, walk the floor and write down what people are actually struggling with. A short list usually covers it:

  • Space. Worksurfaces too shallow for two monitors, or corner units that waste room.
  • Privacy and noise. Panels too low for focus work, or too high for a team that needs to talk.
  • Ergonomics. Fixed-height desks, poor monitor position, worn chairs.
  • Storage. Paper and personal items piling on the desk because there is nowhere else to go.
  • Looks. Stained fabric, chipped laminate, mismatched finishes that make the office feel tired.

If most of the list comes down to accessories, upgrade. If it comes down to the bones of the station, such as panel condition, worksurface size and layout, replacement usually wins.

Upgrades that work on existing cubicles

These improvements attach to most panel systems without rebuilding the station. They are the best value when the frames, panels and worksurfaces are in decent shape.

Monitor arms

A clamp-on arm frees desk depth and lets each person set screen height correctly. On a shallow worksurface, this alone can make a 6'x6' station feel much larger.

Task lighting

Overhead cabinets and tall panels shade the worksurface. An under-shelf or desk-mounted task light is a small fix that people notice every day.

Better chairs

If the complaint is back pain, the chair is often the real problem, not the cubicle. A pre-owned ergonomic chair can outperform a new budget chair at a similar cost. See pre-owned office chairs for what to look for.

Storage additions

A mobile pedestal under the desk or a shared lateral file at the end of a row clears surfaces faster than any policy memo.

Panel height changes

Swapping tall panels for lower ones, or adding glass or fabric toppers, changes the feel of a room. This is where upgrades start to get expensive, because you are buying panels, connectors and labor for every station.

The tipping point. Once you are replacing panels, worksurfaces and storage on every station, add up the parts and labor per desk. If that number is close to $615, a liquidation-grade 6'x6' replacement station usually gives you a cleaner result for similar money.

A worked example: 30 stations

Say you have 30 aging stations and the panels, worksurfaces and storage are all worn. Here is how the furniture cost compares across tiers before delivery, installation and electrical.

  • Liquidation-grade: 30 x $615 = $18,450, less 5% volume pricing = $17,527.50.
  • Remanufactured: 30 x $1,245 = $37,350, less 5% = $35,482.50.
  • New: 30 x $1,920 = $57,600, less 5% = $54,720.

Installation for 30 stations at $95 each adds $2,850. Volume pricing steps up to 8% at 50 stations and 12% at 100, so if you are refreshing more than one floor, price it together.

Now compare that against the upgrade path. If each station needs new panels, a new worksurface and a pedestal, the parts and labor can easily meet or exceed the liquidation-grade number, and you still have old frames.

Mixing tiers across the floor

You do not have to pick one tier for the whole office. A common approach is remanufactured or new stations where clients and candidates walk through, with liquidation-grade stations in operations areas. Keeping panel heights and finish colors consistent within each zone keeps the space from looking patched together.

Free space planning is included, so you can see how a mixed layout fits before you commit.

Checklist before you decide

  1. List the top three employee complaints and mark each as accessory-level or station-level.
  2. Inspect a sample of existing stations for panel damage, sagging worksurfaces and missing connectors.
  3. Price the upgrade path per station, including labor.
  4. Compare it to the liquidation-grade, remanufactured and new prices above.
  5. Confirm electrical needs with your building, since power and data are not included in furniture pricing.
  6. Check your move-in date against lead times for each tier.
  7. Decide what happens to the old stations.

Frequently asked questions

Is it cheaper to upgrade old cubicles or buy used ones?

It depends on how many parts you are replacing. Adding a monitor arm or task light to a sound station is almost always cheaper. Once you are replacing panels, worksurfaces and storage on every desk, a liquidation-grade 6'x6' workstation at $615 furniture-only often costs less than the parts and labor.

What is the difference between liquidation-grade and remanufactured cubicles?

Liquidation-grade stations are pre-owned and sold in the condition graded, with as little as 72 hours lead time when in stock. Remanufactured stations are refurbished before sale and usually ship in 7 to 14 days. Remanufactured costs more, $1,245 versus $615 for a 6'x6' station, and typically looks closer to new.

Do cubicle prices include installation?

No. The per-station prices are furniture only and exclude electrical, delivery and professional installation. Installation is $95 per workstation with a $495 minimum. Greater Phoenix delivery is $195 central or $295 metro, and freight elsewhere is quoted per project.

Can I mix new and pre-owned cubicles in one office?

Often, yes, especially if you keep panel heights and finishes consistent within each zone. Many offices put remanufactured or new stations in client-facing areas and liquidation-grade stations in back-office rows. Free space planning helps you map where each tier makes sense.

How do volume discounts work on workstations?

Volume pricing is 5% at 20 or more stations, 8% at 50 or more, and 12% at 100 or more. The discount applies to the workstation furniture, so it is worth pricing a full floor together rather than in small batches.
