How to Create a Beautiful Conference Room on a Budget

A conference room does not need a new-furniture budget to look sharp. Here is how to plan the room, where pre-owned pieces save the most and which details make an inexpensive room feel finished.

6 minute read

Most conference rooms are used for a few hours a day, yet they carry a lot of weight. It is where you pitch clients, interview candidates and hold the meetings that matter. The good news is that a room can look polished without new-furniture prices. The trick is knowing where to save and where to spend.

Step 1: Plan the room before you shop

Budget rooms go wrong when someone buys a table that is too big and then has to buy chairs that fit around it. Plan in this order:

  1. Decide the room's job. Client meetings, internal standups, video calls and training each call for a different setup.
  2. Set a seat count. Count the people who meet there most often, not the largest meeting of the year.
  3. Measure. Note the room size, door swing, windows, outlets and the wall where a display will go.
  4. Size the table. Allow 24 to 30 inches of edge per person and about 42 to 48 inches from table edge to wall.

If you would rather not do the math, send us your dimensions. Space planning is free, and we will suggest a table size and layout. Our used conference tables page also has a sizing chart by seat count.

Step 3: Match condition grade to the room

Pre-owned pieces we sell are graded A (like new), B (very good) or C (good). You do not need grade A everywhere.

  • Boardroom or client room: grade A or B table, since the top is the first thing guests see.
  • Everyday team room: grade B covers light edge wear that nobody notices once chairs are pulled in.
  • Training room or break-out space: grade C is fully functional and the lowest cost.

Here is a useful rule: spend on the table top and save on the chairs' condition where you can. Small scuffs on a chair base disappear under the table. A scratch across the center of a veneer top does not.

Warranty reality check. Office Station is an independent reseller, not an authorized dealer, and manufacturer warranties generally do not transfer to pre-owned furniture. Look closely at grades and photos, and ask about any piece before you buy.

Step 4: Choose chairs that earn their place

Chairs are where a room either feels comfortable or feels cheap. Pre-owned designer chairs from brands such as Herman Miller, Steelcase, Knoll, Haworth and Humanscale are often built for a long service life, which makes them a smart place to buy used.

  • For meetings over an hour, pick chairs with swivel, tilt and height adjustment.
  • For short meetings or tight rooms, a narrower side chair lets you fit more seats.
  • Keep every chair at the table the same model. Matching chairs make a room look planned.
  • Buy one or two extras for the corner so you are not borrowing desk chairs.

Budget conference room checklist

  • Room dimensions, door swing and display wall recorded
  • Seat count set for typical meetings
  • Table sized with 42 to 48 inches of clearance
  • Condition grade chosen to match how public the room is
  • Matching chairs plus one or two spares
  • Power, data and cable management confirmed
  • Delivery access checked for long table sections
  • Lighting and acoustics addressed

Frequently asked questions

What is the cheapest way to furnish a conference room?

Buy a pre-owned table and chairs in the condition grade that fits the room, and spend what you save on the details people notice, such as lighting, cable management and a clean wall for the display. Graded C furniture costs the least and works well in training and internal rooms. Client-facing rooms usually justify grade A or B.

Should I buy liquidation-grade, remanufactured or new furniture?

It depends on budget and timing. Liquidation-grade is the lowest cost and can be available in as little as 72 hours when in stock. Remanufactured usually takes 7 to 14 days and costs more, and new typically takes 4 to 6 weeks and costs the most. Many offices mix tiers across different rooms.

Do pre-owned chairs and tables come with a warranty?

Manufacturer warranties generally do not transfer to pre-owned furniture. Office Station is an independent reseller, not an authorized dealer. Review the condition grade and ask questions about specific pieces before you buy.

Is space planning really free?

Yes. Send your room dimensions, door and window locations and how many people you want to seat. We will suggest a table size and layout that leaves proper clearance around the chairs.

Can I furnish the rest of the office at the same time?

Yes. If you are also adding workstations, a 6 by 6 foot workstation, furniture only, runs $615 liquidation-grade, $1,245 remanufactured or $1,920 new, with volume discounts starting at 20 stations. Combining the conference room and open office in one plan keeps delivery simpler.
