What We Liquidate: Office Furniture With Resale Value

Almost anything in an office can be removed, but not everything can be resold. Knowing the difference helps you predict whether your net offer lands positive and how to push it higher.

6 minute read

When you are closing, downsizing or moving an office, the first question is usually simple: will someone pay for this furniture, or will I pay to get rid of it? The answer depends on what is in the space.

Office Station gives you a written net offer: the resale value of your furniture minus the cost to remove it. Positive means you are paid. This page breaks down which items tend to add value and which mostly add removal cost, so you can estimate where your number will land before the walkthrough.

How the net offer works

Every item in your space affects the offer in one of two ways.

  • Resale value comes from furniture that someone else will buy: recognized brands, current styles, good condition and quantities that can outfit another office.
  • Removal cost comes from labor, trucks, disposal, building logistics and time. Heavy, bulky, dated or damaged items raise removal cost without adding much resale value.

Net offer = resale value minus removal cost. A floor full of matching designer workstations and task chairs often outweighs the cost of hauling out old bookcases and break room tables. A floor of mismatched, worn furniture may not.

Quantity is a value multiplier. Fifty matching workstations are far easier to resell than fifty different desks. If your furniture was bought as a single system, say so when you request a walkthrough. Uniform inventory is often what pushes an offer into positive territory.

A room-by-room way to predict your offer

Before anyone visits, walk your space with a notepad and sort each area into value or cost. It takes less than an hour for most suites and gives you a realistic expectation.

Open office floor

This is usually where the value lives. Count workstations, note the panel height and worksurface size, and check a few chairs for brand labels. Matching runs of stations and designer seating are the strongest contributors to a positive offer.

Private offices

Desks, credenzas and bookcases vary. Clean, modular pieces that disassemble tend to resell. Heavy, dated or damaged casegoods lean toward removal cost.

Conference and training rooms

Stackable or nesting training tables and matching conference chairs are easier to resell than one oversized boardroom table that needs a crew to move.

Break rooms, reception and storage closets

Break room tables, built-in reception pieces and miscellaneous shelving are often cost items. Old electronics from storage closets belong in the e-waste stream.

How to improve your net offer

  1. Keep sets together. Do not give away a few chairs or split a workstation run before the walkthrough. Complete sets resell better.
  2. Find the keys. Filing cabinets and pedestals without keys are harder to sell.
  3. Gather parts. Panel connectors, worksurface brackets, power components and keyboard trays belong with their stations.
  4. Know your brands. Check labels under chair seats and inside drawers. A quick list of manufacturers helps the valuation.
  5. Give access. Share dock hours, freight elevator rules and COI requirements early so removal can be planned efficiently.
  6. Allow time. Rushed removals can raise labor cost. Request a walkthrough as soon as your exit date is known.

What happens after the walkthrough

Walkthroughs typically happen within 48 hours in Greater Phoenix and within 2 to 3 business days in other markets. You receive a written net offer within 5 business days, built on an itemized inventory so you can see what drives the number.

On removal day, crews can work nights or weekends and coordinate freight elevators and docks with your building. Certificates of insurance are provided for building management. E-waste is handled with certificates of data destruction. When the space is empty, you receive a photo-documented, broom-clean handback and a diversion summary showing where materials went.

Frequently asked questions

How is my liquidation offer calculated?

Your written net offer equals the resale value of the furniture minus the cost to remove it. If resale value is higher than removal cost, the number is positive and Office Station pays you. If removal cost is higher, the written offer makes that clear before you commit to anything.

Do you take furniture that has no resale value?

Yes, removal can include items with little or no resale value so you get a complete cleanout. Those items add to removal cost rather than resale value, which is why the itemized inventory matters. You can see exactly what drives the number.

What do you do with computers and electronics?

E-waste is handled separately, and certificates of data destruction are provided. This matters for devices that stored company or client data, so keep those items on your inventory.

How quickly can I get an offer?

Walkthroughs typically happen within 48 hours in Greater Phoenix and within 2 to 3 business days in other service markets. You receive a written offer within 5 business days.
