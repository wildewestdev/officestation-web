About Downtown Denver

Clearing an office in Downtown Denver

Downtown Denver's office core runs along 17th Street, the traditional financial corridor, through the Central Business District to LoDo and Union Station, bounded roughly by I-25, Speer Boulevard and Broadway. Energy companies, law firms, engineering firms, banks and state agencies fill its high-rise towers.

The stock is dominated by high-rise towers, from 1970s and 1980s buildings along 17th Street to newer Class A towers such as Block 162, plus converted historic warehouses in LoDo. High-rises typically require reserved freight elevators, scheduled dock time and detailed COIs, while older LoDo buildings may have small elevators and alley-only loading, and downtown's elevated vacancy means many decommissioning jobs involve whole floors.

Full Denver liquidation guide

  1. Get a Denver DOTI permit when a truck, container or dumpster will occupy a sidewalk, alley, metered space or loading zone, and allow several business days for processing.
  2. Recycle monitors, computers and printers through a certified electronics recycler, because Colorado has banned disposal of covered electronic devices in landfills since July 1, 2013.
  3. Reserve dock and freight elevator time with tower management well in advance, since many downtown buildings limit moves to nights and weekends.
  4. Build snow days into winter schedules, and route trucks around I-25 and Speer Boulevard peak congestion.
  5. Plan truck access around Union Station, Coors Field game days and 16th Street events, which tighten curb space in LoDo.
