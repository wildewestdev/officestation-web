Before You Buy Office Furniture, Read This

The sticker price is only part of what office furniture costs. This checklist walks through the tiers, condition grades, warranties, lead times and installation costs that decide your real budget.

7 minute read

Buying office furniture looks simple until the invoices arrive. The workstation price you budgeted for turns out to exclude delivery. Installation was assumed to be free. The electrician was never booked. The new furniture ships two weeks after move-in day. None of this is unusual, and nearly all of it is avoidable if you ask the right questions before you order.

Use this office furniture buying guide as a checklist. Work through each section before you sign anything.

1. Define what you actually need

Start with people and work, not catalogs.

  • Headcount today and in 12 to 24 months. Buying for growth is fine. Buying for a guess is expensive.
  • In-office attendance. Hybrid teams may need fewer assigned seats and more shared ones.
  • Work styles. Call-heavy and focus-heavy roles need more privacy than collaborative teams.
  • Storage. Paper-heavy departments need filing, others need almost none.
  • Floor plan and power. Where outlets, data drops and columns are will shape what fits.

Office Station offers free space planning, which is the easiest way to catch fit and power problems before you commit to a configuration.

3. Learn the condition grades

Pre-owned furniture is not one condition. Office Station uses condition grades so you know what to expect.

  • Grade A, like new. Minimal or no visible wear.
  • Grade B, very good. Light signs of use that most people will not notice.
  • Grade C, good. Visible wear, fully usable, the best value for budget-driven projects.

Ask which grade applies to each item on your quote. Mixing grades is a smart strategy: put Grade A pieces in client-facing areas and Grade C in back-office or training rooms.

4. Face the warranty reality

This is the section most buyers skip, and it matters.

Warranties generally do not transfer. Office Station sells pre-owned designer furniture from brands like Herman Miller, Steelcase, Knoll, Haworth, Humanscale, Teknion, Allsteel, Kimball, AIS and Friant as an independent reseller, not an authorized dealer. Manufacturer warranties generally do not transfer to pre-owned furniture, so treat inspection as your warranty.

That does not make pre-owned a bad choice. It means you should inspect with intent.

  • Sit in chairs and test every adjustment: height, tilt, lock, arms and lumbar.
  • Open every drawer and check that locks work and keys are included.
  • Look for missing connectors, brackets and panel hardware on workstations.
  • Run height-adjustable desks through their full range.
  • Check fabric panels and seats for stains or tears under good light.

Also ask your building about after-hours delivery rules, elevator reservations and certificate of insurance requirements. Those can affect scheduling even when they do not add cost.

Quick math example

Say you need 25 liquidation-grade 6'x6' stations delivered in central Phoenix and installed. Furniture is 25 x $615 = $15,375. Volume pricing at 20 or more stations takes 5% off, bringing furniture to $14,606.25. Installation adds 25 x $95 = $2,375. Delivery adds $195. That puts you around $17,176 before electrical. Your actual quote depends on configuration, but this is the shape of a realistic budget.

6. Work backward from your move-in date

  1. Pick your move-in or go-live date.
  2. Subtract the lead time for your tier: 4 to 6 weeks for new, 7 to 14 days for remanufactured, as little as 72 hours for in-stock liquidation-grade.
  3. Subtract time for space planning, approvals and electrician scheduling.
  4. Leave a buffer for building access and punch-list fixes.

If the math does not work for new furniture, a pre-owned tier may be the only way to hit the date.

7. Do not forget the furniture you already have

If you are replacing an existing office, the old furniture has value or cost attached to it. Office Station's office furniture liquidation service provides a written net offer, which is resale value minus removal cost, so you can factor it into the new purchase. For a full move, see corporate relocation.

Final pre-purchase checklist

  • Headcount, work styles and storage needs documented
  • Floor plan reviewed with power and data locations
  • Tier chosen for each area based on budget and timeline
  • Condition grade confirmed for every pre-owned item
  • Warranty expectations understood
  • Delivery, installation and electrical included in the budget
  • Volume pricing applied if you are buying 20 or more stations
  • Building access, elevator and COI requirements confirmed
  • Plan in place for existing furniture

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between liquidation-grade, remanufactured and new furniture?

Liquidation-grade furniture is pre-owned and sold largely as it came out of an office, which makes it the lowest cost and often the fastest option. Remanufactured furniture has been refurbished and typically costs more but looks closer to new. New furniture costs the most and usually takes the longest to arrive.

Do warranties transfer on used office furniture?

Generally no. Manufacturer warranties usually do not transfer to pre-owned furniture. Office Station sells as an independent reseller, not an authorized dealer, so inspect condition and ask questions before you buy.

What costs are not included in furniture prices?

Workstation prices commonly exclude electrical work, delivery and professional installation. At Office Station, installation is $95 per workstation with a $495 minimum, Greater Phoenix delivery is $195 central or $295 metro, and freight elsewhere is quoted per project. Electrical is typically handled by a licensed electrician.

How long does office furniture take to arrive?

It depends on the tier. Liquidation-grade stock can be ready in as little as 72 hours when in inventory, remanufactured usually takes 7 to 14 days, and new furniture typically takes 4 to 6 weeks.

Is there a discount for buying a lot of workstations?

Yes. Office Station applies volume pricing of 5% at 20 or more stations, 8% at 50 or more and 12% at 100 or more.
