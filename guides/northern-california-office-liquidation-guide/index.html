Northern California Office Liquidation and Move-Out Guide

Clearing an office in San Francisco, the Peninsula, San Jose, Oakland or Sacramento means working with strict building rules, lab-heavy submarkets and California's disposal laws. Here is how to plan it.

10 minute read  ·  Updated September 2026

Northern California covers several very different office markets: San Francisco towers, Peninsula campuses, Silicon Valley headquarters, East Bay mid-rises and Sacramento's government and corporate core. What they share is California's disposal law and some of the most detailed building rules in the West. This guide covers office furniture liquidation in Northern California, office decommissioning and planning an office move-out in the Bay Area and Sacramento.

Office districts from San Francisco to Sacramento

  • San Francisco. High-rises in the Financial District and around Salesforce Transit Center, converted warehouse and loft offices in SoMa and Jackson Square, and life-science and office buildings in Mission Bay.
  • The Peninsula: Palo Alto and San Mateo. Stanford Research Park, downtown Palo Alto, Menlo Park, Redwood City and San Mateo, plus the biotech cluster in South San Francisco. Mostly low- and mid-rise campuses with some newer towers near Caltrain stations.
  • San Jose. Downtown San Jose towers and the large North San Jose tech corridor, with headquarters campuses extending into Santa Clara and Sunnyvale.
  • Oakland. City Center, Lake Merritt and Uptown high-rises and mid-rises, plus flex and lab conversions in Emeryville and West Oakland.
  • Sacramento. Downtown and the Capitol Mall corridor, Natomas, Rancho Cordova, and suburban office parks in Roseville and Folsom. Government-adjacent tenants, professional firms and back-office operations with high workstation counts.

Campus and suburban buildings are often faster to clear. Towers take more planning but can hold large, matched sets of recent furniture that resell well.

San Francisco high-rise rules

San Francisco buildings, especially downtown towers, tend to have detailed contractor rules. Expect most of the following.

  • Certificates of insurance with specific limits, naming the owner, the property manager and sometimes other parties as additional insureds.
  • Freight elevator and dock reservations booked well ahead, with strict start and end times.
  • After-hours and weekend work for furniture removal, so lobbies and passenger elevators stay clear during business hours.
  • Protection plans covering floors, walls, doors and elevator cabs.
  • Labor requirements in some buildings. Some downtown buildings include labor-related requirements in their contractor rules and may ask for documentation before approving a move. Others do not. Do not assume either way; ask for the rules in writing.
  • Curb space. Many older buildings and SoMa lofts have no dock. The SFMTA issues temporary no-parking signage for moves and asks for at least 5 business days before the enforcement date.
Building rules drive the number. Allowed hours, labor requirements, dock access and elevator size all change removal cost. Share the building's contractor rules at the walkthrough so your net offer reflects the real job.

Oakland, San Jose and Sacramento towers have similar systems for COIs, freight elevators and docks, though specific rules vary by building.

Life-science and lab buildings

The Bay Area is one of the largest life-science markets in the country, with concentrations in South San Francisco, Mission Bay, Brisbane, Emeryville, the Peninsula and parts of the South Bay. Many buildings mix wet labs with open office floors.

  • Lab scope. Chemicals, biological materials, fume hoods, biosafety cabinets, freezers with samples and anything under a radioactive materials license belong with your EHS team and licensed specialty vendors. Many organizations follow the ANSI/ASSE Z9.11 laboratory decommissioning standard.
  • Office scope. Workstations, private offices, conference rooms, breakrooms, chairs and filing can be liquidated once separated from lab areas.
  • Written release. Get a room-by-room release from EHS before office removal starts, so nothing from a lab ends up in general inventory.

California and San Francisco disposal rules

E-waste and universal waste

The California Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC) regulates electronic waste as universal waste. Covered electronic devices, including monitors, TVs and laptops with screens larger than four inches, cannot be landfilled. Batteries, fluorescent lamps and mercury-containing devices are universal waste too.

Data-bearing devices

California Civil Code section 1798.81 requires businesses to take reasonable steps to make customer personal information unreadable when disposing of records, including by shredding or erasing. Plan for computers, servers, copiers, phones and backup media.

San Francisco Mandatory Recycling and Composting Ordinance

San Francisco's Mandatory Recycling and Composting Ordinance, in effect since 2009, requires businesses to separate recyclables, compostables and trash, and property owners to provide color-coded containers. That still applies during a move-out. Sort paper and cardboard, and size hauler service for the extra volume.

Recycling and organics statewide

Outside San Francisco, California's commercial recycling law and SB 1383 require businesses to have recycling and organic waste service through local programs in San Jose, Oakland, Sacramento and elsewhere. Bridges, freeways and event traffic

Bay Area geography funnels trucks onto a handful of bridges and freeways. Timing loads around them saves crew hours.

  • Bridges. The Bay Bridge, San Mateo-Hayward Bridge, Dumbarton Bridge and Richmond-San Rafael Bridge all back up at commute peaks. Plan crossings for early morning, midday or late evening.
  • Freeways. US-101, I-280, I-880, I-80 and I-580 are the main truck routes between submarkets, and each has recurring peak congestion.
  • San Francisco venues. Giants games at Oracle Park and events at Chase Center in Mission Bay affect SoMa and Mission Bay streets. Moscone Center conventions add congestion in SoMa.
  • South Bay venues. Events at Levi's Stadium in Santa Clara and SAP Center in downtown San Jose slow nearby streets and freeways.
  • Sacramento. Events at Golden 1 Center affect downtown streets. I-5, I-80, US-50 and Business 80 carry most commute traffic, and winter tule fog in the Central Valley can slow early-morning trips.
Night work helps twice. In San Francisco, an after-hours removal often satisfies building rules and avoids the worst bridge and street traffic at the same time.

What shapes your net offer in Northern California

A written net offer is resale value minus removal cost. In the Bay Area and Sacramento, building rules often weigh as heavily as the furniture itself.

  • Matched quantity. Large runs of identical workstations or benching, common on tech floors in San Francisco and San Jose, resell more easily than mixed furniture.
  • Brand, age and condition. Recent commercial lines in clean condition hold value best.
  • Collaboration and private office furniture. Conference tables, lounge seating, phone booths and executive desks can add to the resale side when they are in good shape.
  • Building requirements. After-hours windows, labor requirements where they apply, curb permits and small freight elevators all affect labor.
  • Distance and traffic. Bridge crossings and peak freeway hours add time to each truck run.
  • Scope. Cabling, anchored items and e-waste volume add removal work.

If you are consolidating and keeping some furniture, the offer can cover only what stays behind. To test numbers before a walkthrough, use the liquidation estimator.

What to have ready for a Bay Area or Sacramento walkthrough

  • Your lease end date and the return condition clause, so the scope matches what the landlord expects.
  • The building's move or contractor rules, including allowed hours, COI requirements, dock details and elevator dimensions.
  • A floor plan or headcount by floor, which speeds up the itemized inventory.
  • A list of anything staying with you, such as furniture headed to a new office or equipment going back to a leasing company.
  • An IT contact who can confirm which devices hold data and how they should be documented.

With those details in hand, the written net offer reflects the actual job rather than assumptions, and your removal dates can be planned around the building from the start.

Frequently asked questions

How does office furniture liquidation work in the Bay Area?

A liquidation partner walks your space, inventories furniture and equipment, and gives you a written offer. Office Station's written offer is a net number: estimated resale value minus the cost of removal. When resale value is higher than removal cost you are paid the difference, and when it is not, the offer shows the net cost to clear the space.

Do San Francisco office buildings require union labor for move-outs?

Some do. Certain downtown San Francisco buildings include labor requirements in their contractor rules, and property managers may ask for documentation before approving a move. Many other buildings focus on certificates of insurance, freight elevator bookings and protection plans. Ask your property manager for the building's written contractor rules before scheduling.

What does the San Francisco Mandatory Recycling and Composting Ordinance mean for a move-out?

The ordinance, in effect since 2009, requires everyone in San Francisco, including businesses, to separate recyclables, compostables and trash. During a move-out, paper, cardboard and breakroom waste should still be sorted, and your building's hauler service should be sized for the extra volume.

How are lab spaces in South San Francisco or Mission Bay handled?

Lab equipment, chemicals, biological materials and anything under a radioactive materials license should be decommissioned by your environmental health and safety team and licensed specialty vendors. Office furniture and general equipment can be liquidated once lab areas are released in writing.

How fast can Office Station walk through a Bay Area or Sacramento office?

Walkthroughs outside Greater Phoenix are typically scheduled within 2-3 business days, with written offers typically within 5 business days. Share your lease end date, address and building rules when you reach out.

Do I need a street permit to load trucks in San Francisco?

If your building has no usable dock, you may need to reserve curb space. The SFMTA issues temporary no-parking signage for moves and asks for at least 5 business days of lead time before the enforcement date. Your property manager can tell you whether dock access is available instead.
