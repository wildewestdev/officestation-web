Used Haworth and Knoll Cubicles: Unigroup, Dividends, Morrison and More

Haworth and Knoll systems fill a large share of the used cubicle market. Here is how to identify the major lines, what works together, and what to inspect before you buy.

10 minute read  ·  Updated September 2026

Haworth and Knoll have supplied workstation systems to corporate offices for decades. Their panels, worksurfaces and storage show up constantly when offices close, consolidate or relocate. For buyers, that means a steady supply of commercial-grade cubicles at a fraction of the cost of new.

It also means a lot of different systems. Haworth Unigroup, Premise and Compose, and Knoll Dividends Horizon, Morrison and Reff, are distinct products. Knowing which one you are looking at is the first step to buying a system that goes together, powers up and grows with you.

This guide explains how to identify each line, what to know about compatibility and power, and what to inspect. One note up front: Office Station is an independent reseller of pre-owned furniture, not an authorized Haworth or Knoll dealer, and manufacturer warranties generally do not transfer to pre-owned furniture.

Why buy used Haworth and Knoll workstations

Panel systems are engineered to be installed, taken apart and reinstalled. That durability is what makes a second life practical. Frames and connectors that held up in one office can hold up in yours.

Pre-owned systems also shorten your timeline. There is no manufacturing lead time, so you can often move from space plan to installation faster than with a new order.

The trade-off is that you work with existing finishes, fabrics and panel heights. A good space plan built around real inventory makes the most of what is available.

Decide what you need before you shop

Before comparing systems, get clear on a few basics. How many people need a station today, and how many in two years? Do you need enclosed privacy, open collaboration or a mix? Will stations need power at every desk? Is there storage you need at each station, or can it be shared?

Those answers narrow the field quickly. A team that needs tall panels and powered stations is shopping for something very different from a team that wants low, open desks. Knowing your needs also makes it easier to judge whether a particular lot is complete enough for your project.

Haworth Unigroup, Premise and Compose

Unigroup

If you search for "used Haworth cubicles," Unigroup is likely what you will find most often. It was installed widely in corporate offices, and that volume keeps it circulating in the resale market.

The Unigroup name has been used across related versions over time. That is helpful for availability but means you should not assume every Unigroup part matches. Compare connectors, panel construction and worksurface supports against what you already have.

Premise

Premise is another Haworth panel system. It is a separate line from Unigroup, so treat it as its own system when sourcing parts or adding stations. If you are offered a mix of Premise and Unigroup, ask the seller exactly how the components were installed together and confirm with documentation or an experienced installer.

Compose

Compose is a Haworth system offering a range of finishes and components for different station types. As with any system, completeness matters. Buying whole stations, with their supports and hardware, avoids hunting for small parts later.

Knoll Dividends Horizon, Morrison and Reff

Dividends Horizon

Dividends Horizon is part of Knoll's Dividends family of panel systems. People looking for "used Knoll Dividends" will find it in resale inventory as offices reconfigure. Configurations vary a great deal, from low collaborative stations to more private layouts, so review the exact panel heights and components on offer.

Morrison

Knoll Morrison is a panel system that has been in offices for many years. That long history means it still appears in used inventory, but it also means many installations are older. Look closely at panel fabric, frame condition and any electrical parts, and ask how easy it will be to find matching components if you plan to expand.

Reff

Reff is associated with wood-focused, more executive-style furniture, and it is often seen in private offices and higher-end areas. Workstation components appear in resale less predictably than mainstream panel systems. If you find a complete Reff set in finishes you like, it can be a way to bring a warmer, more tailored look to part of your office.

How to identify the system

Use several clues together. No single feature settles the question, and many installations include added or replaced parts.

  1. Look for labels. Check panel frames, the underside of worksurfaces and inside pedestal drawers for manufacturer labels and part numbers.
  2. Examine panel construction. Note panel thickness, and whether panels are single units or frames with removable tiles.
  3. Study the connectors. How panels join and how corners and end runs are finished varies by system.
  4. Check component attachment. Worksurface supports and overhead storage brackets are useful identifiers.
  5. Look at finishes and trim. Wood veneers, top caps and edge trim can point toward a particular line.
  6. Compare with documentation. Match what you see against Haworth or Knoll product literature, not marketplace photos.

Buying remotely? Request close-ups of labels, connectors, panel edges and supports. A reseller who handles these systems regularly should be able to explain how they identified the line.

Compatibility, parts and power

Mixing systems

Components are designed to work within their own system. Across manufacturers, parts generally do not interchange. Even within one manufacturer, separate lines and different versions of the same line can differ in connectors and heights. Some combinations may be possible with specific transition parts, but confirm with documentation or an experienced installer.

Parts availability

Widely installed systems, such as Unigroup, tend to have more parts circulating through resale. Older or less common lines can be harder to supplement. Before you buy, ask:

  • Is all hardware included, including connectors, brackets, supports and trim?
  • Are spare panels available in matching fabric and finish?
  • If you add stations later, how likely are matching components to be available?

Powered panels

Many Haworth and Knoll panel installations carry power through the panels. Reusing it takes care:

  • Keep electrical parts system-specific. Never mix harnesses, receptacles, jumpers or in-feeds across systems or manufacturers.
  • Confirm the circuit configuration. It determines how many devices each station supports and how the system ties into building power.
  • Check the in-feed. Confirm that the way power enters the panels, from wall, floor or ceiling, works for your space.
  • Replace damaged parts. Cracked housings, damaged insulation, bent contacts and missing covers are not acceptable for reuse.
  • Use a licensed electrician. Connections should meet local code, and permits may be required.
Older systems need a closer look. With long-running lines like Morrison and Unigroup, inspect electrical components and small hardware especially carefully, and confirm replacements are available before you commit.

What to inspect before you buy

  1. Confirm the manufacturer and specific line with labels, construction and connectors.
  2. Verify that all stations come from matching versions of the same system.
  3. Check that each station is complete, including hardware, supports and trim.
  4. Inspect fabric for stains, tears, fading and odor.
  5. Look for bent frames, dented panels and damaged caps or trim.
  6. Check worksurfaces and wood veneers for chips, delamination, scratches and warping.
  7. Open every drawer and door, test every lock and confirm keys.
  8. Confirm finishes and panel heights match across stations.
  9. For powered panels, inspect each electrical component and confirm the configuration.
  10. Get the condition grade and a written description of any refurbishment.

Frequently asked questions

Are used Haworth cubicles a good buy?

They can be. Haworth panel systems are built for commercial offices and designed to be reconfigured, so a well-maintained system often has plenty of life left. Buy complete, matched stations and confirm the specific system before adding to anything you already own.

What is Haworth Unigroup?

Unigroup is a Haworth panel system that has been installed in many corporate offices, which makes it one of the more common Haworth systems on the resale market. The Unigroup name has been used across related versions over time, so confirm the specific components rather than assuming every Unigroup part matches.

Are used Knoll Dividends cubicles easy to find?

Knoll Dividends, including Dividends Horizon, appears on the resale market as offices close and reconfigure. Availability varies by region and timing. Because configurations differ, buying a complete installation is usually the best way to get matched components.

What is Knoll Morrison?

Morrison is a Knoll panel system that has been in offices for many years and still turns up in resale inventory. Because many Morrison installations are older, inspect panels, hardware and any electrical components carefully, and confirm parts availability if you plan to expand.

Can I mix Haworth and Knoll components?

Generally, no. Each manufacturer's systems use their own connectors, panel construction and electrical components, and even systems from the same manufacturer may not interchange. Never mix electrical parts across systems or manufacturers.

How much do used Haworth or Knoll cubicles cost?

Brand pricing varies with system, configuration and availability. As a reference point, Office Station prices a 6' x 6' workstation, furniture only, at $615 liquidation-grade, $1,245 remanufactured and $1,920 new. Electrical, delivery and professional installation are not included.
