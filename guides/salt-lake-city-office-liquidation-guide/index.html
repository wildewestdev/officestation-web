Salt Lake City Office Liquidation and Move-Out Guide

From downtown towers to Silicon Slopes campuses in Lehi and Draper, clearing a Utah office means planning around building rules, winter weather and the I-15 corridor. Here is how to do it.

9 minute read  ·  Updated September 2026

Utah's office market runs along the Wasatch Front: a downtown Salt Lake City core, suburban office in the south valley, and the fast-grown tech corridor known as Silicon Slopes around Lehi and Draper. A winter that can bring snow for months and a single main freeway spine make timing important. This guide covers office furniture liquidation in Salt Lake City, office decommissioning and planning an office move-out from downtown to Utah County.

Salt Lake City office districts and building types

  • Downtown Salt Lake City. High-rise and mid-rise office around Main Street and the central business district, near City Creek Center, the Gateway area and the Salt Palace Convention Center. Financial, legal and corporate tenants with a mix of private offices and open floors.
  • Lehi and Silicon Slopes. Tech company campuses and newer multi-story office buildings near Thanksgiving Point, Traverse Mountain and along I-15 in northern Utah County. Large open floors with benching, workstations, collaboration furniture and phone rooms.
  • Draper. Office parks and corporate buildings near the Point of the Mountain along I-15, with tech, financial services and back-office operations, often in newer buildings with good truck access.

The metro also includes office clusters in Sugar House, the University of Utah Research Park, South Jordan and Sandy. Newer Silicon Slopes buildings often have large quantities of recent, matched furniture, which is exactly what makes resale work. Downtown towers take more coordination but frequently hold higher-end private office and conference furniture.

Tower and campus building rules

Downtown towers

  • Certificates of insurance naming the owner and property manager, delivered before crews start.
  • Freight elevator reservations with set windows, often shared with other tenants.
  • Loading dock scheduling and truck size limits. Downtown Salt Lake City's wide streets help, but docks still get busy.
  • After-hours or weekend removal in many Class A buildings.
  • Protection for floors, elevator cabs, doors and lobby finishes.

Silicon Slopes and suburban campuses

  • Security and badge access for crews, with a facilities contact on site.
  • Shared docks in multi-tenant buildings, sometimes on a reservation system.
  • Campus-wide schedules when several floors or buildings are being cleared at once.
  • Return condition set by the lease, which may include removing cabling, wall-mounted displays and anchored items.
Share the rules before the walkthrough. Allowed hours, dock access and elevator size change the labor side of the offer. The more precise the building information, the more precise your written net offer.

Utah disposal and e-waste considerations

Check county and facility rules

Utah disposal rules for electronics and other office waste vary by county, city and facility, so check with your hauler and the receiving facility before any electronics leave the building. Business waste is often handled differently from household waste. Salt Lake County, for example, states that its household hazardous waste facility at the Salt Lake Valley Landfill accepts computers, CRT screens and TVs from residents only, not businesses. Recycling business electronics through an e-waste handler is the straightforward route and gives you paperwork for your records.

Batteries, lamps and other items

Batteries, fluorescent tubes and other items that contain hazardous materials should be kept out of general trash. Ask your hauler or the facility how they must be handled in your county.

Personal information

Utah Code section 13-44-201 requires businesses that maintain personal information to have reasonable procedures to destroy records they no longer retain, by shredding, erasing or otherwise making the information indecipherable. Plan for computers, servers, phones, copiers with drives and backup media, and keep certificates of data destruction. Winter weather, I-15 and event traffic

Plan for snow from November through April

Snow can fall along the Wasatch Front from roughly November through April, and storms can slow the entire metro. Winter move-outs should include:

  • Buffer days between scheduled removal and the lease end date.
  • Snow and ice removal confirmed for docks, ramps, walkways and parking lots.
  • Floor protection in lobbies and elevators for wet boots and carts.
  • Flexible start times when storms are in the forecast.

The I-15 corridor

I-15 is the spine connecting downtown Salt Lake City, Draper and Lehi, and the stretch over the Point of the Mountain between Salt Lake County and Utah County is a frequent commute bottleneck. I-215, I-80, Bangerter Highway and the Mountain View Corridor carry much of the rest. Scheduling truck runs outside commute peaks keeps crew hours down.

Event and seasonal traffic downtown

  • Delta Center, home of the Utah Jazz and the NHL's Utah Mammoth, draws large crowds on game and concert nights just west of the central business district.
  • Salt Palace Convention Center events add congestion and parking pressure downtown.
  • The Utah Legislature's annual general session, which runs early in the year at the Capitol, adds activity near downtown and Capitol Hill.
Winter lease ends need slack. If your lease expires between November and April, plan removal a week or more before the deadline, and keep a backup weekend available.

What shapes your net offer in Salt Lake City

Every written net offer balances two numbers: what your furniture and equipment can resell for, and what it costs to remove everything your lease requires. Local conditions push on both.

Resale value

  • Matched sets. Large runs of identical benching or workstations, common on Silicon Slopes floors, are easier to resell than mixed furniture.
  • Brand and age. Recent commercial lines from major manufacturers generally hold value better than older or unbranded pieces.
  • Condition. Clean fabric, intact edges and working chair mechanisms matter. Protecting furniture from winter slush on the way out preserves value.
  • Collaboration and private office furniture. Conference tables, lounge seating, phone booths and executive desks can add meaningfully to an offer when they are in good shape.

Removal cost

  • Access. A Draper building with a dock and wide corridors clears faster than a tower floor with one shared freight elevator.
  • Hours and weather. After-hours windows and winter storm delays both affect labor.
  • Scope. Cabling, wall-mounted displays, anchored items and e-waste volume all add work.

If you are consolidating into a smaller office and keeping some furniture, the offer can cover only what stays behind. To test numbers before a walkthrough, use the liquidation estimator.

Frequently asked questions

How does office furniture liquidation work in Salt Lake City?

A liquidation partner walks the office, inventories furniture and equipment, and gives you a written offer. Office Station's offer is a net number: estimated resale value minus the cost of removal. If resale value is higher you are paid the difference, and if not, the offer shows the net cost to clear the space.

Can a Utah business put old computers in the landfill?

Check before you do anything. Rules and accepted materials vary by county, city and facility, and some facilities treat business electronics differently from household electronics. Salt Lake County, for example, states that its household hazardous waste facility at the landfill accepts computers and TVs from residents only, not businesses. Recycling electronics through an e-waste handler avoids the question and gives you documentation.

What does Utah law say about disposing of records with personal information?

Utah Code section 13-44-201 requires businesses that maintain personal information to have reasonable procedures to destroy records they no longer keep, by shredding, erasing or otherwise making the information indecipherable. Computers, servers, phones and copiers with drives should be part of that plan, with a certificate of data destruction as the record.

Does winter weather affect office move-outs in Salt Lake City and Lehi?

It can. Snow is possible along the Wasatch Front from roughly November through April, and storms can slow I-15 and local roads. Build buffer days into your schedule and confirm that docks, ramps and parking areas will be cleared.

How quickly can Office Station walk through a Utah office?

Walkthroughs outside Greater Phoenix are typically scheduled within 2-3 business days, with written offers typically within 5 business days. Share your lease end date and building rules when you reach out so the schedule fits your deadline.

Do Silicon Slopes office buildings in Lehi require after-hours moves?

Some do, especially multi-tenant buildings and larger corporate campuses with security and badge access. Others allow daytime removal with a dock reservation. Ask the property manager or facilities team for move rules in writing, including insurance certificate requirements.
