Orange County Office Liquidation and Move-Out Guide

Orange County offices range from Irvine campuses to Newport Beach towers and Anaheim business parks. Here is how to clear one on time, within California disposal rules.

9 minute read  ·  Updated September 2026

Orange County's office market is spread out: a large master-planned business district in Irvine, towers in Newport Center, a dense cluster near John Wayne Airport, and business parks through Anaheim and the north county. That spread affects how a move-out runs. This guide covers office furniture liquidation in Orange County, office decommissioning and the practical steps of an office move-out before your lease ends.

Orange County office districts and building types

Each part of the county has its own mix of buildings, tenants and access challenges.

  • Irvine. The Irvine Business Complex near the airport, the Irvine Spectrum area along the I-5 and I-405, and corporate campuses throughout the city. Expect a mix of mid-rise towers, large-floor-plate headquarters and single-story office parks.
  • Newport Beach. Newport Center around Fashion Island and the airport-area office corridor. Professional services, financial firms and law offices with private-office-heavy layouts and higher-end furniture.
  • Airport Area, Costa Mesa and Santa Ana. South Coast Metro, the MacArthur Boulevard corridor and the Santa Ana civic and business districts. Towers and mid-rises share streets with retail and performing arts venues.
  • Anaheim. The Platinum Triangle near Angel Stadium and Honda Center, the Anaheim Canyon business area and older office parks. Many buildings have docks or grade-level doors, and workstation counts can be high.

Private offices in Newport Beach tend to hold desks, credenzas and conference furniture with strong resale demand. Open-plan floors in Irvine and Anaheim often have systems furniture where matched quantity drives value. Either way, a detailed inventory is what turns furniture into a real number.

Building rules in towers and business parks

Most Orange County move-outs run through a property manager, and the rules are rarely optional.

Mid-rise and Class A towers

  • Certificates of insurance naming the owner and property manager, submitted before any crew arrives.
  • Freight elevator reservations in blocks, often shared with other tenants moving the same month.
  • Loading dock schedules with truck size limits. Some buildings share a dock with a parking structure that has low clearance.
  • After-hours windows for furniture removal, commonly evenings and weekends.
  • Protection requirements for floors, elevator cabs, doors and lobby finishes.

Office parks and campuses

  • Easier ground-floor access, but some buildings have no dock, which means liftgate trucks and more carrying time.
  • Landlord approvals are still common for truck staging, parking lot use and dumpster placement.
  • Campus tenants may have security check-in and badge requirements for crews.
Ask about the return condition early. Your lease decides whether cabling, wall-mounted whiteboards, anchored furniture and signage must be removed. Those items change the labor estimate, and removal cost is one side of a net offer.

California disposal and e-waste rules

Orange County move-outs follow California's statewide rules, which are stricter than many other states.

Electronics and universal waste

The California Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC) regulates e-waste as universal waste, a category of hazardous waste. Covered electronic devices, including monitors, TVs and laptops with screens larger than four inches, are banned from landfills. Batteries, fluorescent tubes and mercury-containing devices are also universal waste. OC Waste and Recycling states that its landfills do not accept electronics, and its household hazardous waste centers serve residents, so business electronics need an appropriate e-waste handler.

Personal information

California Civil Code section 1798.81 requires businesses to take reasonable steps to dispose of customer records with personal information by shredding, erasing or otherwise making it unreadable. That covers paper files, computers, servers, phones and copiers with internal drives.

Recycling and organics

California's commercial recycling law and SB 1383 require businesses to have recycling and organic waste service through their local hauler. Plan for extra cardboard and paper in the final weeks. Medical, lab and specialty spaces

Orange County has a large medical device and healthcare presence, especially around Irvine. If your space includes labs, clean rooms, clinical areas or regulated equipment, split the project.

  • Regulated items such as chemicals, biological materials, medical devices, imaging equipment and anything under a license belong with your EHS or compliance team and licensed specialty vendors.
  • Office furniture and general equipment can be liquidated once those areas are cleared and released.
  • Document the handoff. A written release for each area keeps regulated items out of the furniture inventory.

For clinical spaces specifically, the medical office decommissioning guide covers the extra steps.

Freeways, toll roads and event traffic

Distances in Orange County are short on a map and long at 5 p.m. Crew time spent in traffic is paid time.

  • Freeways. The I-405, I-5, SR-55 and SR-57 carry heavy commute traffic, and the I-405 through Irvine and Costa Mesa is often slow at peak hours.
  • Toll roads. SR-73, SR-133, SR-241 and SR-261 can shorten some trips but add tolls for trucks.
  • Anaheim venues. Angels games at Angel Stadium, events at Honda Center and conventions at the Anaheim Convention Center affect traffic in the Platinum Triangle and around the Disneyland Resort area.
  • Costa Mesa. The OC Fair at the OC Fair and Event Center runs in summer and adds traffic near Fairview Road and the SR-55.
  • Heat. Inland areas such as Anaheim can get hot in late summer. Early starts help crews and keep loading docks from backing up.
Check the venue calendar. A weekend move-out two blocks from Honda Center or Angel Stadium on an event night can lose hours to street congestion. Pick a quiet date if you have the flexibility.

What shapes your net offer in Orange County

A written net offer balances resale value against removal cost. In Orange County, building type and furniture mix tend to move both numbers.

  • Matched quantity. A full Irvine floor of identical workstations resells more easily than furniture collected over many years.
  • Brand, age and condition. Recent commercial lines in clean condition generally hold value best. Stained fabric, chipped edges and broken chair mechanisms pull value down.
  • Private office furniture. Newport Beach law and financial offices often have desks, credenzas and conference tables that add to the resale side.
  • Access. A single-story Anaheim building with a dock is a quicker job than a tower floor with a shared freight elevator and after-hours rules.
  • Scope. Cabling, anchored items and e-waste volume add removal work.

If you are relocating and keeping some furniture, the offer can cover only what stays behind. You can test numbers before a walkthrough with the liquidation estimator.

What to have ready for a Orange County walkthrough

  • Your lease end date and the return condition clause, so the scope matches what the landlord expects.
  • The building's move or contractor rules, including allowed hours, COI requirements, dock details and elevator dimensions.

Frequently asked questions

How does office furniture liquidation work in Orange County?

A liquidation partner walks the space, inventories furniture and equipment, and gives you a written offer. Office Station's offer is a net number: estimated resale value minus the cost of removal. If the furniture is worth more than it costs to remove, you are paid the difference; if not, the offer shows the net cost.

Can Orange County landfills take old office electronics?

No. OC Waste and Recycling states that its landfills do not accept electronics, and California law treats e-waste as universal waste regulated by the Department of Toxic Substances Control. The county's household hazardous waste centers are for residents, so a business needs an appropriate e-waste handler for monitors, computers and other devices.

How quickly can Office Station see my Irvine or Newport Beach office?

Walkthroughs outside Greater Phoenix are typically scheduled within 2-3 business days, and written offers typically follow within 5 business days. Share your lease end date and building rules up front so the removal plan fits your deadline.

Do Orange County office buildings require after-hours moves?

Many multi-tenant and Class A buildings in Irvine, Newport Center and South Coast Metro limit furniture removal to evenings or weekends and require a certificate of insurance, a freight elevator reservation and a dock time. Rules vary by building, so ask the property manager for them in writing.

What happens to data on computers and copiers during a move-out?

California Civil Code section 1798.81 requires businesses to take reasonable steps to make customer personal information unreadable when disposing of records, including by erasing it. Office Station handles e-waste as part of liquidation and decommissioning projects and provides certificates of data destruction for data-bearing devices.

When is the worst time to schedule an Orange County move-out?

Avoid peak commute hours on the I-405, I-5 and SR-55, and check event calendars near Angel Stadium, Honda Center, the Anaheim Convention Center and the OC Fair and Event Center in Costa Mesa. Summer fair season and large conventions can slow access near those venues.
