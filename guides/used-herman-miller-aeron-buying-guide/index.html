Used Herman Miller Aeron Buying Guide

A pre-owned Aeron can be one of the smartest seating purchases you make, or an expensive mistake. This guide covers sizing, generations, options, wear points and how to spot a replica before you buy.

10 minute read  ·  Updated September 2026

The Herman Miller Aeron is one of the most recognized office chairs ever made, and because so many were bought by companies, a steady supply reaches the pre-owned market when offices close, move or refresh. That is good news for buyers. It also means you will find everything from nearly new chairs to tired, mismatched and outright fake ones.

This guide explains what to check so you buy the right chair in the right condition. A quick note on who we are: Office Station is an independent reseller of pre-owned office furniture. Aeron sizes A, B and C

Unlike most task chairs, the Aeron comes in three distinct frame sizes. Getting the size right matters more than any option, because the seat depth, width and back height are all built around it. An Aeron that is the wrong size will never feel right, no matter how you adjust it.

  • Size A (small) is intended for smaller-framed users.
  • Size B (medium) fits the widest range of people and is the most common size in corporate inventory.
  • Size C (large) is intended for larger or taller users.

Herman Miller publishes sizing guidance, and the best test is to sit in the chair. As a rule of thumb, your thighs should be supported without the seat edge pressing behind your knees, your hips should sit comfortably within the frame, and the back should support you without the top edge hitting your shoulder blades awkwardly.

Where to find the size

The size is typically marked with a letter on the chair itself, commonly on the seat or back frame, and may also appear on the manufacturer label under the seat. If you are buying remotely, ask for a clear photo of the size marking and the label. A seller who cannot tell you the size is not a seller who has inspected the chair.

Buying for a team? Most offices do well with mostly size B plus a smaller number of A and C chairs. Ask employees to try sizes before you order, or order a mix and swap within the group.

Classic Aeron vs Aeron Remastered

The Aeron has been in production since the 1990s. In 2016, Herman Miller released the Aeron Remastered, which kept the recognizable silhouette but reworked much of the chair. In the used market, both generations are common.

Which should you buy?

A classic Aeron in excellent condition with the right size and options is a better purchase than a Remastered chair that does not fit or has worn components. If budget allows and you are choosing between equal-condition chairs, the Remastered generally offers refined back support and mesh. If you are outfitting a large team, a consistent generation looks cleaner and simplifies parts.

Telling them apart takes practice. Look at the mesh texture and frame edges, the back support hardware, the shape of the controls under the seat, and the manufacturing date on the label. A reputable reseller should state the generation clearly.

Options that matter: back support, arms and tilt

Aerons were ordered in many configurations. Two chairs that look identical in a photo can feel very different in use.

PostureFit vs lumbar pad

PostureFit (PostureFit SL on the Remastered) supports the lower back near the pelvis to encourage a neutral spine, and it is adjusted with dials on the back of the chair. The lumbar pad is a pad that slides up and down the back to position support. Some chairs have neither. None is universally better. If you know you prefer firm, low support, PostureFit is often the pick; if you want to move support up and down, choose the pad.

Check that the back support is actually there and works. Lumbar pads go missing, and PostureFit mechanisms should adjust smoothly without loose parts.

Arms

  • Fixed arms do not adjust. They are simple and durable but may not suit every body or desk height.
  • Height-adjustable arms move up and down.
  • Fully adjustable arms add pivot and, on later chairs, depth adjustment. Most buyers who use a keyboard all day prefer these.
  • Armless chairs work for narrow workstations and collaborative spaces.

Tilt limiter and forward tilt

The tilt limiter lets you lock how far the chair reclines. Without it, the chair reclines freely against the tilt tension, which some people like and others find distracting. Forward tilt lets the seat angle forward for focused tasks. A "fully loaded" Aeron typically means adjustable back support, fully adjustable arms and the tilt limiter with forward tilt.

What wears out on a used Aeron

The Aeron's frame and base are built for heavy commercial use. Most of what wears is predictable, and much of it can be replaced. Knowing where to look tells you whether a chair is a good buy.

Seat and back meshTears, fraying at the frame edge, sagging, stains, loose strandsMesh problems affect comfort and are the most significant repair
Arm padsCracking, peeling, splitting, loosenessA common wear item and a visible sign of heavy use

CastersStiff rolling, cracks, flat spots, wrong type for your floorHard floors and carpet may call for different casters
Tilt and controlsLevers that slip, knobs that spin freely, noises when recliningMechanism problems are harder to fix than wear parts

Sit in the chair for a few minutes if you can. Recline through the full range, raise and lower the seat, try every lever and knob, and roll it across the floor. Listen for creaks and clicks, and feel for any spot where the mesh gives more than the rest.

How to tell a fake Aeron

Because the Aeron is so well known, replicas and "Aeron-style" chairs are widely sold, sometimes described in ways that suggest they are genuine. Here is how to check.

1. Find the manufacturer label

Turn the chair over. A genuine Aeron typically has a manufacturer label under the seat with product information such as manufacturing date and serial or part details. A missing label is not proof of a fake, since labels can wear off, but it is a reason to look much harder at everything else. A label with misspellings, blurry printing or information that does not match the chair is a red flag.

2. Judge the construction quality

  • Mesh. Genuine mesh is evenly tensioned and securely held in the frame. Replica mesh often feels thin, loose or uneven.
  • Molded parts. Look for clean edges and consistent finish. Rough seams, flash and cheap-feeling plastic suggest a copy.
  • Metal components. The base and mechanism should feel solid. Replicas often feel light or flex when you push on them.
  • Adjustments. Controls should match the options described and operate smoothly. A "fully loaded" chair with missing or nonfunctional controls deserves questions.
  • Branding. Check logos and markings for correct placement and crisp detail.

3. Watch the listing language

Phrases like "Aeron style," "inspired by" or "ergonomic mesh chair like Aeron" usually mean the chair is not an Aeron. Prices far below the market for the stated configuration and grade are another warning sign.

Ask before you pay. Request photos of the label under the seat, the size marking, the back support and the controls. Any reseller confident in their inventory will send them.

What grade A, B and C mean at Office Station

Do not confuse condition grades with Aeron sizes. Sizes A, B and C describe the frame. Grades describe condition. At Office Station, we grade pre-owned chairs so you know what to expect before you buy:

  • Grade A: The best condition we sell. Minimal signs of use, clean mesh, and controls that operate as intended.
  • Grade B: Fully functional with light, visible cosmetic wear, such as minor scuffs on the frame or base.
  • Grade C: Functional with more noticeable cosmetic wear. A good fit when budget matters more than appearance.

Warranty and pricing on pre-owned chairs

Warranty

Manufacturer warranties generally apply to the original purchaser from an authorized source and generally do not transfer to pre-owned chairs sold by independent resellers. Assume a used Aeron does not carry the original Herman Miller warranty, and ask the seller directly about their own return policy or coverage.

Pricing

There is no single price for a used Aeron. Pricing depends on generation, size, options and condition grade. A Remastered chair with PostureFit SL, fully adjustable arms and a tilt limiter in top grade will be priced differently than a classic chair with fixed arms and cosmetic wear. Office Station pre-owned furniture prices exclude electrical, delivery and professional installation, so factor those in if they apply to your order.

Where used Aerons come from

Many pre-owned chairs come from corporate office closures and relocations. If your company is the one moving out, those chairs may have resale value that offsets removal costs. See office furniture liquidation or read how to liquidate office furniture.

The 10-minute inspection checklist

  1. Confirm the size letter and that it fits the person who will use it.
  2. Photograph the manufacturer label under the seat and note the date information.
  3. Identify the generation: classic or Remastered.
  4. Confirm back support type and that it is present and adjustable.
  5. Test arm adjustments against what the listing claims.
  6. Recline through the full range and test the tilt limiter and forward tilt if equipped.
  7. Raise the seat, sit, and wait to see whether it sinks.
  8. Run your hand along the mesh edges for fraying or separation.
  9. Check arm pads, casters, frame and base for cracks.
  10. Confirm the grade, the return policy and what is excluded from the price.

Frequently asked questions

How do I know what size Aeron I have?

Aeron chairs come in three sizes: A (small), B (medium) and C (large). The size is usually marked with a letter on the chair, commonly on the seat or back frame, and it may also appear on the manufacturer label under the seat. If you cannot find a marking, ask the seller for the label photo before buying.

What is the difference between the classic Aeron and the Aeron Remastered?

Herman Miller introduced the Remastered Aeron in 2016. It updated the mesh, the back support options, the tilt mechanism and several other details while keeping the same overall design and A, B and C sizing. Both generations can be excellent used buys, and the right choice depends on your budget, options and condition.

How can I tell if an Aeron is fake?

Start with the manufacturer label under the seat, which on genuine chairs typically includes manufacturing date and product information. Then check construction quality: consistent mesh tension, clean molded parts, solid metal components, smooth adjustments and correct branding. Replicas often skip the label or get it wrong, feel lighter or looser, and have adjustments that do not match the genuine chair.

Does the Herman Miller warranty transfer to a used Aeron?

Generally, no. Manufacturer warranties are typically extended to the original purchaser from an authorized source, so a pre-owned chair bought from an independent reseller should be assumed not to carry the original warranty. Ask any reseller what, if any, coverage or return policy they offer on their own.

Is a refurbished Aeron worth it?

For many buyers, yes. The Aeron's frame and mechanism are built for long commercial use, and the parts that wear, such as arm pads, casters and cylinders, can often be replaced. The value depends on size fit, options, mesh condition and grade, so inspect those before you compare prices.

What does PostureFit mean on an Aeron?

PostureFit is Herman Miller's back support that presses on the lower back near the pelvis to help support a neutral spine. On the Remastered chair it is offered as PostureFit SL. The alternative is an adjustable lumbar pad that slides up and down the back. Neither is universally better; it comes down to what feels right for your body.

How much does a used Aeron cost?

Pricing for a pre-owned Aeron depends on the generation, size, options and condition grade. A fully loaded chair in top grade will cost more than a basic configuration with visible wear. Request availability to see current inventory and pricing for the configuration you want.
