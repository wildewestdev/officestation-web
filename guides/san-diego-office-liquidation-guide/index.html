San Diego Office Liquidation and Move-Out Guide

Clearing an office in San Diego means working around downtown tower rules, life-science buildings, California e-waste law and some of the busiest event calendars on the coast. Here is how to plan it.

9 minute read  ·  Updated September 2026

San Diego has a varied office market: glass towers downtown, research campuses on the Torrey Pines Mesa, and low-rise business parks stretching from Kearny Mesa to Carlsbad. Each one handles a move-out differently. This guide covers what matters when you need office furniture liquidation in San Diego, a full office decommissioning, or a clean office move-out before a lease ends.

San Diego office districts and building types

Where your office sits shapes access, labor hours and how much of your furniture is easy to resell. These are the areas most move-outs start from.

  • Downtown San Diego. High-rise and mid-rise office in the Core-Columbia area, the Marina district, East Village and Little Italy. Expect managed loading docks, freight elevator bookings and after-hours move windows.
  • UTC and University City. Class A towers along La Jolla Village Drive and Executive Drive, plus a growing number of life-science buildings near UC San Diego.
  • Sorrento Mesa. Tech and life-science campuses, many of them two- and three-story buildings with mixed office and lab space.
  • Carlsbad. Business parks around Palomar Airport Road with corporate headquarters, R&D and flex buildings. Access is usually easier, but some buildings have grade-level doors instead of docks.
  • Kearny Mesa. A central mix of low-rise office, government-adjacent tenants, call centers and industrial flex space, with high workstation counts in some suites.

Low-rise business parks are generally faster to clear. Towers take more coordination but often hold newer, higher-value furniture. Both can produce a solid net offer when the inventory is well documented.

Downtown high-rise rules

Downtown and UTC towers run move-outs through the property manager. The details vary by building, but most rules fall into the same categories.

What to expect

  • Certificates of insurance. Buildings typically require a COI from every vendor, naming the owner and property manager as additional insureds, before a crew is allowed on site.
  • Freight elevator reservations. Many towers have one freight car, shared by every tenant. Book it early and confirm the exact window.
  • Dock scheduling. Downtown docks are small and busy. Trucks that miss their slot may wait or be turned away.
  • After-hours work. Many Class A buildings restrict furniture removal to evenings or weekends so tenants and lobbies stay undisturbed.
  • Protection. Floor runners, wall and door protection, and elevator pads are commonly required.
  • Return condition. Your lease defines how the space must be returned. Cabling, wall-mounted items and furniture anchored to floors or walls often need to go.
Get the rules in writing. Ask the property manager for the building's move-out or contractor rules before you request any quotes. Dock heights, elevator dimensions and allowed hours affect labor estimates, which directly affect your net offer.

Life-science and lab buildings

San Diego is one of the country's major life-science markets, with lab buildings concentrated in Torrey Pines, Sorrento Valley, Sorrento Mesa and UTC. Clearing one of these spaces is really two projects: the lab side and the office side.

  • Lab equipment and materials such as chemicals, biological materials, fume hoods, biosafety cabinets and anything under a radioactive materials license belong with your environmental health and safety team and licensed specialty vendors. Many organizations use the ANSI/ASSE Z9.11 laboratory decommissioning standard as a framework for that work.
  • Office furniture and general equipment such as workstations, private office furniture, conference rooms, breakroom furniture, task chairs and filing can be liquidated like any other office once it is physically separated from lab areas.
  • Clearance before removal. Ask your EHS lead to confirm which rooms and items are released. Lab benches, casework and anything that has been in a lab space should not be mixed into office inventory without that sign-off.

Keeping the two scopes separate protects your schedule. The office side can often move quickly while the lab side works through its own documentation.

California disposal and e-waste rules

California has some of the strictest disposal rules in the country, and they apply to every office move-out.

Electronics are universal waste

The California Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC) treats electronic waste as universal waste, a category of hazardous waste with its own handling rules. Covered electronic devices, which include monitors, TVs, laptops and other devices with video screens larger than four inches, cannot go to a landfill. Batteries, fluorescent lamps and mercury-containing devices are also universal waste and should never go in the dumpster.

Data on old devices

California Civil Code section 1798.81 requires businesses to take reasonable steps to dispose of customer records containing personal information by shredding, erasing or otherwise making the information unreadable. Computers, servers, copiers with hard drives and phones all need a plan, and a certificate of data destruction gives you the record.

Recycling and organics

California's SB 1383 and the City of San Diego's Recycling Ordinance require businesses to have recycling and organic waste service. A move-out usually generates a surge of cardboard, paper and breakroom waste, so tell your hauler before the final weeks. Traffic, events and seasonal timing

San Diego does not have snow days, but it does have freeway bottlenecks and a busy event calendar that can cost hours of crew time.

  • Freeways. The I-5 and I-805 merge north of UTC, SR-52, SR-163 and I-15 all see heavy commute traffic. Loading trucks outside peak hours saves labor.
  • Petco Park. Padres home games and concerts at Petco Park in East Village affect streets, parking and loading downtown on game days.
  • San Diego Convention Center. Large conventions, especially Comic-Con International each July, bring road closures and heavy crowds to the Marina district and Gaslamp.
  • Snapdragon Stadium. Events in Mission Valley affect I-8 and I-15 traffic.
  • Del Mar in summer. The San Diego County Fair and the Del Mar racing season add traffic on I-5 north of the city, which matters for Carlsbad and Sorrento Valley moves.

Check the event calendar before you lock an after-hours or weekend date. A Saturday move downtown on a Comic-Con weekend is a very different job than one in a quiet week.

Lease end dates do not move. If your lease ends in July, book your downtown dates early. Evening and weekend slots near the convention center and ballpark fill up and street access tightens.

What to have ready for a San Diego walkthrough

  • Your lease end date and the return condition clause, so the scope matches what the landlord expects.
  • The building's move or contractor rules, including allowed hours, COI requirements, dock details and elevator dimensions.
  • A floor plan or headcount by floor, which speeds up the itemized inventory.

Frequently asked questions

How does office furniture liquidation work in San Diego?

A liquidation partner walks your space, inventories the furniture and equipment, and gives you a written offer. With Office Station, that offer is a net number: estimated resale value minus the cost of removal. If resale value exceeds removal cost, you receive a payment; if not, the offer shows the net cost to clear the space.

How fast can Office Station walk through a San Diego office?

Walkthroughs outside Greater Phoenix are typically scheduled within 2-3 business days, and written offers typically follow within 5 business days of the walkthrough. If your lease end date is close, say so when you reach out so the schedule can be planned around it.

Can I throw old monitors and computers in the dumpster in San Diego?

No. California treats electronic waste as universal waste, a category of hazardous waste regulated by the Department of Toxic Substances Control, and covered electronic devices such as monitors, TVs and laptops with screens cannot be sent to a landfill. They need to go to an appropriate e-waste handler, and data-bearing devices should be sanitized or destroyed with documentation.

What about lab equipment in a Sorrento Valley or UTC life-science building?

Lab equipment, chemicals, biological materials and anything involving radioactive materials should be handled by your environmental health and safety team and licensed specialty vendors. A furniture liquidation partner can clear office furniture, workstations and general equipment on the office side once the lab areas are separated and released.

Do downtown San Diego high-rises allow move-outs during business hours?

Many do not. Class A towers commonly require moves after hours or on weekends, a reserved freight elevator, a scheduled dock time and a certificate of insurance naming the owner and property manager. Get the building's move-out rules in writing before you book any date.

How far ahead should I start planning a San Diego office move-out?

Start 90 to 120 days before the lease end if you can. That leaves time for a walkthrough, a written offer, building approvals and an after-hours schedule that avoids peak event weeks downtown. Shorter timelines are possible but leave less room for resale.
