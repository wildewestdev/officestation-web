Circular economy guide

Creating a Circular Workspace

Office furniture is built to last far longer than most leases. A circular approach keeps it in use through resale, remanufacturing and responsible recycling, and documents where every piece ends up.

6 minute read

A well-built workstation, conference table or task chair can serve for many years. The typical office lease is shorter than that. So when a company moves, downsizes or closes a location, a lot of furniture with plenty of useful life is suddenly in the way. What happens next decides whether it goes into a dumpster or into another office.

A circular workspace is one where that furniture keeps moving from one useful home to the next. This guide explains how the loop works, what each option means in practice and how to document the result.

The reuse hierarchy for office furniture

Not every outcome is equal. The closer an item stays to its original form and use, the less new material and energy it takes to replace it. Work down this list in order.

PriorityOutcomeWhat it looks like
1Reuse in placeKeep and reconfigure furniture for the new space
2ResaleSell furniture to another business through liquidation
3RemanufactureRefresh parts, surfaces or fabric so a piece can go back into service
4DonationGive usable items with little resale value to organizations that can use them
5RecyclingSeparate metal, wood, plastic and electronics for material recovery
6LandfillOnly for items that cannot be reused or recycled

If you are leaving a space

The company moving out has the biggest influence on whether furniture stays in circulation. Decisions made in the last few weeks of a lease often default to whatever is fastest. A little planning changes that.

Start with what you will keep

If you are relocating, decide early which pieces move with you. Workstations can often be reconfigured for a new floor plan, and good task chairs are almost always worth keeping. Our corporate relocation service covers moving what you keep into the new space.

Turn the rest into a resale opportunity

Office furniture liquidation puts surplus furniture back on the market. Office Station provides a written net offer: the resale value of the furniture minus the cost of removal. When that number is positive, your company is paid. That gives a financial reason, not only an environmental one, to choose resale over disposal.

Walkthroughs typically happen within 48 hours in Greater Phoenix and within 2 to 3 business days in our other markets, and written offers follow within 5 business days. You can get a rough idea first with the liquidation estimator.

Handle electronics separately

Monitors, computers, phones and other devices are e-waste, and they belong in a different stream from furniture. Anything that stored data also carries a security obligation. During a decommission, e-waste is handled with certificates of data destruction, so IT and compliance have a paper trail.

Ask for the paperwork up front. A circular move-out is only as credible as its documentation. Before work begins, confirm you will receive an itemized inventory, certificates of data destruction for e-waste, photo documentation of the broom-clean handback and a diversion summary at the end.

What a well-documented project gives you

Sustainability claims need records behind them. On a full office decommissioning project, the documentation typically includes:

  • Itemized inventory: what was in the space before removal began.
  • Diversion summary: how the contents were handled, so you can see what went to resale and recycling versus disposal.
  • Certificates of data destruction: for devices that stored data.
  • Photo-documented handback: proof the space was returned broom clean, which also helps with your landlord.

These records are useful beyond sustainability reporting. They support asset write-offs, lease exit obligations and internal audits.

If you are furnishing a space

The loop only closes if someone buys what is resold. Choosing pre-owned furniture is the demand side of a circular workspace, and it usually costs less. For a 6 by 6 foot workstation, furniture only, the price is $615 liquidation-grade, $1,245 remanufactured or $1,920 new, excluding electrical, delivery and professional installation.

  • Liquidation-grade: furniture that goes straight back into service. As little as 72 hours when in stock.
  • Remanufactured: refreshed pieces with a newer look. Usually 7 to 14 days.
  • Condition grades: A like new, B very good, C good. Choosing grade C for back-office areas keeps even more usable furniture in circulation.

Circular move-out checklist

  1. Inventory furniture and electronics before you set a move date.
  2. Decide what moves to the new space and what becomes surplus.
  3. Request a walkthrough and a written net offer for the surplus.
  4. Separate e-waste and confirm certificates of data destruction.
  5. Coordinate building rules: certificates of insurance, freight elevator and dock time, and night or weekend work if needed.
  6. Confirm a photo-documented broom-clean handback.
  7. Collect the diversion summary for your records.
  8. Consider pre-owned furniture for the new space.

Frequently asked questions

What does circular economy mean for office furniture?

It means keeping furniture in use for as long as possible instead of treating it as disposable. Pieces are reused in place, resold to another business, remanufactured or recycled for materials, with landfill as the last resort. Both the company leaving a space and the company furnishing one play a part.

What is a diversion summary?

A diversion summary is a record provided at the end of a liquidation or decommissioning project that shows how the contents of the space were handled, such as what was resold, recycled or disposed of. Companies often use it for internal sustainability reporting and to show that a move-out was handled responsibly.

Can liquidating office furniture actually pay us?

It can. Office Station provides a written net offer equal to the resale value of the furniture minus the cost of removal. When the net offer is positive, your company is paid. When the furniture has less resale value, the offer shows the net cost so you can plan for it.

What happens to computers and electronics during a decommission?

E-waste is handled separately from furniture and recycled through appropriate channels. Certificates of data destruction are provided for devices that stored data, which gives your IT and compliance teams a record for their files.

Is buying pre-owned furniture part of a circular workspace?

Yes. Buying pre-owned is the demand side of the loop, and it is what gives liquidated furniture a second life. Pre-owned pieces are graded A, B or C for condition, and manufacturer warranties generally do not transfer to them.
