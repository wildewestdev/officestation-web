The Benefits of Buying Used Office Furniture, by the Numbers

A 6'x6' workstation costs $615 liquidation-grade and $1,920 new, furniture only. Here is what that gap means for a real office, how much faster used furniture arrives, and when new still makes sense.

6 minute read

Most articles about the benefits of buying used office furniture stay vague: it is cheaper, it is greener, it is faster. All true, but you cannot build a budget on adjectives. This guide puts real numbers on the decision so you can compare liquidation-grade, remanufactured and new workstations side by side.

Benefit 3: You can move in weeks sooner

Price gets the attention, but speed often decides the project. New furniture typically takes 4 to 6 weeks. If you signed a lease, hired a team or lost a space unexpectedly, that wait has a cost of its own: people working from folding tables, delayed start dates or paying rent on a space you cannot use.

  • Liquidation-grade: as little as 72 hours when the furniture is in stock.
  • Remanufactured: usually 7 to 14 days.
  • New: 4 to 6 weeks.

A common approach is to furnish immediately with liquidation-grade stations and reserve the new or remanufactured budget for a handful of spaces where finish matters most, such as reception or executive offices.

Pre-owned means independent. Office Station sells pre-owned furniture from brands such as Herman Miller, Steelcase, Knoll, Haworth, Teknion and Allsteel as an independent reseller, not an authorized dealer. Manufacturer warranties generally do not transfer to pre-owned furniture, so factor the condition grade into your decision.

Benefit 4: Better furniture for the same budget

Commercial systems furniture from major manufacturers is built for years of daily use, reconfiguration and moves. Buying pre-owned often lets you choose a higher-quality product line than your budget would allow new. The pre-owned office furniture page lists the manufacturers Office Station resells.

Every pre-owned piece carries a condition grade:

  • Grade A, like new: little to no visible wear.
  • Grade B, very good: light cosmetic marks, fully functional.
  • Grade C, good: more visible wear, fully usable.

Benefit 5: Less waste

Every workstation that goes back into service is one that did not need new steel, laminate and fabric, and one that did not go to a landfill. If your company reports on sustainability, reuse is one of the more straightforward wins to document.

The same logic applies on the way out. If you are leaving a space, Office Station can make a written net offer on your existing furniture, which is its resale value minus the removal cost. Decommissioning projects include a diversion summary. See office furniture liquidation for details.

When new furniture is still the right call

Used is not always the answer. Consider new if:

  • You need a specific finish, fabric or configuration that must match exactly across a large floor.
  • A manufacturer warranty is a firm requirement for your organization.
  • Your timeline comfortably allows 4 to 6 weeks and the budget is set.

Many offices land on a mix. Remanufactured is the middle path when you want more than liquidation-grade but cannot wait or pay for new.

A sample mixed budget

Suppose you are furnishing 30 stations and a few leadership spaces. You could put 26 open-office stations in liquidation-grade at $615 each ($15,990) and 4 stations in remanufactured at $1,245 each ($4,980), for $20,970 in furniture. The same 30 stations new would be $57,600. At 30 stations the order also qualifies for the 5% volume tier, and installation would add $95 per station.

That kind of split lets you spend where people notice and save where they do not. Pair it with pre-owned seating from the pre-owned office chairs page to round out each station.

Checklist before you buy used

  1. Measure the space and confirm station size. Free space planning is available.
  2. Decide which areas need Grade A and which can use B or C.
  3. Confirm power and data locations with your electrician, since electrical is not included.
  4. Add installation and delivery or freight to the budget.
  5. Check whether your station count reaches a volume pricing tier.
  6. Confirm your move-in date against the lead time for each tier.

Frequently asked questions

How much can I save buying used office workstations?

For a 6'x6' workstation, furniture only, liquidation-grade is $615 and new is $1,920, a difference of $1,305 per station. Remanufactured sits in between at $1,245. Prices exclude electrical, delivery and professional installation.

What is the difference between liquidation-grade and remanufactured?

Liquidation-grade furniture is sold in its pre-owned condition and graded A like new, B very good or C good. Remanufactured furniture is the middle tier at $1,245 per 6'x6' station and usually takes 7 to 14 days. Ask what work was done on the specific product you are quoting so you can compare it fairly.

How fast can I get used office furniture?

In-stock liquidation-grade furniture can be ready in as little as 72 hours. Remanufactured usually takes 7 to 14 days, and new furniture typically takes 4 to 6 weeks.

Does used office furniture come with a warranty?

Office Station sells pre-owned furniture as an independent reseller, not an authorized dealer. Manufacturer warranties generally do not transfer to pre-owned furniture. Review the condition grade and inspect before you commit.

Are there volume discounts on used workstations?

Yes. Volume pricing is 5% at 20 or more stations, 8% at 50 or more and 12% at 100 or more. Your written quote will show exactly how the discount applies.
