Used Herman Miller Chairs, Graded Honestly

A pre-owned Herman Miller chair can deliver years of comfort for far less than new. You deserve to know exactly what condition you are getting and what coverage does and does not come with it.

5 minute read

Herman Miller chairs have a reputation for comfort and durability, which is exactly why they hold up well on the pre-owned market. A chair built for commercial daily use can have many good years left after its first office closes. The question is not whether used is good enough. It is whether the specific chair in front of you is.

Office Station sells pre-owned Herman Miller seating as an independent reseller. That means straightforward condition grades, plain talk about warranties and no pretending a used chair is new.

What independent reseller means for you

We are not an authorized Herman Miller dealer. We resell pre-owned furniture across multiple brands and grade each piece. Here is what that means in practice:

  • Warranty. Manufacturer warranties generally do not transfer to pre-owned furniture. Plan as if there is no factory coverage.
  • Pricing. You are paying for a used chair in a stated condition, not dealer list pricing.
  • Inventory. Models, colors and quantities change as lots come in. What is available this month may differ next month.
No fine print surprises. If a seller implies full manufacturer coverage on a used chair, ask for it in writing. The honest default for pre-owned seating is that the original warranty generally does not follow the chair.

How to inspect a used Herman Miller chair

Whether you are buying one chair or fifty, check a sample against this list.

  1. Gas cylinder. Raise the seat to full height, sit down firmly and see if it holds. A sinking seat means a worn cylinder.
  2. Tilt and tension. Recline through the full range. Tension should adjust noticeably, and the tilt lock, if the chair has one, should engage cleanly.
  3. Mesh or foam. Look for tears, stretched or sagging mesh, pulled threads, and flattened or crumbling foam.
  4. Armrests. Test height, width and pivot adjustments. Check arm pads for cracks or peeling.
  5. Seat depth and lumbar. If the model has these adjustments, make sure they move and stay put.
  6. Base and casters. Look for cracks in the base and confirm casters roll freely on your floor type.
  7. Noise. Creaks and clicks under normal movement often point to a loose or worn component.
  8. Labels. Manufacturer labels under the seat can help confirm the model and configuration.

Choosing the right model for your people

Herman Miller has made several well-known task chairs over the years, and they do not all fit the same way. Some use a suspension mesh seat and back, others use foam and fabric, and seat sizes and adjustment sets vary by model and configuration. Two chairs with the same name can have very different adjustment options depending on how the original buyer ordered them.

Before you buy in quantity, consider:

  • User size range. Some models come in multiple sizes. Make sure the size suits most of your team.
  • Adjustments that matter. Fully adjustable arms and lumbar support matter more for eight-hour task work than for conference seating.
  • Climate and comfort. Mesh stays cooler. Foam and fabric feel softer. Both work well when in good condition.
  • Consistency. For a matched look, ask whether the lot has enough chairs in one color and configuration.

Used Herman Miller vs. a new budget chair

Many buyers compare a used Herman Miller chair against a new chair from a big-box store at a similar price. The new chair has a fresh look and whatever warranty its maker offers. The used chair usually has a commercial-grade mechanism and a wider range of adjustments, but no transferable manufacturer warranty.

For people who sit for most of the workday, adjustability and build quality tend to matter more than a spotless finish. That is where a Grade B or Grade C commercial chair can be the more comfortable choice. For a chair that gets occasional use, the difference matters less.

Frequently asked questions

Is Office Station an authorized Herman Miller dealer?

No. Office Station is an independent reseller of pre-owned furniture, not an authorized dealer. We sell used Herman Miller chairs alongside other pre-owned brands and grade each piece so you know its condition.

Does the Herman Miller warranty transfer to a used chair?

Generally, no. Manufacturer warranties generally do not transfer to pre-owned furniture, so you should not assume factory coverage on a used chair. Buy based on the condition grade and your own inspection of the mechanism, cylinder and upholstery.

What do the A, B and C grades mean for chairs?

Grade A is like new, with little or no visible wear. Grade B is very good, with light cosmetic wear on frames or upholstery. Grade C is good, with more visible wear but sound function.

How quickly can I get used Herman Miller chairs?

Liquidation-grade pieces can be available in as little as 72 hours when in stock. Remanufactured furniture usually takes 7 to 14 days. Availability of specific models and quantities varies with incoming inventory, so ask before you plan around a date.

Can I buy a matched set for a whole office?

Often, yes, but matched quantities depend on what is in inventory at the time. Pre-owned chairs usually become available in lots, so larger matched orders are easier with some lead time and flexibility on color.
