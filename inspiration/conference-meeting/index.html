Inspiration: Conference and Meeting

Used Conference Room Furniture

Pre-owned conference tables and meeting seating from designer brands let you furnish boardrooms, huddle rooms and training spaces for less than new. Here is how to size, select and plan them well.

5 minute read

Conference rooms are high-visibility, low-use spaces. They might sit empty half the day and then host the client pitch that matters most that quarter. That makes them a natural fit for used conference room furniture: you get the look and build quality of designer brands without paying new prices for a room that sees modest wear.

Office Station sells pre-owned conference tables and meeting seating from manufacturers including Herman Miller, Steelcase, Knoll, Haworth, Teknion, Kimball, AIS and Friant. We are an independent reseller, not an authorized dealer, and every piece carries a condition grade.

Match the furniture to the meeting

Not every meeting room needs a boardroom table. Start by listing how each room is actually used.

Boardroom and client conference room

A long rectangular or boat-shaped table with high-back or mid-back conference chairs. Boat-shaped tables are wider in the middle, which helps everyone see each other and the screen at the end. This is where Grade A pieces earn their place.

Team meeting room

A standard rectangular table seating six to ten with mid-back chairs on casters. Choose chairs with a simple tilt so people can shift during longer meetings.

Huddle room

A small round or square table for two to four people, often with a wall-mounted display. Compact chairs or stools keep the room from feeling crowded.

Training and flexible rooms

Flip-top or modular tables that can be arranged in rows, U-shapes or clusters, paired with stackable or nesting chairs. These rooms change setup often, so mobility matters more than finish.

How to size a conference table

An oversized table is the most common conference room mistake. People cannot pull out chairs, and anyone arriving late has to squeeze along the wall. Use this approach:

  1. Measure the room wall to wall in both directions.
  2. Subtract about 8 feet from each dimension for 48 inches of clearance on every side. For a tight room, subtract 6 feet for 36 inches of clearance.
  3. The result is the largest table footprint that fits comfortably.
  4. Allow roughly 30 inches of table edge per seated person to estimate seating.

Room sizes assume a table about 42 to 48 inches wide. Wider tables need wider rooms. Also account for credenzas, a display wall or doors that swing inward, since each eats into clearance.

Check the power before you fall in love with a table. Many used conference tables have built-in power and data modules, and some have a cable path through the base to a floor box. Confirm where your room's floor or wall connections are, and whether the table's module types suit your equipment, before you commit.

Choosing meeting seating

Chairs make or break a long meeting. Compare these features when selecting pre-owned seating:

  • Back height: High-back for formal boardrooms, mid-back for most team rooms. Mid-back chairs also keep sightlines open to a screen.
  • Casters or glides: Casters for rooms where people pivot and reposition; glides or sled bases for guest seating and rooms on hard floors where chairs roll too easily.
  • Arms: Check that arm height clears the table apron so chairs tuck in fully.
  • Upholstery: Mesh stays cooler in long sessions; leather or vinyl reads more formal and wipes clean.
  • Matching sets: Count chairs per table plus a few extras along the wall for overflow.

For conference rooms that double as workspace, ergonomic task chairs can be a better fit than traditional conference chairs. See our pre-owned office chairs for options.

Frequently asked questions

How big a conference table fits my room?

A common rule is to leave about 48 inches between the table edge and the walls on every side, with 36 inches as a tight minimum. Subtract that clearance from both room dimensions to find the largest table that fits, then allow roughly 30 inches of table edge per seated person.

Which brands do you carry for conference rooms?

Office Station sells pre-owned furniture from manufacturers including Herman Miller, Steelcase, Knoll, Haworth, Humanscale, Teknion, Allsteel, Kimball, AIS and Friant. Inventory changes as offices are cleared, so specific tables and matching chair sets depend on availability.

Is used conference room furniture covered by a warranty?

Generally no. Office Station is an independent reseller, not an authorized dealer, and manufacturer warranties generally do not transfer to pre-owned furniture. Each piece carries a condition grade of A, B or C so you can judge it before buying.

Can I get matching conference chairs?

Often, because conference chairs usually come out of offices in sets. If you need a specific count, such as 12 or 14 identical chairs, ask early so we can check what is available. Counts larger than one set may mean mixing two closely matched sets.

Do you help plan meeting room layouts?

Yes. Space planning is free. Share room dimensions, door and window locations, where power and AV connections are, and how many people you need to seat, and we can recommend table sizes and layouts.
