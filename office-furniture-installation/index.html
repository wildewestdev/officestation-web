Buying workstations is only half the job. Panels, worksurfaces, pedestals and overhead storage arrive as parts, and someone has to turn those parts into a level, square, working office. What installation covers

A per-workstation install is about assembling furniture into the layout you approved. In practice that means:

  • Unpacking and staging components by station
  • Connecting panels and setting them plumb and level
  • Mounting worksurfaces, returns and any overhead storage
  • Placing pedestals and setting keyed locks if included
  • Following the space plan so aisles and clearances match the drawing
  • Removing packing material generated by the install

What is not included

Being clear about exclusions saves arguments later. The $95 rate does not include:

  • Electrical. Powered panels need a licensed electrician to connect base feeds or poke-throughs to building power.
  • Data and low-voltage cabling. Your IT or cabling vendor pulls and terminates network drops.
  • Delivery. $195 central or $295 metro in Greater Phoenix; freight quoted per project elsewhere.
  • Demolition or removal of existing furniture. If old furniture has to go first, that is a separate job. See office decommissioning.
Schedule the electrician before install day. Panels go up first, then power gets connected. If the electrician is booked a week after the crew leaves, your team moves into desks with no outlets. Share the space plan with your electrician as soon as it is approved.

Why space planning comes first

Every installation quote rests on a station count, and the station count comes from the layout. Office Station's space planning is free, so there is no reason to guess. A plan tells you how many 6'x6' stations fit once you account for aisles, egress paths, columns, conference rooms and storage walls. It also gives the install crew a drawing to follow, which is what keeps a large floor from drifting out of square.

Install day checklist

Most install delays have nothing to do with furniture. They come from access, building rules and timing. Work through this list before your crew arrives:

  1. Confirm the layout is final. Changes after panels are connected cost time.
  2. Clear the floor. Remove old furniture, boxes and anything in the install zone.
  3. Book the freight elevator and loading dock with building management for delivery and install hours.
  4. Ask about a certificate of insurance. Many commercial buildings require one from any vendor working on site.
  5. Check after-hours rules. If the office is occupied, ask whether nights or weekends are allowed.
  6. Mark floor boxes and power locations so panels line up with building power.
  7. Line up the electrician and IT vendor to follow the furniture crew.
  8. Name one on-site contact who can make layout decisions quickly.

Installing pre-owned and remanufactured furniture

Liquidation-grade and remanufactured workstations install the same way new ones do, and the per-station rate does not change. Office Station sells pre-owned furniture from brands like Herman Miller, Steelcase, Knoll and Haworth as an independent reseller, not an authorized dealer. That matters for one reason: manufacturer warranties generally do not transfer to pre-owned furniture, so ask about condition grade (A like new, B very good, C good) before you order.

Lead time also shapes your install date. Liquidation-grade stock can be ready in as little as 72 hours, remanufactured usually takes 7 to 14 days, and new typically runs 4 to 6 weeks. Set your install date after you know which tier you are buying.

Professional install or do it yourself?

Self-installing can make sense for two or three simple, unpowered stations and a team comfortable with hardware. It rarely makes sense for powered panel systems, a floor of 20 or more stations, or anything with overhead storage that has to be mounted securely. At $95 per station, compare the quote with what your own staff time is worth, since hours spent reading assembly sheets are hours not spent on their real jobs.

Frequently asked questions

How much does office furniture installation cost?

Professional installation is $95 per workstation with a $495 minimum per project. A 10-station project is $950, and a 4-station project comes in at the $495 minimum. Delivery and electrical work are priced separately.

Does the installation price include electrical hookup?

No. Workstation prices and installation exclude electrical work. If your panels have powered base feeds or you need new circuits, plan on a licensed electrician to make the final connections to building power.

Do I have to buy installation to buy workstations?

No. Workstation prices are furniture-only, so installation is an add-on. Many teams with a small order and handy staff self-install, while larger or powered layouts usually justify a professional crew.

Is space planning really free?

Yes. Space planning is free when you are pricing workstations with Office Station. The layout is what determines your station count, which is what your installation quote is based on.

What does delivery cost?

In Greater Phoenix, delivery is $195 for central zones and $295 for the wider metro. Outside Greater Phoenix, freight is quoted per project based on your location and order size.
