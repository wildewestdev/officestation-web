Breakroom Tables Your Team Will Actually Use

A good breakroom gets people out from behind their desks. The right breakroom tables make that easy, and you can furnish the room new, remanufactured or liquidation-grade depending on budget and timing.

5 minute read

Start with how your team uses the room

Breakroom tables get more abuse than almost any other office furniture. Hot coffee, wet cups, reheated lunches, chairs dragged across the base. So before you choose a look, think about the traffic.

  • Lunch room. People sit for 30 minutes or more. Dining-height tables and comfortable chairs matter most.
  • Coffee stop. Short visits. Counter or bar-height tables use less floor and keep people moving.
  • Informal meeting space. Teams grab a table for a quick huddle. Larger rectangular tables with power nearby double as work surfaces.
  • All-hands overflow. If the breakroom hosts company meetings, flip-top or nesting tables clear the floor fast.

Size the room in five steps

  1. Count peak users. Figure out how many people are in the room at the busiest lunch hour, not the total headcount.
  2. Allow table edge per person. Plan roughly 24 inches of edge for each seated person.
  3. Leave room behind chairs. People need space to pull out a chair and for others to walk past.
  4. Keep appliance zones clear. Refrigerators, microwaves and coffee stations draw lines. Keep tables out of that queue.
  5. Check accessibility. Include at least one table a wheelchair user can pull up to comfortably, and confirm requirements with your building team.

Space planning is free. Send us a sketch or floor plan and your peak headcount, and we will suggest a table mix.

Choosing a tabletop material

The top takes most of the wear, so it deserves as much thought as the shape.

  • High-pressure laminate. The workhorse choice. It stands up to spills, cleaning sprays and daily wiping, and it comes in wood looks and solid colors.
  • Solid surface or stone look. A more upscale cafe feel. Heavier, so check the base is rated for the top.
  • Wood veneer. Attractive, but less forgiving of wet cups and hot dishes. Better suited to a lightly used lounge than a busy lunch room.

Whatever you pick, a darker or patterned finish hides wear longer than a bright solid color.

Breakroom table checklist

  • Top surface resists moisture and heat; laminate is the practical choice for most breakrooms
  • Edges are sealed so spills do not swell the core
  • Bases sit flat, with working leveling glides
  • Base style leaves knee room at every seat
  • Flip-top and nesting mechanisms lock securely
  • Tops are free of deep burns, stains or delamination on pre-owned pieces
  • Chairs are the right height for the tables you choose

Frequently asked questions

What height should breakroom tables be?

Standard dining height works for most breakrooms and is the most comfortable for longer lunches. Counter and bar heights suit quick coffee stops and give a room a cafe feel. Many offices mix both so people can choose.

How much space does each person need at a breakroom table?

A common rule of thumb is about 24 inches of table edge per seated person, plus enough room behind each chair to pull out and walk past. We can lay out the room for you at no charge.

How much does delivery cost?

In Greater Phoenix, delivery is $195 for central zones and $295 for the wider metro. Outside Greater Phoenix, freight is quoted per project nationwide.

How fast can I get breakroom tables?

Liquidation-grade tables can be ready in as little as 72 hours when they are in stock. Remanufactured pieces usually take 7-14 days and new tables typically take 4-6 weeks.

Do pre-owned tables have a warranty?

Manufacturer warranties generally do not transfer to pre-owned furniture, and Office Station sells as an independent reseller rather than an authorized dealer. Each pre-owned table is condition graded A like new, B very good or C good.
