Height Adjustable Standing Desks

A sit-stand desk only earns its price if it fits the person using it and holds up to daily cycling. Here is how to compare new, remanufactured and pre-owned options and what to check before you order.

6 minute read

Height adjustable standing desks have gone from a perk to a normal expectation in many offices. The catch is that the market ranges from light residential frames to heavy commercial bases built for years of daily adjustment. Buying the right one comes down to three things: the grade you buy, the mechanics under the top, and whether the desk actually fits the people who will use it.

What is under the top

The base does the real work. Two desks with identical tops can feel completely different in use.

Motors and lift capacity

Dual motor bases put a motor in each leg. They lift more weight, move more evenly and resist racking on wide tops. Single motor bases drive both legs through a shaft and suit lighter loads. Add up what will sit on the desk: monitors, arms, a docking station, a CPU holder and whatever you lean on it. Then pick a base rated comfortably above that total.

Leg stages

Two-stage legs are common and cost less. Three-stage legs extend farther, which gives you a lower minimum and a higher maximum from the same base. If you have a mix of very short and very tall users, three-stage legs are usually the answer.

Stability

Wobble at standing height is the complaint people remember. Look for a solid crossbar, wide feet and a top that is deep enough for your monitor setup. Test by typing at full height with a monitor on an arm. If the screen shakes, the base is undersized for the job.

Controls

Simple up and down paddles work fine. Programmable presets help in shared or hoteling spaces where several people use the same desk each week. Anti-collision sensing is worth having near window sills, pedestals and overhead storage.

Ergonomic buying tips

A standing desk is only ergonomic if it is set correctly. Use this checklist when you spec desks for a team.

  • Measure elbow height. The worksurface should sit at or just below elbow height, seated and standing, with shoulders relaxed.
  • Plan the monitor separately. The top of the screen should sit near eye level. A monitor arm does this better than stacking the screen on a riser.
  • Check the low end. Shorter users often struggle more with minimum height than taller users do with maximum height.
  • Leave cable slack. Every cord must travel the full range. A cable tray or spine keeps cords from snagging.
  • Pair it with the right chair. A sit-stand desk still spends most of the day at seated height. See pre-owned office chairs for task seating that adjusts to match.
  • Add an anti-fatigue mat. Standing on a hard floor is what makes people stop standing.
  • Alternate, do not just stand. The benefit comes from changing position through the day, so make presets easy to reach.
Check power before you buy. Each motorized desk needs an outlet or a tap into the station's power. In panel systems, confirm where the base feed sits and whether there is enough circuit capacity for the desks plus monitors. Electrical work is not included in furniture pricing.

Sit-stand in workstations and cubicles

Many teams add height adjustment inside existing cubicles instead of buying freestanding desks. This can work well, but it takes planning. The panel height needs to allow for the desk rising without hitting overhead bins. The station needs enough depth for a monitor at a comfortable distance. And the return or corner piece may need to stay fixed while the main surface moves.

For reference, a 6'x6' workstation runs $615 liquidation-grade, $1,245 remanufactured or $1,920 new for furniture only. Frequently asked questions

Is a pre-owned standing desk worth buying?

Often, yes, if the base is from a solid commercial line and the motor, controller and cabling test clean. Ask for the condition grade and whether the desk was cycled through its full height range. Keep in mind that manufacturer warranties generally do not transfer to pre-owned furniture.

What height range do I need?

A useful commercial range covers seated height for shorter users and standing height for taller users without stretching the base to its limits. Measure elbow height seated and standing for the people who will use the desk, then subtract about an inch for keyboard height. Choose a base whose range covers both numbers with a little room to spare.

Single motor or dual motor?

Dual motor bases generally lift heavier loads, move more smoothly and handle wider tops with less wobble. Single motor bases can work for lighter setups with one monitor. If you run multiple monitors, arms or a long return, a dual motor base is the safer pick.

Can I add sit-stand desks to existing cubicles?

In many cases you can swap a fixed worksurface for a height adjustable one, but it depends on the panel system, the depth of the station and where power comes in. Cable length and overhead storage can also get in the way. Share your layout and we can tell you what will fit.

Do your prices include delivery and installation?

No. Furniture prices exclude electrical, delivery and professional installation. In Greater Phoenix, delivery is $195 central or $295 metro, and freight elsewhere nationwide is quoted per project.
