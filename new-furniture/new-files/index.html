Office Filing Cabinets, Lateral and Vertical

Office filing cabinets are simple until you order the wrong size for your floor plan. Here is how to choose between lateral and vertical files, pick a condition grade, and get them fast.

6 minute read

Lateral or vertical: start with the footprint

The first decision is not brand or color. It is where the cabinet will stand and how the drawers will open. Office filing cabinets come in two basic forms, and each suits a different kind of space.

Lateral files

Lateral files are wide and shallow. Drawers open from the long side, and folders hang side to side or front to back depending on the rails. Because they are shallow, laterals sit comfortably along a wall, beside a desk or at the end of a cubicle run. The top often doubles as a credenza surface for a printer or reference binders.

Vertical files

Vertical files are narrow and deep. Folders run front to back, and the drawer pulls straight out toward you. They use very little wall length, which makes them useful in tight private offices or storage rooms. The tradeoff is depth: an open drawer needs a lot of clear floor in front of it.

How many drawers do you need?

Drawer count controls both capacity and height. A two-drawer lateral tucks under a window or serves as a counter. Three- and four-drawer units store more and create a partial visual divider. Five-drawer laterals are tall, dense storage best kept against a wall.

For a quick estimate, count your current linear filing, then add room for growth. If your office is moving toward digital records, buy for what you will keep, not what you have today. A move is a natural time to purge and scan records you no longer need on paper.

Tall files need anchoring. Taller filing cabinets can tip when several loaded drawers are open at once. Use the anti-tip or interlock mechanism if the cabinet has one, anchor tall units to the wall or to each other, and load bottom drawers first.

Filing cabinet inspection checklist

Whether you shop in person or from photos, check these points on any used file.

  • Every drawer opens fully and closes without grinding or sticking
  • Drawer fronts are aligned and not bent at the corners
  • Hanging folder rails are present in every drawer
  • The lock turns, and keys are included or a replacement core is planned
  • The interlock allows only one drawer open at a time, where equipped
  • No rust on the base or inside the drawers
  • Leveling glides are present so the cabinet sits flat
  • Paint color matches the rest of the order, since shades vary by manufacturer and year

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between a lateral file and a vertical file?

A lateral file is wide and shallow, with drawers that open from the long side so folders sit side to side. A vertical file is narrow and deep, with folders running front to back. Laterals fit along walls and cubicle runs; verticals fit narrow spaces where depth is available.

How quickly can I get filing cabinets?

Liquidation-grade files can be ready in as little as 72 hours when they are in stock. Remanufactured files usually take 7-14 days, and new files typically take 4-6 weeks.

Do used filing cabinets come with keys?

Not always. Keys are one of the most commonly lost items when offices move. Ask whether keys are included before you buy, and plan for a lock core replacement if they are not.
