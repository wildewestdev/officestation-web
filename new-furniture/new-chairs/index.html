New Office Chairs vs Pre-Owned Designer Chairs

New chairs bring a full warranty and your exact specs. Pre-owned designer chairs bring proven ergonomics for less. Here is how to decide, chair by chair.

6 minute read

If you are shopping for new office chairs, you are probably comparing sticker prices across a few models. The better comparison is new versus pre-owned designer chairs. The same commercial chairs that sell new also come back onto the market when companies downsize or move, and some of those are in excellent shape. This page lays out when new is worth it, when pre-owned is the better buy, and what to check either way.

The honest truth about warranty transfer

This is the point most people skip, so we will be direct. Office Station sells pre-owned designer chairs from brands like Herman Miller, Steelcase, Knoll, Haworth, Humanscale, Teknion, Allsteel, Kimball, AIS and Friant as an independent reseller. We are not an authorized dealer for those manufacturers.

That matters because manufacturer warranties generally do not transfer to pre-owned furniture. A premium chair may carry a long warranty when bought new from an authorized source, but when you buy that same chair pre-owned, you should assume that warranty does not come with it.

What you get instead is a lower price and a condition grade that tells you exactly what shape the chair is in. For many offices, the savings more than cover the occasional replacement part. For others, especially organizations with procurement rules that require warranty coverage, new is the right call.

Ask this before you buy. Does your organization's purchasing policy require a manufacturer warranty on seating? If yes, buy those chairs new and consider pre-owned for areas the policy does not cover, like guest seating or training rooms.

When new office chairs make sense

  • You need warranty coverage. Policy, insurance or long-term cost planning calls for it.
  • You need exact specs. A brand-standard fabric, a specific color, or a particular arm and lumbar package.
  • You need a big matching order. Fifty identical chairs in one finish is simple new and hit-or-miss pre-owned.
  • A user has specific ergonomic needs. Seat depth, size or support that has to be fitted precisely.
  • Your timeline allows it. Four to six weeks works for your move-in date.

When pre-owned designer chairs make sense

  • You want top-tier ergonomics on a budget. A Grade A or B designer task chair gives people full adjustability for less than new.
  • You need chairs fast. In-stock pieces can move in as little as 72 hours.
  • You are furnishing secondary spaces. Conference rooms, training rooms and hoteling desks.
  • You are growing unpredictably. Lower cost per seat lowers the risk of buying ahead of headcount.

What to check on any office chair

New or pre-owned, sit in the chair or have someone on your team test these points:

  1. Seat height. Feet flat on the floor with thighs roughly level at your desk height.
  2. Seat depth. A few fingers of space between the seat edge and the back of the knee.
  3. Lumbar support. Adjustable, and it lands at the curve of the lower back.
  4. Tilt and tension. The recline feels controlled, and the tension adjusts for different body weights.
  5. Arms. They adjust low enough to slide under the desk and high enough to support the forearms.
  6. Base and casters. The chair rolls smoothly and the casters suit your floor surface.

For pre-owned chairs, add these:

  • The gas cylinder holds height without sinking over a few minutes.
  • Every lever and knob works through its full range.
  • Mesh or fabric has no tears, sagging or stains beyond what the grade describes.
  • The backrest does not wobble or click under load.

Frequently asked questions

Does the manufacturer warranty transfer on a pre-owned designer chair?

Generally no. Office Station is an independent reseller, not an authorized dealer, and manufacturer warranties generally do not transfer to pre-owned furniture. If a transferable warranty is a requirement for your organization, buy that chair new.

What do the condition grades mean on pre-owned chairs?

Grade A is like new, Grade B is very good with light signs of use, and Grade C is good with visible wear but full function. For task chairs people sit in all day, most buyers choose A or B.

How long do new office chairs take compared with pre-owned?

New furniture typically takes 4 to 6 weeks. Liquidation-grade pieces in stock can move in as little as 72 hours, and remanufactured furniture usually takes 7 to 14 days.

When is a new chair the better choice?

Buy new when you need a transferable warranty, a specific fabric or color, a large matching quantity, or an adjustment package that is not available pre-owned. New also makes sense for users with specific ergonomic needs that call for exact sizing.

Can I buy chairs along with workstations?

Yes. Many offices price chairs with used cubicles or new workstations in one plan. Space planning is free, and workstation installation is $95 per station with a $495 minimum.
