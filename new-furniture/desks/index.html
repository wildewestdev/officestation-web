New Office Desks, Compared With Remanufactured and Liquidation-Grade

New desks are the right call for some offices and an expensive wait for others. Compare all three tiers side by side, with real lead times, so you order what actually fits your schedule and budget.

5 minute read

If you are shopping for new office desks, you probably have a reason: a specific finish, a consistent look across every floor, or simply wanting furniture nobody has used. Those are good reasons. But new also carries the longest lead time and the highest price, and it is worth knowing what the alternatives look like before you place the order.

This page compares new desks and workstations with remanufactured and liquidation-grade options so you can choose by schedule, budget and how the space will be used.

Let the calendar decide first. Count the business days between today and the day people need to sit down, then subtract time for delivery, installation and electrical. If fewer than four to six weeks remain, new desks cannot cover the whole order, and a mixed approach is the safer plan.

When new desks make sense

  • You need an exact finish or configuration that has to match an existing brand standard across locations.
  • You have at least four to six weeks before move-in, plus time for installation.
  • The order is large and uniform, and consistency matters more than speed.
  • You are furnishing a flagship or client-facing floor where every surface should look untouched.

At 100 or more stations, volume pricing of 12% narrows the gap, but the lead time does not change. Plan backward from your move-in date.

Price New, Remanufactured or Liquidation-Grade

When remanufactured is the better fit

Remanufactured furniture is the middle path. At $1,245 for a 6'x6' workstation, it costs roughly a third less than new and usually arrives in 7 to 14 days instead of 4 to 6 weeks. It suits teams that want a fresh, consistent look but cannot wait on production, or that would rather put the difference toward chairs, storage or conference furniture.

When liquidation-grade wins

Liquidation-grade desks and workstations are pre-owned pieces sold with honest condition grades: A like new, B very good, C good. At $615 per 6'x6' workstation and as little as 72 hours for in-stock items, they solve two common problems: a move date that arrived early and a budget that did not grow with headcount.

Pre-owned furniture comes from brands such as Herman Miller, Steelcase, Knoll, Haworth, Teknion, Allsteel, Kimball, AIS and Friant. Making a mixed order look intentional

Most offices do not need a single tier across every room. A common split is new or remanufactured desks on the floors clients see, and liquidation-grade pieces in training rooms, hoteling areas and space held for future hires. The risk is a patchwork look, and it is easy to avoid.

  • Pick one surface finish family and match it across tiers as closely as availability allows.
  • Keep work surface heights consistent within each room so rows line up.
  • Match panel heights in open areas. Mixed panel heights are more noticeable than small finish differences.
  • Group tiers by zone, not desk by desk, so each area reads as one purchase.
  • Stage delivery around lead times. Liquidation-grade zones can be ready first while new pieces are in production.

What to budget beyond the desk

  • Installation: $95 per workstation, $495 minimum.
  • Delivery in Greater Phoenix: $195 central, $295 metro.
  • Delivery elsewhere: nationwide freight quoted per project.
  • Electrical: not included in furniture pricing. Coordinate with your electrician early, especially for powered workstations.
  • Space planning: free.
  • Volume pricing: 5% at 20+ stations, 8% at 50+, 12% at 100+.

Example: 30 remanufactured 6'x6' workstations at $1,245 come to $37,350 before the 5% volume discount, or $35,482.50 after it. Installation adds $2,850 at $95 each. Delivery and electrical are separate.

Desk buying checklist

  1. Confirm your move-in date and work backward through lead time, delivery and installation.
  2. Measure the space or request free space planning before settling on a desk size.
  3. Decide which areas need new or remanufactured and which can use liquidation-grade.
  4. Check surface depth. Deeper surfaces keep monitors at a comfortable distance.
  5. Decide whether you need height-adjustable desks for some or all users.
  6. Confirm power and data needs with your electrician and IT vendor.
  7. Count stations to see which volume pricing tier you reach.
  8. Plan what happens to the desks you are replacing.

That last step can fund part of the new order. Through office furniture liquidation, you get a written net offer equal to resale value minus removal cost, and when it is positive, you are paid.

Frequently asked questions

How long does it take to get new office desks?

New furniture typically takes 4 to 6 weeks. Remanufactured pieces usually take 7 to 14 days, and liquidation-grade items in stock can be ready in as little as 72 hours. If your move date is closer than a month out, new may not be realistic for the whole order.

What is the difference between remanufactured and liquidation-grade?

Liquidation-grade furniture is pre-owned and sold as recovered, graded A like new, B very good or C good. Remanufactured furniture has been restored for a fresher result and sits between liquidation-grade and new on price and lead time. For a 6'x6' workstation, furniture only, the three tiers run $615, $1,245 and $1,920.

Do prices include delivery and installation?

No. Prices exclude electrical, delivery and professional installation. Installation is $95 per workstation with a $495 minimum. Greater Phoenix delivery is $195 central or $295 metro, and freight elsewhere in the country is quoted per project.

Is there a discount for larger desk orders?

Yes. Volume pricing is 5% at 20 or more stations, 8% at 50 or more and 12% at 100 or more. Free space planning helps confirm the count before you order.

Can I mix new and pre-owned desks in one office?

Yes, and it is often the most practical approach. Many offices put new or remanufactured desks in visible areas and use liquidation-grade pieces in training rooms or expansion space. Keeping finishes and heights consistent makes a mixed order look intentional.
