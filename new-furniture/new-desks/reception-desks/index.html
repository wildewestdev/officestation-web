Reception Desks for Offices That Make a Good First Impression

Your reception desk is the first piece of furniture a visitor sees and the one your front desk team uses all day. We source reception desks new or pre-owned and help you plan the space before you buy.

6 minute read

What a reception desk actually has to do

A reception desk is part furniture and part workstation. It needs to look right from the lobby side and work hard from the staff side. Before you look at finishes, get clear on the job the desk has to do.

  • Greet and check in. A transaction counter at standing height lets visitors sign in or hand over paperwork without leaning over a work surface.
  • Hide the clutter. The front panel, or modesty panel, should screen monitors, cables, phones and personal items from view.
  • Support real work. Your receptionist may spend a full shift here. That means a seated work surface at the right height, room for a monitor, and storage within reach.
  • Stay accessible. Plan for a lowered counter section so a visitor using a wheelchair can be served comfortably. Confirm the exact requirements with your architect or building owner.

New vs. pre-owned reception desks

You do not have to choose between a brand-new desk and a tired secondhand one. Office Station sources reception furniture across three tiers so you can match the desk to your budget and move-in date.

Liquidation-grade

Desks recovered from corporate office projects and sold as they are, with a condition grade. This is the fastest option. When we have a match in stock, it can be ready in as little as 72 hours. Selection depends on what is available, so it works best when your dimensions are flexible.

Remanufactured

Pre-owned furniture that has been refurbished, which can include new surfaces or panel refreshes. Remanufactured pieces usually take 7-14 days. This is a good middle ground when you want a like-new look without the new-furniture lead time.

New

New desks give you the full range of sizes, finishes and configurations. Expect lead times of 4-6 weeks. Choose new when your lobby needs an exact size or a specific finish to match the rest of the space.

Plan the lobby before you pick the desk

The most common reception desk mistake is buying a desk that fits the room on paper but crowds the entry in real life. Our space planning is free, and it starts with a few measurements.

  1. Measure the lobby. Note wall lengths, ceiling features and any columns.
  2. Mark doors and sightlines. The receptionist should see the entry door. Visitors should see the desk as soon as they walk in.
  3. Locate power and data. Floor boxes and wall outlets often decide where the desk can go. Our prices exclude electrical work, so plan any new circuits with your electrician early.
  4. Leave circulation space. Keep a clear path behind the desk for staff and in front for visitors and guest seating.
  5. Think about what comes next. If you are also adding workstations or conference furniture, planning the whole floor at once keeps finishes consistent.

What to check on a pre-owned reception desk

If you are considering a liquidation-grade or remanufactured desk, walk through this list before you commit.

  • Transaction counter edges free of chips, deep scratches or peeling laminate
  • Front and side panels straight, with no cracks at joints or corners
  • Grommets and cable pathways intact, so cords stay hidden
  • All connecting hardware included for sectional or modular desks
  • Pedestals and drawers that open smoothly, with keys if they lock
  • Finish that works with your existing lobby seating and flooring
  • Overall dimensions confirmed against your plan, not just the listing
