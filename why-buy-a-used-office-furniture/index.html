Why Buy Used Office Furniture? The Numbers Behind the Decision

Used office furniture can cut your furniture budget by more than half and get people seated in days instead of weeks. Here is how the three tiers compare on price, lead time and tradeoffs.

6 minute read

Most offices do not need brand-new furniture. They need desks that work, chairs that fit, and a layout ready before the lease starts. That is the real case for buying used: you pay for function and quality, not for the first year of ownership.

This guide skips the vague talking points and puts actual prices, lead times and tradeoffs side by side so you can decide which tier fits your project.

The three ways to buy a workstation

When people say "used office furniture," they can mean very different things. It helps to separate the options into three tiers.

  • Liquidation-grade. Furniture recovered from corporate offices, sorted and sold largely as found. It is the lowest-cost tier and often the fastest to deliver.
  • Remanufactured. Pre-owned furniture that has been refreshed, such as new surfaces, fabric or finishes, so it looks and feels closer to new.
  • New. Built to order from the manufacturer, with the longest wait and the highest price.

Pre-owned pieces are graded so you know what you are getting: A means like new, B means very good, and C means good, with visible wear but full function.

Speed is an underrated reason to buy used

Price gets the attention, but timing often decides the project. A new-furniture order that takes 4 to 6 weeks can push back a move-in date, force temporary seating, or leave new hires without a desk.

In-stock liquidation-grade stations can be ready in as little as 72 hours. Remanufactured stations usually land in 7 to 14 days. If your lease date is fixed or you are growing faster than planned, that difference matters as much as the savings.

Know the warranty picture. Office Station sells pre-owned furniture from brands like Herman Miller, Steelcase, Knoll, Haworth, Humanscale, Teknion, Allsteel, Kimball, AIS and Friant as an independent reseller, not an authorized dealer. Manufacturer warranties generally do not transfer to pre-owned furniture, so factor that into your decision and check condition grades carefully.

Quality: why used commercial furniture holds up

Commercial-grade systems furniture is built for years of daily use in demanding offices. Steel frames, panel systems and work surfaces from major manufacturers are designed to be reconfigured and reinstalled, which is why they transition well to a second office.

That is also why buying used from a known brand is usually a better value than buying new from a budget line. You get heavier construction and parts that are easier to match when you expand later.

How to choose the right tier

Use this checklist to match the tier to your situation.

  • Pick liquidation-grade if budget and speed are the priorities, the space is back-office or operations, or you need to seat people this week.
  • Pick remanufactured if clients visit the space, you want a consistent look across every station, or you can wait about two weeks.
  • Pick new if you need a specific finish or configuration that is not available pre-owned, or a manufacturer warranty is a requirement.
  • Mix tiers if you want remanufactured stations in visible areas and liquidation-grade in private or support areas.

What to check before you buy

  1. Confirm the condition grade (A, B or C) for each item on the quote.
  2. Measure your space, or use free space planning so the layout fits before anything ships.
  3. Plan electrical separately, since workstation prices exclude it.
  4. Budget installation at $95 per workstation with a $495 minimum.
  5. Check freight or delivery costs for your address.
  6. Ask whether your station count reaches the 20, 50 or 100 volume thresholds.

Frequently asked questions

Is used office furniture really cheaper than new?

Yes, usually by a wide margin. A 6'x6' workstation runs $615 liquidation-grade, $1,245 remanufactured and $1,920 new, furniture only. That puts liquidation-grade about 68 percent below new and remanufactured about 35 percent below new.

Does used furniture come with a manufacturer warranty?

Generally no. Office Station is an independent reseller, not an authorized dealer, and manufacturer warranties generally do not transfer to pre-owned furniture. Ask about the condition grade and inspect what you are buying so you know exactly what you are getting.

How fast can I get used workstations?

Liquidation-grade workstations can ship in as little as 72 hours when they are in stock. Remanufactured stations usually take 7 to 14 days, and new stations typically take 4 to 6 weeks.

What is not included in the workstation price?

The listed prices cover furniture only. Electrical work, delivery and professional installation are separate. Installation is $95 per workstation with a $495 minimum, and Greater Phoenix delivery is $195 central or $295 metro.

Do I get a discount for buying a lot of stations?

Yes. Volume pricing is 5 percent at 20 or more stations, 8 percent at 50 or more and 12 percent at 100 or more. Confirm how the discount applies to your mix of tiers when you request a quote.
