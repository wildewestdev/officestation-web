How condition grading works

Used furniture varies. A chair from a lightly used conference room is not the same as one from a 24-hour call center. Grading gives you a shared vocabulary before anything is delivered.

GradeWhat it meansWhere Tempe offices often use it
ALike newReception, client meeting rooms, executive offices
BVery goodOpen-plan teams, private offices, training rooms
CGoodBack-office, storage areas, overflow or temporary seating

Mixing grades is a practical way to control budget. Spend on the areas people see first, and save where function matters more than finish.

About warranties. Manufacturer warranties generally do not transfer to pre-owned furniture, even for well-known brands. Review grades, finishes and quantities on your quote carefully before you approve it.

Liquidation-grade, remanufactured or new?

The three workstation options solve different problems, so pick based on your situation rather than price alone.

  • Choose liquidation-grade when move-in is close, budget is tight, or you are furnishing a startup suite that may change again within a few years. In-stock stations can be ready in as little as 72 hours.
  • Choose remanufactured when you want a more uniform, refreshed look across a whole floor and can wait roughly 7 to 14 days.
  • Choose new when you need a specific configuration or finish that pre-owned inventory cannot match and your timeline allows 4 to 6 weeks.

Many Tempe offices combine options: remanufactured stations for the main team area and liquidation-grade for overflow, interns or a training room. Free space planning can map where each option goes before you commit.

Checklist before you order

  1. Get a floor plan or measurements. Free space planning helps confirm how many stations fit and where aisles, columns and power sit.
  2. Confirm power and data. Decide whether panels need powered bases and coordinate electrical early, since it is not included in furniture pricing.
  3. Check building access. Ask your property manager about dock hours, freight elevator reservations and any certificate of insurance requirements.
  4. Pick grades by area. Assign A, B or C to each zone of the office before finalizing quantities.
  5. Match the timeline to the option. If move-in is days away, liquidation-grade in-stock furniture is the realistic choice. If you have a month or more, remanufactured or new opens up.
  6. Count chairs separately. Workstation pricing is furniture for the station. Plan task seating as its own line.

What do the condition grades mean?

Grade A is like new, Grade B is very good, and Grade C is good. Every pre-owned item is graded so you know what to expect before it arrives. Many offices mix grades, putting A pieces in client-facing areas and B or C in back-office rooms.

Liquidation-grade workstations can be available in as little as 72 hours when in stock. Remanufactured stations usually take 7 to 14 days, and new stations take 4 to 6 weeks. Installation scheduling is confirmed with your quote.
