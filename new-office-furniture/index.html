New Office Furniture, Compared Honestly With the Alternatives

New office furniture is the right call for some projects and an expensive habit for others. Here is how new stacks up against remanufactured and liquidation-grade workstations on price, lead time and fit, so you can pick with real numbers.

6 minute read

If you are pricing new office furniture, you are probably weighing more than the sticker. You are balancing a move-in date, a budget someone above you approved, how the space needs to look, and how long the furniture has to last. New is not always the best answer to that equation, and pre-owned is not always the cheaper one once you factor in time and fit.

This page lays out the three tiers side by side, using actual workstation pricing, so you can make the decision on facts instead of a catalog.

When new office furniture makes sense

New earns its premium in a handful of situations. Be honest about whether yours is one of them.

  • You need a manufacturer warranty. Warranties generally do not transfer to pre-owned furniture. If your organization requires one, new is the clean path.
  • You need exact finishes or configurations. If the design calls for a specific laminate, fabric or panel height across every station, new gives you full control.
  • You have time. A 4-6 week lead time is fine when your lease starts in three months. It is a problem when it starts in three weeks.
  • The space is a brand statement. Some lobbies and executive areas justify new because they are seen by every visitor.

When remanufactured is the smarter buy

Remanufactured sits in the middle for a reason. At $1,245 per station, it costs about 35% less than new while delivering a refreshed, uniform look. Lead times usually run 7-14 days, which fits most real-world move schedules.

It is a strong fit if you want the office to feel finished and consistent but do not need a transferable warranty or a custom finish. It works especially well in open-plan work areas where dozens of matching stations matter more than any single piece.

When liquidation-grade is the right call

Liquidation-grade at $615 per station is less than a third of the new price. When it is in stock, it can be ready in as little as 72 hours. That combination solves two of the most common problems in office projects: a budget that came in low and a deadline that came in early.

Pre-owned pieces are graded so you know what you are getting: A is like new, B is very good and C is good. Liquidation-grade makes the most sense for call centers, overflow space, training rooms, startups and any area where function matters more than a perfectly matched finish. Run the numbers at your headcount. At 50 workstations with the 8% volume discount, liquidation-grade comes to about $28,290, remanufactured about $57,270 and new about $88,320, furniture only. The gap between tiers grows with every station you add, which is why the tier decision matters more on large projects than small ones.

A checklist for choosing your tier

Work through these questions in order. The first one that gives you a firm answer usually decides the tier.

  1. What is your move-in date? Under two weeks points to liquidation-grade in stock. Two to four weeks opens up remanufactured. Six weeks or more makes new realistic.
  2. Is a manufacturer warranty required? If yes, choose new for those pieces.
  3. Is a specific finish required? If yes, new or remanufactured is the safer route.
  4. Which areas are client-facing? Consider a mixed approach, with higher tiers where visitors go and liquidation-grade elsewhere.
  5. What is the budget per head? Divide your approved furniture budget by headcount and compare it with the three prices above.
  6. Do you have existing furniture to remove? If so, its resale value can offset your new purchase.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a new 6'x6' workstation cost compared with pre-owned?

For a 6'x6' workstation, furniture only, new runs $1,920, remanufactured runs $1,245 and liquidation-grade runs $615. Those prices exclude electrical, delivery and professional installation. Volume pricing takes 5% off at 20+ stations, 8% at 50+ and 12% at 100+.

How long does new office furniture take to arrive?

New workstations typically take 4-6 weeks. Remanufactured usually ships in 7-14 days, and liquidation-grade can be ready in as little as 72 hours when it is in stock. If your move-in date is fixed and close, lead time often decides the tier for you.

Do manufacturer warranties come with pre-owned furniture?

Generally no. Office Station is an independent reseller of pre-owned designer furniture, not an authorized dealer, and manufacturer warranties generally do not transfer to pre-owned pieces. If a transferable manufacturer warranty is a hard requirement, that is a strong reason to buy new.

Can I mix new and pre-owned furniture in the same office?

Yes. A practical approach is new or remanufactured in client-facing areas and liquidation-grade in back-office or overflow space. Free space planning helps you map which tier goes where before you commit.

What does installation cost?

Installation is $95 per workstation with a $495 minimum. Delivery in Greater Phoenix is $195 for central zones and $295 for the wider metro, and freight elsewhere in the country is quoted per project.
