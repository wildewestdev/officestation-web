Used Breakroom and Cafeteria Tables

Commercial cafeteria tables, café rounds and high-tops from corporate decommissions, graded so you know what you are getting. Here is how to size them, inspect them and get them delivered.

6 minute read

A breakroom table has a hard life. It gets wiped down several times a day, bumped by chairs, loaded with laptops at lunch and occasionally stood on to change a light bulb. That is exactly why commercial tables from corporate offices are a smart used purchase. They were built with heavier bases and tougher laminate tops than retail kitchen tables, and most of that durability is still there when an office is decommissioned.

If you are searching for used breakroom tables, this page covers the shapes and heights you will run into, how to size a room, what to inspect on tops and bases, and what delivery costs.

Shapes and heights, and where each works

Table shape changes how a room feels and how many people fit. Height changes how people use it.

Seat counts are practical guidelines, not rules. People with laptops need more elbow room than people with a sandwich. Pair each table height with the right chair or stool, and browse used breakroom seating to match.

Sizing your breakroom

A common mistake is buying tables by count and discovering nobody can walk between them. Work through these steps with a tape measure before you choose.

  1. Measure the usable floor. Subtract counters, appliances, vending machines and door swings.
  2. Allow chair space. Plan roughly 18 to 24 inches behind each seated person for the chair to pull out.
  3. Keep walkways open. Leave a clear path to the exits, the sink and the refrigerator, and check your local code requirements for aisle widths.
  4. Mix table types. A few rounds, a long rectangular run and a counter along the wall usually fits more people comfortably than a grid of identical tables.
  5. Think about events. Rectangular tables on non-folding bases can still be pushed together for training or all-hands meetings.

Condition grades for tables

Every table is graded before sale. For tables, the grade mostly reflects the top surface and edges, because bases rarely show wear that matters.

  • Grade A, like new. Clean top with no noticeable scratches, intact edge banding, base finish in excellent shape.
  • Grade B, very good. Light surface marks or minor edge wear you notice up close, fully functional.
  • Grade C, good. Visible scratches, scuffed edges or worn base finish, structurally sound and stable.
Match the grade to the room. An employee cafeteria rarely needs Grade A tops, because new scratches will appear within weeks. Save the higher grade for a client-facing café or a space visible from reception, and put the savings toward something people touch all day, like task chairs.

What to inspect on a used table

A table is simple, which makes problems easy to spot if you know where to look.

  • Edge banding. Peeling or chipped edges catch clothing and let moisture into the core. Small chips are cosmetic. Long loose sections are worth avoiding.
  • Top flatness. Lay a straight edge across the top. Warping usually means water damage to a particleboard core.
  • Base stability. Push on the corners. A good base does not rock, and leveling glides should turn freely.
  • Mounting hardware. Check that the screws holding the top to the base bite firmly. Stripped holes cause wobble.
  • Flip-top and nesting mechanisms. If the table folds or nests, cycle it several times to confirm it locks.
  • Height consistency. For matching sets, confirm every table sits at the same height so rows line up.

Frequently asked questions

What sizes of used breakroom tables are available?

Inventory depends on recent liquidations, but corporate breakrooms commonly use 30 to 42 inch squares and rounds, 60 inch rounds, rectangular tables for rows of seating, and counter or bar height tables. Tell us your room size and seat count and we will match what is in stock.

How much is delivery in Phoenix?

Greater Phoenix delivery is $195 in the central zone and $295 across the wider metro. Outside Greater Phoenix, freight is quoted per project. Table prices exclude delivery.

Can table tops and bases be separated?

Many commercial breakroom tables use a separate top bolted to a base, which makes them easier to move through doors and elevators. Before delivery, confirm whether your tables arrive assembled or knocked down so your team is ready.

Are scratches on a Grade C table a problem?

Usually not for function. Grade C means visible wear such as scratches or edge scuffs while the table remains structurally sound. It is a sensible choice for warehouse break areas, shop floors and training rooms.

Do used tables come with a manufacturer warranty?

Generally no. Office Station sells as an independent reseller, not an authorized dealer, and manufacturer warranties usually do not transfer to pre-owned furniture. The condition grade is your guide to what you are buying.
