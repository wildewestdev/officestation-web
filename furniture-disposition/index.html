Office Furniture Disposition, Done With a Written Net Offer

Every desk, chair and panel in a closing or shrinking office has to go somewhere. Office Station puts a number on that decision with a written net offer, an itemized inventory and a diversion summary when the job is done.

6 minute read

Office furniture disposition sounds like a line item. In practice it is a stack of decisions with real money attached: what still has resale value, what can be donated, what must be recycled, and what it costs to get everything out of the building before your lease ends. This page walks through the options and shows how Office Station turns that stack into a single written number.

What disposition actually covers

Disposition is everything that happens to furniture after someone decides it is no longer needed. That includes the assessment, the pricing, the physical removal, the handoff to its next home, and the paperwork that proves it happened.

Most offices hit this moment during a lease expiration, a downsizing, a consolidation into fewer floors, or a move to a new space with a different layout. The deadline is usually fixed by the landlord, which is why planning early matters.

Your disposition options compared

There are four common paths. Most real projects use more than one, because a floor of systems furniture rarely has the same value from end to end.

OptionBest forCost pictureWatch out for
ResaleName-brand workstations, task chairs, storage and tables in good shapeCan offset or exceed removal costValue depends on brand, condition, quantity and demand
DonationServiceable pieces with little resale demandYou usually still pay for labor and transportCharities often cannot take large lots or accept pickup on your schedule
RecyclingDamaged steel, broken components, worn laminateLabor and hauling, sometimes offset by scrap valueMixed materials must be separated
LandfillItems with no other viable pathLabor, hauling and dump fees with no offsetThe most expensive outcome per item and the worst for sustainability goals

The goal is to push as much inventory as possible up that table, toward resale, and to be clear about what cannot go there.

How the written net offer works

Office Station prices disposition with one simple formula:

Net offer = resale value of the furniture minus the cost to remove it.

If the result is positive, Office Station pays you. If the furniture is worth less than the labor, trucks and handling needed to clear it, the offer shows the difference so you can plan for it. Either way, you get the number in writing before anyone lifts a panel.

That matters because disposition quotes are often split across vendors. One firm buys the chairs, another hauls the rest, a third handles electronics. A single net offer puts the whole picture on one page.

The disposition process, step by step

  1. Walkthrough. Typically within 48 hours in Greater Phoenix and 2 to 3 business days in other service markets.
  2. Written net offer. Typically delivered within 5 business days, based on what was seen on site.
  3. Itemized inventory. A list of what is being removed, so facilities, finance and your landlord all work from the same record.
  4. Building coordination. Certificates of insurance for property management, freight elevator reservations and loading dock scheduling.
  5. Removal. Nights and weekends crews are available so the work does not collide with tenants or your own remaining staff.
  6. E-waste. Electronics are handled separately, with certificates of data destruction for data-bearing devices.
  7. Handback. The space is left broom clean and photo-documented.
  8. Diversion summary. A record of how material was routed, which you can use for internal reporting or sustainability goals.

What affects the value of your furniture

Resale value is not a fixed rate per workstation. These factors move the number the most:

  • Brand and product line. Established lines from major manufacturers have steadier demand in the secondary market.
  • Condition. Stains, broken mechanisms, missing parts and heavy wear reduce value quickly.
  • Quantity and consistency. Matching sets of the same workstation or chair are easier to resell than a mix of one-offs.
  • Access. A dock and a freight elevator mean faster, cheaper removal than stairs and a passenger elevator.
  • Timing. A compressed deadline can raise labor cost if work has to happen in fewer, longer shifts.

Checklist before you request a walkthrough

  • Your lease end date and any landlord restoration requirements
  • Building rules for after-hours work, insurance and elevator use
  • A rough count of workstations, chairs, conference tables and storage
  • Which items, if any, are moving to a new office instead of being sold
  • Who owns IT equipment, and whether any of it is leased
  • Photos of representative furniture, if you have them

You do not need a perfect inventory to start. The walkthrough is where the real count happens.

Frequently asked questions

What is office furniture disposition?

Disposition is the process of deciding what happens to furniture you no longer need and then carrying it out. The usual paths are resale, donation, recycling and landfill. A good disposition plan picks the best path for each item and documents where everything went.

How is the net offer calculated?

The net offer is the resale value of your furniture minus the cost to remove it. If the number is positive, Office Station pays you. If removal costs more than the furniture is worth, the offer shows that too, so you can budget for it before you commit.

How fast can I get an offer?

Walkthroughs typically happen within 48 hours in Greater Phoenix and within 2 to 3 business days in other service markets. Written offers typically follow within 5 business days of the walkthrough.

Do you handle computers and other electronics?

Yes. E-waste is handled as part of the job, and you receive certificates of data destruction for devices that carried data. That keeps your IT and compliance teams covered along with facilities.

Which markets do you serve for disposition?

Liquidation and disposition service covers Phoenix, Tucson, Flagstaff, Las Vegas, Reno, San Diego, Orange County, Northern California (Bay Area and Sacramento), Denver, Salt Lake City and Albuquerque.
