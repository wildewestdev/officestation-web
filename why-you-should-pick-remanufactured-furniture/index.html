Why You Should Pick Remanufactured Office Furniture

Remanufactured furniture gives you a refreshed, consistent look at about 35 percent less than new, usually in 7 to 14 days. Here is how it compares and when it wins.

6 minute read

If you have priced office furniture, you have probably felt stuck between two options: cheap and tired, or new and expensive with a long wait. Remanufactured furniture is the middle path, and for a lot of offices it is the best one.

This guide explains what remanufactured means, how it compares on cost and timing, and how to tell whether it fits your space.

What "remanufactured" actually means

Remanufactured furniture starts as pre-owned commercial furniture, often from well-known manufacturers. Instead of being sold as recovered, it is refreshed so it looks and feels closer to new. Depending on the piece, that can mean new work surfaces, fresh panel fabric or updated finishes.

The underlying structure, such as steel frames and panel systems, was built for heavy commercial use. Refreshing the parts people see and touch gives that durable core a second life with a cleaner appearance.

Why remanufactured furniture is worth considering

Commercial furniture rarely wears out evenly. The frame, panels and hardware usually outlast the surfaces and fabric people see every day. A worn laminate top or faded panel fabric can make an otherwise solid workstation look tired, even though the structure still has years of use left.

Remanufacturing targets that gap. You pay to refresh the visible parts instead of paying for an entirely new build, which is where most of the savings come from. It also keeps usable furniture in service rather than sending it off as waste.

When remanufactured is the right choice

Remanufactured tends to be the best fit when several of these are true:

  • Clients or candidates visit your office. First impressions matter, and refreshed surfaces and fabric look the part.
  • You want every station to match. A consistent look across a whole floor is easier with refreshed furniture than with mixed as-recovered pieces.
  • You have about two weeks. A 7 to 14 day lead time works for most planned moves but not for next-day emergencies.
  • New is over budget. You want something close to a new look without paying new prices or waiting 4 to 6 weeks.
  • You plan to keep the furniture for years. Starting from a refreshed baseline makes sense for a long-term space.
Liquidation-grade still has its place. If you need people seated this week, or the space is a warehouse office, call center or back room where appearance matters less, liquidation-grade at $615 per station in as little as 72 hours may be the smarter spend.

The hybrid approach

You do not have to pick one tier for the whole office. A practical layout might use remanufactured stations along the reception side and in team areas clients walk past, then liquidation-grade stations in operations or support space.

That approach can put your budget where people actually see it. Free space planning helps map which zones get which tier before anything is ordered.

Questions to ask before you order

  1. What specifically was refreshed on these pieces: surfaces, fabric, finishes or something else?
  2. Which finish and fabric options are available for my order?
  3. Will every station in the order match?
  4. What is the expected lead time for my quantity?
  5. Does my station count reach the 20, 50 or 100 volume thresholds?
  6. What will electrical, delivery and installation add?

Frequently asked questions

What is remanufactured office furniture?

Remanufactured furniture is pre-owned commercial furniture that has been refreshed so it looks and performs closer to new. That can include items like new surfaces, fabric or finishes, depending on the piece. It differs from liquidation-grade furniture, which is sold largely as recovered.

How much does a remanufactured workstation cost?

A 6'x6' remanufactured workstation is $1,245, furniture only. That compares with $615 for liquidation-grade and $1,920 for new. Electrical, delivery and professional installation are priced separately.

How long does remanufactured furniture take?

Remanufactured workstations usually take 7 to 14 days. That is slower than in-stock liquidation-grade, which can be ready in as little as 72 hours, but much faster than new furniture at 4 to 6 weeks.

Does remanufactured furniture include a manufacturer warranty?

Generally no. Office Station is an independent reseller, not an authorized dealer, and manufacturer warranties generally do not transfer to pre-owned furniture, including refreshed pieces. Ask what was refreshed on your specific order.

Can I mix remanufactured and liquidation-grade furniture?

Yes, and many offices do. A common approach is remanufactured stations in client-facing or high-visibility areas and liquidation-grade in support areas. Free space planning can help you decide where each tier goes.
