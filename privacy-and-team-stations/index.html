Office Privacy Workstations and Team Stations

Privacy and collaboration are not opposites. Panel height, layout and station type let you give each team the right amount of both, at a price tier that fits your budget.

6 minute read

Every office has a tension between people who need quiet to concentrate and people who need to talk to get work done. Office privacy workstations solve one half of that. Team stations solve the other. The right plan uses both, and the main tool you have for tuning the balance is panel height.

This guide explains how panel heights change the way a station feels, the common layouts from private to open, and how to spend wisely across liquidation-grade, remanufactured and new tiers.

How panel height shapes privacy

Panel heights are usually described by what they block, not by an exact number, because heights vary by manufacturer and product line. Think in terms of sightlines for a seated and a standing person.

Panel heightWhat it blocksHow it feelsBest for
Low, around desk height or just aboveClutter on the work surfaceOpen and bright, easy eye contactTeam stations, project groups, short-stay seating
Mid, about seated eye levelSeated sightlines to neighborsPrivate while sitting, social when standingMost general office roles
High, above standing eye levelMost standing sightlinesEnclosed, fewer visual distractionsFocus work, confidential calls, finance and HR

Two practical notes. First, taller panels soften sound but do not stop it, so a loud team next to a quiet one still needs distance. Second, tall panels block light, so keep them away from windows when you can and run lower panels along the glass.

You can also mix heights within a single station. A common approach is a taller panel on the aisle side for privacy from foot traffic, with lower panels between teammates.

Layouts from most private to most open

Individual privacy workstations

Each person gets a fully enclosed L-shaped or U-shaped station with mid or high panels on all sides. This is the most private open-plan option, and it uses the most floor space and panel material per person.

Paired or back-to-back stations

Two stations share a spine panel. People face away from each other for focus but can turn around to talk. This is a good middle ground for pairs who work closely.

Clusters

Three to six stations are arranged around a shared center, often in a pinwheel. Mid-height panels between seats give some privacy while keeping the group connected.

Team stations and benching

Long shared work surfaces with low dividers or none at all. This uses the least space per person and makes collaboration effortless. It is the least private, so pair it with nearby phone rooms or quiet areas.

Zone before you pick furniture. Map which teams need focus and which need conversation before choosing stations. Put privacy workstations for focus-heavy roles away from high-traffic aisles, and place team stations near break areas and meeting rooms where noise is expected. Planning checklist

  • List each team and rate its need for focus versus conversation.
  • Note confidential roles that need higher panels or enclosed offices.
  • Mark windows, aisles, break areas and meeting rooms on your floor plan.
  • Decide where power and data will run before choosing panel types.
  • Pick a tier per zone based on budget and move-in date.
  • Plan phone rooms or quiet areas near open team stations.
  • Check whether a count of 20, 50 or 100 stations unlocks volume pricing.

Frequently asked questions

What panel height gives the most privacy?

Panels tall enough to block a seated person's sightline to neighbors give the most visual privacy, and full-height panels also block most standing sightlines. Taller panels also soften sound, but they do not make a station soundproof. They also cut daylight and make the floor feel more closed in, so use them where focus work matters most.

What is a team station?

A team station, sometimes called a benching or cluster layout, groups several work surfaces together with low or no dividers between them. It uses less floor space per person and makes quick conversation easy. It works best for teams that collaborate throughout the day.

Can I mix privacy workstations and team stations on one floor?

Yes, and most offices should. A common approach is to zone the floor, with higher-panel stations for focus-heavy roles and lower-panel team stations for collaborative groups. Free space planning from Office Station can help you map those zones.
