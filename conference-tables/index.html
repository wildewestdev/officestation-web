Used Conference Tables

Pre-owned conference tables that come out of corporate decommissions, graded for condition and matched to your room size. Tell us your room dimensions and seat count, and we will help you find a table that fits.

5 minute read

A conference table is often the largest single piece of furniture in an office and one of the first things visitors see. It is also a piece that holds up very well over time. A solid table from a corporate meeting room usually has years of life left when the company that bought it moves out. That is why used conference tables are one of the best values in pre-owned office furniture.

Office Station handles corporate office furniture liquidation and decommissioning across the Southwest, and we resell quality pieces that come out of those projects. Below you will find how to size a table, what the condition grades mean and what to check before you buy.

How to size a conference table

Start with the room, not the table. Measure wall to wall, then note doors, windows, columns, credenzas and any display wall. The two numbers that matter most are space per person and clearance around the table.

  • Space per person: plan on 24 to 30 inches of table edge for each seated person. Use 30 inches if people will spread out laptops and papers.
  • Clearance: leave about 42 to 48 inches from the table edge to the wall so chairs can pull out and someone can walk behind them. Tighter than 36 inches feels cramped.
  • Width: 36 to 42 inches works for smaller tables. Tables 10 feet and longer usually run 42 to 48 inches wide so people across from each other have room.

These are starting points. A boat-shaped table seats a few more people at the widest point and improves sight lines. A round table of 48 inches seats four; 60 inches seats five or six.

Measure the path, not just the room. Long tables are usually built in sections, but a single 8 foot top still has to turn corners. Check elevator depth, stairwell turns, hallway width and door openings before you commit, especially in older buildings.

Shapes, bases and tops

Shapes

  • Rectangular: the most seats per foot and the easiest to find in pre-owned inventory.
  • Boat or racetrack: wider in the middle so everyone can see each other and the screen.
  • Round and square: good for small huddle rooms of four to six.
  • Modular or flip-top: separate tables that join into one, useful for training rooms that change setup often.

Bases

Pedestal and panel bases look clean but can knock into knees if placed poorly. Leg and trestle bases give more legroom. On long tables, check where the bases sit so chairs at the ends are not blocked.

Tops

High-pressure laminate is the most durable and forgiving of scratches. Wood veneer looks warmer but shows wear and cannot be refinished forever. Glass and stone tops are heavy, so confirm delivery access and floor loading.

What to check before you buy

  1. Confirm the overall dimensions and whether the top ships in one piece or several.
  2. Look at photos of the top in raking light to see scratches, water rings and edge chips.
  3. Check that all connecting hardware, brackets and leveling glides are included.
  4. Ask whether the power and data module works and what outlet and port types it has.
  5. Confirm cable management parts such as grommet lids and wire troughs.
  6. Match the finish to any credenza or chairs you plan to reuse.
  7. Confirm delivery access and who will assemble a multi-section table.

Pair the table with the right chairs

Conference chairs see less daily use than task chairs, so pre-owned models are a strong fit. Choose chairs with a narrow enough footprint to fit your seat count, and consider whether you want swivel and tilt for long meetings or simpler side chairs for short ones. Frequently asked questions

Where do your used conference tables come from?

Pre-owned tables come out of corporate office decommissions and liquidations, where companies are closing, downsizing or relocating. Each table carries a condition grade of A, B or C. Availability changes as projects come in, so ask about current sizes and finishes.

Are used conference tables covered by a manufacturer warranty?

Generally no. Office Station is an independent reseller, not an authorized dealer for any manufacturer, and manufacturer warranties generally do not transfer to pre-owned furniture. That is one reason the condition grade and a close look at photos or the table itself matter before you buy.

How big a room do I need for a conference table?

A practical rule is to add about 8 feet to both the length and the width of the table. That leaves roughly 4 feet on every side for chairs to pull out and people to walk behind them. A 10 by 4 foot table, for example, fits comfortably in a room around 18 by 12 feet.

Do used conference tables still have working power and data?

Many do, but you should confirm it. Ask whether the power module, grommets and cable troughs are included, whether all outlets work, and whether the module uses a plug type compatible with your building. Older data ports may need a newer insert.

Can you help me lay out the conference room?

Yes. Space planning is free. Send your room dimensions, door and window locations and how many people you need to seat, and we will suggest table sizes and a layout that leaves proper clearance.
