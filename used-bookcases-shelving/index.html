Used Office Bookcases and Shelving

Pre-owned bookcases and shelving, graded by condition and delivered on the same truck as your desks, chairs or workstations. Here is how to pick storage that stays straight, stable and useful for years.

5 minute read

Bookcases and shelving rarely get much attention when an office is being furnished, yet they shape how a room works every day. A good bookcase holds binders, reference manuals, samples and files without bowing, wobbling or crowding the walkway. Office bookcases also tend to see light use, which makes them a smart place to save money by buying pre-owned.

Office Station sells pre-owned office furniture as an independent reseller. Bookcases and shelving are graded by condition and can be delivered with the rest of your order, so your storage arrives on the same day as your desks and chairs instead of on a separate schedule.

Types of used office bookcases and shelving

Office storage comes in a few distinct styles. Knowing which one you need makes it easier to compare options when you ask about availability.

  • Laminate bookcases. The most common office style. Durable surfaces, clean edges and adjustable shelves. Good for private offices and team areas with moderate loads.
  • Wood veneer bookcases. A warmer, more formal look suited to executive offices, law libraries and reception areas. Veneer shows scratches more than laminate, so the condition grade matters.
  • Steel bookcases and shelving. Heavier duty and very tolerant of weight. A strong choice for supply rooms, file rooms and anywhere shelves will hold dense paper.
  • Open shelving units and towers. Often part of a desking or casegoods family, used to divide space or add storage beside a workstation.
  • Low bookcases and credenza-height units. Waist-high storage that doubles as a surface for printers or a spot to lean during a quick conversation.

Pre-owned inventory changes as furniture comes available, so matching finishes and exact heights is not always possible on the first try. If a consistent look matters, tell us the finish and dimensions you are after and we will let you know what is realistic.

What condition grades mean for bookcases

Every pre-owned piece is assigned a grade so you know what you are getting before it arrives. For storage, the grade mostly describes the surfaces. Structure should be sound at every grade.

GradeDefinitionWhat you might see on a bookcaseBest fit
ALike newClean tops and sides, crisp edges, little or no visible wearExecutive offices, client-facing rooms
BVery goodLight marks on shelves or edges that you notice up closePrivate offices, team areas
CGoodVisible wear such as edge scuffs, shelf rub marks or small surface blemishesSupply rooms, file rooms, back-of-house storage

A practical approach is to spend on grade A or B where people will see the furniture and save with grade C where storage is purely functional.

What to check before you buy

Whether you inspect a piece in person or review photos, run through this list.

  1. Shelf sag. Look along each shelf edge. A visible bow means the shelf has carried too much weight over too long a span, and it will not straighten out.
  2. Shelf pin holes. Check that the holes are clean and not stripped or blown out. Damaged holes let shelves tilt or drop.
  3. Shelf and pin count. Confirm how many adjustable shelves come with the unit and that every shelf has four pins.
  4. Back panel. The back keeps the case square. It should be fully attached with no loose corners or cracks.
  5. Square and stable. The case should sit flat without rocking. Adjustable levelers help on uneven floors.
  6. Height and depth. Measure the wall, and check for outlets, thermostats, light switches and door swings that a tall unit could block.
  7. Anchor hardware. Tall bookcases should be secured to the wall. Ask whether anchor brackets are included.
Anchor anything tall. A loaded bookcase that tips can cause serious injury. Secure tall units to the wall with appropriate hardware, load heavy items on the lowest shelves, and never let anyone climb shelves to reach the top.

Matching shelving to what it will hold

The biggest mistake with office shelving is putting the wrong load on the wrong shelf. A wide laminate shelf that looks fine holding a few books can bow within months under a row of full binders. Paper is heavier than it looks.

  • Binders and dense paper. Choose steel shelving or shorter shelf spans, and keep the heaviest items low.
  • Books and reference material. Laminate or veneer bookcases work well when shelves are not overloaded. Leave some room on each shelf.
  • Display and decor. Almost any style works. This is where grade A veneer earns its price.
  • Supplies and boxed stock. Steel units are forgiving and easy to wipe clean, and grade C is usually all you need.

If you are not sure what a room will need, space planning is free. Share your floor plan and how each area will be used, and we can suggest storage that fits alongside your workstations and walkways.

Frequently asked questions

Can bookcases ship with the rest of my furniture order?

Yes. Bookcases and shelving can be added to an order for workstations, desks, chairs or tables so everything arrives together. In Greater Phoenix, delivery is $195 for central zones and $295 for the wider metro. Outside Greater Phoenix, freight is quoted per project.

What do the condition grades mean for a bookcase?

Grade A is like new, with little or no visible wear. Grade B is very good, with light marks you notice up close. Grade C is good, meaning fully usable with visible wear such as edge scuffs or small surface marks. Every grade should stand square with shelves that sit level.

How do I keep a shelf from sagging?

Match the load to the shelf. Long, thin laminate shelves bow under books and binders over time, so keep heavy items on shorter spans or lower shelves. Steel shelving and thicker or center-supported shelves handle weight better. Check existing shelves for a bow before you buy.

Do used bookcases come with shelf pins and hardware?

They should, but always confirm. Ask how many adjustable shelves and shelf pins are included, and whether wall anchor hardware is part of the piece. Missing pins are inexpensive to replace but annoying to discover on move-in day.

Is a manufacturer warranty included?

Generally no. Office Station is an independent reseller, not an authorized dealer, and manufacturer warranties generally do not transfer to pre-owned furniture. That is why we grade condition and encourage you to ask for details on any piece.
