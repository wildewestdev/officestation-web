How Pre-Owned Office Furniture Is Refurbished

Pre-owned furniture is not one thing. It moves through a clear path from a decommissioned office to your floor, and the tier and grade you choose decide how much work went into it and what you pay.

6 minute read

If you are pricing furniture for an office, you will see three labels over and over: liquidation-grade, remanufactured and new. On top of that, pre-owned pieces carry a condition grade. Knowing how pre-owned office furniture is refurbished, and what each label actually promises, is the fastest way to stop comparing apples to oranges.

This guide walks through where pre-owned furniture comes from, the typical refurbishing steps, what the A, B and C grades mean, and how to pick a tier for each area of your space.

Where pre-owned furniture comes from

Most quality pre-owned office furniture starts in a corporate office that is closing, downsizing or moving. When a company decommissions a floor, the workstations, desks, chairs and storage have to go somewhere. Office Station handles that side of the business too, through office furniture liquidation, where a company receives a written net offer based on the resale value of the furniture minus the removal cost.

That matters to you as a buyer. Furniture removed with an itemized inventory keeps its parts together. A workstation that leaves a building with its panels, connectors, work surfaces and brackets accounted for is far easier to rebuild than a pile of mixed components.

The typical refurbishing steps

Every piece is different, but the path from removal to resale usually follows the same sequence. The further along the path a piece travels, the higher its tier.

  1. Inventory and sorting. Pieces are counted, matched by manufacturer and product line, and grouped so compatible components stay together.
  2. Inspection. Frames, mechanisms, drawer slides, locks, casters and height adjustment are checked. Anything structurally unsound is pulled.
  3. Cleaning. Surfaces, fabric and upholstery are cleaned. For many liquidation-grade pieces, this is the main step before sale.
  4. Parts replacement. Remanufactured pieces get worn or missing components replaced, such as trim, connectors, keys, glides or damaged work surfaces.
  5. Surface refresh. Remanufactured workstations may receive new panel fabric or refinished surfaces so a whole run of stations looks consistent.
  6. Grading. Each piece is assigned a condition grade so you know what to expect before it arrives.

What the A, B and C grades mean

Tier and grade are two different questions. The tier describes the work done. The grade describes how the piece looks and feels when it is ready to ship.

  • Grade A, like new. Little or no visible wear. A good fit for reception, conference rooms and executive offices.
  • Grade B, very good. Light signs of use, such as faint scuffs, that do not affect function. The practical choice for most open-plan areas.
  • Grade C, good. More visible cosmetic wear, with sound structure and working mechanisms. Well suited to training rooms, call centers, storage and overflow space.

How to choose a tier for each area

You do not have to buy one tier for the whole office. Mixing tiers is often the smartest way to stretch a budget.

  • Client-facing spaces. Consider remanufactured or new, or Grade A pre-owned, where first impressions count.
  • Main open plan. Remanufactured stations give you a matched look across long runs at roughly 35% below new.
  • Back office and overflow. Liquidation-grade in Grade B or C keeps cost low and can be ready in days.
  • Tight move-in dates. If you need to be seated within a week, in-stock liquidation-grade is usually the only tier that fits.

Scale also changes the math. Volume pricing takes 5% off at 20 or more stations, 8% at 50 or more and 12% at 100 or more. For example, 20 remanufactured 6'x6' stations at $1,245 each come to $24,900 before the 5% discount, or $23,655 after it.

What to check before you buy

Use this checklist when you compare pre-owned quotes from any source.

  • Is the tier stated clearly, and does the price match the work described?
  • Is a condition grade listed for each item?
  • Are all components included, such as connectors, power infeeds if needed, keys and mounting hardware?
  • Does the price include delivery, installation and electrical, or are those extra?
  • What is the realistic lead time for the quantity you need?
  • If you are buying chairs, has the mechanism been tested through its full range? See pre-owned office chairs for options.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between liquidation-grade and remanufactured furniture?

Liquidation-grade furniture is sold close to the condition it came out of the previous office, after inspection, cleaning and sorting. Remanufactured furniture goes further, with worn or damaged parts replaced and surfaces such as panel fabric or work surfaces refreshed. That extra work is why a 6'x6' workstation is $615 liquidation-grade and $1,245 remanufactured at Office Station.

What do the A, B and C condition grades mean?

Grade A is like new, with little or no visible wear. Grade B is very good, with light signs of use that do not affect function. Grade C is good, with more visible cosmetic wear but sound structure and working mechanisms.

Does the manufacturer warranty transfer on pre-owned furniture?

Generally, no. Office Station sells pre-owned designer furniture as an independent reseller, not an authorized dealer, and manufacturer warranties generally do not transfer to pre-owned pieces. Ask about the condition and grade of any specific item before you buy.

How long does each tier take to arrive?

Liquidation-grade stations in stock can be ready in as little as 72 hours. Remanufactured stations usually take 7 to 14 days because of the added work. New stations take 4 to 6 weeks.

Is pre-owned furniture a good choice for a whole office?

For many offices it is, especially when budget or timeline is tight. Many teams mix tiers, using remanufactured or new pieces in client-facing areas and liquidation-grade stations in back-office or overflow space.
