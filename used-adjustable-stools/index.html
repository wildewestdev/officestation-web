Used Adjustable Stools

Counter-height work needs a seat built for it. Pre-owned drafting and lab stools give your team proper height range and support at a lower price, with condition graded before you buy.

6 minute read

A standard office chair does not work at a standing-height desk, a drafting table or a lab bench. The seat bottoms out too low, feet dangle, and people end up perched on the edge. Used adjustable stools fix that at a fraction of what new seating costs, and pre-owned pieces that are already in stock can be ready far sooner than a factory order.

This page covers the main stool types, how to size them, what each condition grade means for stools specifically, and what to inspect before you buy.

How to size a stool to your work surface

Most stool returns and complaints come down to height. Follow these steps before you choose one:

  1. Measure the work surface height from the floor to the top of the desk, counter or bench.
  2. Subtract about 10 to 12 inches to get a comfortable starting seat height. Adjust for your team: taller people often prefer a bit more room.
  3. Check the stool's full range. Your target should sit near the middle of the range so each user can go up or down.
  4. Confirm a foot ring. At counter and standing heights, feet need a place to rest. Without one, circulation and posture suffer.
  5. Check for obstructions. Drawers, keyboard trays and under-counter storage can block knees at raised seat heights.
Casters or glides? Casters make it easy to roll between stations, but a tall stool on casters can slide on hard floors when someone climbs on. For labs, tile, sealed concrete or anyone getting on and off often, fixed glides are usually the safer choice. Tell us your floor type and we will point you to the right base.

What condition grades mean for stools

Every pre-owned piece we sell is graded A, B or C. On stools, the grade tells you mostly about the seat, the base and the cylinder.

  • Grade A, like new. Upholstery and base show minimal to no visible wear. A good fit for client-facing counters and showroom-style spaces.
  • Grade B, very good. Light signs of use such as minor scuffs on the base or foot ring. Fully functional and a strong everyday choice.
  • Grade C, good. Visible wear consistent with prior use, but structurally sound and working. The value pick for labs, workshops and training rooms where stools take a beating anyway.

Grade by room, not by order. You can put Grade A stools at a front counter and Grade C stools in a back-room lab without paying Grade A prices across the board.

Inspection checklist for used adjustable stools

Whether you inspect in person or review photos, check these points:

  • The cylinder raises and lowers smoothly and holds height with weight on the seat.
  • The stool does not slowly sink during a few minutes of sitting.
  • The foot ring is tight, adjusts if designed to, and does not slip under a standing load.
  • The base has no cracks, and all casters or glides are present and matching.
  • The seat and back controls lock and release as intended.
  • Upholstery has no tears at the front edge, where wear shows first on stools.
  • For labs and clinics, the seat surface is non-porous and can handle your cleaning routine.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between a drafting stool and a lab stool?

A drafting stool is usually an upholstered task chair with a tall cylinder and a foot ring, built for long sessions at a raised work surface. A lab stool is typically simpler, often backless or low-backed, with a wipeable seat that stands up to spills and cleaning products. Both adjust in height, but they are built for different environments.

How do I know what stool height I need?

Measure from the floor to the top of the work surface. A common starting point is to leave roughly 10 to 12 inches between the seat and the underside of your forearms at the surface. Choose a stool whose adjustment range puts that target height near the middle, not the very top, of its range.

Can I order stools with workstations or benching?

Yes. Many offices add stools for standing-height desks, collaboration counters or a lab bench at the same time they order workstations. Combining items in one order means one delivery and one install schedule, and larger workstation orders qualify for volume pricing.
