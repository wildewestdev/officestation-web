How to Choose the Right Office Chair

The right office chair is the one that fits your body and your work, not the one with the most levers. Use this fit checklist to test any chair, new or pre-owned.

7 minute read

Shopping for an office chair usually starts with brand names and price. It should start with your body. A premium chair set up wrong can be less comfortable than a basic chair set up right, and a chair that cannot adjust to your size will never feel right no matter what it cost.

This guide gives you a fit checklist you can use on any chair in a few minutes, explains what each adjustment actually does, and shows how to judge graded pre-owned chairs from major brands.

Before you test: know your work

Answer three questions first. They narrow the field quickly.

  • How many hours a day will you sit? Under two hours, a simple chair can work. For a full workday, adjustability matters much more.
  • What do you do in the chair? Heavy typing favors an upright posture and supportive arms. Phone calls and reading favor a chair that reclines comfortably.
  • Who else will use it? A shared seat at a hot desk or a training room needs to adjust fast for many body sizes.

The fit checklist

Take this list with you. Work through it in order, because each step depends on the one before it.

  1. Seat height. Raise or lower the seat until your feet rest flat on the floor and your thighs are roughly level, with knees about even with or slightly below your hips. If your desk is fixed and too high, you may need a footrest.
  2. Seat depth. Sit all the way back. Leave about two to four fingers of space between the seat edge and the back of your knees. A seat slider solves this for most people.
  3. Seat width. You should sit comfortably without your hips pressing against the sides or the arm supports.
  4. Lumbar support. The backrest should meet the natural inward curve of your lower back. Adjust height or depth until it feels supportive, not pushy.
  5. Backrest height. For long days, the backrest should support at least up to your shoulder blades.
  6. Recline and tension. Lean back. The chair should follow you smoothly and hold you without pushing you forward or letting you drop. Set tension to your weight.
  7. Recline lock. Check whether you can lock upright for focused typing or leave it free to rock.
  8. Arms. With shoulders relaxed and elbows near your sides, arms should support your forearms lightly. They should lower or move out of the way enough to let you pull close to the desk.
  9. Casters and base. Roll on your actual floor type. Carpet and hard floors can call for different casters.
  10. The ten-minute test. Sit and work, or pretend to, for ten minutes. Pressure points show up after a few minutes, not seconds.
Most chairs are never adjusted. A capable chair set to the last person's settings is not doing its job. After you buy, spend five minutes walking each person through the checklist above. It costs nothing and it is where most of the comfort comes from.

Mesh or upholstered?

Mesh backs and seats breathe, which helps in warm rooms and during Southwest summers if your office runs warm. Mesh also flexes with your body. Upholstered foam seats feel softer at first and many people find them more comfortable over long stretches. Quality varies within both types, so trust the ten-minute test over the material label.

How to judge a pre-owned chair

Commercial chairs from brands like Herman Miller, Steelcase, Knoll, Haworth, Humanscale, Teknion and Allsteel are engineered for years of daily use, which is why a graded pre-owned chair can be a better value than a new consumer chair. When you inspect a pre-owned chair, add these checks to the fit checklist:

  • Cycle every lever and knob. Each should engage and release cleanly.
  • Raise the seat, then sit down hard. It should hold height without sinking.
  • Look for tears or sagging in mesh and flattening in foam seats.
  • Check arm pads for cracks, since they get the most contact.
  • Roll the chair to confirm all casters spin freely.

Manufacturer warranties generally do not transfer to pre-owned chairs, so ask about the specific chair and its grade before you buy.

Frequently asked questions

What is the most important feature in an office chair?

Fit comes first. Seat height and seat depth have to match your body, or no other feature will help. After that, adjustable lumbar support and arms that let your shoulders relax make the biggest difference for most people.

Is a pre-owned designer chair better than a new budget chair?

Often, yes. Chairs from major commercial brands are built for heavy daily use and many adjust in more ways than budget chairs. Check the condition grade and test every mechanism, and keep in mind manufacturer warranties generally do not transfer to pre-owned chairs.

Should I choose mesh or upholstered?

Mesh breathes and suits warm rooms or people who run hot. Upholstered foam seats feel more cushioned and some people prefer them for long sitting. Both can be excellent, so choose based on comfort and climate rather than looks.

How do I know if the seat depth is right?

Sit all the way back against the backrest. You should be able to fit roughly two to four fingers between the front edge of the seat and the back of your knees. If the edge presses into your knees, the seat is too deep or needs to slide back.

Can I buy the same chair for my whole team?

You can, but people come in very different sizes. If you standardize, pick a model with a wide adjustment range, including seat depth, and consider a size option for your tallest and shortest team members.
