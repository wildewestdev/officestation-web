Conference Room Furniture: How to Size It and What It Costs

The right conference table leaves room to walk behind every chair and gives each person enough edge to work. Start with the measurements, then choose pre-owned designer furniture by condition grade.

6 minute read

Conference room furniture is usually the most visible furniture in an office. Clients see it, leadership meets around it and remote attendees stare at it on camera. It is also easy to get wrong. A table that is too big turns the room into a squeeze, and chairs that do not match the table height make every meeting uncomfortable. This guide walks through sizing first, then how to compare pre-owned designer options by condition grade.

Step 1: Measure the room and work backward to the table

The table should fit the room, not the other way around. People need space to pull a chair back and walk behind someone who is seated.

  • Ideal clearance: about 4 feet from the table edge to the wall on every side where people sit.
  • Practical minimum: about 3.5 feet in smaller rooms, knowing people will need to shift to let others pass.
  • Credenza or wall storage: add its depth before measuring clearance.
  • Display wall: leave enough distance from the screen that the nearest seat is not craning upward.

A quick rule: subtract 8 feet from the room length and 8 feet from the room width. The result is the largest table that keeps about 4 feet of clearance all the way around.

Step 2: Match table size to seat count

Plan on about 30 inches of table edge per person for working meetings with laptops. Tables with a screen at one end lose those end seats.

Step 3: Choose a shape for how the room is used

Rectangular

The most efficient use of a narrow room and the easiest shape to find pre-owned. Works well for a clear head of the table.

Boat-shaped or racetrack

Wider in the middle and tapered at the ends, so people along the sides can see each other and the screen. A good choice for video-heavy rooms.

Round or square

Best for small huddle rooms of four to six. Nobody sits at the head, which suits collaborative conversations.

Modular or flip-top

Separate tables that join into one surface. They cost flexibility in looks but let a room convert into a workshop or classroom. If that is the main use, see our training room furniture page.

Step 4: Plan for power and video

Check whether the table needs a power and data box in the top and whether a floor outlet sits under the planned table location. A beautiful pre-owned boardroom table with no cutout may need a surface power module instead. Any new floor outlets or hardwired power is electrical work and is priced separately from the furniture. For hybrid meetings, keep the far end of the table clear of tall chairs that block the camera view.

Measure the elevator and doors, too. A one-piece 16-foot boardroom table may not fit the freight elevator or turn the corner into the room. Multi-section tables avoid that problem, and it is far easier to confirm before delivery day than after.

Step 5: Pick conference chairs

Conference chairs sit for long stretches, so comfort matters as much as looks. Look for a tilt mechanism, height adjustment and casters that suit the flooring. Arms should clear the table apron when pushed in. Mesh backs stay cooler in long meetings, while upholstered backs look more formal. What to check before you buy

  1. Tabletop finish under raking light for scratches, rings and edge chips
  2. Seams between table sections line up flat
  3. Power module works and grommet covers are present
  4. Every chair in the set matches in model, fabric and arm style
  5. Chair tilt and height hold under weight
  6. Casters roll freely and none are missing

Frequently asked questions

How big a conference table fits my room?

Subtract about 8 feet from the room length and 8 feet from the room width. That leaves roughly 4 feet of clearance on every side for chairs and walking. In tight rooms, 3.5 feet per side is a practical minimum.

How much table edge does each person need?

Plan on about 30 inches of edge per person for comfortable meetings with laptops. You can squeeze to about 24 inches for short meetings, but chairs with arms will bump. Do not count the table ends if a screen sits at one end.

What do condition grades A, B and C mean?

Grade A is like new, B is very good and C is good. The grade describes cosmetic wear such as surface marks or fabric wear. Higher grades cost more, so many buyers put A in client-facing rooms and B or C in internal meeting rooms.

Do pre-owned designer conference chairs come with a warranty?

Office Station is an independent reseller, not an authorized dealer, so manufacturer warranties generally do not transfer to pre-owned furniture. Inspect the mechanism, casters and upholstery, and ask about the condition grade before buying.

Can I sell our current conference furniture?

Yes. Large boardroom tables and matched sets of designer chairs often have resale value. Office Station sends a written net offer, which is resale value minus removal cost, and a positive number means you are paid.
