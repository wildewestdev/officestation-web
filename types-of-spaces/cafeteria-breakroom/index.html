Cafeteria and Breakroom Furniture

A breakroom works when there is a seat for the lunch rush and a mix of tables that suits how people actually eat, talk and meet. Plan the seating, choose pre-owned or new and know your delivery and install costs up front.

5 minute read

The breakroom is the one room nearly everyone in the office uses. It is where people eat, refill coffee, take a quick call and run into colleagues from other teams. That makes breakroom furniture more important than its budget line suggests. Get the seat count or table mix wrong and the room either sits empty or overflows every day at noon.

Step 1: Count seats for the lunch rush

Do not seat the cafeteria for your full headcount. Seat it for the peak.

  1. Find your busiest in-office day. With hybrid schedules, this may be well below headcount.
  2. Estimate the share of people who eat onsite at the same time. Staggered lunches, field staff and shift work all lower this.
  3. Add a small buffer for guests, catered meetings and team lunches.

Then check that number against the room. A common planning range for cafe-style seating is about 15 to 20 square feet per seat, including chair pull-out and walkways.

Step 2: Mix table types on purpose

A room full of identical four-tops looks tidy on paper and works poorly in practice. Two people end up at a table for four while a group of six has nowhere to sit together. A mix serves more situations.

Two-tops and four-tops

The workhorses. Square two-tops can be pushed together for larger groups, which gives you flexibility without extra furniture.

Communal and high-top tables

A long table at counter or bar height encourages people to sit with colleagues they do not know, and it doubles as a standing spot for a quick coffee. Bar stools need footrests and enough knee clearance.

Lounge seating and booths

Soft seating turns the breakroom into an informal meeting space between lunch hours. It takes more floor per seat, so use it in corners or near windows rather than in the main traffic path.

Step 3: Choose surfaces that clean easily

Breakroom furniture sees spills, heat and constant wiping. Prioritize:

  • Laminate or solid-surface tabletops over veneer
  • Plastic, metal or wood chair shells, or seats with wipeable upholstery
  • Table bases that level on uneven floors
  • Stackable chairs if the room also hosts events
  • Chair glides that will not scratch the flooring

Step 4: Keep traffic moving around food and coffee

The busiest spots in any breakroom are the coffee station, the refrigerator and the microwaves. Keep seating out of the path between them and the door. Leave a clear queue zone in front of microwaves so people waiting do not stand beside someone eating. If the room has a sink and counter run, a counter-height bar on the opposite wall gives people a quick perch without blocking the kitchen side.

Pre-owned vs new breakroom furniture

Breakroom seating is well suited to pre-owned. Cafe chairs and tables are simple, durable pieces, and a good-condition set looks just like new once the room is in use. Office Station sells pre-owned furniture graded by condition:

Office Station is an independent reseller, not an authorized dealer, so manufacturer warranties generally do not transfer to pre-owned pieces. New furniture typically takes 4 to 6 weeks. Pre-owned in-stock items move much faster, with liquidation-grade pieces shipping in as little as 72 hours when available.

Check the full set before you commit. Breakroom sets come from real offices, so chair counts and finishes can vary between batches. Confirm the exact quantity of matching chairs and tables available, and consider buying a few spares so one broken chair does not leave a gap in the set.

Frequently asked questions

How many seats should a breakroom have?

Plan for the busiest lunch period, not total headcount. Count how many people are in the building on a peak day and estimate what share eat onsite at the same time. Staggered shifts and hybrid schedules usually bring the number well below headcount.

How much space does each breakroom seat need?

A common planning range is about 15 to 20 square feet per seat for cafe-style seating, including space to pull out chairs and walk between tables. Lounge seating and booths need more. A space plan confirms the real count for your room.

What does delivery cost?

In Greater Phoenix, delivery is $195 for the central zone and $295 for the wider metro. Everywhere else in the country, freight is quoted per project. Prices exclude electrical work.

Is pre-owned breakroom furniture hygienic?

Look for hard, wipeable surfaces such as laminate or solid tabletops and plastic, metal or wood chair shells. Upholstered pieces should be inspected closely. Condition grades of A, B and C describe cosmetic condition so you know what to expect.

Can you remove our old breakroom furniture?

Yes. Office Station can include it in a walkthrough and a written net offer, which is resale value minus removal cost. You get an itemized inventory, and the space is handed back broom-clean and photo-documented.
