Office Personal Storage Lockers for Hybrid Teams

When people stop owning a desk, they still need somewhere to put their things. Personal storage lockers solve that, and sourcing them pre-owned keeps the cost in check.

6 minute read

Hybrid work changed the math on office furniture. If a team shares desks, the pedestal under each desk stops being personal. People start leaving laptops, headphones and coats on chairs, or carrying everything around all day. Personal storage lockers give each person a secure spot and let desks stay clear for the next person.

This page walks through how to size a locker program, which locker styles fit which offices, and how to keep the cost down by sourcing pre-owned where you can.

Why lockers matter in a hoteling office

A clean-desk policy only works if people have somewhere to put their things. Without lockers, shared desks turn into claimed desks. A good locker setup does three jobs:

  • Protects belongings so employees feel comfortable leaving items at work.
  • Frees up desks so any open seat is genuinely available.
  • Replaces bulky storage like pedestals and overhead bins, which can open floor space for collaboration.

For many offices, removing individual pedestals and adding a locker bank is the most visible sign that the space now supports hybrid work.

How to size a locker program

  1. Find your peak attendance. Use badge data, desk bookings or a simple head count on your busiest days for a few weeks.
  2. Decide on assigned or day-use. Assigned lockers need roughly one per regular in-office employee. Day-use lockers can be sized to peak attendance plus a buffer.
  3. Choose the compartment size. Measure a typical bag and laptop. Many offices find a compartment that fits a backpack and a laptop sleeve covers most needs.
  4. Place lockers near where people work. A single bank by the entry is convenient on arrival, but neighborhood banks cut walking during the day.
  5. Plan for accessibility. Keep some compartments at a reachable height and leave clear floor space in front of every bank.
Pilot before you buy everything. Put in one bank for a single team and watch how it gets used for a month. You will learn whether people want assigned lockers, what size they fill, and where the bank should go. Then order the rest with real data.

Lock and access options

How people open lockers affects cost, maintenance and daily frustration.

  • Keyed locks: lowest cost and easy to understand. Plan for lost keys and a master key process.
  • Built-in combination locks: no keys to lose. Someone needs to own resets when codes are forgotten.
  • Padlock hasps: employees bring their own lock. Cheap, but inconsistent and harder to clear for day use.
  • Electronic locks: PIN, badge or app access. Best for day-use programs, but more expensive and dependent on batteries or power.

When buying pre-owned, confirm the lock type and whether keys, codes or master access come with the lockers. Replacing locks can change the economics.

Sourcing lockers pre-owned

We source pre-owned furniture where possible, and lockers are no exception. Availability depends on what comes out of current decommissioning and liquidation projects, so matching quantities and finishes can take some flexibility. Every pre-owned item carries a condition grade: A like new, B very good, C good.

What to check on a used locker:

  • Doors close flush and latch without force.
  • Hinges are tight and not sagging.
  • Locks work, with keys or codes available.
  • Interiors are clean, with shelves and hooks present.
  • Vent slots and corners are free of rust or dents that affect function.
  • Anchoring hardware is included or easy to add.

Frequently asked questions

How many lockers does a hybrid office need?

Start with the number of people who will be in the office on your busiest day, not your total headcount. Add a buffer for visitors and growth. If employees want to leave items overnight, you may need closer to one locker per regular in-office employee.

Should lockers be assigned or first come, first served?

Assigned lockers suit people who come in often and store items between visits. Day-use lockers suit teams that bring what they need and take it home. Many offices use a mix, with a block of assigned lockers and a block of day-use units.

Can I buy office lockers pre-owned?

Often, yes. Office Station sources pre-owned furniture where possible, and availability depends on what comes out of current projects. When the quantity or lock type you need is not available used, we can talk through other options.

What lock type is best for office lockers?

It depends on how you manage access. Keyed locks are simple but keys go missing. Combination locks remove keys but need a reset process. Electronic locks add flexibility for day use and can be managed centrally, though they cost more and need power or batteries.

Do warranties transfer on pre-owned lockers?

Generally no. Office Station is an independent reseller, not an authorized dealer, and manufacturer warranties usually do not transfer to pre-owned furniture. Condition grades and a careful inspection help you buy with confidence.
