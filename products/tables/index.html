Used Office Tables for Conference, Training and Breakrooms

Commercial tables are built to be moved, reconfigured and reused, which makes them one of the smartest categories to buy pre-owned. This guide walks through sizing, table types, condition grades and delivery.

6 minute read

A table is rarely the most exciting purchase in an office build-out, but it is often the most used. Conference tables host every client meeting, training tables get rearranged every week, and breakroom tables see coffee, lunch and all-hands gatherings. Buying used office tables lets you put commercial-grade surfaces in every room for far less than new, and many table styles show their age much less than seating does.

Below, you will find how to size tables for each room, the tradeoffs between common styles, what condition grades mean for a work surface, and how delivery works.

Choosing tables by room

Conference and boardroom tables

Conference tables set the tone for client meetings, so this is where condition matters most. Rectangular and boat-shaped tables seat more people and give everyone a better sightline to a display at one end. Round and square tables work better for small huddle rooms where conversation matters more than presentation. Larger tables usually arrive in sections, which helps with doors and elevators.

Training and flip-top tables

Training rooms change layout constantly: classroom rows one day, U-shape the next, cleared out for a company event on Friday. Flip-top tables fold and nest against a wall, and casters make them easy to move. Shallow depths, often 24 inches, let you fit more rows in a room.

Breakroom and café tables

Breakroom tables need surfaces that tolerate spills and cleaning products. Round tables in the 36 to 42 inch range seat two to four people, while bar-height tables give a more casual feel and take up less visual space. Pedestal bases free up legroom compared with four-leg designs.

Collaborative and project tables

Standing-height and bench-style tables work well in open collaboration zones. They pair with stools or simply give teams a spot to spread out plans.

Measure the path, not just the room. A table that fits the conference room still has to get there. Measure door widths, hallway turns and the elevator interior, and note any loading dock or freight elevator rules for your building. Share those details when you ask for a quote.

Condition grades for tables

Work surfaces show wear differently from chairs or cabinets, so it helps to know what each grade looks like on a table.

  • Grade A, like new. Clean tops and edges with minimal to no visible wear. The right choice for boardrooms and client-facing meeting rooms.
  • Grade B, very good. Light wear such as faint surface marks or minor edge scuffs. Great for internal meeting rooms and training spaces.
  • Grade C, good. Visible wear on tops or bases but structurally sound. A practical choice for breakrooms, warehouses and project rooms.

Laminate tops hide wear well and suit training and breakroom use. Wood veneer looks premium but shows scratches and water rings more easily, so grade matters more there.

Inspection checklist for used tables

  1. Stability. Push on a corner. The table should not rack or wobble, and levelers should adjust.
  2. Top surface. Look for delamination at the edges, deep scratches, burns and water damage.
  3. Edge banding. Loose or chipped banding catches clothing and gets worse over time.
  4. Base and hardware. Check welds, bolts and the attachment between top and base.
  5. Mechanisms. On flip-top and folding tables, test the release and lock several times.
  6. Casters. Casters should roll and lock. Missing brakes are a safety issue.
  7. Power modules and grommets. Confirm what is included and whether it works.
  8. Matching sets. For training rooms, confirm that tables share the same height, finish and connector hardware.

Frequently asked questions

What size conference table do I need?

A common rule of thumb is about 30 inches of table edge per seated person, plus at least 42 to 48 inches of clearance between the table edge and the wall so chairs can pull out. Measure the room first, then subtract clearance on every side to find the largest table that fits. Free space planning can confirm the fit before you buy.

Are used training tables worth it?

Usually yes. Training tables are designed for frequent folding, flipping and rearranging, so a well-maintained used table still has plenty of life. Check the flip or fold mechanism, casters and any modesty panel before you buy.

How much is delivery for used tables in Phoenix?

Delivery in Greater Phoenix is $195 for central zones and $295 for the wider metro. Outside Greater Phoenix, freight is quoted per project based on the size of the order and site access.

Can a large conference table fit through my door or elevator?

Most large boardroom tables ship in sections or with a removable top and base, but you should still measure doors, hallway turns and elevator interiors. Tell us about any tight spots when you request a quote so we can plan the delivery.

Do used tables include power and data modules?

Some do and some do not. Built-in power modules and grommets vary widely by table and era, so confirm what is included and whether the module still works. Power and data wiring to the building is not included in furniture pricing.
