Modular Office Desks

Modular office desks let you build, grow and reconfigure a floor from the same set of parts. Compare the three tiers, see real per-station pricing, and plan installation before you order.

6 minute read

A modular office desk is built from interchangeable parts: work surfaces, supports, panels, screens, storage and power. Instead of buying fixed desks, you buy a system you can configure into single stations, clusters, rows or L-shapes, then reconfigure when the team changes.

That flexibility is worth a lot, but modular systems also come with more decisions than a simple table desk. This page lays out the three tiers Office Station offers, what each one costs per station, and what else to budget for.

How modular desk systems work

Most modular systems share the same basic building blocks:

  • Work surfaces in straight, corner and extension shapes.
  • Supports such as panels, legs or a beam that carries the surface.
  • Panels and screens that set the height of the divider between neighbors.
  • Storage including pedestals, overhead bins and lateral files.
  • Power and data routed through panels, raceways or a beam, then connected by an electrician.

Because the parts connect, one floor plan can mix low-panel collaborative rows with taller-panel focus stations using the same product line.

When modular beats fixed desks

Fixed desks are simpler and cheaper per piece, so modular is not automatically the better buy. It tends to pay off in a few situations:

  • Your headcount changes often. Adding a station to an existing run is easier than finding matching standalone desks months later.
  • You need power at every seat. Systems with powered panels or beams keep cords off the floor across long rows.
  • Teams need different levels of privacy. Swapping panel heights changes the feel of a zone without replacing the desks.
  • You expect to move. Modular parts break down and rebuild in a new layout, which matters if a relocation is on the horizon.

If none of those apply, compact freestanding desks may do the job for less. See laptop desks for office use for that route.

Three tiers of modular office desks

Liquidation-grade

Pre-owned systems recovered from corporate offices and sold with a condition grade: A like new, B very good, or C good. This is the fastest and lowest-cost path. Finishes reflect what came out of the source office, so it works best when you can be flexible on color.

Remanufactured

Pre-owned systems that are reworked for a more consistent look. You pay more than liquidation-grade and wait a little longer, but you get a more uniform result across a large floor.

New

Brand-new systems with the widest choice of finishes and configurations. The tradeoff is the highest price and the longest lead time.

A worked example

Say you need 24 liquidation-grade 6' x 6' stations delivered in central Greater Phoenix and professionally installed.

  1. Furniture: 24 x $615 = $14,760.

  3. Installation: 24 x $95 = $2,280 (above the $495 minimum).
  4. Delivery, Greater Phoenix central: $195.

That brings the estimate to $16,497 before electrical. The same count in new furniture would be $43,776 after the 5% discount, before installation and delivery. Outside Greater Phoenix, delivery is freight quoted per project.

Installation and electrical

Modular desks are not flat-pack furniture. Panels need to connect tightly, surfaces need to sit level, and runs of stations need to line up with aisles and power sources.

  • Professional installation is $95 per workstation with a $495 minimum.
  • Electrical is not included. Powered panels and beams need a licensed electrician to connect to building power, and your landlord or building may have its own requirements.
  • Data cabling is usually handled by your IT vendor. Coordinate their schedule with installation so cables route through the system before surfaces are closed up.
Plan before you buy. Space planning is free. A layout confirms station counts, aisle widths and where power needs to land, which prevents the most expensive modular mistake: buying the wrong number or configuration of parts.

Modular desk buying checklist

  • Confirm the station size your roles actually need. Not everyone needs 6' x 6'.
  • Choose a panel height for each zone: low for collaboration, taller for focus.
  • Locate floor boxes, walls and columns that feed power.
  • Decide on storage per station: pedestal, overhead, lateral or none.
  • Pick the tier by zone. Liquidation-grade for back-of-house, new or remanufactured where finish matching matters.
  • Record the manufacturer and product line so future additions are compatible.
  • Match your move-in date to the tier's lead time.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between liquidation-grade, remanufactured and new modular desks?

Liquidation-grade desks are pre-owned systems sold as they come out of an office, graded for condition, and priced lowest. Remanufactured systems are reworked for a more uniform look and cost more. New systems cost the most and take the longest to arrive.

How much does a modular office desk cost?

For a 6' x 6' workstation, furniture only, Office Station pricing is $615 liquidation-grade, $1,245 remanufactured and $1,920 new. Those prices exclude electrical, delivery and professional installation. Volume pricing takes 5% off at 20 or more stations, 8% at 50 or more, and 12% at 100 or more.

Do I need professional installation for modular desks?

Modular systems have panels, connectors, surfaces and often power components that need to be assembled square and level. Professional installation is $95 per workstation with a $495 minimum. Electrical hookup is separate and should be handled by a licensed electrician.

Can I add to modular desks later?

That is the main advantage of a modular system, but compatibility matters. Adding to the same manufacturer and product line is the safest path. Keep a record of what you bought so future additions match.

How long does it take to get modular office desks?

Liquidation-grade systems in stock can be ready in as little as 72 hours. Remanufactured usually takes 7 to 14 days, and new typically takes 4 to 6 weeks.
