Used L-Shaped Desks

An L-shaped desk gives one person two work zones in one footprint. Here is how to choose the size and orientation, read condition grades and plan the room before you buy used.

5 minute read

The L-shaped desk earns its place in private offices because it gives one person two distinct zones: a main surface for the computer and a return for paperwork, a second screen or a quick conversation across the corner. Used L-shaped desks let you furnish a row of offices or a single executive suite with well-built pieces for less than new. The key is choosing a desk that fits both the room and the way the person actually works.

The parts of an L-shaped desk

A used desk is only a good buy if every component is there, so know what you are counting.

  • Main worksurface: the longer run where the user usually faces the door or the room.
  • Return: the shorter surface joined at a right angle. It may be the same depth or slightly narrower.
  • Supports: full-height end panels, legs, or a combination with a pedestal carrying one corner.
  • Modesty panel: the front panel that hides legs and cables from visitors.
  • Storage: box drawers, file drawers or a hanging pedestal. Some desks pair with a hutch or overhead storage.
  • Connector hardware and grommets: the brackets that join the return to the main surface and the cable openings.

Choosing a size and orientation

Start with the room, not the desk. Measure wall to wall, note the door swing and windows, and decide where the user faces.

These are planning starting points, not rules. Leave about 36 to 42 inches behind the desk for the chair to roll, and check that the door clears the return when it opens.

Left or right return

Sit where the user will sit, facing the main surface. If the return extends to the right, it is a right return. Most people put the return on the side of their dominant hand for writing, or on the side facing the window. When you are furnishing several offices along a corridor, mirrored rooms often need a mix of left and right returns, so count them office by office.

Power, data and screens

Before you lock in orientation, find the outlets and data ports in each room. A desk that puts the monitor on the wall opposite the only outlet means cords running across the floor. Also think about glare: a screen facing a window washes out in afternoon light, so turning the main surface or swapping the return to the other side can solve a problem that no monitor setting will fix.

Condition grades and where to use them

Every pre-owned desk is graded A like new, B very good or C good. Private offices vary in visibility, so grades can be matched to the room.

  • Grade A for executive offices and any room where clients sit across the desk.
  • Grade B for manager and staff offices where light wear will not be noticed.
  • Grade C for interior offices, temporary space or budget-driven build-outs.

Pre-purchase checklist

  1. All pieces present: main surface, return, supports, modesty panel and connector hardware.
  2. Surfaces flat, with no swelling, lifted laminate or loose edge banding.
  3. Drawers open fully and close smoothly, and file drawers accept your folder size.
  4. Keys present for locking pedestals, or a plan to re-core.
  5. Finish matches across pieces, especially if a hutch or credenza is added.
  6. Grommets line up with where your monitor, dock and phone will go.
  7. Desk is stable with no rack when you push the corner.

Frequently asked questions

How do I tell a left return from a right return?

Sit where the user will sit, facing the main work surface. If the shorter return extends to your right, it is a right return; to your left, a left return. Some pre-owned L-shaped desks can be built either way, so confirm before ordering.

What size L-shaped desk fits a typical private office?

A common setup pairs a 60 to 72 inch main surface with a 42 to 48 inch return, with about 36 to 42 inches behind the desk for the chair. Room shape, door swing and guest seating all change the answer, which is why space planning is free.

What do condition grades A, B and C mean?

Grade A is like new, Grade B is very good and Grade C is good. A Grade C desk may show wear on edges or the finish while remaining solid and fully usable. Many buyers match grades to how visible each office is.

Do used L-shaped desks include a manufacturer warranty?

Manufacturer warranties generally do not transfer to pre-owned furniture. Office Station is an independent reseller, not an authorized dealer, so the condition grade and your inspection are what you rely on.
