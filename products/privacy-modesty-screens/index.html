Used Office Privacy and Modesty Screens

You do not always need new workstations to fix a noisy, exposed open office. Used office privacy screens and modesty panels can add separation to the desks you already have for a fraction of the cost.

6 minute read

Open offices save space, but they also leave people staring at each other across a desk all day. The fix does not have to be a full furniture replacement. Pre-owned desk screens and modesty panels can add a clear visual boundary to the workstations you already own, and buying them used keeps the project at liquidation-grade cost.

This page explains the types of screens, how to make sure a used screen fits your desks, and how condition grades apply to panels that people will see all day.

Types of desk and modesty screens

Desktop privacy screens

These sit on or above the back or side edge of the work surface. A front screen blocks the line of sight to the person across from you. A side screen separates neighbors in a row. Height is the main decision: low screens preserve an open feel, while taller screens give a real sense of a personal zone when seated.

Modesty screens

A modesty screen hangs below the front of the desk, from the surface down toward the floor. It hides legs, bags and cable clutter from anyone walking by or sitting across. On desks that face an aisle or a reception area, a modesty screen often makes a bigger difference to how the office looks than anything above the surface.

Combination and wraparound screens

Some screens run from above the surface down past the front edge in one piece, or wrap around a corner. These add the most separation but have the strictest fit requirements.

Measure before you buy. For each desk, record the width, the surface thickness, whether it is a freestanding desk or part of a workstation system, and the brand if you can find it. Those four details answer most fit questions before a screen ever ships.

Condition grades for used screens

Screens sit at eye level or face the aisle, so their condition is on display. Office Station grades pre-owned items the same way across its inventory:

  • Grade A, like new. Minimal to no visible wear. Use it for screens that face visitors, reception or a main walkway.
  • Grade B, very good. Light wear visible up close, such as slight fabric pilling or small edge marks. Fine for most employee rows.
  • Grade C, good. Visible wear such as faded fabric, scuffs or marks from previous mounting. A practical choice for back-of-house areas and modesty panels that sit below eye level.

Material matters too. Fabric-wrapped screens can show fading and pilling, while solid laminate, metal or acrylic-style panels are more likely to show scratches or chips. Ask what a screen is made of when you compare grades.

Fit and condition checklist

  1. Confirm screen width matches or slightly undercuts the desk width.
  2. Check the mounting hardware is included and complete.
  3. For clamp-on screens, confirm your desk thickness is within the clamp range.
  4. Check for bent brackets, which cause screens to lean.
  5. Inspect fabric for stains, tears and fraying at corners.
  6. Look at solid panels for cracks, chips and sharp edges.
  7. Make sure a modesty screen will not block power access, a keyboard tray or knee space.
  8. Match color across a row, or plan to put mixed colors in different zones.

Screens or new workstations?

Screens are the right call when your desks are sound and the complaint is mostly visual: people feel watched, cords look messy, or the front of a desk faces an aisle. They are fast to add and do not disrupt the floor plan.

Full workstations make more sense when people also need storage, more surface, taller panels or a different layout. Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between a privacy screen and a modesty screen?

A privacy screen mounts on or above the work surface to block sightlines between people sitting across from or beside each other. A modesty screen, sometimes called a modesty panel, hangs below the front edge of the desk to hide legs, bags and cords from view. Many offices use both.

Will a used screen fit my existing desks?

It depends on the desk thickness, the mounting method and the width. Clamp-on screens are the most flexible across brands, while system screens are made for a specific product line. Measure your desks and share the brand if you know it before ordering.

What condition grades apply to used screens?

Office Station uses grade A like new, grade B very good, and grade C good. Fabric screens show wear differently than solid or glass-look panels, so ask what material a screen is before deciding which grade you need.

How fast can I get used privacy screens?

Liquidation-grade items in stock can be ready in as little as 72 hours. Remanufactured furniture usually takes 7 to 14 days, and new typically takes 4 to 6 weeks.

Should I add screens or replace my workstations?

If your desks are in good shape and the main complaint is visual distraction or exposure, screens are usually the cheaper fix. If people also need more storage, surface or acoustic separation, compare the cost of a full liquidation-grade workstation, which starts at $615 for a 6' x 6' station.
