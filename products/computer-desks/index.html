Used Computer Desks for Office Teams

A computer desk has one job: hold screens and equipment so people can work comfortably for eight hours. This guide covers monitors, cables and ergonomics, and how to roll out used desks across many seats.

7 minute read

Most office work happens on a screen, which means the desk under that screen does a lot to decide whether someone ends the day comfortable or sore. When you are outfitting ten, fifty or a hundred seats, small choices about depth, cable routing and height add up fast.

Used computer desks can meet all of those needs at a lower cost than new, as long as you know what to look for. Office Station sells pre-owned desks and workstations as an independent reseller and offers volume pricing for multi-seat rollouts.

Start with the equipment, not the desk

Before you compare desks, list what each seat needs to hold. Different roles in the same office often need different setups.

  • Laptop with dock: one or two monitors, a dock, keyboard and mouse.
  • Desktop tower: add floor or under-surface space for the computer and airflow around it.
  • Multi-screen roles: finance, dispatch, design and support teams often run three monitors or wide screens.
  • Phone and headset users: a spot for a headset stand and a phone within easy reach.
  • Specialty hardware: scanners, label printers or signature pads need their own surface and power.

Sizing a computer desk for monitors

Screens should sit about an arm's length away, with the top of the display at or slightly below eye level. That distance is what drives desk depth.

If an existing floor plan forces shallower desks, dual monitor arms are usually the fix. They clamp to the back edge and let each person push screens back or pull them forward.

Cable management that holds up

Loose cables are the most common complaint about computer desks, and they are a trip and snag risk. Good cable management has three parts.

A way through the surface

Grommets or a flip-up power access door let cords reach the top without hanging off the edge. On used desks, confirm the grommet caps are present.

A path under the surface

Cable trays, baskets or raceways hold power strips and excess cable off the floor. Many can be added after purchase if a desk lacks them.

A path to the building

Power and data have to come from somewhere: a wall, a floor core or a power pole. On panel-based workstations, the panels themselves can carry power. This is electrical work, and it is not included in furniture pricing, so plan it with your electrician early.

Test before you roll out. On a large order, set up one complete seat first with the real monitors, dock and chair. Have a couple of employees use it for a day. It is much easier to change a monitor arm or cable tray choice on one desk than on eighty.

Ergonomics at the computer desk

A comfortable computer seat depends on the desk, the chair and the screen working together.

  • Surface height. Standard fixed desks sit around 29 to 30 inches. Shorter and taller people often need a keyboard tray or an adjustable desk to keep elbows near 90 degrees.
  • Sit-stand options. Height-adjustable desks let people change posture during the day and fit a wider range of body sizes.
  • Keyboard trays. An articulating tray can lower the keyboard below a fixed desk height and add adjustability for less than a new desk.
  • Chairs. The chair sets the eye height and arm position. Pair your desks with pre-owned office chairs that adjust in seat height, depth and arm height.
  • Lighting and glare. Place screens perpendicular to windows when you can, which cuts glare without blinds drawn all day.

Rollout checklist for used computer desks

  1. Count seats by role and list the equipment each role needs.
  2. Choose desk depth and width from the monitor setup, not from habit.
  3. Decide which seats need sit-stand or keyboard trays.
  4. Confirm all units in the order share the same finish and condition grade.
  5. Map power and data sources with your electrician.
  6. Build and test one pilot seat.
  7. Schedule delivery and installation around move-in, including dock and elevator access.
  8. Label each desk with its seat number so equipment lands in the right place.

Frequently asked questions

What makes a desk a good computer desk?

Enough depth to put monitors at a comfortable distance, a clean path for power and data cables, and a work surface at the right height for typing. Keyboard position and monitor height matter more than the look of the desk.

How deep should a desk be for two monitors?

Plan on 24 to 30 inches of depth for dual monitors. If you are working with shallower desks, monitor arms can pull the screens off the surface and give you back several inches.

Is there volume pricing for multi-seat rollouts?

Yes. Office Station offers 5% off at 20 or more stations, 8% at 50 or more and 12% at 100 or more. Ask for a quote with your seat count so the discount is reflected.

Are sit-stand desks available pre-owned?

Pre-owned height-adjustable desks come up regularly, though availability changes with inventory. Test the full range of travel, and check that the motor and controls work smoothly on every unit in a multi-desk order.
