Used Adjustable Height Desks

Pre-owned sit-stand desks give your team the option to stand without new-furniture prices or wait times. Here is how grades, lead times and delivery work.

5 minute read

Sit-stand desks used to be a perk for a few people. Now many teams expect them. The catch is cost: outfitting a whole department with new height-adjustable desks adds up fast, and new orders take weeks. Used adjustable height desks close that gap, as long as you know what to look for.

Adjustable height desks also come as standalone tables, as the primary surface in a private office, or built into workstations. If you are planning a full open plan, adjustable surfaces can be specified as part of a used cubicle configuration.

Choosing the right size and range

A sit-stand desk only helps if it fits the person using it at both heights. Before you pick a model, think about who will use it and what sits on top of it.

  • Height range. The lowest setting should let shorter users sit with forearms level to the surface, and the highest should let taller users stand without hunching. If one desk will serve several people, a wider range matters more.
  • Top depth. Deeper tops keep monitors at a comfortable distance. Shallow tops can work for laptop users or tight rooms.
  • Top width. Wide tops suit dual monitors and paperwork. Narrow tops fit training rooms and touchdown spaces.
  • Load. Heavy monitor arms, multiple screens or equipment call for a sturdier base, often dual motor.
  • Clearance. Leave room behind the desk so it does not hit a wall, window sill or panel as it rises.

Shared desks in hoteling or flex areas benefit most from electric models with a memory keypad, since each person can return to a saved height quickly.

Condition grades for sit-stand desks

Every pre-owned piece gets a condition grade. For sit-stand desks, the grade speaks to appearance. The mechanism should work properly at every grade.

  • Grade A, like new. Little or no visible wear on the top or base. Good for executive offices and client-facing areas.
  • Grade B, very good. Light marks on the surface or base that do not affect use. The value pick for most teams.
  • Grade C, good. More visible cosmetic wear with a sound frame and working lift. Fine for training rooms, labs and overflow desks.
Check the whole range of travel. The most common problem with a used sit-stand desk shows up only at one end of its range. Run it from lowest to highest and back with a typical load on top. It should move steadily, stay level, stop cleanly and hold its height without drifting.

Inspection checklist for used adjustable height desks

  • Full range of travel tested, up and down, with weight on the top.
  • No grinding, clicking or uneven motor sounds.
  • Top stays level through the whole range.
  • Minimal wobble at standing height, which is where wobble is worst.
  • Control keypad, control box and all power cords included for electric models.
  • Crank handle included for crank models.
  • Top attaches securely with no stripped screw holes.
  • Top size and depth fit your monitors and the space available.
  • Enough cable length and slack for power and data at full height.

Frequently asked questions

Are used adjustable height desks reliable?

A well-built sit-stand base is designed for years of cycling up and down, so a used desk can serve well when the mechanism checks out. The key is testing the full range of travel, listening for strain and confirming the desk stays level. Buy from a seller who states a condition grade for each desk.

Electric or crank: which should I buy used?

Electric desks adjust at the push of a button, so people actually use them, but they have motors, control boxes and cables to check. Crank and counterbalance desks have fewer electrical parts but take more effort to adjust. For most offices, electric is the better daily experience.

How fast can I get used sit-stand desks?

In-stock liquidation-grade pieces can be ready in as little as 72 hours. Remanufactured pieces usually take 7 to 14 days. New furniture takes 4 to 6 weeks.

Do used sit-stand desks come with a warranty?
