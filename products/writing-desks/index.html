Used Office Writing Desks

Writing desks are simple, open tables built for laptop-first work, small offices and shared rooms. Pre-owned commercial writing desks give you a clean, quality look quickly, especially when you choose in-stock pieces.

5 minute read

Not every office needs a desk with three surfaces and a wall of drawers. With most work happening on laptops and documents living in the cloud, a writing desk often does the job better: a solid top, an open base, and nothing extra. Used office writing desks are a fast, affordable way to furnish small private offices, home-office stipends, touchdown rooms and shared spaces with commercial-quality pieces.

This page explains when a writing desk is the right fit, how to pick a size, how condition grades apply, and how to get one in place quickly.

When a writing desk is the right choice

  • Small private offices. An open base makes a tight room feel larger and leaves space for a guest chair.
  • Laptop-first teams. If people carry their work with them, they rarely need built-in file drawers.
  • Shared and hoteling offices. Clean surfaces with no personal storage are easy to reset between users.
  • Reception and front-of-house. A good-looking writing desk makes a welcoming check-in point without the bulk of a reception station.
  • Quick expansions. When you need to add a few seats fast, in-stock writing desks can be ready in days.

If you need a credenza, a return and lots of storage in one piece, a used U-shaped desk is a better fit.

Standard desk height is around 29 to 30 inches. If several people will share a desk, consider an adjustable-height base, and plan at least 36 inches behind the chair so it can roll out comfortably.

Choosing a condition grade

A writing desk is mostly top surface and base, and there is nowhere for wear to hide. That makes the grade decision more visible than it is for storage or under-desk pieces.

  • Grade A, like new. Minimal to no visible wear. Choose it for executive offices, reception and anywhere clients sit across from you.
  • Grade B, very good. Light cosmetic wear that most people will not notice. A strong value for staff offices.
  • Grade C, good. Visible wear but fully sound. A practical pick for touchdown rooms, project spaces and back offices.

A desk pad or monitor arm covers minor surface marks on a Grade B top, so you can often save without any visible compromise.

Speed tip. If you need desks this week, ask for in-stock liquidation-grade pieces. They can be ready in as little as 72 hours. Remanufactured items usually take 7 to 14 days, and new furniture typically takes 4 to 6 weeks.

Quick inspection checklist

  1. Wobble test. Press down on a front corner. Open bases should stay rigid, with tight bolts and working levelers.
  2. Top condition. Check for scratches, veneer lifting, water rings and chipped edges.
  3. Base finish. Look for rust, chipped paint or bent legs, especially on metal frames.
  4. Cable management. Confirm grommets or a cable tray are in useful positions, or plan to add clamp-on management.
  5. Modesty panel. If the desk faces a door or visitors, decide whether you want a panel for privacy.
  6. Matching sets. When furnishing several offices, ask whether desks share the same finish and dimensions.

Pairing and planning

Writing desks look best when the rest of the room stays equally light. Add a mobile pedestal for a drawer or two, a slim bookcase, and a good task chair. Laminate, veneer or metal?

Laminate tops are the most durable choice for shared desks and busy rooms, since they resist scratches and clean up easily. Wood veneer adds warmth and a more polished look for executive or client-facing offices, but it marks more readily, so a Grade A or B veneer desk is usually worth the step up. Glass and metal-framed designs feel modern and open but show fingerprints and dust. Choose the finish based on who uses the desk and how often, not just how it looks in a photo.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between a writing desk and a regular office desk?

A writing desk is essentially a table-style desk with an open base and little or no built-in storage. A traditional office desk usually has pedestals, a modesty panel or a return. Writing desks look lighter and fit smaller rooms, and you can add a mobile pedestal if you need drawers.

How quickly can I get a used writing desk?

In-stock liquidation-grade pieces can be ready in as little as 72 hours. Remanufactured items usually take 7 to 14 days, and new furniture typically takes 4 to 6 weeks. In Greater Phoenix, delivery is $195 central or $295 metro.

What size writing desk should I get?

For laptop work, 48 inches wide by 24 inches deep is usually enough. If you use one or two external monitors, look at 60 inches wide and at least 30 inches deep so the screens sit at a comfortable distance. Free space planning can help you pick the right size for the room.

Is a used writing desk a good choice for a hoteling or shared office?

Yes. Writing desks are easy to rearrange, clean and share because they have no personal storage built in. Pair them with lockers or storage towers so people have somewhere to keep belongings.
