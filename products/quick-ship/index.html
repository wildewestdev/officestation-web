Quick Ship Office Furniture: Real Lead Times Compared

When your move-in date is fixed, lead time decides what you can actually buy. Here is how liquidation-grade, remanufactured and new furniture compare on speed, price and tradeoffs.

6 minute read

"Quick ship" is one of the most loosely used phrases in office furniture. Sometimes it means a short list of new items that ship in three weeks instead of eight. What you actually need to know is simpler: what can be standing in your space, assembled and usable, by the date you need it? The honest answer depends on which tier of furniture you choose.

This page lays out the three tiers Office Station sells, the realistic lead time for each, what they cost for a standard 6'x6' workstation, and how to build a schedule that does not fall apart in the last week.

Liquidation-grade: speed first

Liquidation-grade furniture is pre-owned inventory that is already on hand. Nothing has to be built, refinished or ordered from a factory, which is why it can move in as little as 72 hours. The catch is that "in stock" means what exists right now. If you need 40 matching stations in one finish, confirm the count before you commit your team to a date.

Pieces are condition graded: A is like new, B is very good and C is good. Mixing grades can stretch availability if a single grade runs short.

Remanufactured: the middle lane

Remanufactured furniture usually takes 7 to 14 days. You pay more than liquidation-grade and get a more consistent, refreshed result. If your move is two or three weeks out, this tier often gives you the best balance.

New: predictable, not fast

New furniture runs 4 to 6 weeks. That is not quick ship by most definitions, but it is reliable when you have the runway. If you are reading this page with less than a month to go, new is probably off the table for the bulk of your order.

The lead time is not your whole schedule

A 72-hour lead time does not mean your office is ready in 72 hours. Furniture arriving is one step in a longer chain. Work backward from your move-in date and account for each of these:

  1. Space plan. Know how many stations fit and where power and data land. Space planning is free, and it is the step that prevents ordering the wrong quantity.
  2. Inventory confirmation. Lock in quantities, finishes and grades before you set a date.
  3. Electrical. Powered workstations need an electrician to connect them. That work is not included in furniture pricing, and it is often the slowest piece to schedule.
  4. Delivery. Greater Phoenix delivery is $195 central or $295 metro. Outside Greater Phoenix, freight is quoted per project and transit time adds to your window.
  5. Installation. $95 per workstation with a $495 minimum. Book the crew when you order, not when the truck arrives.
  6. Building access. Confirm freight elevator and dock time with your property manager, plus any certificate of insurance the building requires.
The usual bottleneck. On fast projects, furniture is rarely what runs late. Electrical scheduling and building access are. Call your electrician and property manager the same day you confirm inventory.

Splitting an order across tiers

You do not have to pick one tier for the whole office. A split order is often the smartest way to hit a hard date without settling for less everywhere.

  • Phase one: in-stock liquidation-grade stations for the open work area, so people can sit down the first week.
  • Phase two: remanufactured pieces for client-facing zones or leadership areas, arriving a week or two later.
  • Seating separately: chairs are easy to swap later, so buy what is available now and upgrade if needed. See pre-owned office chairs.

Plan the split with the final layout in mind so phase two drops into place without moving phase one.

Quick ship checklist

  • Move-in date and the last day furniture can arrive
  • Station count from a space plan, not a guess
  • Station size and whether you need powered panels
  • Acceptable condition grades (A, B, C)
  • Electrician booked for connection
  • Dock and freight elevator reserved, COI requirements confirmed
  • Installation included in the quote
  • A plan for the old furniture if you are leaving a space

If you are also moving out

Fast moves usually have two sides: furnishing the new space and clearing the old one. If your current furniture has resale value, it can offset removal cost. Office furniture liquidation produces a written net offer, meaning resale value minus removal cost, and a positive number means you get paid. Walkthroughs typically happen within 48 hours in Greater Phoenix and 2-3 business days elsewhere, with offers within 5 business days. For the full sequence, see corporate relocation.

Frequently asked questions

What is the fastest way to furnish an office?

In-stock liquidation-grade furniture is the fastest option, with lead times of as little as 72 hours. Availability depends on what is in inventory when you order, so confirming quantities and finishes early matters. Delivery and installation also need to fit inside your window.

How long does remanufactured office furniture take?

Remanufactured furniture usually takes 7 to 14 days. It is a good middle ground when you want a more uniform, refreshed look but cannot wait for new production.

How long does new office furniture take?

New furniture typically takes 4 to 6 weeks. That works for a planned move but is often too slow for an emergency expansion or a lease that ends early. A 6'x6' new workstation is $1,920, furniture only.

Do quick ship prices include delivery and installation?

No. Workstation prices are furniture only and exclude electrical, delivery and professional installation. Installation is $95 per workstation with a $495 minimum. Greater Phoenix delivery is $195 central or $295 metro, and freight elsewhere is quoted per project.

Can I combine tiers in one order?

Yes. Many offices place in-stock liquidation-grade stations first so people can work on day one, then add remanufactured or new pieces where appearance matters most. Free space planning helps you sequence the two.
