Pre-owned sit-stand desks

Used Height Adjustable Desks

Sit-stand desks are worth buying pre-owned if you know what to test. Here is how to inspect the moving parts, what the warranty situation really is, and what installation costs.

6 minute read

A sit-stand desk is not just a desk. It is a frame, a lift system, a controller and a power supply that all have to keep working together. That is why buying used height adjustable desks rewards a little homework. Done right, you get the posture flexibility your team wants from designer brands at a fraction of new pricing. Done carelessly, you inherit a wobbly frame or a controller that quits.

This page walks through the mechanism types, a practical inspection checklist, honest warranty guidance and the real costs of getting desks installed.

The three ways a desk changes height

The mechanism tells you what can wear out and what to test.

  • Electric, dual motor. One motor in each leg, synchronized by a control box. Smooth, strong and the most common choice in offices. Check that both legs stay level through the whole travel.
  • Electric, single motor. One motor drives both legs through a shaft. Usually lighter load capacity and a bit slower. Listen for noise from the crossbar.
  • Counterbalance or crank. Gas springs or a hand crank with no power required. Fewer electronic parts to fail, but the spring must be matched to the load on the surface.

Most pre-owned inventory from brands such as Herman Miller, Steelcase, Knoll, Haworth, Humanscale, Teknion and Allsteel falls in the electric categories. Office Station sells these as an independent reseller, and which models are on hand changes with each project.

What the condition grades mean for moving parts

Every piece carries a grade: A like new, B very good, or C good. On a fixed desk, the grade is mostly about appearance. On a sit-stand desk, pay attention to function too.

  • Grade A suits client-facing areas and anyone who wants a desk that looks and feels close to new.
  • Grade B is the practical middle for most open office rows. Expect light signs of use.
  • Grade C makes sense for training rooms, overflow seating or budget-driven projects. Cosmetic wear is expected, so run the lift test before you commit.

Mixing grades is common. You might put Grade A desks in a visible front row and Grade B or C in back areas where appearance matters less.

Honest warranty guidance. Office Station is an independent reseller, not an authorized dealer for any manufacturer. Manufacturer warranties generally do not transfer to pre-owned furniture, and that includes motors and controllers. Buy on the grade and the lift test, and budget a small reserve for occasional parts such as a replacement controller or power supply.

Planning a room full of sit-stand desks

Height adjustable desks create a few layout issues that fixed desks do not.

  1. Check clearance above. At full height, monitors on arms can hit shelving, overhead bins or low soffits.
  2. Plan cable slack. Power and data cables must reach the desk at its highest setting. A vertical cable spine or a tray under the surface keeps cords from snagging.
  3. Mind neighboring desks. Back-to-back desks with privacy screens need screens attached to the moving surface or mounted independently so they do not bind.
  4. Count outlets. Every electric desk needs power. A daisy chain of power strips is not a plan.
  5. Match chairs. People who stand part of the day still sit most of it. See our pre-owned office chairs to complete each station.

Frequently asked questions

Do used height adjustable desks come with a manufacturer warranty?

Generally, no. Office Station is an independent reseller, not an authorized dealer, and manufacturer warranties generally do not transfer to pre-owned furniture. Plan your purchase around the condition grade and a hands-on check of the lift system rather than an assumed warranty.

What should I test first on a used sit-stand desk?

Run the desk through its full height range under a realistic load. Listen for grinding, watch that both legs rise evenly, and confirm the controller responds every time. A desk that moves smoothly and quietly across its full range is usually a sound buy.

How much does installation cost?

Installation is $95 per workstation with a $495 minimum. Furniture prices exclude electrical, delivery and professional installation, so each of those is shown separately on your quote. Greater Phoenix delivery is $195 central or $295 metro, and freight elsewhere is quoted per project.

Do I need special electrical for electric desks?

Electric sit-stand desks plug into a standard outlet, but a room full of them needs a sensible power plan. Each desk needs an outlet within reach of its cable at full height. Electrical work is not included in furniture pricing, so involve your electrician or building team early.

How quickly can I get pre-owned sit-stand desks?

In-stock liquidation-grade furniture can be ready in as little as 72 hours. Remanufactured pieces usually take 7 to 14 days, and new furniture typically runs 4 to 6 weeks. Specific desk models depend on current inventory.
