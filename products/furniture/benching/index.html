What benching is, and when it beats cubicles

A benching workstation is a run of desks sharing one frame. Picture a long table split into individual work surfaces, with people seated side by side and often facing a partner across a center beam. Screens between seats are low or optional, and cable management runs down the spine.

Benching tends to be the better choice when:

  • Your team collaborates constantly and does not need acoustic privacy at every seat.
  • You need to fit more seats into a fixed square footage.
  • People work hybrid schedules and share desks.
  • You want an open, lighter look than panel systems provide.

Cubicles still win for heads-down work, phone-heavy roles and teams that need personal storage and privacy. If you are torn, see used cubicles and compare both layouts in the same space plan.

Planning a benching layout

Benching is dense, which is the point, but density creates its own problems. Free space planning works through them before you buy. Expect to answer these questions:

  • Surface size. Deeper and wider surfaces suit dual monitors. Shallower surfaces fit more seats.
  • Row length. Long runs are efficient but make it harder to get out of middle seats. Break rows where people need to move.
  • Aisles and egress. Leave clear walking paths and confirm your layout with your landlord and local code requirements.
  • Power and data. Decide where the building feed meets each row. Electrical work is not included in furniture pricing and usually needs a licensed electrician.
  • Screens. Low screens between facing seats help with focus and visual clutter without closing the space in.
  • Storage. Benching has no panels to hang storage on, so plan mobile pedestals or storage towers for personal items.

Checklist for buying used benching

Pre-owned benching is modular, which means missing parts matter. Confirm these before you approve a quote:

  1. Every row includes all its beams, legs, end supports and connecting hardware.
  2. Work surfaces match in size, color and edge profile across the rows you are buying.
  3. Screens are the same height and finish, with their mounting brackets.
  4. Cable trays or spines are present and undamaged.
  5. Any power components are identified, so your electrician can confirm what is usable.
  6. Height-adjustable benching includes working motors, controls and cords for every seat.
  7. The condition grade is stated for each lot, since manufacturer warranties generally do not transfer to pre-owned furniture.

Pre-owned benching can come from brands such as Herman Miller, Steelcase, Knoll, Haworth, Teknion and Allsteel, depending on stock. Office Station is an independent reseller, not an authorized dealer.

Frequently asked questions

What is a benching workstation?

Benching is a desk system where several work surfaces share a common frame or beam, usually in rows with people facing each other. Instead of tall cubicle panels, it uses low screens or none at all. Power and data typically run along the shared beam rather than through individual panels.

How much do used benching workstations cost?

Office Station publishes a 6'x6' workstation benchmark for furniture only: $615 liquidation-grade, $1,245 remanufactured and $1,920 new. Actual benching pricing depends on the system, surface sizes, screens and storage, so every project is quoted. Prices exclude electrical, delivery and professional installation.

How long does benching take to arrive?

In-stock liquidation-grade furniture can be ready in as little as 72 hours. Remanufactured furniture usually takes 7 to 14 days. New furniture typically takes 4 to 6 weeks.

Is space planning really free?

Yes. Send your floor plan and headcount and you get a layout showing how benching, or a mix of benching and cubicles, fits your space. It is the easiest way to compare seat counts before you commit to a tier.

Can I mix benching tiers in one office?

Yes. A common approach is remanufactured or new benching in visible, client-facing areas and liquidation-grade benching in back-office zones. Mixing tiers balances appearance, budget and lead time within one project.
