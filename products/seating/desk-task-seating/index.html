Used Task Chairs for All-Day Work

A good task chair is the piece of furniture your team touches most. Pre-owned ergonomic chairs from designer brands give you real adjustability at a lower cost, as long as you know what to inspect.

6 minute read

People sit in a task chair for six to ten hours a day. That makes it the one piece of office furniture where cheap choices show up fastest, in sore backs, broken gas cylinders and constant replacement orders. Used task chairs from commercial brands solve that problem at a lower price, because they were engineered for years of daily use to begin with.

This page covers the kinds of task chairs Office Station carries, how our A, B and C grades work, and a hands-on checklist for inspecting any pre-owned ergonomic chair.

Types of task chairs

Task chairs fall into a few broad groups. Knowing which one fits your team makes it easier to compare options.

  • Mesh-back ergonomic chairs. Suspension backs that breathe well and flex with the body. The Herman Miller Aeron is the best-known example of this style.
  • Upholstered ergonomic chairs. Foam and fabric backs with adjustable lumbar and a flexing seat edge. The Steelcase Leap is a widely used example.
  • Weight-activated chairs. Chairs that adjust recline tension automatically to the person sitting, with fewer knobs. Humanscale is known for this approach.
  • Intensive-use and 24/7 chairs. Heavier-duty builds for dispatch centers, security desks and shared shift seating.
  • Stools and drafting chairs. Taller chairs with foot rings for standing-height counters and labs.

Adjustments that matter most

Not every chair needs every feature. These are the adjustments that make the biggest difference for typical desk work, in rough order of importance.

AdjustmentWhy it mattersWho needs it most
Seat heightFeet flat on the floor, thighs roughly levelEveryone
Recline tension and lockLets people lean back without fighting the chairAnyone who works long stretches
Lumbar supportSupports the lower back curvePeople with back discomfort, long sitters
Arm height and widthKeeps shoulders relaxed at the keyboardHeavy typists, shared desks
Seat depthFits shorter and taller people on the same modelHot desks and shared seating

If a chair will be shared across shifts or hot desks, seat depth and arm adjustments move up the list, since the chair has to fit many body types.

What our A, B and C grades mean for task chairs

Grades describe cosmetic condition. Every chair we sell should adjust and support as designed, regardless of grade.

  • Grade A, like new. Clean mesh or upholstery, arm pads without wear, bases with little or no scuffing.
  • Grade B, very good. Light wear such as faint arm-pad marks or small scuffs on the base. Hard to spot once the chair is at a desk.
  • Grade C, good. Visible wear on arms, fabric or base, with sound structure and working controls.

A mixed approach works well for many offices. Put grade A or B chairs in open areas that visitors walk through, and use grade C where a team simply needs a good chair and the budget is tight.

Inspection checklist for any used task chair

Spend two minutes with each style of chair before you buy in quantity. Sit in it and work every control.

  1. Gas cylinder. Raise and lower the seat several times. It should hold height without slowly sinking.
  2. Tilt mechanism. Lean back through the full range. Listen for grinding or clunks and confirm the lock engages at each position.
  3. Tension knob. Turn it both ways and confirm you can feel the recline get firmer and softer.
  4. Mesh or foam. Press into the seat and back. Mesh should be taut with no frayed edges. Foam should spring back rather than stay compressed.
  5. Arms. Move each adjustment. Arms should lock in place and not wobble side to side.
  6. Casters and base. Roll the chair on your floor type. Check for cracks in the base arms and casters that drag or stick.
  7. Labels. Look under the seat for the manufacturer label. It helps confirm the model and makes future parts easier to find.

Frequently asked questions

Are used task chairs worth it compared to new budget chairs?

Often, yes. A pre-owned chair from a commercial brand was built for heavy daily use and has more adjustments than most budget chairs. The key is buying a chair whose mechanisms still work properly, which is what the inspection checklist on this page helps you confirm.

Do you sell Aeron and Leap chairs?

We carry models like the Herman Miller Aeron and Steelcase Leap when they come into inventory, along with comparable chairs from Haworth, Humanscale, Knoll and others. Availability changes, so contact us with your quantity and preferred grade.

Does a manufacturer warranty come with a used task chair?

Generally, no. Office Station is an independent reseller, not an authorized dealer, and manufacturer warranties usually do not transfer to pre-owned chairs. We share the condition grade before you buy.

Which grade should I buy for a team that sits all day?

Grade is about cosmetic wear, not comfort, so any grade should adjust and support properly. Many buyers choose grade A or B for visible open offices and grade C for call centers, labs or overflow desks where appearance matters less.

Can I order chairs together with workstations?

Yes. Many buyers pair task chairs with used cubicles so a whole floor arrives on one schedule. Use the quote builder on our used cubicles page and mention the chair count in your request.
