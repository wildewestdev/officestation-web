Used Office Seating for Every Room

Every room in your office asks something different of a chair. Use this guide to match pre-owned seating to each space, decide where to spend and where to save, and know what to check.

6 minute read

Seating is where furniture budgets quietly run over. You need a task chair at every desk, a matched set for each conference room, guest chairs in the lobby and private offices, lounge pieces for collaboration areas and enough chairs for the breakroom. Priced new, that list gets expensive fast. Used office seating from established manufacturers lets you furnish every room properly without paying new prices for chairs that spend most of their lives under a table.

Use this page as a starting point. It breaks seating down by room, shows where condition grade matters, and links to deeper guides for specific categories.

Task chairs

If you only spend carefully in one place, spend it here. A task chair is used for eight hours a day, and a worn mechanism or a flattened seat shows up as complaints within a week. Look for seat height adjustment that holds, tilt tension that actually changes the feel, working tilt lock, adjustable arms if the model has them and casters that roll freely.

High-end ergonomic models from brands like Herman Miller, Steelcase, Haworth and Humanscale are often built to be serviced, which makes them good candidates for a second life. For a deeper breakdown, see pre-owned office chairs.

Conference seating

In a conference room, consistency matters more than any single chair. A table of mismatched heights and colors looks unfinished even if every chair is in good shape. Count the seats you need for each room, add one or two spares so a damaged chair can be swapped without breaking the set, and confirm that the whole group is the same model, finish and grade.

Check arm height against the table apron. Chairs with fixed arms that do not clear the table edge cannot tuck in, which eats floor space in a small room.

Guest and side chairs

Guest chairs sit in private offices, lobbies and outside meeting rooms. People use them for minutes, not hours, so comfort needs are modest. Appearance is what counts. A sled base or four-leg chair with a clean frame and fresh-looking upholstery reads as professional in a lobby. For internal offices, a grade B chair usually looks just as good once it is in place.

Lounge seating

Lounge chairs, sofas and modular soft seating define collaboration areas and waiting rooms. They are also the pieces where upholstery wear is easiest to spot, because the fabric covers so much of the surface. Look closely at seat cushions for sagging, arm fronts for pilling or fading, and seams for separation. Modular pieces should include every connector so sections sit tight together.

Cafe and breakroom seating

Breakroom chairs see short sits but constant use, spills and dragging across hard floors. Durable, wipeable materials beat plush upholstery. This is also the room where a grade C chair makes the most sense, especially in training rooms and back-of-house lunchrooms. Buying from an independent reseller

Office Station sells pre-owned seating from Herman Miller, Steelcase, Knoll, Haworth, Humanscale, Teknion, Allsteel, Kimball, AIS and Friant. It does so as an independent reseller, not an authorized dealer for any of those brands. That affects two things you should understand before buying.

  • Warranty. Manufacturer warranties generally do not transfer to pre-owned furniture. Your protection comes from buying the right grade and checking the chair.
  • Availability. Inventory depends on what comes in. Large matched quantities of one model are not always on hand, so confirm counts before planning around a specific chair.
Test the controls, not just the look. A chair can look grade A and still have a tilt lock that slips. Before you approve a large order, sit in a sample, run every lever and adjust the height up and down a few times.

Seating inspection checklist

  • Gas cylinder holds height without sinking
  • Tilt tension and tilt lock work as intended
  • Arms adjust and lock, with no cracked pads
  • Seat and back upholstery free of tears, stains and flattened foam
  • Mesh backs and seats taut, with no frayed edges
  • Base and frame free of cracks, casters or glides intact
  • Every chair in a set matches in model, color and grade

Frequently asked questions

What brands of used office seating do you carry?

Office Station sells pre-owned seating from brands including Herman Miller, Steelcase, Knoll, Haworth, Humanscale, Teknion, Allsteel, Kimball, AIS and Friant. Specific models depend on current inventory. Office Station is an independent reseller, not an authorized dealer.

Is used office seating covered by a manufacturer warranty?

Generally no. Manufacturer warranties generally do not transfer to pre-owned furniture. Use the condition grade and a hands-on check of the controls to judge each chair.

Where should I spend the most on seating?

Task chairs, because people sit in them for a full workday. Adjustability and a healthy mechanism matter most there. Guest and breakroom chairs see shorter sits and can often be a lower condition grade.

How are used chairs graded?

Grade A is like new, grade B is very good and grade C is good. For seating, look at upholstery, frame finish and how the controls work. Ask about specific wear before ordering a large quantity.

Can seating be delivered with workstations?
