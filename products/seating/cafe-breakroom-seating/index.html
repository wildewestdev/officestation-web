Used Breakroom Chairs and Cafe Seating

Breakroom chairs take more abuse per hour than almost anything else in your office. Here is how to choose durable pre-owned cafe seating and refit the whole room at once without overspending.

6 minute read

The breakroom is where your team actually spends time together, and it is also where chairs live the hardest life. They get dragged across tile, stacked in corners, splashed with coffee, tipped back on two legs and used as step stools. New cafe seating costs more than most people expect for such a simple piece. Used breakroom chairs make it realistic to replace every chair in the room at once, so the set matches and nothing wobbles.

This guide is about the breakroom specifically: which chair styles hold up, how to count seats, how condition grades apply and how to plan a full refit.

For a room people use mostly for meals, molded or wood seats with no fabric are the easiest to keep clean. If the breakroom doubles as a meeting or training space, a seat pad adds comfort for longer sits.

How to count breakroom seats

Ordering by headcount is the most common mistake. A 60-person office rarely has 60 people eating in the same room at the same time. Use this approach instead:

  1. Find your peak. Estimate how many people use the breakroom at the busiest point of the day, including shift overlaps.
  2. Match your tables. Count seats per table in your planned layout. Four-person rounds, six-person rectangles and high-top counters all seat differently.
  3. Add event use. If the room hosts all-hands meetings or lunch-and-learns, stacking chairs can cover the extra without crowding daily layout.
  4. Keep spares. Two to four extra chairs of the same model let you swap a damaged chair and keep the set matched.

Space planning is free, so you can check table spacing and walkways before you commit to a quantity.

Leave room to pull out. Breakroom tables often get packed too tight on paper. Chairs need space to slide back, and people need a path behind seated diners to reach the fridge and the coffee. Plan the aisles first, then fit the tables.

Condition grades in the breakroom

Pre-owned pieces are graded A like new, B very good and C good. The breakroom is one room where stepping down a grade can make a lot of sense.

  • Grade A for a client-facing cafe, a recruiting showcase space or a front-of-house coffee bar.
  • Grade B for most employee breakrooms, where minor cosmetic wear disappears in daily use.
  • Grade C for training rooms, warehouse and production lunchrooms, and overflow seating.

Whatever the grade, structure is not negotiable. A breakroom chair has to be solid, because it gets leaned back on more often than any other chair in the building.

Plan a breakroom refit

Breakroom chair checklist

  • No rocking when you shift your weight side to side
  • Glides or feet present on every leg
  • No cracks where shells, seats or backs attach to the frame
  • Welds and joints intact on metal frames
  • Seat pads, if any, free of stains and tears
  • Stacking chairs stack cleanly without binding
  • Every chair in the set matches in model, color and finish

Planning a full breakroom refit

Replacing chairs a few at a time leaves you with a room of mismatched pieces. A full refit is usually cleaner and easier to schedule. Here is how to approach it:

  1. Measure the room and mark fixed items: counters, appliances, doors and vending machines.
  2. Choose the table mix, then the chairs that suit those tables and the floor surface.
  3. Confirm the quantity is available in one matched model before ordering.
  4. Schedule delivery for an evening or weekend, or right after lunch, so the room is only out of service briefly.
  5. Decide what happens to the old chairs and tables before the new ones arrive.

Frequently asked questions

What should I look for in used breakroom chairs?

Prioritize wipeable surfaces, tight joints and intact glides. Breakroom chairs get short sits but constant turnover, spills and dragging. A chair with light cosmetic wear and a solid frame is a better buy than a spotless one that wobbles.

What condition grade is right for breakroom seating?

Grade B, very good, suits most employee breakrooms. Grade C, good, works for training rooms and back-of-house lunchrooms. Choose grade A, like new, for a cafe that clients or job candidates will see.

Is there volume pricing for a breakroom refit?

Office Station's published volume pricing is based on workstation count: 5% at 20+ stations, 8% at 50+ and 12% at 100+. If your breakroom refit is part of a larger workstation order, ask how pricing applies to the full project. For a seating-only order, request a quote for your exact quantity.

How many breakroom chairs do I need?

Base it on how many people use the room at the busiest time, not on total headcount. Match that number to your table layout and add a few spares for visitors and for replacing a damaged chair without breaking the set.

Can breakroom chairs ship with other furniture?

Yes. Putting them on the same delivery as workstations or other furniture avoids a second trip. Greater Phoenix delivery is $195 central or $295 metro, and freight elsewhere is quoted per project.
