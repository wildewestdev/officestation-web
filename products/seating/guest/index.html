Used Guest Chairs for Reception and Private Offices

Guest chairs are the first seat a visitor takes in your office. Pre-owned side chairs from designer brands let you finish reception areas and private offices so they match what you already own.

5 minute read

Guest chairs are easy to overlook until you notice the mismatched set in front of a manager's desk or the worn fabric in reception. Because visitors sit in them for a few minutes at a time, appearance matters more than deep adjustability. That makes used guest chairs one of the best places to buy pre-owned: the frames are simple, durable and often look nearly new.

This page covers the common guest chair styles, how to match them to finishes you already have, and how to size a reception area.

Matching chairs to finishes you already own

Most people shopping for guest chairs are not starting from scratch. You have desks, casegoods or conference chairs in place, and the new chairs need to fit in. Here is how to give us what we need to find a match.

  1. Find the label. Flip an existing chair over and photograph the manufacturer sticker or model number if it has one.
  2. Photograph the frame finish. Chrome, polished aluminum, black, silver and wood tones all look different in person. A photo in natural light helps.
  3. Note the upholstery. Leather, vinyl, fabric or mesh, plus the color. A close-up of the fabric weave is useful.
  4. Measure the seat height. Mixing chairs with different seat heights in one room looks off, even if the finishes match.
  5. Count what you need. Include one or two spares if you want to replace a damaged chair later.

If an exact model is not available, a chair with the same frame finish and upholstery color usually reads as intentional. We will tell you plainly whether a chair is an exact match or a close one.

Grade guidance for guest chairs. Grade A is like new, B is very good and C is good. Because guests look at these chairs closely, grade A or B is the usual choice for reception and client offices. Grade C is a sensible pick for training rooms and back offices. Planning a reception area

Size the waiting area to how many people actually arrive at once, not how many could. A few practical guidelines:

  • Count your peak simultaneous visitors, then add one or two seats of buffer.
  • Leave a clear walking path from the entry to the reception desk, typically at least 36 inches wide.
  • Place a small table between pairs of chairs so guests have a spot for a bag or coffee.
  • Choose arms on at least some chairs. They help people sit down and stand up more easily.
  • For longer waits or a more relaxed feel, mix in lounge seating alongside upright guest chairs.

Guest chairs in private offices

In a private office, guest chairs sit across from the desk and are seen next to the executive chair. Keeping them in the same finish family pulls the room together. A high-back executive chair in black leather, for example, pairs naturally with black-frame side chairs in a similar upholstery.

Check the clearance in front of the desk. Guests need enough room to pull the chair back and stand without hitting a wall or bookcase. If the office is tight, armless or compact sled-base chairs take up less space. For the desk chair side of the pairing, see our conference and executive seating page.

What to inspect

  • Frame joints. Rock the chair gently. It should not wobble or creak at welds or wood joints.
  • Glides. Confirm each foot has a glide suited to your floor, and that none are missing.
  • Upholstery edges. Seat fronts and arm tops show wear first.
  • Arm caps. Look for cracks or loose caps on metal-arm chairs.

Frequently asked questions

Can you match used guest chairs to my existing furniture?

We try. Send photos of your current chairs or desks, the manufacturer label if you can find it, and the finishes you want to match. We will look for pre-owned chairs in the same line or a close match in frame finish and upholstery color.

How many guest chairs does a private office need?

Most private offices use two guest chairs in front of the desk. Larger offices with a small table may add two more. Measure the clear floor space first, since guests need room to pull a chair back and stand up.

Are sled-base or four-leg chairs better?

Sled bases slide easily and suit carpet well. Four-leg chairs look lighter and are common in reception, but they can mark hard floors without glides. Either works when you pick the right glides for your floor.

Do guest chairs come with a manufacturer warranty?

Generally, no. Office Station is an independent reseller, not an authorized dealer, and manufacturer warranties usually do not transfer to pre-owned furniture. We give you the condition grade before you buy.
