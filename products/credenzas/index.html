Used Office Credenzas for Private Offices and Conference Rooms

A credenza adds storage, display space and a finished look to a room without eating floor space. Here is how to pick the right style and size, and what to check on a pre-owned unit.

6 minute read

Offices tend to run out of storage before they run out of floor space. A credenza solves that quietly. It tucks against a wall, holds what would otherwise pile up on a desk and gives the room a finished, organized look.

Office Station sells pre-owned credenzas as an independent reseller, at resale pricing rather than new retail. Each unit is graded A, B or C, and delivery and installation are available when you want them.

Where a credenza earns its space

Behind the desk in a private office

This is the classic spot. The credenza holds active files and supplies within a chair swivel, and its top can carry a printer or a second monitor. Paired with an L-shaped desk, it can turn the layout into a U. See used corner office desks for that setup.

Along a conference room wall

In a meeting room, a credenza hides AV equipment, cables, spare chargers and presentation supplies. The top is a natural place for water, coffee or lunch during long meetings, so a durable surface is worth prioritizing here.

Under a wall-mounted screen

A low credenza beneath a display gives cables and hardware somewhere to go. Look for units with back cutouts or removable panels for ventilation and wiring.

In reception and shared areas

A credenza near the entry can store visitor badges, mail and brochures while giving the space a clean, furnished look.

Sizing a credenza for the room

Credenzas are usually around desk height and 18 to 24 inches deep, with lengths that vary widely. Use these checks to find the right fit.

  • Behind a desk: measure from the back edge of the desk to the wall. Subtract the credenza depth. You want roughly 36 inches left for the chair.
  • Conference rooms: measure from the table edge to the wall and leave enough room for a chair pushed back plus someone walking behind it.
  • Length: keep the credenza shorter than the wall so it does not block outlets, thermostats or light switches.
  • Under windows: check sill height so the top does not cover the glass.
  • Getting it in: credenzas often ship as one piece, so measure doorways, hall turns and elevator openings.

What to check on a used credenza

A credenza has more moving parts than most desks. Use this checklist before you buy.

  1. Condition grade. Grade A is like new, B is very good and C is good. Conference rooms with clients usually call for A or B.
  2. Doors. Open and close each one. Doors should hang evenly and close without rubbing.
  3. Drawers. Pull each drawer to full extension and push it closed. It should glide without catching.
  4. File hardware. Confirm hanging file bars are present and set for letter or legal size as you need.
  5. Locks and keys. Test the lock and ask whether keys are included.
  6. Top surface. Look for water rings, heat marks and lifting veneer, especially on units from conference rooms.
  7. Back panel. If it will hold AV gear, check for cable cutouts and ventilation.
  8. Anti-tip and anchoring. Units with hutches or tall storage should be anchored to the wall.
  9. Matching finish. If it pairs with a desk, compare the wood or laminate color in daylight.

Why pre-owned credenzas make sense

Credenzas are one of the easier pieces to buy used. They are mostly solid boxes with simple hardware, so a well-kept unit can look and work much like new for years. Buying pre-owned at resale pricing lets you put storage in every private office and meeting room instead of choosing only a few.

They also tend to match easily. A neutral laminate or common wood finish can sit alongside desks from a different lot without looking out of place.

Frequently asked questions

What is an office credenza used for?

A credenza is a low storage cabinet that usually sits behind a desk or along a conference room wall. It holds files, supplies and equipment, and its top works as a surface for a printer, reference binders or catering during meetings.

How deep is a typical office credenza?

Most office credenzas are about 18 to 24 inches deep and roughly desk height. Before buying, check that the depth still leaves about 36 inches of clearance for the desk chair when the credenza sits behind a desk.

Can a credenza be locked?

Many office credenzas have locking doors or drawers, but used units do not always come with keys. Ask whether keys are included. If not, a locksmith or a replacement lock core can usually solve it.

Do you install credenzas?

Installation is available and quoted with your order. It is most useful for credenzas that attach to a desk as part of a U-shape, units with overhead hutches, or anything that should be anchored to the wall.

What does resale pricing mean for a credenza?

Pre-owned credenzas are priced for the secondary market, which is typically well below the cost of buying the same piece new. Condition grade, brand and finish all affect the price of a specific unit.
