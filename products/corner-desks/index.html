Used Corner Office Desks and L-Shaped Layouts

Corner and L-shaped desks turn the edges of a private office into working surface. Learn which layout fits your room, how to check a used unit, and how to plan it before it arrives.

6 minute read

A private office is rarely a perfect rectangle with nothing in the way. There is a door, a window, maybe a column or a thermostat. Corner and L-shaped desks use the walls you already have and give one person a lot of surface without pushing into the middle of the room.

How to fit an L-shaped desk to your room

Measure before you fall in love with a desk. A few numbers tell you almost everything.

  1. Wall lengths. Measure each wall and note where windows, outlets, vents and the door fall.
  2. Door swing. Mark the full arc of the door so the return does not block it.
  3. Chair zone. Leave about 36 inches behind the desk so the chair can roll back and swivel between surfaces.
  4. Guest space. If visitors sit across from you, leave room for one or two guest chairs and a path to them.
  5. Power and data. Note outlet locations. The main surface usually belongs near them so monitor cables stay short.
  6. Delivery path. Measure doors, halls and elevator openings. Large one-piece tops can be hard to turn into narrow offices.

Send those measurements with a request for free space planning and you will get a layout that shows the desk, the clearances and where guests sit.

Choosing the return side and orientation

The return is the shorter side of the L. Getting it on the right side makes the desk feel natural.

  • Reach side. Most people turn toward their dominant hand for phones, notes and files.
  • Facing the door. Many executives prefer to see who is walking in, which usually means the main surface faces the entry.
  • Window glare. Put monitors at a right angle to windows so glare does not hit the screen.
  • Meeting use. If the office hosts conversations, a peninsula or open end lets a guest pull up to the desk.

When buying used, confirm whether the desk is left-handed, right-handed or reversible. Ask for photos of the underside so you can see the mounting points.

Condition grades and what to inspect

Every pre-owned piece is graded so you know what to expect.

  • Grade A, like new: minimal or no visible wear. A good choice for executive and client-facing offices.
  • Grade B, very good: light signs of use, such as faint surface marks, with full function.
  • Grade C, good: visible wear that does not affect function. Often the value pick for internal offices.

Corner desks have a few extra points to check beyond a normal desk:

  • The corner joint. Look for sagging where the main top meets the return, and confirm connector plates and brackets are included.
  • Top alignment. Both surfaces should sit at the same height with a tight seam.
  • Supports. Some L-shapes rely on a corner leg, a panel or a pedestal. Make sure every support piece comes with the desk.
  • Veneer and edges. Wood veneer tops can show lifting at edges and corners. Laminate is more forgiving.
  • Adjustable bases. On sit-stand L-shapes, run the full travel and confirm both sides rise evenly.

Completing the private office

An L-shaped desk is usually the center of a private office, but the pieces around it matter too.

  • A credenza or storage wall behind the desk can turn an L into a U. See used office credenzas.

  • Guest chairs and, in larger rooms, a small round meeting table.
  • A mobile pedestal that can sit under either side.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between a corner desk and an L-shaped desk?

People often use the terms interchangeably. An L-shaped desk has a main surface and a return set at 90 degrees. A corner desk usually has a curved or angled corner piece that joins the two sides, which can give more reach and a better spot for a monitor.

Which side should the return go on?

Put the return on the side you reach toward most, which is often the same side as your dominant hand. Also think about the door: many people like to face the entry, so the return often ends up along a side wall.

Can a used L-shaped desk be switched from left to right?

Many modular L-shaped desks are reversible, but not all. Confirm with photos of the underside and the mounting points before buying, and note that some corner units with a shaped top are made for one hand only.

What size private office do I need for an L-shaped desk?

A common L-shaped setup around 72 by 72 inches fits a room of about 10 by 10 feet or larger with space for guest chairs. Smaller offices can work with a shorter return. Free space planning can check your exact room.
