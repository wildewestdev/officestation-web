Used Reception Desks

Your reception desk is the first thing visitors see and one of the hardest-working pieces in the building. Buying pre-owned gets you more desk for the budget, as long as you check the right things first.

6 minute read

A reception desk does two jobs at once. It has to look sharp to every visitor who walks in, and it has to be a real workstation for the person who sits behind it all day. It also takes more abuse than most furniture: packages, leaning elbows, clipboards and constant contact with the front edge. Used reception desks let you get a well-built, better-looking desk than your budget would buy new.

This guide covers the common desk configurations, how condition grades apply to a piece this visible, what to measure and how delivery works.

How condition grades apply to reception

Pre-owned pieces are graded on a three-step scale. For most furniture, a grade C piece in a back room is a smart saving. Reception is different, because the front of the desk is essentially signage.

  • Grade A, like new. The safe choice for client-facing lobbies where the desk sets the tone.
  • Grade B, very good. A strong value when wear is minor and mostly on surfaces visitors will not study, such as the staff side or the return.
  • Grade C, good. Best for internal reception points, such as a warehouse check-in or a back-office front counter.
Look at the visitor side first. The transaction top and front panel carry most of the visual weight. Minor wear on the work surface behind the counter is often hidden by a monitor and a phone, but a scratch across the front face is seen by everyone.

What to measure before you choose a desk

Reception desks are large, and lobbies tend to be tighter than they look on a floor plan. Measure these before you fall for a specific piece:

  1. Overall footprint. Width and depth of the desk plus the returns.
  2. Chair clearance. Room behind the desk for the receptionist to roll back and stand up comfortably.
  3. Visitor approach. A clear path from the entry to the counter, with space for more than one person to wait.
  4. Door swings. Entry doors, office doors and any closets that open into the lobby.
  5. Accessibility. Whether your space needs a lower counter section or specific clearances. Confirm requirements with your architect or building.
  6. Power and data. Where outlets and data ports sit, and whether the desk has a path for cables.
  7. Delivery route. Door widths, hallway turns and the elevator, since long counter sections need a clear path in.

What to check on a used reception desk

Because manufacturer warranties generally do not transfer to pre-owned furniture, your inspection does the job a warranty would. Ask about or check each of these:

  • Edges and corners on the transaction top, where chips show first
  • Front panel finish, especially near the floor where carts and feet hit
  • Drawer slides and locks, and whether keys are included
  • Levelers or glides that let the desk sit flat on an uneven floor
  • Grommets and cable management openings
  • All connecting hardware between the main desk and returns

Timing a lobby refresh

If you are staying in your space, a reception swap is usually quick: remove the old desk, install the new one and reconnect power and phones. Consider scheduling it for an evening or a weekend so visitors never see an empty lobby. Frequently asked questions

What condition are used reception desks in?

Every piece carries a condition grade. Grade A is like new, grade B is very good and grade C is good. Because a reception desk faces every visitor, most buyers choose A or B for the front and transaction surfaces.

Do you help plan where a reception desk will fit?

Yes. Space planning is free. The desk footprint can be checked against your entry, door swings, walkways and any accessibility clearances you need to keep before you commit to a specific desk.

How much is delivery for a reception desk in Phoenix?

Greater Phoenix delivery is $195 for central locations and $295 for the wider metro. Outside Greater Phoenix, freight is quoted per project based on the piece and the destination.

Do used reception desks come with a manufacturer warranty?

Generally no. Office Station is an independent reseller, not an authorized dealer, and manufacturer warranties generally do not transfer to pre-owned furniture. That is why condition grades and a careful inspection matter.

Can a reception desk ship with the rest of my office furniture?

Yes. If you are also buying workstations or seating, the reception desk can go on the same delivery so you are not paying for a second trip. Ask for installation to be included in the quote.
