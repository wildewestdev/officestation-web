Cubicle Panel Refurbishment: When to Refresh and When to Replace

Cubicle panel refurbishment can make tired stations look new again, but it is not always the cheapest path. Here is how to tell whether to refresh what you have or swap in pre-owned stations.

6 minute read

Worn cubicles are rarely worn out. Most panel systems are built with steel frames that outlast the fabric, trim and worksurfaces attached to them. That is why cubicle panel refurbishment is so appealing: keep the bones, refresh the skin and avoid buying new.

Sometimes that is exactly the right call. Other times the refresh bill climbs until it costs more than simply replacing the stations with remanufactured or liquidation-grade furniture. This page helps you figure out which situation you are in.

The three levels of panel refresh

Panel refurbishment is not one thing. It helps to think of it in three levels, each with a different price profile.

Level 1: Deep cleaning

Fabric panels collect dust, stains and odors. Professional cleaning of the fabric and wiping down trim and worksurfaces can make a big visual difference. This is the lowest-cost option and involves little or no disassembly.

Level 2: Cosmetic refresh

This adds touch-up paint on trim, replacement of cracked end caps or damaged hardware, and new worksurface edges where banding has peeled. The panels stay in place, but a crew spends real time at each station.

Level 3: Full refurbishment

Panels are reupholstered with new fabric, worksurfaces are replaced and worn components like pedestals, overheads or keyboard trays are swapped. At this level, stations often come apart, which means you are paying for teardown and reinstall labor on top of materials.

Doing the math

To compare fairly, add the full replacement cost per station: furniture, plus $95 installation, plus your share of delivery, minus any volume discount. Then compare that to your refresh quote per station, including any labor to disconnect and reconnect power.

For example, 40 liquidation-grade stations at $615 is $24,600, or $23,370 with the 5% volume discount. Adding $3,800 in installation brings the total to $27,170, or about $679 per station before delivery and electrical. If a full refurbishment quote on your existing panels comes in well above that, replacement deserves a serious look. If a deep clean gets you where you need to be for a fraction of that, refresh is the clear winner.

Price replacement stations

When refreshing your panels wins

  • The frames and connectors are solid. No bent frames, no stripped connectors, panels stand straight.
  • The layout still works. You are not adding seats or changing the footprint.
  • The problem is mostly surface-level. Dirty fabric and scuffed trim, not broken parts.
  • Power and data are already routed. Leaving powered panels in place avoids rewiring costs.
  • Your team cannot move out. A clean can happen after hours at each desk without clearing the floor.

When replacing wins

  • You need a different layout or panel height. Buying parts for an older system can be hard, and reconfiguration labor adds up.
  • Parts are failing across the floor. Broken pedestals, sagging worksurfaces and missing connectors turn a refresh into a rebuild.
  • The refresh quote approaches $1,245 per station. At that point a remanufactured station is on the table with less disruption.
  • Your deadline is tight. In-stock liquidation-grade stations can be ready in as little as 72 hours.
  • You are moving anyway. Paying to tear down, refresh, move and reinstall old stations rarely beats installing replacements at the new site. See corporate relocation for how moves are planned.
Your old panels may be worth something. If replacement wins, do not assume the old stations are a disposal cost. In Office Station's liquidation markets, we give a written net offer: resale value minus removal cost. A positive number means you are paid, which can narrow the gap between refreshing and replacing. Inspection checklist before you decide

Walk the floor with this list and take photos as you go:

  1. Note the manufacturer and product line, usually on a label under the worksurface or on the panel frame.
  2. Count stations and record panel heights and sizes.
  3. Push gently on panel tops. Wobble can mean loose or damaged connectors.
  4. Check fabric for tears, fraying and stains that did not come out with spot cleaning.
  5. Look at worksurface edges for peeling banding and at undersides for sagging.
  6. Open every pedestal drawer and test locks and slides.
  7. Identify which panels carry power and data.
  8. Write down what you want to change about the layout, if anything.

With that list in hand, send photos and counts to hello@officestation.com. We will price pre-owned replacement options so you can weigh them against your refresh quote with real numbers on both sides.

Frequently asked questions

What does cubicle panel refurbishment include?

It ranges from a deep clean of the fabric and trim to full reupholstery, new trim and replaced worn parts. The more you replace, the closer the cost gets to a remanufactured station. Frames and connectors must be sound for any level of refresh to make sense.

Is it cheaper to refurbish panels or buy remanufactured cubicles?

A light clean on panels in good structural shape is usually the cheapest option. Once you add reupholstery, new worksurfaces, parts and reconfiguration labor, compare the total per station with $1,245 for a remanufactured 6x6 or $615 for liquidation-grade. If refresh costs approach those numbers, replacement often wins.

Can I refurbish panels if I also need a new layout?

You can, but reconfiguration adds labor and sometimes parts, and older systems may not have the pieces you need. If you are changing both the look and the layout, get a replacement quote too so you can compare.

What happens to my old panels if I replace them?

In Office Station's liquidation markets, we can make a written net offer on them: resale value minus removal cost. If the panels have resale value, that offer can offset part of the replacement cost.

How do I get an honest recommendation?

Email hello@officestation.com with the brand, station count, panel heights and a few photos, including close-ups of worn spots and connectors. We will price remanufactured and liquidation-grade replacements so you can weigh them against your refresh quote.
