The Advantage of Office Furniture Liquidators: How Net Offers Work

A liquidator does not just haul furniture away. It weighs what your furniture will resell for against what it costs to remove, and puts the difference in a written net offer.

6 minute read

When an office closes, shrinks or moves, the furniture has to go somewhere. You can sell it piece by piece, pay a junk hauler, or hand the job to office furniture liquidators. The real advantage of a liquidator is that the value of your furniture is counted against the cost of removing it, so good furniture helps pay for its own exit.

The net offer formula

Office Station prices every liquidation the same way:

Resale value of the furniture minus removal cost equals your net offer.

  • Resale value is what the furniture can realistically sell for on the pre-owned market.
  • Removal cost is the labor, trucks, access coordination and disposal needed to clear the space.
  • A positive net offer means resale value is higher than removal cost, and you are paid.
  • A negative net offer means removal costs more than the furniture will bring, and the offer shows what the job costs you.

You get this in writing before anything leaves the building, so you are comparing a real number, not an estimate that changes on move-out day.

What raises resale value

  • Brand. Commercial lines from manufacturers such as Herman Miller, Steelcase, Knoll, Haworth, Teknion and Allsteel have steady pre-owned demand.
  • Matched quantity. Twenty identical workstations resell better than twenty different desks, because buyers furnish whole teams at once.
  • Condition. Clean worksurfaces, intact fabric panels and working chair mechanisms all count.
  • Completeness. Keys for pedestals, panel connectors, power components and all worksurface brackets keep a system resellable.
  • Current demand. Popular sizes and finishes move faster than dated ones.

What raises removal cost

  • Access. Upper floors, shared freight elevators and limited dock time add labor hours.
  • Timing. Nights and weekends crews are available, and building rules often require them.
  • Scope beyond furniture. Trash, abandoned files, cabling and fixtures all add work.
  • E-waste. Computers, monitors and printers need proper handling, with certificates of data destruction where drives are involved.
  • Handback standard. Leases often require a broom-clean space, which takes time after the last piece leaves.
Protect the top line. Before your walkthrough, gather pedestal keys, leave connector hardware with the furniture and hold off on letting anyone take pieces home. Broken sets and missing parts are the fastest way to turn a positive offer into a smaller one.

How the process works, step by step

  1. Walkthrough. Typically within 48 hours in Greater Phoenix and 2 to 3 business days in other markets.
  2. Itemized inventory. The furniture is counted and described so the offer ties to specific items.
  3. Written net offer. Delivered within 5 business days, showing resale value, removal cost and the net.
  4. Building coordination. Certificates of insurance, freight elevator reservations and dock scheduling are arranged with your property manager.
  5. Removal. Crews work nights or weekends if needed, and e-waste is handled with certificates of data destruction.
  6. Handback. The space is left broom clean and photo-documented, and you receive a diversion summary showing where material went.

Timing: start before your lease deadline

The net offer is only half of the advantage. The other half is time. A liquidation that starts two weeks before your lease ends leaves little room to schedule elevator windows, after-hours crews and a clean handback. Starting earlier gives you room to compare the written offer against other options and still hit your date.

Work backward from your move-out day:

  • Walkthrough: allow up to 48 hours in Greater Phoenix, or 2 to 3 business days elsewhere.
  • Written offer: allow up to 5 business days after the walkthrough.
  • Building approvals: leave time for your property manager to review certificates of insurance and book the freight elevator and dock.
  • Removal and handback: plan the crew window, plus time for the broom-clean handback before your landlord inspection.

What to have ready for the walkthrough

  • A rough count of workstations, chairs, tables and storage, even if it is approximate.
  • Your lease end date and any handback requirements in the lease.
  • Building contact information for insurance certificates, elevator and dock rules.
  • A list of anything staying with you, such as equipment moving to a new office.
  • Where computers, monitors and other e-waste are located.

Frequently asked questions

How do office furniture liquidators calculate an offer?

Office Station starts with the resale value of your furniture and subtracts the cost to remove it. The result is a written net offer. If resale value is higher than removal cost, the offer is positive and you are paid.

When do I get paid for my office furniture?

You are paid when the net offer is positive, meaning resale value is greater than removal cost. The written offer spells out the amount so you know the number before any crew arrives. If removal costs more than the furniture will resell for, the offer shows that difference instead.

What if my furniture is not worth much?

Older, mismatched or damaged furniture may resell for less than it costs to remove, which produces a negative net offer. Even then, one crew handling removal, e-waste and a broom-clean handback is usually simpler than coordinating several vendors. The written offer lets you compare before you decide.

How fast can I get an offer?

Walkthroughs typically happen within 48 hours in Greater Phoenix and within 2 to 3 business days in other service markets. Written offers follow within 5 business days of the walkthrough.
