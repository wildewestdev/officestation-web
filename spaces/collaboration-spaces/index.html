Collaboration Space Furniture

Team areas get used hard and redesigned often, which makes them a smart place to buy pre-owned. Furnish huddle rooms, project tables and open meeting zones with graded designer furniture for a fraction of new.

6 minute read

Collaboration spaces are where furniture budgets quietly go sideways. A single new conference table and a set of matching chairs can cost as much as several workstations, and team areas tend to be reworked every time the way people meet changes. Buying pre-owned collaboration space furniture lets you build well-designed team areas from brands like Herman Miller, Steelcase, Knoll, Haworth and Teknion without committing new-furniture money to a room you may reshape in two years.

Choosing collaboration tables

The table sets the tone for the whole space. A few practical checks help you pick the right one.

  • Allow about 30 inches of edge per seated person for comfortable working sessions. Tighter spacing works for short meetings only.
  • Leave clearance behind chairs so people can push back and walk around the table without squeezing past colleagues.
  • Look at the base, not just the top. Pedestal bases are easier on knees at small tables. Leg bases at the corners suit longer rectangles.
  • Plan power. Grommets or surface power modules save a tangle of extension cords. Confirm whether a pre-owned table has them before you plan around them.
  • Consider mobility. Tables on casters, or flip-top training tables, make it realistic to rearrange a room weekly.

Choosing collaboration seating

Seating is where meeting length matters most. People tolerate a simple side chair for a 15-minute stand-up but will be uncomfortable in one for a three-hour planning session.

Task chairs for working sessions

If teams spend long stretches at the table, use real task chairs with height and recline adjustment. Pre-owned designer task chairs are one of the best values in the used market. Browse options on our pre-owned office chairs page.

Side and guest chairs for short meetings

Four-leg or sled-base chairs are lighter, cheaper and easier to move. They suit huddle rooms and areas that double as overflow seating.

Lounge seating for informal zones

Lounge chairs and small sofas signal that an area is for conversation, not formal meetings. Pair them with side tables at a usable height for a laptop or coffee.

Stools for counter-height tables

Choose stools with a footrest and a height that matches the table. Mismatched heights are one of the most common mistakes in stand-up spaces.

Grade by use, not by room. A Grade B or C table in a busy project room makes sense because it will pick up wear anyway. Save Grade A pieces for spaces clients see, like a front meeting room.

Understanding condition grades

Every pre-owned piece we sell carries a condition grade so you know what you are buying:

  • Grade A, like new. Little to no visible wear. Best for client-facing and executive areas.
  • Grade B, very good. Light signs of use that most people will not notice from across the room.
  • Grade C, good. Structurally sound with more visible wear. Strong value for high-traffic team areas.

Checklist before you buy

  • Measure the room, including door widths and any path the furniture must travel
  • Count seats for normal use and for the biggest regular meeting
  • Decide on sitting, standing or both
  • Note where outlets and data drops are, and whether tables need built-in power
  • Ask for the condition grade on each table and chair
  • Check finish consistency if pieces from different sources will sit side by side
  • Confirm lead time against the date the room needs to be usable

Collaboration areas inside an open plan

Team areas work best when they are planned alongside workstations, not squeezed into leftover corners. A small huddle table between rows of stations gives people a place to talk without disturbing neighbors. A project room near the teams that use it most gets used far more than one at the end of a long hallway.

Frequently asked questions

What furniture do I need for a collaboration space?

Most team areas combine a table sized to the group, seating that suits meeting length, and a surface for sharing ideas such as a whiteboard or screen wall. Short stand-up huddles work with tall tables and stools, while longer working sessions need task chairs. Power access at the table is worth planning for either way.

How do your condition grades work?

Pre-owned furniture is graded A like new, B very good or C good. Grade A pieces show little to no wear, Grade B has light signs of use, and Grade C is structurally sound with more visible wear. Ask for the grade on every piece in your order so there are no surprises.

Do pre-owned tables and chairs come with a manufacturer warranty?

Generally no. Office Station is an independent reseller, not an authorized dealer, and manufacturer warranties generally do not transfer to pre-owned furniture. Condition grading is how we set clear expectations before you buy.

How fast can I furnish a team area?

In-stock liquidation-grade furniture can be ready in as little as 72 hours. Remanufactured product usually takes 7 to 14 days and new furniture runs 4 to 6 weeks. Greater Phoenix delivery is $195 central or $295 metro, and freight elsewhere is quoted per project.

Can you help me lay out a collaboration area?

Yes. Space planning is free. Send your floor plan, how many people use the area and how they meet, and we will suggest table sizes, seating counts and a layout that leaves room to move.
