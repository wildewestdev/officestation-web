The Benefits of Standing Desks, Minus the Hype

Height-adjustable desks can make a workday more comfortable, but they are not magic. Here is an honest look at the pros, the cons, and how to add them without paying new-furniture prices.

6 minute read

People shop for standing desks because they are tired of sitting. That is a good reason. But the marketing around height-adjustable furniture tends to overpromise, and buyers end up surprised by the cost, the noise, the cable mess, or the fact that they rarely stand. This guide covers what a standing desk can realistically do for you, where it falls short, and how to add adjustable surfaces to an office on a sensible budget.

The real benefits of standing desks

The biggest advantage is not standing itself. It is choice. A height-adjustable desk lets you change position whenever your body asks for it, instead of being locked into one posture for eight hours.

  • Posture variety. Switching between sitting and standing breaks up long static periods, which many people find reduces end-of-day stiffness.
  • A better fit for more bodies. A fixed desk is built for an average person. An adjustable surface can be set correctly for a tall employee, a short employee, and anyone in between, at both sitting and standing height.
  • Energy during long tasks. Some people feel more alert standing through calls, reviews or afternoon slumps.
  • Easier informal collaboration. A coworker can stand at your raised desk to look at a screen without crouching over your shoulder.
  • Flexible and shared seating. In hoteling or hybrid setups, one desk has to work for whoever sits there that day. Adjustability solves that.

The drawbacks nobody mentions

Standing desks come with tradeoffs. Knowing them up front saves money and frustration.

  • Standing all day is not the goal. Swapping one static posture for another does not solve much. Standing for long stretches can bring its own discomfort in the feet, legs and lower back.
  • Many desks stop moving. If adjusting is slow or awkward, people pick a height once and never change it. You paid for a feature nobody uses.
  • Cables need slack. Monitors, docks and power cords must travel with the surface. Without cable management, raising the desk can unplug equipment or snag cords.
  • Mechanisms can fail. Electric bases have motors, control boxes and hand switches. Crank and counterbalance models have their own wear points. More moving parts means more things to check, especially on used units.
  • Cost adds up fast. A single adjustable desk is manageable. Outfitting a whole floor at new prices is a real budget line.
Make it easy to move. The best predictor of whether a standing desk gets used is how effortless it is to adjust. Pair adjustable surfaces with a good task chair, an anti-fatigue mat, and monitor arms so the screen follows the change in height.

How to add standing desks affordably

You do not need to buy everything new, and you do not need to convert every seat on day one. A phased, pre-owned approach stretches the budget much further.

1. Start with who actually wants one

Survey your team before you order. Some people will use an adjustable desk constantly, others never. Buying for demand instead of headcount is the single easiest way to save.

2. Look at pre-owned designer furniture

When offices downsize or relocate, quality height-adjustable surfaces often come out of those spaces. Office Station sells pre-owned office furniture from makers like Herman Miller, Steelcase, Knoll, Haworth, Humanscale and Teknion as an independent reseller. Pieces are sold with a condition grade: A like new, B very good, or C good. Availability of adjustable pieces changes with what comes in, so ask what is on hand.

3. Know the pre-owned warranty reality

Office Station is not an authorized dealer, and manufacturer warranties generally do not transfer to pre-owned furniture. For an adjustable desk, that means you should inspect it closely:

  • Run the desk through its full range several times, loaded if possible.
  • Listen for grinding, clicking or uneven travel between legs.
  • Test every button on the keypad, including any memory presets.
  • Check the top for wobble at full standing height.
  • Confirm the power cord, control box and hand switch are all present.

4. Consider adjustable surfaces inside workstations

If your team already works in cubicles, you may not need freestanding desks at all. Swapping fixed worksurfaces for adjustable ones can keep your panels, storage and privacy intact. For context, a 6'x6' workstation runs about $615 furniture-only at liquidation grade, $1,245 remanufactured and $1,920 new. Those figures exclude electrical, delivery and professional installation, and adjustable components will change the configuration price, so treat them as a baseline rather than a quote.

The bottom line

Standing desks are worth it when people actually move between sitting and standing, and when the desk makes that effortless. They are not worth it as a blanket purchase that sits at one height forever. Buy for real demand, inspect pre-owned mechanisms carefully, and use your existing workstation system where you can.

Frequently asked questions

Are standing desks actually good for you?

The main benefit is the ability to change posture during the day. Sitting all day and standing all day both have downsides, so the value comes from moving between the two. Talk to a health professional about any specific medical concern.

How long should I stand at a standing desk?

There is no single right number. Many people start with short standing periods and build up gradually, switching whenever they feel stiff or fatigued. A desk that is easy to adjust makes switching more likely.

Can I buy a pre-owned height-adjustable desk?

Yes. Office Station sells pre-owned designer furniture as an independent reseller, and height-adjustable surfaces show up in inventory when offices are liquidated. Availability varies, so ask what is in stock and what condition grade it carries.

Do manufacturer warranties cover used standing desks?

Generally no. Manufacturer warranties usually do not transfer to pre-owned furniture, and Office Station is an independent reseller rather than an authorized dealer. Test the lift mechanism and controls before you commit.

Can I convert existing cubicles to standing height?

Often, yes. Many workstation systems accept height-adjustable worksurfaces in place of fixed ones, but compatibility depends on the system and the panel configuration. Share your current layout during a free space planning session to find out.
