Used Ergonomic Task Chairs

A well-built ergonomic task chair can serve several owners. Here is how to pick a pre-owned chair that fits your team, what our grades mean and what to inspect before you buy.

6 minute read

Why buy a pre-owned ergonomic task chair

A task chair is the piece of office furniture your team touches the most. It is also where the gap between budget and quality is most noticeable. A commercial ergonomic chair from an established manufacturer is designed for eight or more hours of use a day, year after year, with replaceable parts and adjustable mechanisms that hold up.

That durability is why used ergonomic task chairs are one of the best values in office furniture. When companies downsize or relocate, their chairs often still have years of life left. Buying them graded and inspected gets your team better seating than a similarly priced new chair from a lower tier.

Ergonomic adjustments that actually matter

Not every chair labeled ergonomic adjusts the same way. These are the features that make the biggest difference over a workday. Use them to compare models side by side.

AdjustmentWhat it doesWho needs it most
Seat heightLets feet rest flat with thighs roughly levelEveryone, especially very short or tall users
Seat depthSlides the seat so the edge does not press behind the kneesShared chairs, users with longer or shorter legs
Lumbar supportSupports the lower back curve, fixed or adjustableLong-hour desk workers
Recline tensionControls how much effort it takes to lean backUsers of different body weights
Recline lockHolds the back at a set angleFocused typing work
Armrest height and widthKeeps shoulders relaxed and arms close to the keyboardHeavy keyboard and mouse users
Armrest pivot and depthMoves arms out of the way or closer to the deskAnyone at a narrow or height-adjustable desk
Headrest (optional)Supports the neck when reclinedUsers who recline for calls or reading

For shared or hot-desk seating, prioritize chairs that adjust quickly or self-adjust. For assigned seats, a fully adjustable chair lets each person dial in a fit once and leave it.

What our condition grades mean for chairs

  • Grade A, like new. Upholstery and mesh are clean and intact, armrest pads show minimal wear, and all mechanisms work smoothly. Good for executive offices and client-facing areas.
  • Grade B, very good. Light signs of use such as small marks on the base or slight softening of armrest pads. Every adjustment works. The sweet spot for most teams.
  • Grade C, good. Visible cosmetic wear, but structurally sound with working mechanisms. A practical choice for training rooms, overflow seating or tight budgets.

What to check on any used task chair

Whether you buy from us or anyone else, walk through this checklist before you commit.

  1. Gas cylinder. Raise the chair to full height, sit down firmly and confirm it does not sink over a few minutes.
  2. Tilt mechanism. Recline fully, adjust the tension and test the lock at every position. Listen for grinding or clunking.
  3. Mesh or upholstery. Look for sagging, fraying at the frame edge, tears, pilling and stains.
  4. Seat foam. Press the front edge. Foam should rebound, not stay compressed.
  5. Armrests. Test every direction of adjustment and check pads for cracks or peeling.
  6. Lumbar. Make sure adjustable lumbar moves and holds its position.
  7. Base and casters. Look for cracks at the base arms and confirm the casters roll smoothly and suit your floor type.
  8. Matching sets. If you need many chairs, confirm model, frame color and fabric match across the whole group.

Buying task chairs for a whole team

Outfitting a floor is different from buying one chair. A few tips:

  • Standardize on one or two models. It simplifies training staff on adjustments and makes replacement parts easier to find.
  • Mix grades by area. Use Grade A where visitors see seating, and Grade B or C in back-office rows.
  • Match the desk. Chairs with adjustable-width arms fit better under benching and narrow work surfaces. See our desking systems if you are replacing desks too.
  • Plan for timing. Liquidation-grade inventory can be ready in as little as 72 hours when in stock, remanufactured furniture usually takes 7 to 14 days, and new runs 4 to 6 weeks.
  • Coordinate delivery. In Greater Phoenix, delivery is $195 central or $295 metro. Elsewhere in the country, freight is quoted per project.

Frequently asked questions

Are used ergonomic chairs worth it?

Often, yes. Commercial ergonomic chairs from major manufacturers are built for years of daily use, and most wear shows up in parts you can inspect, such as armrest pads, casters and upholstery. Buying graded pre-owned lets you get a higher-end chair for your budget.

Do used Herman Miller or Steelcase chairs come with a warranty?

Generally, no. Manufacturer warranties usually do not transfer to pre-owned furniture. Office Station is an independent reseller, not an authorized dealer for any brand, which is why every chair is condition graded before sale.

What do the A, B and C grades mean for chairs?

Grade A is like new with minimal visible wear. Grade B is very good, with light signs of use and full function. Grade C is good, with noticeable cosmetic wear but working mechanisms.

Can I buy a large quantity of matching chairs?

Yes, when inventory allows. Matching quantities usually come from office liquidation projects, so availability changes. Tell us the model, color and count you want and we will let you know what we can match.

Can you take our old chairs when we upgrade?

Yes. Our liquidation service provides a written net offer on existing furniture, which is resale value minus removal cost. If it is positive, you are paid.
