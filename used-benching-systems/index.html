Pre-owned benching budgets

Used Benching Systems, Priced by the Seat

Benching is bought in seats, so it should be budgeted in seats. Here is how pre-owned benching compares to our workstation tiers, and how volume pricing changes the math at 20, 50 and 100 seats.

6 minute read

When you shop used benching systems, the first number you want is simple: what does each seat cost? Benching runs share frames, beams and cable trays, so a price for "one bench" does not tell you much. This page breaks down pre-owned benching budgets per seat, compares the three condition tiers, and shows how our volume pricing works at 20, 50 and 100 seats.

If you want a primer on what benching is and how it compares to cubicles, start with our used cubicles page. This one is about the numbers.

The per-seat benchmark

We quote benching per project, because a six-seat run of shallow fixed-height tops and a six-seat run of deep sit-stand surfaces are very different products. To keep budgets grounded, we price benching against our standard workstation tiers. A 6' x 6' workstation, furniture only:

Volume pricing at 20, 50 and 100 seats

Benching projects tend to be large, which is where volume pricing makes a real difference. Discounts are 5% at 20 or more stations, 8% at 50 or more, and 12% at 100 or more. Here is what that looks like using the benchmark prices, furniture only:

At 100 seats, the gap between liquidation-grade and new is more than $114,000 on furniture alone. Even remanufactured, which has been refreshed before sale, comes in about $59,000 under new at that size.

Check the threshold before you finalize. Because the discount applies to the whole order, landing just under a tier can cost you. At benchmark pricing, 20 seats at 5% off costs the same as 19 seats at full price. And 50 seats at 8% off costs less than 49 seats without the discount, just as 100 seats at 12% off costs less than 99. If you are close to a threshold, price the next tier up and keep the extra seats for hoteling or growth.

Building the full project budget

The furniture line is the biggest number, but it is not the only one. Prices exclude electrical, delivery and professional installation. A realistic benching budget includes:

  • Installation: $95 per workstation with a $495 minimum. That is $1,900 for 20 seats, $4,750 for 50 and $9,500 for 100.
  • Delivery: $195 in central Greater Phoenix or $295 in the metro. Freight elsewhere nationwide is quoted per project.
  • Electrical: benching runs need power at the spine. Get your electrician or landlord involved early, because this work is separate from the furniture.
  • Seating: every seat needs a chair. Budget it at the same time using pre-owned office chairs.
  • Space planning: free, and worth doing before you lock in a seat count.

Choosing a tier for each part of the floor

You do not have to buy the whole floor in one tier. Large benching projects often split like this:

  • Visible zones near entrances and meeting rooms: Grade A liquidation-grade or remanufactured product.
  • General team areas: Grade B liquidation-grade, which keeps the per-seat cost near the low end of the range.
  • Training rooms, project rooms and overflow: Grade C, structurally sound and fully functional with visible wear.

The catch is consistency. Benching looks best when runs within one area share the same system and finish, so split tiers by room or zone rather than within a single run.

What to confirm on a large pre-owned benching order

  1. Enough matching inventory. Confirm the full seat count is available in one system and finish before you commit, especially for 50 or more seats.
  2. Complete components. Every run needs its end supports, beams, screens and cable trays. Missing parts are the most common cause of install delays.
  3. Surface size per seat. A 60-inch-wide seat and a 72-inch-wide seat change how many seats fit per run and the price per seat.
  4. Height adjustability. Decide at the start whether each seat needs sit-stand, since it narrows inventory and changes the budget.
  5. Building logistics. Freight elevator access, dock times and after-hours rules affect install scheduling for large orders.
  6. Warranty expectations. Office Station sells pre-owned furniture as an independent reseller, not an authorized dealer. Manufacturer warranties generally do not transfer to pre-owned pieces.

Frequently asked questions

How is used benching priced?

Benching is quoted per project because brand, run length, surface depth and features all change the price. We use our workstation tiers as the benchmark: a 6' x 6' workstation, furniture only, is $615 liquidation-grade, $1,245 remanufactured and $1,920 new. Your quote will list the actual benching price per seat.

When does volume pricing start?

Volume pricing is 5% at 20 or more stations, 8% at 50 or more, and 12% at 100 or more. If your seat count lands just under a threshold, compare the cost of adding a few seats, since the discount can make the larger order cost the same or less.

What costs are not included in the per-seat price?

Furniture prices exclude electrical, delivery and professional installation. Installation is $95 per workstation with a $495 minimum. Greater Phoenix delivery is $195 central or $295 metro, and freight elsewhere nationwide is quoted per project.

How fast can used benching be installed?

In-stock liquidation-grade furniture can be ready in as little as 72 hours. Remanufactured product usually takes 7 to 14 days, and new furniture typically runs 4 to 6 weeks. Large seat counts are easier to fill in one matching system when you give yourself some lead time.

Can I sell my old workstations to help pay for benching?

Yes, if you have enough furniture to justify removal. Our liquidation service gives you a written net offer equal to the resale value minus the removal cost. When that number is positive, we pay you, which can offset part of the new benching budget.
