The Actual Cost of Assembling Your New Office Furniture Yourself

Assembling workstations yourself looks free until you count the hours, the missing parts and the work that did not get done. Here is a simple way to compare DIY labor against a $95 per workstation install rate.

6 minute read

When you get a furniture quote, installation is an easy line to cut. Your team is capable, the furniture comes with hardware, and a weekend seems like plenty of time. But the cost to assemble office furniture yourself is not zero. It is just hidden in payroll, missed deadlines and the station that never quite sits level.

This guide gives you a straightforward way to compare the true cost of DIY assembly with a published professional rate, so you can make the call with numbers instead of optimism.

The five hidden costs of DIY assembly

1. Paid hours that are not their real job

Every hour an office manager, IT lead or sales rep spends with an Allen wrench is an hour you are paying for at their normal rate. Use a loaded hourly rate that includes payroll taxes and benefits, not just base pay.

2. The learning curve

Panel-based workstations are not flat-pack bookshelves. Connectors, panel alignment, work surface brackets and cable management all have a sequence. The first station takes the longest, and mistakes early on often mean taking panels apart and starting again.

3. Lost productivity and overtime

If assembly happens during business hours, the work those people would normally do waits. If it happens after hours, you may owe overtime. Either way, it shows up somewhere.

4. Tools, damage and missing parts

You may need drivers, levels, carts and ladders. Pre-owned and new components alike can be scratched or chipped if handled without pads and proper lifting. A missing bracket discovered on Sunday night can stall an entire row.

5. Injury and liability

Panels and work surfaces are heavy and awkward. A strained back or a dropped panel is a real cost, and it lands on your business.

Time one station first. Before committing to DIY, have your team assemble a single workstation from start to finish and write down the total people-hours. Multiply by your station count and your loaded hourly rate. That one test gives you a far better estimate than any rule of thumb.

When doing it yourself makes sense

  • You are setting up a few freestanding desks, not panel systems.
  • Someone on staff has assembled the same system before.
  • There is no move-in deadline and no clients arriving Monday.
  • Your team has slack time that is genuinely unbilled.

When professional installation pays for itself

  • Six or more panel-based workstations.
  • A fixed move-in date or a lease start that cannot slip.
  • Stations that must line up with power and data locations.
  • Staff whose hourly value is higher than the per-station rate.

Prep checklist either way

Whether your team or a crew does the work, these steps prevent most delays:

  1. Confirm the floor plan and mark station locations with tape before delivery day.
  2. Reserve the freight elevator and loading dock with building management.
  3. Clear the floor of boxes, old furniture and anything blocking aisles.
  4. Count components against the order as soon as they arrive.
  5. Schedule the electrician after stations are placed, not before.

Frequently asked questions

How much does professional workstation installation cost?

Office Station installs workstations at $95 per workstation with a $495 minimum. That means orders of one to five workstations pay the $495 minimum, and six or more are priced at $95 each. Electrical work is not included.

How long does it take to assemble a cubicle yourself?

It depends on the system, the size of the station and whether your team has done it before. First-time assemblers often take much longer than expected, especially on panel systems with connectors and wire management. Time one station before scheduling the rest.

Is DIY assembly cheaper for a small office?

Sometimes. For a handful of simple desks, a capable team may come out ahead. Once you are into panel-based workstations or larger counts, lost productivity and rework usually close the gap quickly.

Does installation include electrical hookups?

No. Workstation prices exclude electrical, delivery and professional installation, and installation does not include hardwiring. Plan for a licensed electrician if your stations need power connected to the building.
